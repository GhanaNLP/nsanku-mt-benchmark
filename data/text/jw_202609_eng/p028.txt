SHUN UNWHOLESOME SPEECH
<<<P>>>
6 The Bible foretold that in the last days, people would be “boastful, haughty,” and “not open to any agreement.” (2 Tim. 3:1-3) People today often display those traits in their speech. For example, news reports tell of verbal disputes that escalate into violence. Abusive speech is also common in many homes. So is obscene language, which can be heard coming from the mouths of adults and children alike. (Eph. 5:3, 4) Such speech certainly reflects the spirit of the world.
<<<P>>>
7 As Christians, we should be exemplary in our speech whether we are communicating in person or electronically. (Jas. 1:26, 27) In examining our speech, we can ask ourselves: ‘Do I use foul language when I am provoked, perhaps when I am dealing with people who don’t know that I am one of Jehovah’s Witnesses? Do my conversations with fellow believers tend to be negative, cynical, even sarcastic? Do I speak harshly to my family members? What would they say about my speech?’

8 What can we do if we discern that our speech reflects the spirit of the world? We need to change more than our words. Remember what Jesus said: “Whatever comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and those things defile a man.” (Matt. 15:18) As Jesus indicated, our speech usually reflects how we feel inside. That is why the Bible links “abusive speech” with negative emotions—such as anger, wrath, and malicious bitterness. (Eph. 4:31) If you realize that you need to put away such traits, ask Jehovah in prayer to help you to cultivate more fully “the new personality.” (Read Colossians 3:8-10.) You might also do research to find articles in our publications that offer help on this topic. Be resolved not to let the spirit of the world influence your attitude or pollute your speech.—Eph. 4:29.
