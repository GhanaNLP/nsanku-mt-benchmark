4 How did the priests of Israel help worshippers to approach Jehovah and serve him acceptably? In two important ways. First, priests represented God before man by serving as teachers. How so? Malachi 2:7 says: “The lips of a priest should safeguard knowledge, and people should seek the law from his mouth, because he is the messenger of Jehovah of armies.” So the priests had the assignment to teach the Law of Moses to the people of Israel, helping them to understand it and to live by it. As an example, in Nehemiah’s day, 13 different Levites assisted priest Ezra to explain the Law to the people. They put meaning into it and helped the people to understand what was read. In this way, the Israelites were able to apply God’s laws in their lives and thereby draw closer to him.—Neh. 8:2, 3, 6-8.

5 Second, priests represented man before God. The priests did this by offering sacrifices in behalf of the people. The Israelites were well-aware of their daily errors and failings. They knew that they needed to have their sins wiped away. (Ps. 51:9) The priests and the sacrifices helped make that possible. What a relief it was for repentant Israelites to know that their sins had been forgiven! Through the priesthood arrangement, we see Jehovah’s love, compassion, and interest in the well-being of his people and, ultimately, in their salvation.
