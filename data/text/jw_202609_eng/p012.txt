12 Build your Bible knowledge. Jehoshaphat’s prayer revealed his depth of knowledge. He understood how Israel had inherited its land through God’s promise to Abraham. (Gen. 12:7) Jehoshaphat’s words were similar to those Solomon had uttered when praying at the inauguration of the temple. (2 Chron. 6:28-30; 20:5-9) Jehoshaphat’s knowledge strengthened his trust in Jehovah and his resolve to obey Him. To strengthen our resolve, we need to keep building our “treasure store” by adding new truths to what we already know. (Matt. 13:52) As we do, we strengthen our determination to obey Jehovah even during challenging situations.
<<<P>>>
13 Cultivate humility. Jehoshaphat’s prayer also reflected his humility. As king, he could have been tempted to rely on his strength and ability to handle the threat. Instead, in a public prayer, he humbly acknowledged that Judah needed God’s help. (2 Chron. 20:12) The king’s humble attitude made it easier for him to accept God’s direction. Humility will likewise help us to maintain an obedient heart in any circumstance.—Phil. 2:5, 8.
<<<P>>>
14 Consider how your actions affect others. Jehovah answered Jehoshaphat’s prayer by giving him clear instructions through the prophet Jahaziel, who told the people that they would not need to fight. Instead, he instructed them: “Take your position, stand still, and see the salvation of Jehovah in your behalf.” The Bible says that Jehoshaphat then “bowed low with his face to the ground.” At that, all the people “fell down . . . to worship Jehovah.” The next day as they went out, the king urged the people to “put faith in Jehovah” and “in his prophets.”—2 Chron. 20:14-20.
