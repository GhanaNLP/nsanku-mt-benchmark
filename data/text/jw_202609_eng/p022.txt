7 The first person specifically called a priest in the Scriptures was Melchizedek. (Gen. 14:18) He was not only a priest but also a king. Jehovah later promised that the Messiah would be both a king and a priest, as Melchizedek was. But the Messiah's rule would benefit mankind forever.-Ps. 110:4; Heb. 7:17.
<<<P>>>
8 In time, Jehovah formed the nation of Israel and gave them the Mosaic Law, which included instructions regarding the priesthood. Moses' brother, Aaron, and his descendants were assigned to serve as priests. (Ex. 40:12-15) With the assistance of the Levites, the priests would offer sacrifices in behalf of the nation and of individual Israelites. Jehovah gave specific guidelines regarding the types of offerings to be given, the purpose of the offerings, and how they were to be presented. This arrangement provided a foretaste of what was to come.

9 At his baptism, Jesus became the High Priest serving in Jehovah's great spiritual temple. (Heb. 8:1) Jesus later gave up his perfect human life to atone completely for man's sins. His sacrifice was far superior to the animal sacrifices offered by the priests of Israel. Jesus continues to serve in this priestly capacity, appearing before God on our behalf. (Heb. 9:24) Under God's Kingdom arrangement, 144,000 anointed ones will serve alongside our High Priest, Jesus, as kings and priests. (1 Pet. 1:3, 4; 2:9) Once resurrected to heaven, they will work along with God and Christ to bring obedient mankind to perfection and make the earth a paradise. How we look forward to seeing their work completed!
