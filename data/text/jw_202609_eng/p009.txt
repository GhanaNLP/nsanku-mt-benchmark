The Israelites obeyed Jehovah regardless of how the Egyptians might have reacted
<<<P>>>
(See paragraph 3)
<<<P>>>
To avoid the tenth and final plague, they were to sacrifice a ram or a male goat and splash its blood on the doorposts of their houses. (Ex. 12:5, 7) Jehovah would pass over the dwellings marked with the blood and spare the firstborn of man and of beast of each household. However, the Israelites could have been tempted to disobey. Why? They might have feared retaliation from the Egyptians, who considered rams to be sacred. (Ex. 8:25-27) Still, the Israelites saw the urgent need for obedience and “did just as Jehovah had commanded.” (Ex. 12:28) Jehovah blessed them by sparing their firstborn.
<<<P>>>
4 That very night, the Israelites left Egypt. Jehovah first led them toward the northern end of the Red Sea, but then he gave them instructions that defied human logic. He told them to turn south and encamp between the sea and the mountains. At that campsite, it seemed that they would be trapped between Pharaoh’s army that was pursu-ing them and the Red Sea. Yet, the Israelites once again did as Jehovah had commanded through his servant Moses. (Ex. 14:1-4) But when they saw the Egyptians approaching, they “became terrified” and even accused Moses of leading them to their death! (Ex. 14:5-11) Why did they begin regretting their decision to obey? And how did Jehovah view their attitude?
<<<P>>>
5 The Bible says that at the Red Sea, the Israelites “did not appreciate [Jehovah’s] wonderful works” in Egypt or “remember” his loyal love. (Read Psalm 106:7 and footnote.) They had observed how Jehovah protected their firstborn during the tenth plague. But they did not fully grasp Jehovah’s love for them or trust that he would save them again. Discerning their heart condition, Jehovah said that the people “rebelled . . . by the Red Sea.” Even though they followed Moses through the Red Sea, they never corrected their attitude.
