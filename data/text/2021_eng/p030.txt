Glossary (Budget Terminology)
<<<P>>>
1D1F (One District One Factory): The setting up of factories and industries which, will in turn, move the country towards greater industrialization. It is aimed at creating more jobs for Ghanaians across the length and breadth of the country.
<<<P>>>
Accountability: This is when Government is answerable to its citizens by making known its activities and disclosing the results of such actions. This may include the responsibility for money or other properties that Government holds in trust.
<<<P>>>
Allocation: The process of sharing or distributing resources amoung Government ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs).
<<<P>>>
Alleviation: This is the process of making a problem less severe.
<<<P>>>
BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): a term used to group together these academic disciplines. This term is typically used when addressing education policy and curriculum choices in schools to improve competitiveness in science and technology development.
<<<P>>>
Budget: A budget is a plan that outlines where to get money from and what to spend it on. For instance, a family can draw up a budget that will show how much money is available and how it should be prioritised for spending (for example, rent, utilities and food). If more money is needed, the family would need to consider raising more income or taking a loan.
<<<P>>>
Budget deficit: This occurs when Government expenditure is more than the revenue it mobilises.
<<<P>>>
Capital Expenditure: This is money spent on major infrastructure projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water systems, plant and machinery.
<<<P>>>
Compensation of Employees: This is made up of wages & salaries and salary-related allowances such as social security, gratuities and pensions paid to public sector workers.
