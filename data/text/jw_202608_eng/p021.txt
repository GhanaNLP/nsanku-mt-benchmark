That “powerful savior” was to be the Messiah. The Jews of Zechariah’s day already expected the promised Messiah and knew that he would be a descendant of Abraham through the royal line of David. (Gen. 22:17, 18; Matt. 22:42) Why, then, did Jehovah move Zechariah to announce the coming of this “powerful savior”? Because the Messiah’s arrival was closer than ever! Within six months, a young woman named Mary would give birth to Jesus, who, as the angel Gabriel said, would be called “God’s Son” and would rule as King on “the throne of David his father.” (Luke 1:30-35) The “powerful savior” was raised up when Jesus got baptized at age 30. At that time, Jehovah anointed him with holy spirit as the promised Messiah. (Matt. 3:16, 17) An exciting development indeed!

3 The first-century Jews needed to be saved. But from whom or from what? Zechariah answers: “Salvation from our enemies and from the hand of all those hating us.” (Luke 1:71) Did this mean that Jesus would save the Jews from the oppressive Roman authorities? No. Rather, the Jews needed to be saved from the chief source of oppression, Satan. (Heb. 2:14, 15) Satan is responsible for causing mankind, including the ancient Jews, to become enslaved to sin and death. Additionally, the Jews were
<<<P>>>
unable to keep the Mosaic Law perfectly. For that reason, they came under a curse from which they needed to be saved. (Rom. 3:20; Gal. 3:10, 13) Thus, they needed what Jehovah promised through Zechariah—“salvation . . . by forgiveness of their sins.”—Luke 1:77.
<<<P>>>
4 What enemies do we need to be saved from today? Like the Jews in Jesus’ day, we are enslaved to sin and death. (Rom. 5:12) In addition, Satan and the demons are sources of oppression. They try to deceive us, hoping to turn us away from true worship. They even influence individuals and organizations—some of whom are among “those hating us”—to oppose our worship. We need to be saved from such enemies.
<<<P>>>
5 How does our Savior rescue us from these enemies? Consider how Jesus delivers us from sin. During his earthly ministry, Jesus taught his followers how to worship Jehovah acceptably. Then he gave his life as a ransom, “so that everyone exercising faith in him might not be destroyed but have everlasting life.” (John 3:16; Matt. 20:28) We do our best to follow what Jesus taught about giving Jehovah acceptable worship. But we are still imperfect, so we ask Jehovah to forgive us when we fall short of his standards. Then on the basis of the ransom, Jehovah kindly accepts our worship. (1 John 2:1, 2) In these ways, our Savior Jesus delivers us from the condemnation of sin.
