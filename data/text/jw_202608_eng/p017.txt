LESSONS TO LEARN FROM ONESIMUS’ EXAMPLE
<<<P>>>
10 We all do and say things that offend others. Onesimus’ example teaches us how to restore peace when that happens. He did something that sounds simple but is often difficult to do. He admitted his mistake and did his part to reconcile with Philemon. Onesimus surely knew that Roman law required that he return to his master. So he made a costly and exhausting trip of over 1,400 kilometers (900 mi) to return to Philemon. He had to be willing to give up his freedom and possibly accept other consequences for his actions. That could not have been an easy choice. And what if he felt that he was justified in running away from Philemon? If he did feel that way, he did not let it stop him from returning to his master and doing what he could to promote peace between them as spiritual brothers.
<<<P>>>
11 If you have offended someone, take the initiative to restore peace. (Matt. 5:23-25) True, this may be difficult, but the resulting peace will be worth it. Start with a sincere apology. Be willing to listen to the other person and acknowledge his feelings. Accept responsibility for your mistake. Avoid justifying your actions or shifting the blame. Doing so can weaken an apology, prolong a dispute, or even make things worse. It takes humility to apologize, but that is often a quicker way to peace.—Rom. 14:19.
