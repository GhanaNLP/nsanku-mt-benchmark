8 Paul regularly thanked Jehovah for Philemon’s fine qualities. (Philem. 4, 5) He did the same for many others. (1 Cor. 1:4-7; Phil. 1:3-5; Col. 1:3, 4; 1 Thess. 1:2, 3) Paul was not blind to the negative traits of his brothers and sisters, but he looked for their good qualities. And it was his custom to thank Jehovah for such friends. If you find it difficult to speak positively of others, remember this: If you cultivate the habit of thinking positively about others and of praying sincerely for Jehovah to bless them, you will be more inclined to speak positively about them.

9 Despite facing challenging circumstances, Paul treated Philemon kindly. Have you ever lost your patience with someone when you were tired or under stress? When we have been experiencing difficult circumstances, it can be easy to take our frustrations out on others. With that in mind, consider Paul’s circumstances around the time when he wrote to Philemon. Just a few years before, Paul was violently attacked and then unjustly detained. He was held in custody in Jerusalem and in Caesarea. Then, he was transferred to Rome but suffered shipwreck along the way. And now as he wrote to Philemon, Paul was still “a prisoner,” under house arrest in Rome. (Philem. 1) But did Paul allow his stressful experiences to affect the way he addressed Philemon? No. Paul’s letter to Philemon was kind and tactful. The lesson? We can contribute to peace by doing our best to treat others kindly even when we are tired, stressed, or enduring a trial. In turn, the peaceful atmosphere we promote helps us cope with our challenges.
