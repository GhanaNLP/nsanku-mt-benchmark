GLOSSARY (BUDGET TERMINOLOGY)
<<<P>>>
1D1F (One District, One Factory): The setting up of factories and industries which, will in turn, move the country towards greater industrialization. It is aimed at creating more jobs for Ghanaians across the length and breadth of the country.
<<<P>>>
African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA): A market created to eliminate tariffs on intra-Africa trade, making it easier for African businesses to trade within the continent and benefit from the growing African market.
<<<P>>>
Alleviation: This is the process of making a problem less severe.
<<<P>>>
Allocation: The process of sharing or distributing resources amoung government agencies and departments.
<<<P>>>
Ancilliary: Additional or providing additional support or help
<<<P>>>
Apparel: Clothes of a particular type when they are being sold in a shop
<<<P>>>
Balance of Payments: Is a statement of all transactions made between entities in one country and the rest of the world over a defined period, such as a quarter or a year.
<<<P>>>
Budget: A budget is a plan that outlines where to get money from and what to spend it on. For instance, a family can draw up a budget that will show how much money is available and how it should be prioritised for spending (for example, rent, utilities and food). If more money is needed, the family would need to consider raising more income or taking a loan.
<<<P>>>
Budget Deficit: Occurs when expenditures exceed revenue
<<<P>>>
Canine: Relating to or resembling a dog or dogs.
<<<P>>>
Capital Expenditure: This is money spent on major infrastructure projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water systems, plant and machinery etc.
<<<P>>>
Carriageway: It is the part of a road used by vehicular traffic.
<<<P>>>
Compensation of Employees: This is made up of salaries and salary-related allowances such as social security, gratuities and pensions paid to public sector workers.
<<<P>>>
Endoscopy: It is a test to look inside your body
