ADYA ANSE lives and lives in Afdotwo, Cowan-knife, Tikonono. Ananse told us, "You people don't love me very much." These days' pass; we are walking, we are walking, we are walking, we are walking over, we are lying down, we are lying down, we are sitting down, and the rest are lying down. "There is a hole in the hole," he added. He said, "E! I'm going to break my leg." The Tikokono said, "You're lucky you're going up. Osei, "E! I am up to my garden, and you are up to me." And Tikokono went up. “He has heard the sound of the child,” he said. “Well?” The people said, "Aha." He heard it; and he asked us again, "Well?" The people said to one another, "Hump your head and smell." He has shouted to him, and he has looked down on his head and has sneezed, and his head is talking to him 'kill'. Eight o'clock in the morning, she smiled, and she ran away, went out! The guinea fowl says that it has run away to catch the ostensible snake, and its legs will break out in the back of the baobab. The chicken has shed its soul, says it will throw away its eggs, the hyena will chew and chew, and the leg will be broken in pieces. He speaks with a nasal inflection:

"She says laughs, Laughs with her lips.

Osei is laughing, And his lips are coughing."

So the elders said, “If anyone is hungry, go and bath, and don’t laugh.” The Akokoni dances, so his mouth became weary, and the previous message was a beautiful one.

What I have said is that, if it is not for him, you can’t forgive him, and you can’t blame him.
