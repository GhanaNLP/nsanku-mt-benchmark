SƐ (THE) HOE KAME TO ASHANTI

KWAKU ANANSE, ASA, ne ne mma Tikononkono (Gyi-gyi-ho), Afudotwedotwe (Beli-te-a-bura), Nyiwankonfwea (Thin-shanks), lived at home along with Kotoko, the Porcupine. And the Porcupine started working for the first time on a new farm, and Kwaku, the ASA, begged him to let him have a piece (of the farm) to cultivate (for himself). And he gave him some. And the time when Kwaku Ananse and his children were hoeing, the Porcupine went home to eat. And when the Porcupine returned and took up his hoe and applied it to the ground, he raised his song, saying:

"Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa."

Na Hoe, bere a ɔkɔɔ Hoe no, ɔkɔɔ so yɛɛ Hoe. Na ɔkɔɔ so yɛɛ Hoe dedaw. Ɛnna ɔkɔɔ Sea-god's water. Ɛnna ɔkɔɔ White-men's land-far, na White-men's gyee ho, na wɔhwɛe ho, na wɔyɛɛ ho (a ɛte saa). Ɛno ne sɛnea (European) hoes no kɔɔ Ashanti. Nkurɔfo no, Kotoko, the Porcupine, na na ne ho.
