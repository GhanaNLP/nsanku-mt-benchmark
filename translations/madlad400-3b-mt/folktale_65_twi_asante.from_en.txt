SƐ WƆDE AKETEKYIRE, THE CRICKET, ANYIM NEA ONYINA NO

AKETEKYIRE, the Cricket, and Ananse, the Spider, began off, saying they were going to search for corn to plant it. And they got just two grains of corn. And Ananse said, "I am going to plant mine." And the Cricket said, "I am going to roast mine to chew it." Ananse went and planted his. The Cricket, he, too, took his, and put it on the fire. It was long in getting cooked, and he sat down there (beside it). The Spider, he, too, went off to the plantation; he was about to peep under the sheath covering the corn. The Corn said, "Don't peep into my eye, lest your finger go into my eye." Ananse was turning his eyes to look at his Dog; his Dog said, "You don't need to look at me, it wasn't I who did it." Ananse was about to break off a stick to beat the Dog, when the stick said, "Don't break me, for (if you do) your Dog will bite me." Ananse came home and told the Cricket all about it. While he was laughing, he fell into the fire. That is how the Cricket got his teeth burned. This, my story, which I have related, if it be sweet, (or) if it is not sweet, take some elsewhere, and let some come back to me.
