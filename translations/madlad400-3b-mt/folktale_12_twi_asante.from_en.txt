KWAKU ANANSE, the Spider, and his children Tikononkono (Big-big-head), Afudotwedotwe (Belly-like-to-burst), Nyiwankonfwea (Thin-shanks), lived in home together with Kotoko, the Porcupine. And the Porcupine began work for the first time on a new farm, and Kwaku, the Spider, begged him to let him have a piece (of the farm) to cultivate (for himself). And he gave him some. And the time when Kwaku Ananse and his children were howing, the Porcupine went home to eat. And when the Porcupine returned and took his how and applied it to the ground, he raised his song, saying:

"Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa."

The Hoe turned over a huge tract (of land). Then they stopped work and went away, and the Porcupine took the hoe and hid it. And Kwaku, the Spider, saw (where he put it). He said, "This hoe that I have seen, tomorrow very, very early I shall come and take it to do my work." Truly, very, very, very early, the Spider went and got it; he took it to his farm. Now, the Spider did not know how to make it stop, and he raised his song:

"Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa."
