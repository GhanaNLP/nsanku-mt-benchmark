How to Prepare

The COVID-19 pandemic has affected the global economy and the country of Ghana is not exempt from it. The Government regrets that the pandemic has caused suffering, death and loss of jobs to its citizens. I am proud to say that we have acted with urgency and courage in addressing these challenges. In fact, our actions have been appreciated by the international community. The Government has taken steps to revive the economy with the aim of restoring our standard of living.

Additionally, in response to the challenges and opportunities created by the pandemic, the Government has initiated the implementation of the COVID-19 Patient Insurance Recovery, Rehabilitation and Rehabilitation Program with a budget of 100 billion Ghanaian dollars under the leadership of President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo. The implementation of this program has resulted in the immediate recovery of all citizens and is improving the country's economic situation.

We thank you for your trust in the Government as we work hard to restore our financial stability. At a time when we are facing many challenges to our integrity, the Bible often teaches us the steps we need to take at this time. As Isaiah 9:10 says: "The bricks have fallen, but we will build with hewn stones; the sycamore trees have been cut down, but we will replace them with timber; so the Government is determined to rebuild and fulfill its promises. For this reason, the Government will continue to work on various programs to repair all the damage and improve the quality of life of the citizens.

One of the main objectives of the Budget 2022 is to promote self-employment issues in the country from now until the next three years with the aim of increasing our income inequality and reducing unemployment. In addition, everyone should participate in the implementation of the budget to provide employment to people, especially young people. Therefore, the Government's policies will help young people to show their talents and skills by providing equal opportunities for everyone, so that employment opportunities are enjoyed by all citizens.

In order to instill the spirit of self-reliance and self-motivation in the youth, the Government will provide funding to enable the youth to start or expand their careers through the YouStart Programme in February 2022.

In Budget 2022, we plan to continue working on urgent economic crime issues with a view to seeing economic crime emerge after the COVID-19 pandemic.The government is committed to working on a wide range of initiatives that will develop young talent and promote self-reliance among young people.As we strive to achieve the President's goal of Ghana Will No Longer Need Aid, I would like to call on all citizens to participate in these crucial initiatives and to innovate. In addition to this, they have to participate in the promotion of self-employment for their tourism in the country.

14. The Government will undertake various measures to revive the commodity industry, and to improve the quality of commodities to be traded both domestically and abroad. Therefore, it has become necessary for the Government to make the following changes in policies:

• In order to maintain profits and expand production, the Government will continue to pay VAT to the country's textile industries for another two years starting in 2019;

• Re-evaluate the two-and-a-half-year tax relief program as part of its efforts to address tax evasion issues. This will allow the tax relief on imported goods to benefit the country and protect the Government's objectives;

• Reducing the tax on uninsured gold miners from 3 percent per annum to 1.5 percent per annum;

• From June 2022 onwards, the Government will provide the Ghana Revenue Agency to assist Municipalities, Metropolises and Local Governments in collecting and reporting property taxes;

• The Government will revise the fees charged to public sector entities for the provision of Government services, which will come into effect on the first day of Dzove in 2022. These fees will be kept on a rolling basis and will be adjusted according to the annual frequency of commodity prices;

Explaining Some of the Fundamentals of Economics

The One Small Business Center Strategy: Establishment of industrial and commercial centers to promote the country's manufacturing and economic growth. The goal of this strategy is to attract more jobs to the country to provide employment to the citizens.

Accountability: The responsibility of a government to be accountable to its citizens for its actions, the use of public funds, and the use of assets under its supervision.

Distribution of funds and assets: The extent to which the government allocates funds and assets to government agencies.

To alleviate a problem: To alleviate a problem or to alleviate suffering.

Budgeting: Budgeting is the process by which the government determines where money comes from and how it will be used. For example, a family may decide how much money it has and how it will be used (for rent, electricity, water, and food). If the family needs more money, it may borrow money or reduce its spending.

Deficit: This occurs when the government spends more money than it receives or when the amount spent exceeds the amount received.

Public expenditure on development: This is the amount of money that the government spends on major development projects such as the construction of trucks, schools, hospitals, bridges, roads, drinking water, roads, and so on.

Wages and salaries of civil servants: These include salaries and allowances paid to civil servants by the State, Social Security benefits paid to retired civil servants, bonuses, and so forth.

Development Partners: These are countries and organizations that provide aid and assistance to other countries in order to safeguard their development goals. Partners may be corporations or founding fathers of some countries, for example, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank

Inflation: The periodic rise or fall in the price of goods and services over a period of time.
