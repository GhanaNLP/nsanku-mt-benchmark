14. The Government has put in place a number of policies to stimulate industry and help create a more efficient and sustainable industry in the country and abroad. As a result, it has become necessary to make the following policy changes:

• In order to sustain the industry's profitability and expand its operations, the Government will extend the period of VAT Relief for the textile industry by two years, which was launched in 2019;

• After two years of implementing a foreign trade policy on imported goods, in which we have provided some relief to foreign traders, we will make changes to this policy to ensure that it benefits the specific foreign traders that this policy is intended to serve;

• Reduce the withholding tax rate from 3% to 1.5% for small businesses that sell gold without using a machine;

• From the beginning of April 2022, the Ghana Revenue Authority will assist the Municipal, Township and Municipal Assemblies (MMDAs) to seek equal opportunities to help them take steps to improve the tax collection system in order to achieve better acceptance and accountability;

• The Government will make changes to the fees charged by the MDAs for their services with effect from 1st January 2022. The changes in fees charged by the MDAs will be based on the annual increase in the prices of goods and services;

• As soon as this Budget Bill is approved by the parliament, as soon as possible, the process of paying our taxes will be completed;

• Introducing the Electronic Transaction Levy (E-Levy) to increase the number of taxpayers who are not registered with the government. This levy will cover mobile payments, bank transfers, mobile payment services and international payments, with a flat rate of one percent and then a flat rate of seven and a half (1.75%) to be paid by the person making the payment, except for international payments where the payment will be subject to the levy. This rate will be effective from 1 February 2022 and will not apply to transactions of less than or equal to GH¢100 per day.

15. In order to ensure that resources are used where they are most needed and contribute to economic stability, action will be taken on a number of action-oriented economic policies in 2022 and beyond:

a. The Government will continue to safeguard the salary system of civil servants and, in this regard, will ensure that only civil servants who have been duly recruited in the course of their duties are entered in the Government salary register;

b. In 2022 and for the foreseeable future, conduct a feasibility study of the functioning of the Civil Service in order to determine the number of personnel needed for the various functions of the Civil Service in order to ensure the availability and performance of the required number of Civil Servants;

c. All other infrastructure projects identified in this 2022 Budget to be implemented, and those included in the Medium-Term Development Plans of Government Departments (MDAs), Municipal, Urban and Rural Administrations (MMDAs) and State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), irrespective of the source of funding for the project, shall be implemented by means of legislation (L.I. 2411) in order to facilitate the selection of financial intermediaries to facilitate the implementation of public infrastructure projects for the benefit of the public.

16. We will observe the following economic measures in order not to err in the near future in the course of the economic discipline we have outlined:

• To award procurement contracts, we will only deal with items that have been approved by the Public Services in their Procurement Plans that have recently been published on the official website of the Public Procurement Agency (PPA);

• The annual procurement requests made by each Government agency should be the budgeted expenditures of that year's Budget. In addition, the source of funding for these procurements and the amount of funds available for these procurements should be identified;

• No government official is allowed to sign any Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to financially bind the Government of Ghana without the approval of the Minister of Finance.

Glossary of terms (economics terms)

1D1F: 1D1F is an initiative of the Ministry of Industry and Trade of the Republic of Ghana aimed at creating employment opportunities for Ghanaians across the country.

Accountability: This refers to the accountability of the Government to the citizens for its actions and the reporting of the steps taken in this regard. This includes financial and other assets managed by the Government for the benefit of all citizens.

Allocation: This is the process of allocating public funds to various Government departments and agencies for use by the public.

Alleviation: This is the process of mitigating the consequences of a problem.

Budgeting: Budgeting is the process by which a government determines how it will raise and spend its money. For example, a household may make a budget by listing the amount of money it has and the number of items it needs to spend that money on (such as housing, water, electricity, and food).

Budget deficit: This occurs when government spending exceeds government revenues.

Capital Expenditure: This is the amount of money spent on capital expenditures such as rent, school fees, hospital bills, maintenance, transportation, water, heavy machinery and so on.

Compensation of Employees: This is the monthly salary, allowances, life insurance, travel expenses and other benefits paid to Government employees.

Development Partners: This refers to countries or organizations that assist developing countries in achieving their development goals. This can include organizations such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank, and others. It can also include individual countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States, and others.

End-Year Inflation: This is the rate at which inflation rises to $1,000 per month in a given year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when expenditures exceed the budget approved by the legislature.

Take-me-kwa Senior Secondary School (FREE SHS): The Take-me-kwa Senior Secondary School is a new Government initiative announced by the government of President Nana Akuffo-Addo in April 2017 that every JHS student who performs well in the examination will have access to free secondary education.

Grants: These are types of financial assistance provided by the Port Authority to the Government that are not the responsibility of the Government.

Grants to other Government Units: This refers to the amount required by law to be paid into the Manitoba Parliamentary Fund DACF, the Road Fund, the Education FundGETFUND and the Petroleum Related Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period of time.

Gross International Reserves (GIR): A measure of a country's ability to buy goods and services from abroad at a given point in time. It is used to compare a country's current account with that of another country to determine its current account strength.

Inflation: This refers to the increase in the cost of goods and services over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP) is one of the most ambitious projects of the NPP Government with the ultimate goal of bringing electricity to rural areas, especially the poorest communities, thereby contributing to poverty alleviation and reducing the socio-economic gap between rural and urban populations.

Macroeconomic targets: These are measures of a country's economic performance. Examples include economic growth, inflation, unemployment rate, domestic and international trade growth, as well as government budget deficits or shortfalls such as decisions taken by the Central Bank of Ghana affecting inflation in the country.

Small Business Development Programme - MSME (Micro And Small Buisness Developmet Programme): Small businesses are private enterprises that can employ a small number of people and whose turnover does not exceed a certain amount.

Nation-building Volunteers- NABCo (Nation Builders Corps): This is a new concept introduced by the Government of Ghana to provide young university graduates with employment opportunities in the private and public sectors to help develop their skills in solving life's problems, and to help them gain experience that will contribute to the economic development of the Nation.

National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI): The National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI) is a non-profit public corporation under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, which is mandated by law to promote the development of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs).

Non-Oil Real GDP: This is a measure of the value of all goods and services produced in a country, excluding oil revenues, based on inflation.

Non-Tax Revenue: Non-taxable income earned by the government, such as royalties, interest, and taxes.

Outstanding Obligations: The outstanding debts of the Government are the sums owed by the Ministry of Finance to the Government for the provision of public services. Since 2016, the Government has been required by law to pay outstanding debts in the form of statutory debts.

Primary balance: This is the amount of money left over after the Government has spent all of its anticipated receipts, excluding interest on loans, (i.e. excluding loans to be repaid by the Government). It is calculated as a percentage of the total value of National Assets.

Projected Growth Rate: This is how much Ghana's economy is expected to grow in a given year.

Government Revenue: This is the amount of money that the Government receives from its activities in this country and abroad.

Rearing For Food and Jobs (RFJ): This is a new development initiative of the Government of Ghana that aims to increase the productivity of the rearing industry and generate revenue to increase the consumption of livestock and reduce the amount of livestock imported into the country, thereby increasing the number of jobs created by the population.
