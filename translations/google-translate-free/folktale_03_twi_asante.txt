DO NOT ’YOYE A WISE OMANM’

YE that Kwaku Ananse was there, and he cleaned the wise cow boaa’no and put it in the toam’. He says he climbs a tree to fight over it, and wisdom does not stand on the ground. And he lifted it up as one, and when he reached the tree, he passed it over, underneath, and he put a rope to the barrel, and yaneyed, and the barrel went before him’, and he touched the tree to climb. Forth, forth, forth a, twon! And he's left it again, but he climbs, climbs, climbs, twon! And his son, Ntikuma, stood by and said, "E! you're ashamed, if you had turned the bottle behind you, he would have been able to fall.

“So ho is your mpan’insem,” he said. And he's up again sara, smile pasa! Then he would kiss here, and put the bottle behind him. Then he said, soul! order! order! ona the ship. He reached the top of the tree and said, “Kwaku Ananse I am dead Afio, my son, kete, kete, I, I am here, I have gathered wisdom wood, and if there is any left I myself will not find it, and my son, totofefewa, and he will catch me!” And he grabbed the bottle, and tintini! and threw the bottle, and tesee! It is that wisdom everyone got some, and you who did not fight for it immediately, it is (like) 'foolish.

My story that I have told, whether it is good or bad, just take some and give me some.
