CONCLUSIONS

The COVID-19 pandemic has affected the global economy and Ghana is no exception. The Government regrets that the disease has caused suffering, death and loss of employment to the public. I am proud to say that we have taken action to address these challenges with urgency and courage. In fact, all the countries of the world have appreciated our efforts. The government has again introduced measures to revive the jobs with the intention of restoring our lives to the way they were before.

In addition, addressing the challenges and opportunities posed by the outbreak, the Government has launched the COVID-19 Insurance Reduction, Infrastructure Rehabilitation and Infrastructure Support Program with a budget of Ghana 100 billion under President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo- Addo. The implementation of this program has brought immediate relief to all citizens and is reviving the national economy.

We thank you for your confidence in the Government as we work hard to stabilize our revenue recovery. Meanwhile, we are facing many challenges in our viuli for resilience issues. The Bible often shows us the necessary steps at these times. As Isaiah 9:10 says, "The stones have fallen, but we will build with hewn stones; the fig trees have been cut down, but we will plant cedars in their place” so the Government is determined to restore and keep its promises. For this reason, the Government will continue to implement various programs to restore all the damage and improve the lives of citizens.

One of the key objectives of the Debt 2022 Budget is that between now and the next three years, we will promote self-employment in the country with a view to growing and stabilizing our financial sector. In addition, everyone should be involved in the economy to create jobs, especially the youth. Consequently the Government’s policies will support the youth to unleash the talents and skills they possess by providing equal opportunities for all, to ensure employment issues for all citizens.

To instill a spirit of self-employment and encourage the youth, the Government will in February 2022 provide the youth with access to funds to start or expand their businesses under the “YouStart” Scheme.

In the FY 2022 Budget, we plan to continue to address urgent fiscal challenges with a view to stabilizing the economy in the post-COVID-19 pandemic. The government has committed itself to implement various schemes that will develop the skills of the youth and promote self-employment among the youth. As we strive to achieve the President’s goal of ‘Ghana No Longer Needs Aid’, I would like to challenge all citizens to participate in these charity projects as well as innovate. Along with this, they should be involved in the field of self-employment promotion for their sustainability in the country.

c. New developments announced in the 2022 Budget and planned government, Municipal, Metropolitan and Local Government developments will be considered in accordance with Act L.I This will ensure that the Government's debt development activities are carried out in an efficient manner.

Interpretation of Certain Special Provisions of the Financial Statements

The One Small Region One Industry Policy: The establishment of industries and manufacturing industries will enable the country to move forward in terms of manufacturing and income generation. The aim of this scheme is to attract more jobs to the country to create employment for the citizens.

Accountability: It is the duty of the Government to be accountable to the public for its operations, the use of public funds and resources under its control.

Allocation of funds and resources: The process by which the Government allocates funds and resources to government agencies.

Problem alleviation: How to alleviate a problem or suffering.

Budget: The budget is the plan of the Government for the source of funds and how it will spend the funds. For example, a family can plan how much money they have and how they will spend it. (e.g. rent, latrine, water and food) If the family needs more money, it has to borrow or reduce its spending.

Deficit: This occurs when the Government spends more money than it receives, or when the amount spent is more than the amount it receives.

Government Spending on Development: This is the amount of money spent by the Government on major development projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transportation, drinking water supply, roads, etc.

Salaries of Government Employees: Payment of Salaries to Government Employees. This consists of salaries and other payments made by the Government to government employees, payments made by the Government to retired government employees (Social Security), thank-you money and so on.

Development Lenders: These are countries and organizations that provide assistance to other countries by providing them with funds to meet their development goals. Creditors may be the sea father of certain companies or countries. For example, the “International Monetary Fund” (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank

End-of-Debt Inflation: The regular rise or fall in the price of goods and services at the end of the year or in the Spring.

Excess Budget: This occurs when the budget spent by the Government exceeds the Budget approved by the Parliament.

Free Secondary Education: The Free Secondary Education Scheme introduced by the Government of Ghana under President Akufo Addo in February 2017 provides free secondary education to primary school students who pass the final examination.

Commodity Prices: The regular rise or fall in the price of goods and services over a period of time.
