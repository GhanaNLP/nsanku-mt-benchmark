IF EYOYE IS GOING TO BE AWAY

FATHER ANANSE lives there and with him are the sons of Afudotwedotwe, Nyiwan-konfwea, Tikonokono. Ananse told us, "You just want odoma time I don't eat. Enie nkoda this siim'; we meat, meat, meat, and we fought for some of the odoma and abede, and the cows went down. Afudotwedotwe said, Nyiwan-konfwea said, "Come up and hear something. He said, "E! I'm going to break my leg. Tikonokono said, "Afudotwedotwe is on the climb. He said, "E! I climb up and my ass will burst, and you climb up. And Tikonokono forehead. He heard the odoma 'ba hear hear, he said, "Are you all right?" The crowd said, "Ah. And ote’a and ote; and he asked us again, "Are you all right?" The squirrel said, "Get your head down and kiss. He said he bowed his head, and kissed, and his head spoke to the ground’, kill! Afudotwedotwe said, he laughed, and his stomach burst, ta! Nyiwankonfwea said ofuntum ran abeka akyere ose, and his leg be broke kaa paitem’ back. The cockroach washed his soul, he said he would throw his cocoon-hono, he would kiss Nyiwankonfwea and he would fall down and his legs would be broken and he would be left behind. He laughed, and his mouth feared:

“He says he laughs, Ene’s mouth fears.

He says he laughs, Ene his mouth fears.”

’That’s why the old saying goes, “If someone falls, go kiss him, and don’t laugh.” And the Cockroach tried to laugh 'so his mouth was open, and the message 'before his mouth was open.

My story that I have told, whether it is good or bad, take some, and bring some.
