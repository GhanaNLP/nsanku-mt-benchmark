IF ’YOU NEVER DANCE

OPETE and Anene are there, and Anene says, “Let’s go cut a ’twene and swim.” We siim’koo wild’, and we go too onyina. Opete sees the old man and says, “This is mine, and I am the oldest so I will cut the kesie.” “Mate,” he replied. Anene ’so if he bites, he will bite like a twenebea and stands here, and he soso ko twaye. Our mother senee atwene.

Opete se obo and twenem’, which means:

The future! The future! know soon!

'Pete Kwakye! know soon!

Anene so boo and deem’, this is what he says:

Anene Kwa! Kwa! Anene Kwa! Kwa!

It’s gone, gone, gone, and ’Nyame teeye, and send Akroma come, say, “Ago!” He said, “Man!” He said, “ ’God says he has heard a game here, so bring the laws and play’ for him to hear,” The animals went’ to ’God’, and we played, played, played and ’God said, “I heard it was good, two games ’were so I will give you something.” Enie ’God went and took two boxes and built them, one clean, the other for the sea, the other for the tantantan, the silver for the sea. 'God said, “You two take it.” Opete said, "I am the chief, and let me go first. When he went, he took out the white one, Anene took the tantan one. We gave it up, and we took it to our village. and he said to ’Pete, “Ye se your Mother-in-law is dead.” He said, “Great for me tonight some memmo mensa and I alone funeral.” He took the pokwa booye, and went to the funeral. He says he goes to the yane, he goes up, he says he gets up, and the pokwa has fallen on the yane drink’. He said, “Yes! Anene thing is gone, and I’m going ’fie sen?” He jumped on the yane drink’ and ope.

That is, you will see 'Pete da, and ofwete fwete yanem', and he wants the Anene pokwa abere.

That’s why the mpan’infuo says adufurupe doesn’t.

Believe me in my story, whether it is good or bad, and accept me with it.
