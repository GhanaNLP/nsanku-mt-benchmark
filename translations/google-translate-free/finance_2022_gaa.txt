MESSAGE FROM THE PRESIDENT

The devastation caused by the COVID-19 pandemic has been a major challenge for the economy everywhere, including Ghana. The consequences were felt by all Ghanaians through deaths, loss of life and jobs, but the Government stood firm on its feet. I am proud to say that the steps we took to achieve it were courageous and determined, and the whole world witnessed it with joy. The authorities also made laws to help restore jobs and restore the economy.

Furthermore, with the challenges and opportunities that emerged in the wake of the pandemic, the Governors, led by His Excellency Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, on the spot swiftly orchestrated a development program with GH¢100 billion invested in activities and projects in order to boost social resilience in the face of the economic damage caused by COVID-19 was (the GhanaCARES “You’ll Be Dad”) event. This gave citizens immediate breathing room and relief and the city's activities began to stand on its feet again.

We appreciate your faith in these Governors even as efforts are being made to rebuild public services after the devastation of this pandemic. This is our time of trial, it is our time of need, and we are struggling to get out of this crisis. The Holy Scriptures always guide us in times like these. As the book of Isaiah witnessed in chapter 9:10 “The brick hearts are broken down, but we will build the hearts with hewn stone, and the sycamore trees will be laid out, but we will plant cedars in their place” (Isaiah 9:10) Governors are determined to keep their promises. Therefore, efforts will be made to establish good policies and enact laws that will support the country's development and thus contribute to a better quality of life.

One of the objectives of the 2022 Budget for the next three (3) years is to strengthen all sectors to support economic development, strengthening, and leaving no one behind, so that every sector is perfect, especially youth sector. Government policies in particular will strengthen young people to help them recognize their gifts and talents, which they can do to strengthen themselves for the development of everyone.

To strengthen the spirit of faith of young people, in 2022 the Governors will launch a program called “YouStart” to help them find funds to open or invest in their businesses.

In the 2022 budget some measures have been put on the books to continue and arrest the problems with the financial affairs, so that the post-pandemic affairs can be started with encouragement. Governors are making sacrifices to strengthen activities so that young people can achieve entrepreneurship and personal growth. As we embark on our President’s vision of moving the country forward beyond the vision of “Ghana Beyond Aid”, I appeal to all Ghanaians, especially the youth and women, to take it upon themselves, think deeply to participate in the national process and thus correct and rebuild the country’s affairs.

I ask that everyone contribute to building the country as it should.

PROPOSED FINANCING REGULATIONS FOR THE 2022 BUDGET

Investment law programs

14. The authorities will implement certain measures, which will enable businesses to withstand competition both in this country and in foreign markets. Due to this, it is necessary to create the following rules:

• To inject benefits into the sector and maximize production, Governors will postpone textile manufacturers’ VAT exemption introduced in 2019, on textiles manufactured in Ghana for another two years.

• If this practice continues for two and a half years, the planning rule that allows temporary reductions in imports will be implemented in order to take into account the owner's expectations and expectations.;

• The tax on unrefined mined silver will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• From January 2022 Governors, through the Ghana Revenue Authority initiative, will assist members of the Municipal Assembly (MMDAs) to pass legislation to support the taxation and accountability of real estate.

• Officials will revise taxes and levies on activities for use by MDA’s from January 1, 2022. The taxes and duties on property shall be maintained at the exchange rate of the country's currency in the country, to be purchased at once;

• Taxes charged at all roads will no longer be charged after this budget is charged.

• An “Electronic Transaction Levy” or “E-Levy” will be launched to facilitate electronic payment of taxes in the private sector. Transactions that are subject to these taxes include mobile money transfers, bank transfers, remittances, and gifts, which will be charged at 1.75% and will be paid by the sender except for the gift and the recipient. This will be implemented from the first day of February 2022. A tax-free event is an amount of GH¢100 or less, on the day, is not included in this tax honor.

DEFINITION OF SOME TERMS IN ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

1D1F (One District One Functional): Strike in every district, which will transform Ghana into a revolutionary country. The purpose is for the people to have jobs in every corner of this Ghana

Accountability: This is the process by which Governments hold citizens accountable for their actions and provide results. Coins and other japades are among the assets maintained by the Governors for these public accounts.

Distribution: Methods of allocating resources to Government agencies in a public manner

Release: Identifying ways to reduce a stressor

Budget: A plan that outlines how much money will be spent, where it will be found, and what the money will be used for. A family is able to write its budget and it is determined how much money the family has and how it will be spent, for example: rent, electricity food and so on. If money is needed to meet the requirements, the family may seek a loan, cut back on expenses, or find another source of income.

Behind-budget debt: This occurs when Governors borrow more than the money coming into the photograph

Japadei expenditure: This refers to the expenditure on japadee projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water supply, factories and their infrastructure.

Employee benefits: It is the monthly salary of employees and other benefits that go to employees such as allowances, disability benefits, and other benefits.

Helpers: These are foreign countries, or companies, that help foreign countries to survive in the economy. These donors include the IMF, the World Bank, the ADB, or countries that have signed agreements with Ghana to provide assistance. Some of these countries include Germany, USA, UK, knkn.

Stock Market At The End Of The Year: It’s stock market in December.

Excess Spending: This occurs when Governors spend more than a stone’s throw in the budget and the Legislature has approved it.
