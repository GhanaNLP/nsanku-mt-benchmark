FINANCIAL PLANNING MEASURES IN THIS 2022 FINANCIAL PLAN

National Monetary Policies

14. The Government has put in place various programs to stimulate agriculture and help in the creation of sustainable trade that will gain market share in the country and abroad. Consequently, we have had to make the following set of policy changes:

• In order to consolidate the benefits of the mills and enable them to expand their operations, the Government will add two years to the Vat Relief period granted to the textile mills which it introduced in 2019;

• Two years ago we implemented a foreign trade policy in which we gave some relief to foreign traders. We will make changes to this policy to make it more accurate and help the specific foreign traders that this policy should help;

• Reduce the withholding tax rate on small-scale mining companies selling raw gold from 3 percent to 1.5 percent (withholding tax rate reduction from 3 % to 1.5 %);

• From early February 2022, the Ghana Revenue Authority will assist Municipal, Municipal and District Authorities (MMDAs) to seek a common framework to help them take steps to ensure efficiency and accountability in the collection of tobacco taxes;

• Government will revise the various fees charged by the Multiple Government Agencies (MDAs) for their services with effect from January 1, 2022. The adjustment of various amounts collected by Government Services will depend on the rate of annual inflation;

• Once this Budget is approved by Parliament, the road tax will immediately end;

• Introduce an Electronic Transaction Levy (“E-Levy”) to expand tax collection and include unregistered taxpayers in the country. This tax will apply to ‘phone’ transfers, bank transfers, ‘phone’ transfer services and foreign remittances, and the remaining 1.75% tax will be paid by the sender, except for remittances coming from abroad. This tax will begin on January 1, 2022. Transactions of GH¢100 or less per day are not subject to this tax.

Definition of Terms (Financial System Terminology)

One District One Farm (1D1F): This is the opening of various farms in the districts to improve farming in Ghana. The aim is to increase employment in the country and create jobs for Ghanaians in all parts of the country.

Accountability: This represents the accountability of the Government to the citizens for its performance and the reporting of the steps taken. This includes all types of finances and assets managed by the Government for the public.

Allocation: This is the process of allocating public funds to the various Government agencies and departments for public use.

Alleviation: This is a process of mitigating the effects of a problem.

Budget: A budget represents a plan made by the Government on how to raise money and what to do with that money. For example, a family can create a budget by determining the amount of money they have and the order in which the money needs to be spent. It could be rent, water and electricity bills and food bills) If they need more money, the family will need to raise more money or even go out on loan.

Budget deficit: This occurs when the Government's expenditure exceeds the actual receipts.

Capital Expenditure: This is the expenditure on capital expenditure such as roads, schools, hospitals, sewers, transportation, water, large stadiums and so on.

Compensation of Employees: This consists of monthly salary, various funds, social security, travel allowance etc. paid to Government employees.

Development Partners: These represent Countries or agencies that help developing Countries achieve their development goals. This could be some groups like International Monetary Fund- IMF,World Bank, African Development Bank and so on. !can also be some individual countries like Germany, English Rice, United States, and so on.

End Year Inflation: This is the rate at which inflation reaches in the month of February in a particular year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when the expenditure of various expenditures exceeds the budget allocation approved by the legislature.

“Fa-me-kwa” Secondary School (FREE SHS): This Fa-me-kwa Secondary School is a new Government initiative launched by the government of President Nana Akuffo-Addo in April 2017 that every JHS student who passes the examination will get free admission to Secondary School.

Grants: These are types of financial assistance provided by Travel Assistants to the Government that are not payable by the Government.

Grants to other Government Units: This represents the amounts that are required by law to be paid into the District Legislative Fund “DACF”, Road Fund “GETFUND” and Petroleum Related Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This refers to the value of all the goods produced in the country during a given period.

Gross International Reserves:This is a measure of the purchasing power of a country at a particular point in time. This helps to compare the budget of one country with that of another country by seeing the economic strength of the country.

Inflation: This represents the increase in the prices of services and goods over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Program - IPEP (Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Program is one of the flagship programs of the NPP Government with the overall objective of delivering infrastructure to rural areas, especially poor communities the welfare gap between rural and urban populations has changed.

Macroeconomic targets: These are measures of a country’s economic performance. Examples of this are economic growth, inflation, high employment growth, domestic and international trade, the level of government deficit or deficit and the impact of decisions by the Bank of Ghana on inflation in the country.
