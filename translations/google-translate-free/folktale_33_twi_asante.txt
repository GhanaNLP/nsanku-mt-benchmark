WE'RE GOING TO BABI A, BUY YOU TO 'FIE NTEM

A SON we'called Abena was given in marriage by his family. She went to her husband's country and forgot her abusuafuo; he did not want the son's hometown', Someone's hometown' to be his. He was ho'a, and he was dead; maybe he said he was dead; 'again the grandmother is dead; fua'ra wamma. It’s been about ten years since he came. And one day Abena you and a bird will stand on the roof and he'll cry:

"Abena oni clothes, ose clothes, onana clothes!

Toto pom gun! pomm! to pom! pomm! to pom! pomm!

'Twene in the kun! this is! 'your head! this is! 'your head! this is!

Don't e'su, yo! yo! 'su yo! yo! 'su yo! yo!

Abena oni clothes, ose clothes, onana clothes!"

Abena see, "What's the matter?" Ankye and her husband come; he suffered the oboo; and the bird cried again. The husband said, "I don't hear it right, and I'm going to call Adanko. He freed it for the bride; and the bird has raised:

"Abena oni clothes, ose clothes, onana clothes!

Toto pom gun! pomm! to pom! pomm! to pom! pomm!

'Twene in the kun! this is! 'your head! this is! 'your head! this is! Don’t e’su, yo! yo! ’su yo! yo! ’su yo! yo!

Abena oni kills, ose kills, gives clothes!”

Adanko said, "Oh! Oh! he said, 'your eyes are dead; your teeth are dead; your grandfather is dead. Now Abena had her hands on her head, "Come out! That's it! 'Grandma! Dad!" He got up’ and came to his town’; and ’fie and pass! That’s why we’re saying, “If you fight ’way, cross it.” And Abena fought for his nkrofuo mother to die behind him.

Believe me in my story, whether it is good or bad, and accept me with it.
