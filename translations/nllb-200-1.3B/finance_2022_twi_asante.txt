Plans for the National Budget

14. The Government has put in place a number of initiatives to stimulate innovation and contribute to the development of sustainable products in the country and abroad. Therefore, the following policy reforms will be required:

• In order to consolidate the profitability of the technology sector and expand its scope, the Government will extend the period of two years for the introduction of the VAT Relief for the telecommunications sector in 2019;

• Two years after the introduction of the export trade policy for imports, in which we have given some relief to the foreign traders. We will make changes to this policy to implement it and help the specific foreign traders who should be supported by this policy.

• Reduce the withholding tax rate from 3% to 1.5% on the sale of gold by micro-exporters;

• From the beginning of April 2022, the Ghana Revenue Authority (Ghana Revenue Authority) will assist Municipal, Town and Country Legislative Assemblies (MMDAs) in seeking equality of opportunity to help them take steps in the field of tax administration to ensure effective collection and accountability;

• The Government will make changes in the amount of money collected by the Public Service Departments (MDAs) for their operations and will come into effect from 1st April 2022.

• As soon as the legislature approves this Finance Plan, the current road tax will be abolished as soon as possible;

• The Electronic Transaction Levy (E-Levy) will be introduced to expand the tax burden to include non-registered taxpayers in the country. This levy will include electronic money, bank money, electronic money services and foreign money, with the remaining 1.5 per cent (1.7 per cent) to be paid by the borrower, unless the borrower is a foreigner. This rate will start on 1 October 2022.This rate does not include cash flows of one hundred yen (GH¢100) or less per day.

Financial Planning for the Workplace

15. In order to implement the necessary fiscal measures and contribute to economic sustainability, we will launch a series of structural fiscal policies in 2022 and in the short term:

a. The Government will continue to protect the system of remuneration of civil servants and in this regard, it will ensure that only civil servants who have been successfully recruited to the workplace will be included in the payroll of the Government;

b. In 2022 and in the period immediately preceding it, conduct a basic evaluation of the performance of the Government personnel by determining the number of personnel required in various Government services in order to ensure adequate and effective supply of Government personnel;

c. Any other measures envisaged in the Financial Plan 2022, as well as those envisaged in the Medium-Term Development Plans of Government Departments (MDAs), Municipal, Municipal and Rural Assemblies (MMDAs) and State-owned Enterprises (SOEs), irrespective of the source of funding for the project, shall be regulated by law (L.I. 2411) in order to ensure transparency and to facilitate the effective implementation of the national intelligence programmes of the Government on behalf of the public.

16. We will take the following economic measures so that we do not make mistakes in the short term in the direction of financial discipline:

• Only the items approved by the Government Departments in their Procurement Plans which appear on the website of the Government Procurement Authority (PPA) will be used for the purpose of awarding procurement contracts;

• The annual demand for the sale of goods by each Government company should be as specified in the Financial Statement for that year. In addition, the source of the proceeds of the sale of goods and the amount of money available for the sale of goods should be determined;

• No chief executive officer shall be entitled to sign any Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to bind the Government of Ghana financially unless he receives an advance payment from the Minister of Finance.

Glossary of terms (Financial terms)

Mansini One Food, One Business (1D1F): This is a series of initiatives to open up rural industries and thereby improve the quality of life in Ghana. The aim is to create jobs in the country and provide employment opportunities for Ghanaians in all parts of the country.

Accountability: This refers to the accountability of a government to its citizens for its actions and the reporting of various steps taken by the government. This includes financial and other assets that the government maintains for the general public.

Allocation: This is the process of allocating public funds to government departments and agencies for use by the public.

Alleviation: This is a technique to minimize the effects of a problem.

Budget: Budget refers to the government's plan for how and where to spend the money. For example, a household may make a financial plan by listing the amount of money they have and the things they need to spend the money on, such as housing, water, electricity, and food.

Budget deficit: This occurs when government spending exceeds government revenues.

Capital Expenditure: This is the cost of capital expenditures such as transportation, school fees, hospital bills, decorations, roads, water, heavy machinery, and so on.

Compensation of Employees: This is the monthly salaries, allowances, social security contributions, travel allowances etc. paid to Government employees.

Development Partners: This refers to countries or organizations that support developing countries in achieving their development goals. These may include organizations such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank, etc. It may also refer to private countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States, and their affiliates.

End-Year Inflation: This is the average price of goods and services in a given year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when expenditures exceed the limits set in the budget approved by parliament.

Fa-me-kwa High School (FREE SHS): Fa-me-kwa High School is a government initiative launched in April 2017 by Prime Minister Nana Akuffo-Addo's government to provide free access to secondary education for any JHS student who passes the exam.

Government Grants: These are types of financial assistance provided by the Ministry of Finance to the Government that is not paid by the Government.

Grants to other Government Units: This refers to grants that are required by law to be paid into the Mansini Parliamentary Fund DACF, the Road Fund , the Education Fund GETFUND and the Petroleum Related Fund .

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is the total value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a given period of time.

Gross International Reserves (GIR): A measure of the ability of a country's economy to absorb foreign direct investment (FDI) at a given time. It is used to compare the relative strength of a country's foreign exchange reserves with those of other countries.

Inflation: This refers to the rise in the prices of goods and services over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP) is one of the most ambitious initiatives of the NPP Government, with the ultimate goal of reaching out to rural communities, especially those in poverty-stricken areas, thereby reducing poverty and reducing the economic disparity between rural and urban communities.

Macroeconomic targets: These are measures used to measure the performance of a country's economy. Examples include economic growth, inflation, labour productivity, the dynamics of domestic and foreign trade, the impact of government fiscal policies on inflation, and the impact of inflation on the economy.

Micro and Small Business Development Programme (MSME): Micro and small businesses are privately owned enterprises that can employ a small number of people with a fixed income of no more than a certain amount.

Nation Builders Corps (NABCo): This is a new initiative launched by the Government of Ghana to provide young graduates with employment opportunities in the private or public sector and to help them use their skills to tackle the challenges of life, thereby providing them with the skills that will contribute to the economic development of the country.

National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI): The National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI) is a non-profit public body under the Ministry of Industry and Technology, which is mandated by law to oversee the development of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs).

Non-Oil Real GDP: This is the total value of the country's gross domestic product excluding oil revenues, based on gross domestic product.

Non-Tax Revenue: These are government revenues that are not subject to taxation, such as licensing fees and fees for other purposes such as taxes and fees.

Outstanding Obligations: The outstanding obligations of the Government are the amounts payable by the Ministry of Finance to the Government Services and are included in the Statutory Instruments as of 2016.

Primary balance: This is the amount of money remaining after the government has exhausted its anticipated reserves, excluding interest on loans (i.e. loans to be repaid by the government). The method of calculating this is expressed as a percentage of the total value of the public goods.

Projected Growth Rate: This is the expected growth rate of Ghana's economy in a given year.

Government Revenue: This is the total revenue the Government receives from its domestic and foreign operations.

Rearing For Food and Jobs (RFJ): This is a new initiative of the Government of Ghana which aims to increase the productivity and productivity of the rearing industry by increasing the supply of rearing beef to the population and by reducing the import of rearing beef into the country, thereby increasing the number of jobs for the people.
