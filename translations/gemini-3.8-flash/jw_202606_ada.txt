BIOGRAPHY

I Served Jehovah for Nearly 70 Years in Cuba

GUSTAVO JOSEPH'S STATEMENT

I was born in the year 1947 on a beautiful island surrounded by water in Cuba. This island is where the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet. Three of us were born, one boy and two girls, and I am the eldest. We lived with our parents in a village in Esmeralda.

I remember that there was peace in our small village. We were not far at all from my father's relatives. Some of them were my aunt, my uncle, and my grandfather and my grandmother. We had enough food and money. This made us happy.

When I was five years old, my parents began studying the Bible with a man named Walton Jones. He carried out the preaching work with zeal, walking for about ten hours to reach our village. Whenever he came, many members of my family gathered at my grandfather’s house, and he studied the Bible with them for many hours. My parents, my uncle Pedro, and my aunt Ela all enjoyed what they were learning, and soon they were baptized as Jehovah’s Witnesses. Now, Ela is almost 100 years old, but she is still serving as a regular pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, Jehovah’s Witnesses in Cuba were able to worship Jehovah freely. The people in Cuba knew Jehovah’s Witnesses very well, because we regularly preached from house to house and gave them many Bible study publications. We were very active! When I think about what we were able to accomplish in Jehovah’s service before we faced difficulties, I feel happy. But it was not long before we began to encounter difficult situations.—2 Tim. 4:2.

WE BEGAN TO FACE DIFFICULT CIRCUMSTANCES

Sadly, during their journey, the water they drank caused them to contract typhoid. When they returned home, my uncle lost all his hair, but my father died. He died when he was only 32 years old.

After my father died, my mother decided that we should all go and live with a relative of hers in a village in Lombillo. This meant that we would be away from our relatives and my beloved grandparents. But we continued to serve Jehovah as a family.

What two covenants is the apostle Paul talking about in Galatians 4:24?

■ After the apostle Paul spoke about the relationship between Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar, he said: “These things may be taken as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, we explained that these two covenants were the Law covenant and the new covenant. But when we reexamined this scripture, we realized that we needed to make an adjustment in our understanding of it. It may be that Paul was not talking about the new covenant, but rather about the covenant that God made with Abraham. Why do we say so?*

Paul compared the Law covenant given at Mount Sinai in 1513 B.C.E. to Hagar. (Exodus 19:5, 6) Because all the Jews were imperfect, they could not keep the whole Law completely. This made them realize that they were slaves to sin. Furthermore, the Law covenant was to help the Israelites clearly identify the Messiah, for he alone could keep the Law perfectly. After the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, this opened the way for all sinful humans to be freed from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) Since Jesus fulfilled God's purpose, the Law was no longer needed.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul spoke about the difference between Hagar and Sarah, who was “the free woman” and who gave birth “through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise was the covenant Jehovah made with Abraham. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) In Genesis 22:18, Jehovah said regarding the covenant: “All nations of the earth will obtain a blessing for themselves by means of [Abraham’s] offspring because you have listened to my voice.”

This covenant explains other things concerning the promise that Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Genesis 3:15) It shows that the promised “offspring” would come through Abraham’s family line. Paul clearly explained that this “offspring” is primarily Jesus Christ. But the 144,000 anointed ones who ‘belong to Christ’ and will rule with him as kings are also part of this offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) On the basis of the covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham, many people who will accept Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings.

Why did Paul use ‘these illustrations’? Paul did so to help the Jews of his day see that if they continued to rely on the Mosaic Law, they would remain slaves, as was the case with Hagar. But if they accepted Abraham’s “offspring,” who is Jesus Christ, they would no longer be slaves, and they would be free like Sarah. In fact, they would be set free from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) But if they did not accept Jesus, it meant that they did not understand the purpose of the Law covenant, which was to lead them to Christ.

Many of the Galatians who were Christians to whom Paul wrote the letter were not Jews, and they were not under the Law. But some Jews who were in the congregation were pressuring the Gentiles who had become Christians to observe the Law, including circumcision. But Paul made them see clearly that it was not wise for them to do so. Because they had accepted Christ, they were free, and it was not necessary for them to become slaves to the Law again.—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

SOME THINGS THAT THE BIBLE TALKS ABOUT

Ancient Methods People Used to Catch Fish

Some of Jesus' followers were fishermen. (Mark 1:16-20) There is no doubt that they used boats in their fishing work on the Sea of Galilee.

This is how fishing nets are made. They take small ropes and weave them together, leaving many tiny holes in them just like a spider's web. If a fisherman wants to catch big fish, he weaves a large net and makes the mesh wide. Likewise, if he also wants to catch small fish, he weaves a small net. With the small nets, they put lead weights on them so that they become heavy and can sink to the bottom of the water. With the large nets, they put cork on top so that it floats on the water, and they also put large lead weights at the bottom so that it can sink to the bottom of the water.

At that time, nets were very expensive, and it took a lot of effort to maintain them so that they would last. But the rocks in the sea and the large catches of fish could tear the nets. (Luke 5:6) The Bible speaks of James and John who “were mending their nets.” They likely used needles and small cords to do that. (Matt. 4:21) It may be that every time the fishermen went to sea and returned, they mended their nets. Luke also speaks of some fishermen who “were washing their nets.” (Luke 5:2) Afterward, the fishermen spread out their nets so that they would dry. They probably did that so the nets would not rot.—Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus invited fishermen to follow him and become “fishers of men.”
