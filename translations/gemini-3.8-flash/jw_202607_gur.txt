30 | HEALING THE SICK

Act quickly to resolve the issue!

HAS a brother done something that upset you to the point that your relationship with him is no longer good? If you want to resolve the matter so that you can be friends again, you need to act quickly and see what you can do to bring about peace.*

For example, if a person's leg is broken, a doctor must treat it so that it can heal properly. But if he does not allow a doctor to treat it, the leg will not be able to heal. What is worse than that is, perhaps the bones may not align properly. That will also make it impossible for him to walk well.

Just as a person’s leg gets fractured and he quickly seeks treatment, in the very same way, if you and a brother have an issue with each other, it is fitting that you act quickly to settle the matter. If you do not do so, the relationship between you and your brother will spoil and become painful. That can make you nurse grievances. Jesus advised us, saying: “Settle matters quickly.” (Mt. 5:25) Therefore, go to your friend so that you can converse and understand each other well. Consider these two reasons why doing that can be difficult for you.

First, because the matter hurts you so much, you may not want to settle it. Consider the case of Euodia and Syntyche. They were so angry with each other that it was difficult for them to settle the matter. Even the apostle Paul was in Rome and heard about their matter. This shows that it had been a long time and they had not settled their matter. Paul told them to strive to settle the matter so that there would be peace. (Phil. 4:2) If they humbled themselves, forgave each other, and talked to understand each other well, they could become friends again.

No, you might think that since it is your friend who has offended you, he or she is the one who should come and tell you that they are sorry. But what matters is that you want to be friends again, not to accuse them. When a person's leg is broken and a doctor is treating it, the doctor looks at what he can do to heal the leg properly, not investigating to know everything that caused the leg to break. When you meet to talk with your friend, do not look at who is at fault, but rather consider what you can do to resolve the matter so that your friendship will become strong again.

Therefore, if you have an issue with your brother, meet with him and resolve the matter today so that your friendship will be strengthened again. “Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry.” (Eph. 4:26) If you act quickly to resolve the matter, it will help prevent “the peace that you have” from being ruined.—Eph. 4:1-3.

* If a brother or sister does something to you that you consider to be a minor matter, meet with the person to settle the matter and forgive each other. (Mt. 5:23, 24) But if it is a serious matter, you should tell the elders so they can help you. (Mt. 18:17) However, before you tell the elders, you must first apply the counsel found in Matthew 18:15, 16.

# If a fellow believer does something that upsets us but does not ruin our friendship, we can overlook the matter.—Prov. 26:20.

Humility Can Give a Good Answer in the Congregation Study

At congregation meetings, we have the opportunity to give comments that encourage our brothers and sisters. (Heb. 10:24, 25) How can we prepare ourselves to give comments that encourage others?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask him to help you give an answer that will honor him and encourage others.—Ps. 35:18.

• Try to give the answer in your own words instead of reading directly from the publication. Giving the answer as you understand it will strengthen the faith of your brothers and sisters.

• Keep your answers brief. Brief answers encourage others and allow more people the opportunity to comment.—Prov. 10:19.

• Have a good attitude when you are giving an answer. When you are giving an answer, do not speak as if you are lecturing others or scolding them.

Even if you do not get an opportunity to comment, you can still encourage your brothers and sisters. Listen carefully and show by your expression that you appreciate their comments.—Prov. 10:21.
