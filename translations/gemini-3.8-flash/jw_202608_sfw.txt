What was happening at the square or the entrance to the ancient cities at that time?

In ancient times, when a traveler arrived at a town, they would meet a public square. However, it was not an ordinary entrance. Rather, because people usually entered the town at regular times, many things took place there at that time. We find many accounts concerning public squares in the Bible, and it also lets us know how things took place at that time.

And they traded in front of the gates. The Hebrew word rendered "gate" did not only mean the entrance to a town, but it was often a place where people gathered or traded. In some places, they even made booths or built houses where many people could trade.—Neh. 8:1.

And merchants sold goods in places near the city gates. If you go to Jerusalem, some of the gates were called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. The goods that were sold in those squares are likely what gave those places their names.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

And people even met there to trade, or they would sit there and converse. People like Abraham and Boaz bought property from others at the city gate. And witnesses were present. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Also, if travelers came to a city and found no one to welcome them into their home, they would spend the night out in the open at the city gate. (Judg. 19:15-21) Furthermore, if someone wanted to hear news of what had happened, that is where they went.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

And the judges judged cases at the entrance of the city gate. If a matter arose among them, they judged it before all those who were present. When it happened like that, it allowed those people to clearly hear the matter that occurred and how the person knelt down. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because they judged the matter in public, it made them decide

The prophets and other men stood at the city gate to make their proclamations, because they knew that many people gathered there. PEOPLE AND THINGS THE BIBLE TALKS ABOUT

The River That Was Cold, or River of Crying Today

Among the about 30 trees mentioned in the Bible, one of them is the almond tree found in Israel. When winter is ending, it is among the trees that bloom, and it is on the mountains. Its flowers are white, and some are also pink, and it is very beautiful. It is at the end of the month of January that its flowers appear. Therefore, it is not surprising that in the Hebrew language, the name used to call that almond tree means "the one that awakens early." But what does this help us understand regarding what happened with that tree?

In the Hebrew language, the expression used in Jeremiah 7:25 shows that Jehovah woke up early every day. The Watchtower of March 15, 2011, explained: “Jehovah compared himself to that almond tree. That tree wakes up early or blooms early. Jehovah also wakes up early every day. What does that mean? It means that he continually sends his prophets to his people so that they might know the consequences of their disobedience. It also means that he does not rest until everything he has spoken comes to pass.”—Jer. 1:11,12.

Because some of the flowers of this tree are white, the Bible compares it to the gray hair of those who have grown old. (Eccl. 12:5) Also, because of the beauty of these flowers, when Bezalel made the lampstand that would be placed in the tent of meeting, he made the cups for the oil resemble these flowers.—Ex. 37:17-20.
