WHEN RINGWORM AND BIRDS CAME INTO THE TOWN

IT IS SAID that there was a certain small town, and whenever a certain Bird arose, it would come and tear apart the people's jaws. And when Ananse heard of this matter, he set off and went to that town. When he arrived in the town, he said to the people of the town, "Why is it that in a huge town such as this, all the people in it have your jaws torn apart?" The chief of the town said, "Father, a certain little bird, whenever the sun stands overhead at noon, it comes and catches us, and tears apart our jaws." Kwaku Ananse said, "As for this, I do not agree to it, and as for me, I will kill it." The chief said, "Ah! this entire town could not do anything, and you, one single person, what are you going to do?" Ananse said, "Look at this knife of mine, right now when I sharpen it, I will use it to cut off its head like this, bam!" When the sun reached noon, the Bird came and perched on a tree, and lifted up its voice:

Oh, oh parrots, parrots oh, Oh!

Do not take me along, oh!

Welcome!

And Ananse also began:

"The chin and teeth, oh, Ah!

Chin and toothless jaw, oh, Oh!

His father, oh his father, Oh!

And says I have arrived!

Yes, his father."

Then the Bird came and stood at the crossroads. He said, "Who has responded to me today and insulted me, when I called out to all the townspeople and hunters to respond, yet no one spoke?"

And he raised it again:

Oh, oh parrots, parrots oh, Oh!

Do not take me along, oh!

Red cheeks!" And Ananse said:

Chin and teeth, oh, oh!

The chin and its toothless jaw, oh, Oh!

His/her teeth, oh his/her teeth, Oh!

And that I have come!

Yes, his father.”

And Kwaku Ananse came and sat at his doorway. And the noise and commotion were great, and all the townspeople fled, leaving Ananse behind. Then the bird flew and came to Kwaku Ananse's doorway; and Kwaku Ananse ran into the room and pulled the woven mat over the entrance, and the bird began:

"Oh, oh red, red oh, Oh!

Don't abandon me, oh!

Welcome!

And Kwaku Ananse had fled and hidden in the pasture, and kept completely quiet:

Chin and teeth, oh, oh!

The chin and its toothless jaw, oh, Oh!

His father, oh his father, oh!

And say, I have arrived!

Yes, his father.”

When the Bird was about to climb the house to open it and eat from the broad dish, Ananse showed himself, and Kwaku Ananse cut off its head with a knife, bam! And the rest of the Bird's body flew away to the Coast. Kwaku Ananse took the crumbs that the Bird had scattered, poured them there, placed the Bird's head on top of them, and went to call the people, saying, "Come, for I have killed the Bird." They said, "Ah! That is Kwaku Ananse, a liar; he is surely lying." They said, "Choose one wise person among you to go and see." The wise person went and hid somewhere to look, and he saw the beast's head lying there, so he went back and said, "Friends, come, for Kwaku Ananse is not lying." All the people came. Whenever this one lifted his chin, he put some in his mouth. But you who did not get yours and went to take your neighbor's; that is why you suffered toothache. As for the Bird, too, its remaining body was brought from the Coast, cut into small pieces, and thrown into the bush. That is why many birds came into the world.

This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some to eat and let the rest return to me.
