NOTHING IS ANYWHERE

It is said that Leopard and his wife had two children. One day, the children's mother and father went into the forest, caught game, and brought it to them. One day, their mother and their father told them, "When you wake up, cry out, 'There is nothing anywhere, there is nothing anywhere'." Then they went into the forest and left their children there. Then Dankwata came, and the children were crying:

There is nothing anywhere, there is nothing anywhere!

Dankwata said, "Who told you to cry like this?" They said, "It was Father and Mother who said so." Dankwata said, "They are lying; you should cry like this: 'Time doesn't delay to change, time doesn't delay to change'." And the children's mother and their father came, and they heard their children crying:

Time doesn't delay in changing, time doesn't delay in changing!

They asked, "Who said you should cry like this?" They replied, "A certain man said so." Then their mother and their father said, "Do not cry like that, but cry saying, 'There is nothing anywhere, there is nothing anywhere'." They gave meat to the children and went back. Dankwata came, having forged two spears. Now where the children were lying, two trees stood there. When Dankwata arrived, he broke the trees and placed the spears before the stumps. He told the children, "Cry saying, 'Times soon change, times soon change', and when your mother and your father come, tell them that I was the one who said so." Dankwata flew and perched on the tip of the spear. It was not long before the children's mother and their father arrived. They asked, "Who said you should cry like this?" Dankwata said, "It is me!" When the male leopard attempted to jump and catch him, Dankwanta flew away, and the leopard went and pierced itself on the spear. Dankwanta went and perched on the other spear. The female leopard attempted to jump and catch Dankwata, and she too went and pierced herself on the spear. Dankwanta told the children, "You see that you will not get meat to eat again. That is why I told you, 'Times soon change'."

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
