A city gate is the way people enter the city. The place was a hub of activity because many people were gathered there. The Bible mentions more than 50 city gates and describes the various events that took place there.

The people gathered at the gate of the city. The Hebrew word "gate" stands for the gate through which they enter or exit the city. Sometimes, it was also a place where a market was held. In some places, the city gate through which they exited had a wide area where people gathered.—Neh. 8:1.

And merchants sold their goods at the city gates. In Jerusalem, there were gates called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate, and it is likely that they got their names from the goods that were sold there.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

Some traded at the city gate, and others also went there to conduct their own business. For example, both Abraham and Boaz made legal arrangements with others before witnesses at the city gate. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Also, if travelers did not find someone to take them into their home, at night they would sleep in the open square near the city gate until daybreak. (Judg. 19:15-21) Others also went there to listen to any new news that had occurred.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

The judges decided cases in a place near the city gate. The judges handled the cases before all those who were present there. And this gave the people the opportunity to listen carefully to the matter and also to see how the judges would decide the case.

And many things were happening throughout the whole area

(Deut. 21:18-20) This arrangement helped the judges to investigate the matter thoroughly. Once the judges made a decision, the elders acted quickly on that decision. (Deut. 25:2) It was expected that the judges who judged cases at the city gates would ensure that justice and good morals prevailed in the nation of Israel.—Deut. 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Some of Jehovah’s faithful servants encouraged the Israelites who were at the city gate to have confidence in Jehovah. Because people often gathered at the city gate, prophets and other people sometimes stood there to deliver messages to the nation. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu stated before all the people that what Jehovah said he would do to Ahab’s house had come true. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) King Hezekiah also gathered his military commanders at the city gate and encouraged them to rely on Jehovah.—(2 Ch. 32:6-8)

The Bible says that “wisdom” calls out in “the public square.” (Prov. 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, the public square was where elders gave good advice and judged righteously. Today as well, we find Jehovah’s wisdom in the Bible. And his servants throughout the world help people to seek true wisdom in the Bible.

The Bible mentions about 30 kinds of trees. One of them is the almond tree. If you go to Israel, the almond is among the very first trees to blossom toward the end of winter. The almond tree is seen with delicate flowers of pink or white before the flowers of any other tree are seen. The name that the Israelites called this tree means “the tree that awakens early.” Does the almond tree reveal any of the Creator’s qualities?

If we look at the footnote of Jeremiah 7:25, Jehovah spoke of himself as being like someone who rises up early in the morning. In the March 15, 2011 Watchtower, it was stated: “Jehovah likens himself to the almond tree. Just as this tree blossoms early, so Jehovah figuratively ‘got up early’ every day to warn his people about the trouble they would face when they did bad things. Furthermore, he showed them that he would watch over all his words to see them fulfilled.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

Sometimes, the Bible compares the gray hair of those who are elderly to the white flowers of the almond tree. (Eccl. 12:5) And when Bezalel made the lampstand that would be placed in the holy tent, he shaped the cups carved on the branches of the lampstand like almond flowers.—Ex. 37:17-20.
