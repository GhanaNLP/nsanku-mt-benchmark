DID YOU KNOW?

What things took place at the city gates in ancient times?

In ancient times, the city gates were busy, because every day, many people passed through there, and various activities also took place there. The Bible mentions city gates many times, and it also shows what took place there.

People gathered at the city gates. The Hebrew word translated "gate" refers not only to the door of a city. Rather, it also refers to the areas around the gate where people pitched their tents or set up their stalls. In some places, there were large gathering areas at the city gate where many people met.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants sold goods near the city gates. In Jerusalem, some gates were named after the things that were likely sold near those gates. For example, one gate was called the Fish Gate, and another was also called the Sheep Gate.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

Some people came to trade and others also came to attend to their needs there. Abraham bought a piece of land from someone at the city gate, in front of witnesses, in order to bury his wife. (Genesis 23:10-15, 17, 18) Moreover, it was at the city gate that Boaz redeemed Ruth in the presence of witnesses according to what the law of the Israelites stated, when the person who had the right of redemption refused to do so. (Ruth 4:1, 10) Some travelers also came to spend the night in the public squares at the city gates if no one offered them a place to stay. (Judges 19:15-21) People also went to the city gates to hear the latest news.—2 Samuel 18:24, 25.

Judges judged at the city gates. They judged cases before the people gathered there so that they too could hear the matter that occurred and how it would be settled. (Deuteronomy 21:18-20) Because they judged cases publicly, it caused the judges to render righteous judgment and settle matters in a right way. When the judges decided a matter, the elders acted on it immediately. (Deuteronomy 25:2) It was expected that when the judges were judging at the city gate, they would render righteous judgment, behave well, and encourage the people of the city to do likewise.—Deuteronomy 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Faithful men encouraged the Israelites at the city gates to put their trust in Jehovah. Prophets and others spoke God’s word to the people at the city gates because they knew that people gathered there. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu told the people that Jehovah had fulfilled the promise he made to punish the house of Ahab. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) Furthermore, King Hezekiah gathered his military commanders at the city gate and encouraged them to put their trust in Jehovah.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that “true wisdom” raises its voice and cries out loudly “at the city gates” (Proverbs 1:20, 21). In ancient Israel, it was at the city gates that trustworthy men gave good advice to the people and settled matters in a righteous way. Today too, we see Jehovah’s wisdom in the Bible, and Jehovah’s servants everywhere are helping people to study the Bible and come to acquire this true wisdom.

SOME THINGS THAT THE BIBLE TALKS ABOUT

The Tree That Bears Fruit Earlier Than Other Trees

The Bible mentions about 30 different kinds of trees. One of these trees is the almond tree. It is among the trees that bloom early as soon as the cold season begins. It produces beautiful blossoms that are pink or white toward the end of January, even before other trees bloom. In the Hebrew language, the Israelites call this almond tree "one that awakens." If you look, is there a connection between this tree and its Creator? Yes, there is a connection.

In the footnote of Jeremiah 7:25, Jehovah indicated that he gets up early in the morning. The March 15, 2011 English Watchtower stated: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakens early,’ so Jehovah figuratively ‘gets up early’ . . . to warn his people of the consequences of their disobedience. . . . Furthermore, Jehovah will not rest, meaning that his eyes will remain on his word until what he said comes to pass.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

At times, the Bible compares the white hair of older people to the white blossoms of the almond tree. (Eccl. 12:5) Because these blossoms are beautiful, when Bezalel made the lampstand in the tent of meeting, he shaped the cups that held the lamp oil exactly like the almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
