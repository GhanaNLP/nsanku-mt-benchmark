If a Problem Arises Between You and a Brother, Settle It Quickly!

HAS a brother or sister done something to hurt you deeply, damaging the friendship between the two of you? If so, you need to have the right attitude and take action quickly so that a good relationship can be restored between you.*

For example, if someone has a broken bone, only when a doctor treats it and sets the broken bone will the pain gradually subside. But if it is not attended to immediately, it will continue to hurt. What is even worse is that the broken bone will not heal properly, and it will remain permanently crooked.

It is the same with us. If a problem arises in the friendship between us and a brother or sister, we need to resolve it quickly. If we do not do so, the hurt within us will not go away, and that can cause us to resent the brother or sister, and gradually, it can completely ruin the good friendship between us. Jesus showed that it is important that we ‘settle matters quickly’ when a problem arises. (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, go see your friend, speak with him or her calmly, and tell him or her what is bothering you. But doing so is not easy at times. Why? Let us look at two reasons why.

THINGS THAT WILL HELP YOU

Prepare Yourself to Give an Answer That Will Encourage Others

When we attend congregation meetings, we have the opportunity to encourage our brothers and sisters through the comments we give. (Heb. 10:24, 25) But what can help us give comments that will build others up?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask Jehovah to help you so that the answer you give will bring him glory and encourage others. —Ps 35:18.

• Try not to read the answer directly from the publication. When you answer in your own words, others will see how much you love Jehovah, and they will benefit from it.

• Keep your answer brief. Often, a brief answer is very encouraging to others. In addition, it allows others the opportunity to also speak during the congregation meeting.—Prov. 10:19.

• Let your answer encourage others. When giving an answer, do not use your words to condemn the brothers or the congregation.

Even if you raise your hand and are not called on, you can still encourage others. You can do so by listening to others and commending them for the comments they give.—Prov. 10:21.
