The hope is that this year will be the year our education truly returns to our land, in our schools. As in all countries of the world, the devastating COVID-19 pandemic took us back three years before the start of the year 2022, causing our education to lag behind, and forcing our upper education level to also bring in education that is not in the formal education system to help fight the pandemic.

Just as we thought we were moving forward from the hardship that the deadly disease brought upon us, as we began to resolve our economic issues, another difficulty emerged from the situation to disrupt the economic gains we made from 2017 to 2019. The major one is the Russia-Ukraine war, which not only hindered the global economic recovery efforts in 2022, but also brought a severe economic crisis. In addition, it resulted in an unprecedented challenge as other countries tightened their monetary policies to our disadvantage, domestic borrowing conditions became difficult with high borrowing costs, all of which led to a shortage of foreign exchange and the depreciation of the cedi.

Therefore, even as other countries' inflation rates from the inflation basket are falling and our other country's inflation rate is rising, our interest rates are increasing, and their inflation is behind our desire to raise interest rates in this country, and our foreign exchange rates are continuously falling and depreciating against the currencies. This brings a high cost of living that affects all of us, the government and individuals. The rise in fuel prices brings an increase in the cost of food and transport, and inflation has risen to 40.4%, which we have never seen in this country since October 2022.
