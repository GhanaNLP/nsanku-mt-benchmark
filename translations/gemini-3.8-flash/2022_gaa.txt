MESSAGE FROM THE MINISTER

The great devastation brought by the deadly COVID-19 disease caused a major economic crisis everywhere, including Ghana. All Ghanaian citizens experienced its hardships through deaths, disrupted livelihoods, and ruined businesses, but the Government stood firm. I say with pride that the measures we took to contain it were bold and decisive, and the whole world bore witness to this with admiration. The Government also introduced policies to help revive businesses so that livelihoods could return to normalcy again.

Furthermore, through the challenges and opportunities that arose from this deadly disease, the Government, led by His Excellency Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, acted swiftly to establish a development programme that injected GH¢100 billion into activities and businesses to financially empower people and repair the damage caused by COVID-19, that is, the GhanaCARES “Obaatan Pa” initiative. This gave the citizenry immediate relief and respite, and the nation's enterprises began to get back on their feet again.

We appreciate your faith in these Governors, even at a time when efforts are being made to properly restore public operations after the devastation of this deadly disease. This time of testing is a period of hardship, and we are striving to emerge from this distress. The Holy Scriptures always guide us in times like these. As the book of Isaiah testifies in chapter 9:10: “The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place” (Isaiah 9:10). The Governors have resolved to work to fulfill the promises. Therefore, efforts will be made to put good measures in place and enact laws that will support the nation's representation so as to promote a good quality of life.

One of the key objectives of the 2022 Budget to be implemented over the next three (3) years is to firmly establish businesses to support economic growth, empowerment, and leave no one behind, so that every job is fulfilling, especially jobs for the youth. Government policies, in particular, will empower young men and women to help them realize their gifts and talents, enabling them to develop themselves for the progress of everyone.

To strengthen the spirit of confidence among the youth, in 2022 the Government will establish an initiative called “YouStart” to help them obtain capital to start or expand their businesses.

In the 2022 budget, certain measures have been outlined to continue tackling financial challenges so that post-pandemic recovery can be pursued with vigor. The government is committing itself to supporting initiatives so that young men and women can gain livelihoods and private sector growth. As we align with our Leader’s vision to advance the country through the “Ghana Beyond Aid” agenda, I appeal to all Ghanaians, especially the youth and women, to take an interest, think broadly, and actively engage in national affairs to rebuild and reposition the nation's economy.

I am asking everyone to get involved so that the nation can be built properly.

FINANCIAL ARRANGEMENT LAWS PROPOSED FOR THE 2022 BUDGET

Legal arrangements for fundraising

14. The government will implement certain measures that will promote businesses so that they can withstand competition in both domestic and foreign markets. Therefore, it is necessary to make the following laws:

• To bring benefits to the industry and increase production, the Government will extend the VAT relief for textile manufacturers introduced in 2019 on locally manufactured textiles for another two years.

• If this measure continues beyond two and a half years, the regulation that permits temporary suspension of imports will be applied in order to assess its immediate impact and expectations.;

• The tax charged on unprocessed mined gold will be reduced from 3% to 1.5%;

• From August 2022, the Government, through the Ghana Revenue Authority, will assist the Municipal Assemblies (MMDAs) to put in place a comprehensive system that will help with the collection of property rates and its accounting.

• The Government will review fees and charges collected on services for MDAs to implement starting from 1st January, 2022. The fees and charges to be collected will be indexed to the country's inflation rate and adjusted automatically;

• Tolls collected on all roads will no longer be collected once this budget is approved.

• An “Electronic Transaction Levy” or “E-Levy” will be introduced, a measure that helps widen the tax or revenue collection net into the informal sector through electronic transactions. The transactions on which this levy will be applied are mobile phone money transfers, bank transfers, money transfers, and merchant payments, and this levy will be at a rate of 1.75% to be paid by the sender, except for inward remittances where the recipient will also pay the tax. This implementation will take effect from February 1, 2022. Transactions of amounts that do not exceed GH¢100 or less per day are exempt from this tax payment.

THE PROGRESS AND STABILITY OF MAJOR INFRASTRUCTURE PROJECTS

33. Since the implementation period for certain national development policies has elapsed from (2018-2021), the year 2022 is the time to transform and reshape the nation under the ‘Ghana Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (MTNDPF)’ 2022-2025. This new policy framework is anchored on the multi-year national development activities known as the ‘Long-Term National Development Framework’ (Ghana@100), proposed interventions covering economic and social policies from (2017-2024), the Ghana Beyond Aid Agenda and its strategies, the COVID-19 relief and revitalization of enterprises, and supportive initiatives such as the (GhanaCARES) programme. These arrangements are designed to help implement and achieve the goals and targets set out in the UN 2030 (SDGs) and the African Union Agenda 2063.

DEFINITION OF SOME FINANCIAL TERMS

1D1F (One District One Factory): Industrialization in every district, which will make Ghana an industrial/transformation nation. The purpose is for the citizens to get jobs everywhere in this Ghana

Accountability: This is how the Governors account to the citizens for the things that have been done, and it makes the outcome visible. Money and other properties are among the things that the Governors oversee for the citizens regarding these matters of accountability.

Distribution: The ways by which things are distributed to Public Officers so that everyone gets a share

Exemption: Ways of reducing trouble

Budget: It is a plan that shows the money that will be needed, where it will be obtained from, and what the money will be used for. A family can write its budget to show the money the family has and how it will be used, for example: rent payment, savings, health, food, and others. If money is needed for the desired things, then the family looks for a loan, or reduces its needs, or looks for another way that will bring in money.

Budget deficit: This happens when the government borrows more than the money coming into the treasury

Capital expenditure: This refers to the money spent on capital projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water supply, factories and their machinery.

Employees' compensation/remuneration: This refers to employees' monthly salaries and other monies that go to employees, such as allowances, overtime pay, and other monies given to employees.

Benefactors/Donors: They are foreign nations, or organizations that help developing nations to progress financially. Some of these donors are the IMF, World Bank, ADB, or countries that have partnered with Ghana for assistance. Some of these countries are Germany, USA, UK, etc.

Year-End Bonus: It is a bonus in December.

Excess Expenditure: This happens when the Government spends money beyond the limit allocated in the budget that was approved by Parliament.

Free Senior High School or FREE SHS: The Government of Ghana, led by Nana Akufo-Addo, introduced the Free SHS policy for people who have completed JHS education and qualify to go to SHS without paying fees, in September 2017.

Grant Money: These are funds given by development partners to the Governments. Those funds are neither repaid nor reimbursed to those who provided them.

Statutory Transfers: These are funds allocated to certain institutions by law. Some of these funds include District Assemblies’ Common Fund, (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund and National Health Fund

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This refers to the total money that will be obtained as the value of the country's work over a certain period of time

Foreign Exchange Reserves: This is used to measure the nation's funds that can be relied upon to acquire foreign goods at a certain time. It is used to back the currency circulating within the country.

Inflation: It refers to the rising cost of goods and the amount of things money can buy at a given time.

IPEP (Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication is one of the policies of the NPP Government whose purpose is to eradicate poverty and completely bridge the gap between the poor and the rich, especially in villages and less developed areas, through the provision of developmental projects in these areas.

Expectations for Macroeconomic targets: It is a measure for the country's economic standing. Some examples are economic growth and its changes, inflation, changes in employment, growth in our trade with other countries, the success or failures of the government's fiscal policy, and the Bank of Ghana's objectives regarding money supply and its distribution in economic activities

MSME (Micro and Small Business Development Programme): It refers to small enterprises that employ few people and keep financial records, or that show profits that do not exceed a specific amount of money.

NABCo (Nation Builders Corps): also known as NABCO, is an initiative introduced by the Government to engage unemployed university graduates to help address social challenges in public and private sectors, so that they gain work experience for future employment in the country and contribute to national development.

NBSSI (National Board for Small Scale Industries): National Board for Small Scale Industries: It is a body under the Ministry of Trade and Industry. Their work is to oversee the development of micro and small enterprises (MSEs)

Non-oil Gross Domestic Product: This is the total amount of money generated from national economic activities excluding oil revenue. It is used to measure the growth of wealth in the country.

Non-Tax Revenue: This is the money that the government obtains from sources without tax. Some of these are fees for permits, fees for services rendered, profits, and others.

Other Routes: The other routes expected are from the other Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) to the Controller and Accountant General's Department and other payments owed to certain public funds.

Difference between total revenue and total expenditure: This is the difference between total government revenue and total expenditure from which revenue is derived. Interest payments are not included. This is used to measure all the revenue that the country will receive.

Projected progress: How the financial system of Ghana will proceed in the year.

Income: All money received by the nation for its activities, including what is obtained within the country and abroad

RFJ (Rearing for Food and Jobs): This is an initiative and effort by the government to foster competition in the livestock rearing business, to enable us to produce more livestock to help reduce meat imports from abroad, and for people to find employment and growth in livestock rearing.

Public subsidy: This is money that Governments pay to reduce the cost of certain things like electricity, water and petrol that citizens incur.

Subsidy: It is money paid by the government to reduce the price of something so that the citizens can get relief.

Transparency: It is the timely information that the Administrators/Governors give to the citizens about the things they do.
