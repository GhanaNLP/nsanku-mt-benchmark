KWAMENA OF ADUKROM

It is said that a certain queen mother's child was born, and whenever she was given to anyone in marriage, she said she did not want him. Then the King of the Ghosts, Adu Gyamfi, came to marry the queen mother's child. The queen mother's child said to her husband, “I will go with you to your hometown.” The man said, “Let me go, and when the Adae falls on Sunday, on Monday I will come and take you.” The woman said, “Never, I will go with you.” And so they went. And they went and saw a certain huge town with an iron gate at its entrance, and iron surrounded the town. And as for that town, if none of your relatives is dead, when you go and stand behind the gate and call out, they do not answer you. And as for the queen mother's child, none of her relatives had ever died. Adu Gyamfi spoke with her gently, and when they reached the gate, he went inside the house, and he did not speak to the queen mother's child again. And the Soul and the queen mother's child were left standing behind the iron gate, unable to find a way to enter the house. Then the queen mother's child raised her voice:

Adodowa, open the door for me, for it is I who have arrived; Adodowa Kwasi Kampire, open the door for me; Adodowa, open the door for me, I beg you, open the door for me.

And Adodowa did not know it. And she too did not know the way back to her hometown. And she and Kra went to the edge of the pit. And the queen's child took one brass basin and sat in it, and covered her head with another; and Kra sat beside her. That means you will go to the edge of the pit and the queen's child and her maidservant Kra are sitting there, having turned into Kwamena.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
