HOW ANANSE BECAME POOR

KWAKU ANANSE went to Nyankonpon and said, "Give me a grain of corn so that I may go and get a whole town for you." Nyankonpon said, "You cannot." Ananse said, "If I am unable to, I swear by your mother Nsia that I shall pay with my head." Nyankonpon said, "Take it and go." Ananse set off and went to a certain town. He reported his mission to the chief, saying, "Nyankonpon sent me to take this grain of corn somewhere, and if I stay, I should not let this corn sleep with the fowls." The chief said, "Ah! If fowl and corn sleep together, will it turn out well?" Ananse said, "As for you, let it go and sleep with them." The chief said, "Take it and put it there." When day broke in the morning, Ananse went to look, and the corn was not there. Ananse said, "Chief, the fowls have taken and eaten Nyankonpon's corn." The chief said, "As for this, I cannot contest it, so take the fowl and go." Ananse went and took one huge fowl and set off with it to another town. When he arrived there, he reported his mission to the chief, saying, "Nyankonpon sent me, and I will sleep here, but the fowl I am holding sleeps with sheep." The chief said, "Put it there." In the night, Ananse went into the sheepfold and killed the fowl. He took the fowl's intestines and wrapped them around the horns of a big ram. When day broke in the morning, he went to the chief and said, "Give me the fowl." When they went to get it for him, the fowl was dead. Ananse said, "Ah! A sheep has killed Nyankonpon's fowl." The chief said, "I cannot contest it, so take the sheep and go." Ananse set off with the sheep. He arrived at another town. He went to the chief's house. The chief said, "What is your mission?" Ananse said, "Nyankonpon sent me, and this sheep I am holding sleeps with cattle." The chief said, "Oh! Let it go and sleep with the sheep." Ananse said, "No, it is not Nyankonpon's ordinary sheep; it sleeps with cattle." The chief said, "Take it and put it there." Ananse went and put it there. In the night, Ananse went and killed the sheep and smeared its blood on the head of one cow. When day broke in the morning, Ananse said, "Chief, give me my sheep." They went to the pen; the sheep was dead. Ananse said, "Chief, a cow has killed Nyankonpon's sheep." The chief said, "I cannot contest it, so the cow that killed it, take it and go." Ananse took the cow and set off, arriving at another town. He tied the cow there and went to sleep. While he was sleeping there in the night, people came to tell the chief that one of his slaves had died. The chief said, "Go and throw him away, I have no coffin." Ananse said, "As for me, I want it, for Nyankonpon told me that if I find a corpse that is going to be thrown away, I should exchange the cow for it." The people said, "No." Ananse said, "Give it to me, and if you sell the cow, you will get two suru, and you can use it to buy a new slave." The people said, "You are not lying." And they accepted. Ananse lifted the corpse, set off, and went to another town. When he arrived there, he told the village chief, “Let this child of mine go and sleep with your children on this veranda; but the child is bad, he farts at night, so if he farts, let the children beat him.” This was the person who had been dead for three days. The children slept with him. Then they smelled something very foul. They said, “It is the child who has farted, let us beat him.” They pounced on him, titim! titim! titim! In the morning at daybreak, Ananse went to call his child. When he went, there he lay, dead. Ananse said, “Chief, it is God’s messenger who was with me, and your children have killed him.” The chief said, “I cannot say anything about it, so take the children away.” Ananse said, “Unless you, the chief, and all the people of the town go before God to defend yourselves.” The chief beat the gong-gong, saying, “All of you must go to God so we can defend ourselves.” Ananse led the way; all the people followed him. When Ananse reached the outskirts of the town, he sent a messenger to tell God that he was bringing the townspeople. God held a court for him and sat down. And as Ananse was going, he raised his voice: “Ananse bue! bue! I took a grain of corn to win a fowl. Ananse bue! bue! I took a fowl to obtain a sheep.

I used a sheep to exchange for a cow, I used a cow to exchange for a corpse, I used a corpse to exchange for an entire town!

Everyone said, "Listen to a song that Ananse sang." Ananse started up again:

Ananse, alas! alas! I used a grain of corn to win a fowl.

Ananse alas! alas! I traded a fowl to get a sheep, I traded the sheep to get a cow, I traded the cow to get a corpse, I traded the corpse to get a town!

Ananse went and arrived before Nyankonpon, and Nyankonpon asked Ananse for his news. Ananse said, "The single grain of corn that I took, saying I would bring a town with it; there stands the town." Nyankonpon said, "Praise him." "Yes! yes! yes!" Nyankonpon said, "What shall I give to thank you?" Ananse said, "I do not want anything, but it is 'trouble' that I want." Nyankonpon said, "Oh! oh! you servant, poverty is what you will die of, for you have gone and brought many things, and you say you do not want any." And so Ananse's poverty never left him.

This tale that I told, whether it is sweet or not, take some with you, and bring some back.
