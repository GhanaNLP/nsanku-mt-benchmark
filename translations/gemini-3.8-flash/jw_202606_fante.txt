I Have Served Jehovah in Cuba for About 70 Years

GUSTAVO JOSEPH'S OWN WORDS

I was born in 1947 on a beautiful Caribbean island called Cuba. The island of Cuba lies between the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean. We were three siblings; I was the eldest, and the remaining two were girls. We all lived with our parents in a village called Esmeralda.

I remember very well that our village was very peaceful. We lived close to some of our family members. Food was not scarce at all, and whatever we needed, we got it, so it made us happy.

When I was about five years old, a brother named Walton Jones began studying the Bible with my parents. He did not take his preaching work lightly at all; he would walk for about ten hours to come to our village to preach the good news. As soon as he arrived, many of our family members would gather at our grandfather and grandmother’s house, and he could discuss the Bible with us for many hours. My parents, an uncle of mine named Pedro, and an aunt of mine named Ela enjoyed what they were learning. So it was not long before they got baptized and became Jehovah's Witnesses. As I speak, my aunt is here in Cuba and is almost 100 years old, and she is still a pioneer.

When my parents accepted the truth, the Cuban government had not yet placed a ban on the activities of Jehovah’s Witnesses, so we were able to go out preaching and attend meetings. We were very well-known in Cuba because we went to people’s homes to preach the good news. Whenever people saw us, we were carrying our briefcases and bags, packed full of congregation literature. And as for walking, we did plenty of it! Those times were ‘favorable times,’ and when I remember how diligently we served Jehovah, it brings me great joy. However, ‘difficult times’ lay ahead of us.—2 Tim. 4:2.

The Communist government wanted many soldiers in the country, so they decreed that everyone should join the military. This deeply troubled Jehovah’s servants who were in the country, because Jehovah’s Witnesses do not involve themselves in politics and they do not join the military either. Even though the government had placed a ban on our activities, we continued to meet together to worship Jehovah. At one time, a brother even gave us the place where he kept his sheep to use for a meeting. We really appreciate the hard work the brothers did at that time to help us continue serving Jehovah. Sometimes, only two brothers were assigned to give all the talks and record them on cassette. The brothers had to hide themselves to do this work, so sometimes you could hear roosters crowing in the background, and you could also hear some small noises in the background. If there was no electricity where they were to hold the meeting, what the brothers did was attach a device to a stationary bicycle and have one brother sit on it and pedal so that they could generate electricity. This enabled them to play the cassette for the brothers to listen to the program. True, we did not have the freedom to serve Jehovah as we wished, and we did not receive the congregation publications that our Christian brothers in other lands received. Even so, we received everything we needed to keep our relationship with Jehovah strong. And this helped us to continue serving Jehovah together with joy.—Neh. 8:10.

What two covenants was the apostle Paul speaking about in Galatians 4:24?

◼ After the apostle Paul finished speaking about Abraham, his wife Sarah, and her servant girl Hagar, he said: “These things stand as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) Previously, we understood these two covenants to be the Law covenant and the new covenant. But after we reexamined this matter, we saw that an adjustment was needed. Paul was likely referring to the covenant Jehovah made with Abraham, and not the new covenant. To understand the reason for this adjustment, let us examine the matter a little more closely.*

Paul compared the servant girl Hagar to Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) He said that the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai in the year 1513 B.C.E. corresponds to Hagar. (Ex. 19:5, 6) There was no human who could keep that Law perfectly. Therefore, the Law constantly reminded the Israelites that they were slaves to sin. Furthermore, that covenant could help the nation of Israel to identify the Messiah when he came. The Messiah was the only one who could keep the Law perfectly. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, sinful humans could then be freed from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) Therefore, when Jesus died, it was no longer necessary to keep the Law.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul contrasted Sarah with Hagar. And he said that Sarah was a “free woman” and that she “bore her child through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise was the covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) This promise is mentioned in Genesis 22:18. There Jehovah told Abraham: “All nations of the earth will be blessed through your offspring, because you have listened to my voice.”

* This is an updated understanding. It replaces what was published in The Watchtower of March 15, 2006, pages 10-12.

This covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham shed a little more light on the promise that Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) This covenant made it clear that the offspring spoken of would come through Abraham. Paul explained that Jesus Christ is the primary ‘offspring.’ However, those who “belong to Christ,” meaning the 144,000 who will rule with him in his Kingdom, are also part of that “offspring.” (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) Because of the promise God made to Abraham, those who accept Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings.

Then, what did Paul mean when he said that "these things stand for something"? He meant that if the Jews of his day insisted on following the Law, then they would be like Hagar; they would be slaves forever. But if they accepted Jesus Christ, who was the true 'offspring' of Abraham, then they would be like Sarah, and they would be free forever. They would gain true freedom from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) But if they did not accept Jesus, it meant that they did not understand the Law covenant, because the Law covenant was to help them recognize the Christ.

Many of the Galatians to whom Paul was writing this letter were not Jews, so the Law did not apply to them or bind them. Yet some Jews insisted that the non-Jewish Christians must obey the Law. For instance, they insisted that they must be circumcised. Paul explained that it made no sense at all for them to keep the Law that made them "slaves," because Christ had given them "freedom."—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

A BIBLE STORY YOU WILL ENJOY

An Animal That Was Used for Fishing in Ancient Times

The Bible makes us see that some of Jesus’ disciples were fishermen. (Mark 1:16-20) They actively fished on the Sea of Galilee.

Ropes were used to weave the net, and it was woven with holes in it. The net with large mesh sizes was used to catch big fish, and the one with small mesh sizes was also used to catch small fish. Stones or lead were often fixed to the edge of the small-meshed net so that when it was cast, it would sink to the bottom of the sea. As for the one with the large mesh, stones or lead were fixed to one part of it, and cork or pieces of wood were also fixed to another part. Therefore, when it was cast, the heavy part sank to the bottom of the sea, while the light part floated on the surface of the sea.

The nets were very expensive. Stones at the bottom of the sea could tear the nets. Also, if they caught a lot of fish, it could tear the nets. (Luke 5:6) The Bible says that at times James and John were “mending their nets,” and they probably used a needle and thread to mend them. (Matt. 4:21) It is likely that whenever the fishermen returned from the sea, they mended their nets. Luke also spoke of some fishermen who were ‘washing their nets’ to remove any dirt from them. (Luke 5:2) When they were done, they would spread them out to dry so that the nets would not rot.—Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus called some men who knew how to fish well to be ‘fishers of men.’
