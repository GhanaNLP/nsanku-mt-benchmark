We are not alike, not alike at all

Whose actions caused doubt in the nation?

There was a man called Kyiriakyinnyee (Hater-of-Contradiction), and because of that, he went and built his village and lived in it. Then the beast, Antelope, went to him, and he took him and sat under a palm tree. Some palm nuts fell down from the tree. Antelope said, “Father Kyiriakyinnyee, your palm nuts are ripe.” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “That is how the palm nuts are; the bunch of palm nuts ripens three times in three palm branches; and when I mash it and make palm soup with it, it produces three water-pots of oil; and I take it to Akase, and go and buy the old woman of Akase; the old woman of Akase gives birth to my grandmother, so that my grandmother gives birth to my mother, so that my mother gives birth to me; and when my mother gave birth to me, I was standing there.” Antelope said, “As for this, you are lying.” Then Kyiriakyinnyee took an iron rod, struck Antelope on the head, and killed him. Then Palm-rat also came. And he took him to sit under the palm tree in the very same way. And all the animals came in the very same way. Now Kwaku Ananse took his cloth and took his large bag, and went to Kyiriakyinnyee's town. He said, “Father, good morning.” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “Welcome, Aku, where are you going?” He said, “I am coming to you.” And he took a stool and placed it for him under the palm tree. Kyiriakyinnyee said, “Cook food for Ananse to eat.” While they were cooking the food, Ananse and Kyiriakyinnyee were sitting under the palm tree. Then some of the palm nuts fell down. And Ananse took them and put them into his bag. He took them until his bag was full. They brought the food, and Ananse ate. When he finished eating, some of the palm nuts fell down again. Ananse said, “Father Kyiriakyinnyee, your palm nuts are ripe.” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “That is how they ripen, and they ripen three times in three branches; that bunch of palm nuts, when I mash it and make palm soup with it, it produces three water-pots; and I take it to Akase to go and buy the old woman of Akase; the old woman of Akase gives birth to grandmother, so that grandmother gives birth to mother, so that mother gives birth to me. Mother gave birth to me, and I was standing there.” Ananse said, “You are not lying, it is the truth; and as for me, I have some okra growing on my farm, and when it bears fruit, I join seventy-seven hooked poles together, yet they do not reach it; but when I lie on my back, I am able to pluck it with my penis.” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “Ah! I have heard; tomorrow I will come and see.” Ananse said, “Very well.” As Ananse was going, he chewed the palm nuts he had gathered, and was spitting the water from them onto the path. When day broke, Kyiriakyinnyee set off and went to Ananse’s town. But Ananse had gone beforehand and told his children, saying, “A certain man will come here, he hates contradiction; when he comes and asks for me, tell him that yesterday my father went somewhere and his penis broke into seven pieces, and he took it to the blacksmith to forge it, but he could not finish it, so he said he was going to finish it.” Before long, Kyiriakyinnyee arrived. He said, “Where has your father gone?” They said, “Ah! Father, yesterday he went somewhere, and his penis broke into seven pieces, and he gave it to the blacksmith, but he did not finish it, so he said he was going to finish it; and Father, did you not see blood on the path?” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “I saw it.” Kyiriakyinnyee said, “And where is your mother too?” They said, “Mother too, yesterday she went to fetch water, and suddenly her pot began to break; she mended it and mended it, but did not finish mending it, so she said she was going to finish mending it.” Kyiriakyinnyee did not say a word. Just then, Ananse arrived. He said, "Cook food for Kyiriakyinnyee to eat." While the children were cooking the food, they went and took only one tilapia and a lot of pepper; they used it to make very, very hot soup. When they finished, they brought it and placed it before Kyiriakyinnyee. Kyiriakyinnyee ate it. Now, as the pepper burned him painfully, he began to groan. He said, "Ntikuma, fetch water for me to drink." Ntikuma went to the water; he never brought the water. Kyiriakyinnyee said, "Ntikuma, where is the water?" Ntikuma said, "Ah! As the water is there, it has three kinds in it: father's own is at the top, mother's very own is in the middle, and this other one is at the bottom; so I want to bring you mother's own, and if I fetch it and do not fetch it properly, it will bring a dispute." Kyiriakyinnyee said, "Ah! You child, you are lying." Right there, Ananse said, "Beat him until he falls." And Kyiriakyinnyee said, "Why should I be beaten until I fall?" Ananse said, "You said you hate arguing, yet you have argued; that is why I say you should be beaten until you fall." Then they beat Kyiriakyinnyee until he died. Then Ananse cut up his meat and scattered it about. That is why so much disputing has come into the world.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
