DO YOU KNOW?

In ancient times, what went on at the city gates?

In ANCIENT times, city gates were not just places where people entered a city. It was a very busy place, because many people were always coming and going there. City gates appear many times in the Bible, and the Bible speaks about the things that took place there.

People used to gather at the city gates. The Hebrew word translated “gate” does not mean just the gate at the entrance of a city. It also refers to the area around the city gate where people could set up booths. In some places, when you enter the city gate, there was a public square where a multitude of people could gather.—Neh. 8:1.

People sold goods at the city gates. The gates that were in Jerusalem had names; some were called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. The things people sold there likely gave them those names.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People traded at the city gates, and others also went there to transact certain matters. Both Abraham and Boaz made legal arrangements with others before witnesses at the city gate. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Furthermore, when travelers were on a journey and night fell, and they entered a city and no one provided them with a place to sleep, they could spend the night in the city square near the city gate. (Judg. 19:15-21) Besides that, people went to the city gates to hear news of what had happened.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

Judges used to hold court at the city gates. If a case came before them, they heard it in the presence of all the people present, so that everyone would hear the testimony of the witnesses and the judgment of the case. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because they heard the case publicly like this, it made the judges judge righteously. When the judges finished hearing a case, they had elders who ensured that the judgment was carried out right away. (Deut. 25:2) The judges who heard cases at the city gate,

Everyone expected that they would ensure justice was done. Moreover, they were expected to treat everyone well and to encourage the people to do the same.—Deut. 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Men who served Jehovah faithfully went to the city gates to encourage the Israelites there to trust in Jehovah. Because people often gathered at the city gates, prophets and others frequently went there to make proclamations. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu stood at the city gate and told all the people that Jehovah had fulfilled the word He spoke against the house of Ahab. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) When we also look at King Hezekiah, he gathered his military commanders at the city gate and encouraged them to trust in Jehovah.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that “wisdom” cries out, and it raises its voice “at the city gates.” (Prov. 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, when you went to the city gates, trustworthy men offered counsel there, and they also ensured that justice was administered. Today, we can find Jehovah’s wisdom in the Bible, and his servants all over the world are helping people search the Bible to find that wisdom.

A BIBLE TRUTH

The Tree That Wakes Up Early in the Morning

Among the some 30 trees mentioned in the Bible is the almond tree. It is one of the trees that blossoms immediately after the cold season. By the end of January, its beautiful pink or white flowers have already begun to appear. The literal meaning of the tree’s name in the Hebrew language is “the awakened one.” Looking at it, can this tree help us learn something about the Creator?

Looking at Jeremiah 7:25, the footnote there says of Jehovah that he rises up early in the morning. The March 15, 2011 Watchtower states: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakes’ early, so Jehovah was, as it were, ‘getting up early’ . . . to warn his people about the consequences of their disobedience. . . . Jehovah would not rest, but he would ‘keep awake’ until the prophecy he had spoken was fulfilled.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

Sometimes, the Bible compares the white almond blossoms to the gray hair of elderly people. (Eccl. 12:5) Furthermore, the lampstand that stood in the tent of worship had cups on its branches into which oil was poured. Because the almond blossom is beautiful, it was likely for this reason that Jehovah told Bezalel to make the cups like almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
