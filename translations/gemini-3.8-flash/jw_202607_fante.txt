I Have Let Jehovah Continue to Train Me

KEN KIUCHI'S OWN WORDS

I will never forget what I saw the first day I set foot where our books were printed at Brooklyn Bethel in New York, U.S.A. The printing presses were noisy and running back and forth at full speed, and I had never seen anything like it. The paper being worked on had filled the room with dust, and the smell of machine oil was also all over the place.

Of the many things I saw there, what touched my heart the most was the brothers and sisters who were working there. Many people considered the work that the young brothers and sisters were doing there to be menial work. Also, with the hard work they did, people did not often even see them to praise them. Yet, they were happy with the work they did and they were also content. If it is said that someone is serving God with humility, these brothers and sisters made me see what it truly means.

Jehovah has trained me for many years and has helped me to serve him well. Before I came to know the truth, I was a Buddhist. But first let me tell you how I came to know the truth.

YOU SOUGHT GOD IN YOUR YOUTH

I was born in Chicago, Illinois, U.S.A., and that is also where I was raised. My parents had four children, and I am the eldest. My parents moved from Japan to live in Chicago, with the hope that if they lived in America, things would go very well for them. They also wanted their children to attend good schools so that it would be well with them in the future.

My parents were Buddhists, and they did not take their religion lightly at all. In the house where my mother was raised, there was a Buddhist temple. My father and his two brothers were Buddhist priests. So when I was a child, we did not neglect Buddhism at all. Every day, when I returned home from school, I would first burn incense for our Buddha idol, and I would give it rice and water. Also, on Sundays, we would go to the Buddhist temple to chant in front of an idol similar to the one in our home, but this one was larger and covered entirely with gold.

When I was seven years old, I remembered that one day, I stared quietly at the idol in our house, and I asked myself, ‘If I die, what will happen to me?’ Buddhism had taught me that if I died, I would go on to continue my life where the spirits live. Right then, I thought, oh, so one day I will die and I too will become a spirit? If so, would that not mean I could never enjoy myself as human beings do again? Settle matters with him quickly!

Has a fellow Christian done something that has hurt you so much that, as we speak, your relationship is strained? If so, then do not let the matter linger; make an effort to quickly settle things with your brother so that peace may reign between you.*

Now let us use this illustration: If someone breaks a bone, he goes to see a doctor to have it set so that with time, the bone will mend and will no longer cause him pain. If the person just sits back and does not go to see a doctor, the pain will not stop, and the bone might even heal crookedly.

Just as a person needs to see a doctor immediately to have a broken bone set, so too when we have a problem with a fellow Christian, we must settle the matter between us quickly. If we do not do so, we will continue to feel hurt by our brother, and we might even harbor anger toward him. In the end, our relationship with him would be completely ruined. Jesus advised us that when we have an issue with someone, we should ‘settle matters quickly with him.’ (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, if you have a dispute with a brother, go to him and talk it over calmly with him. However, that is easier said than done. Why do we say this? Let us consider two reasons.

First, perhaps what happened has really upset you, so you do not even want to talk about it, let alone resolve it. Therefore, if you feel it will be okay, then overlook it and let the matter rest.# As for you, let us think

Concerning Euodia and Syntyche. It is likely that a dispute arose between them that was left unresolved. He encouraged them to do their best to resolve their differences and be united. Second, you may feel that your brother is the one who has wronged you, so he should apologize to you. However, what matters is that peace is restored between you, not who is right in the matter. A doctor treating someone with a broken bone does not care about what the person did that caused the bone to break; rather, what matters to him is setting the bone back in place. So when you go to talk to your brother about what happened, that is not the time to determine who is guilty and who is innocent. Instead, focus on how valuable the friendship between you and your brother is to you, and do everything necessary on your part to make things right between you and him.

So if a fellow Christian has done something that has hurt you deeply, go and settle matters with him right now so that peace will be restored between you.
