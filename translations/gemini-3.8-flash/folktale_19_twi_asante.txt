IF THE CHILDREN ARE MORE WELL-BEHAVED THAN THE WITCH

It is said that a great famine occurred, and Father Ananse, his wife Aso, and his children Ntikuma, Nyiwankonfwea, Afudotwedotwe, and Tikonokono made a village and lived in it. Every day Ananse got up and went to fetch food, wild yam, and they cooked and ate it. Then one day Father Ananse went into the forest; he saw a very beautiful earthenware bowl sitting there. He said, "This bowl is beautiful." The bowl said, "I am not beautiful." Ananse said, "What is your name?" It said, "My name is Wash-a-little-and-eat." Ananse said, "Wash a little so that I may see." And the bowl washed up palm nut soup filled to the brim, and Ananse ate all of it. When he finished, he asked the bowl, "What is your taboo?" The bowl said, "My taboo is red clay and pentowa." Ananse took the bowl home and placed it on the scaffold. From then on, when Ananse went to the forest, brought food, and Aso finished cooking and called Ananse, he would say, "Oh! You are the ones who need it, as for me, an old man, what will eating do for me? You children are the ones who need it; if you are satisfied, then my heart is at peace." While they were eating, Ananse went behind the house and climbed onto the scaffold where the bowl was. He said, "This bowl is beautiful." It said, "I am not beautiful." He said, "What is your name?" It said, "My name is Wash-a-little-and-eat." Ananse said, "Wash a little so that I may see." And it washed up peanut soup, and Ananse ate it. Whenever he went up, it was like that. When Ntikuma looked at him, he saw his father was not growing lean, and yet he was not eating with them; so Ntikuma followed him to go and find out what his father had obtained. His father set off into the forest; Ntikuma climbed up and saw the bowl. He called his mother and his siblings, and they went up. Ntikuma said, "This bowl is beautiful." It said, "I am not beautiful." He said, "And what is your name?" It said, "My name is Wash-a-little-and-eat." He said, "Wash a little so that I may see." And the bowl washed up palm nut soup filled to the brim. Ntikuma, his mother, and his siblings finished eating. Then Ntikuma asked the bowl, "What is your taboo?" The bowl said, "My taboo is red clay and pentowa." Ntikuma told Afudotwedotwe, "Bring me some." And he brought it, and he smeared it with the red clay and also smeared it with the pentowa. Then they all came down. Father Ananse brought food home. Aso finished cooking. They called Ananse; he said, "Why, did you not hear what I said yesterday? I said from today onwards, when I bring food, you should eat it, for you are the ones who need it." Aso and her children ate. Father Ananse bathed; he climbed onto the scaffold; he said, "This bowl is beautiful." Silence! "This bowl is beautiful." Silence! Father Ananse said, "Ah! It is because of this cloth, it is not nice; let me go and get my oyokoman cloth and wear it." And he came down, fetched the oyokoman cloth, and wore it. He took his sandals and put them on; he climbed back up there. He said, "This bowl is beautiful." Silence! "This bowl is beautiful." Silence! He turned it over, and when he looked inside, he saw that red clay and pentowa were in it. Ananse said, "It was none other than Ntikuma." Ananse smashed the bowl to pieces and came down; he stripped off the oyokoman cloth and threw it down; he went into the forest. As he went, a very fine whip was hanging there. He said, "Look, this is better than the first one; this whip is beautiful." The whip said, "My name is not 'beautiful'." He said, "Then what is your name?" It said, "My name is Abiridiabrada." He said, "Lash me a little so that I can see." Then the whip fell upon him, wham! wham! wham! Father Ananse said, "Pui! pui!" A certain bird told Ananse, "Say, Adwobere." And Ananse said, "Adwobere." Then the whip stopped beating him. Ananse took this whip and brought it home; and he went and put it on the drying rack. When Aso finished the food, she said, "Ananse, come and eat." He said, "You there, sleep in a hollow tree and sleep in your ears, I will not eat." Ananse climbed up into the rafters and sat there quietly. Then he climbed down; and he went back to sit somewhere else. Then Ntikuma climbed up into the rafters. He said, "Aha, Father has brought something again." Ntikuma said, "Mother! Nyiwankonfwea! Afudotwedotwe! Come, all of you! For what Father has brought far surpasses the first one." Then all the people climbed up into the rafters, and Ntikuma said, "This thing is beautiful." It said, "My name is not 'beautiful'." He said, "Then what is your name?" It said, "My name is Abiridiabrada." He said, "Lash me a little so that I can see." Then the whip fell upon them, and beat them severely! Ananse was standing somewhere, saying, "Tighten it, tighten it, whip that Ntikuma well, tighten it!" Now when Ananse looked and saw that it had beaten them too much, he said, "Adwobere." Ananse came and took the whip, cut it into little pieces, and scattered them. That is why whips came into the nation. That is also why when you tell your child something and they do not listen, they are flogged. This my folktale that I have told, whether it be sweet, or whether it be not sweet, take some away and bring some back.
