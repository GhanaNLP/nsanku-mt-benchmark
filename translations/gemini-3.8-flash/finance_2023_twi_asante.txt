THE MINISTER'S STATEMENT

There is no doubt that this year will be the year in which this nation experienced the greatest economic hardship in our history. As happened in various countries across the world, the COVID-19 pandemic struck us in the first half of the year 2020, bringing economic activities to a halt, which reduced the revenue flowing into the national purse, and we also incurred unforeseen expenditures to fight against this pandemic.

Just as we began taking steps towards the Positive Transformation Programme and thought we were doing our best to recover from the impacts of this pandemic, other problems emerged, which in turn overturned all the economic stability gains we carefully built from 2017 to 2019. The first is the Russia and Ukraine war, followed by the time we lost regarding the approval of revenue mobilization measures in the 2022 budget, which resulted in international credit rating agencies downgrading our creditworthiness. Furthermore, for the first time in about a decade, international capital markets were closed to us, the domestic debt market also experienced severe stress leading to a sharp rise in interest rates, and this increased borrowing costs, causing the value of our currency to depreciate steadily.

This means that, while foreign exchange earnings from international capital markets and from exports were declining, and due to our strong appetite for foreign goods, expenditures on imports continued to rise, and our external debt also kept rising, the value of our currency kept depreciating. This has made life difficult for both the Government and individuals. Because of the rising cost of fuel, the cost of goods has become high, such that in the month of October 2022, inflation reached forty point four percent (40.4% in October 2022).

We have been through a very severe trial, and the Government knows the hardship that this situation has brought upon you. The Government extends deep gratitude to farmers

INTRODUCTION

1. This 2023 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the Government's performance in 2022 and the amount of revenue the Government expects to raise to implement its policies and programmes in 2023. The theme of this Citizens' Budget is "Working together to restore strength to the National Economy through Growth and Entrepreneurship and ensure stability".

2. In recent years, almost every new Budget we prepare has introduced new development projects while turning our eyes away from ongoing ones. This situation has not only brought distress to our finances, but has also caused the nation to continually pay debts on development projects, leading to an extremely high increase in the cost of development projects because the deferred payments that should have been settled attract interest, which also rises.

3. In the year 2023, the Government will make new plans and activities, but we will ensure that it is our priority to complete all ongoing development projects.

STEPS TOWARDS FINANCIAL PLANNING FOR THE EARLY PART OF THE YEAR 2023

Wealth Planning

18. In order to anchor the revenue mobilisation effort, the Government will submit to this Parliament, for your consideration and approval, revenue measures equivalent to one point thirty-five percent of the country’s gross domestic product (1.35 percent of GDP). Some of these measures are as follows:

• Cigarette and tobacco product taxes will be restructured to align with the ECOWAS tax protocols, and excise taxes on spirits will be increased above that of beers.

• Increase the Value Added Tax (VAT) rate from 12.5 percent to 15 percent, expand the tax base of items subject to Value Added Tax (VAT), and take various steps regarding items exempt from Value Added Tax (VAT).

• The National Fiscal Stabilisation Levy (NFSL) will be changed to the Growth and Sustainability Levy (GSL) to address all the various issues.

• The Electronic Transfer Levy will be reduced from one point five percent (1.5%) to one percent (1%), and the daily threshold will be removed.
