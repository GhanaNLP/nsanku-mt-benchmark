THE MINISTER'S STATEMENT

There is no doubt that this year will be the year the nation experienced the greatest economic hardship in our history. As happened in various countries across the world, the COVID-19 pandemic struck us in the first half of the year 2020, crippling the economy and thereby reducing revenues coming into the national coffers, and we also incurred unexpected expenses to fight this pandemic.

Just as we began taking steps towards the Positive Transformation Agenda and thought that we were now making efforts to free ourselves from the effects of this pandemic, other crises struck, which consequently rolled back all the macroeconomic stability gains we achieved from 2017 to 2019. The first was the Russia and Ukraine war, followed by the delay in approving revenue-generating measures in the 2022 budget, which led international rating agencies to downgrade our creditworthiness. Then, for the first time in about a decade, the International Capital Market was closed to us, the domestic debt market also suffered severe distress with interest rates rising sharply and consequently increasing the cost of borrowing, which led to the continuous depreciation of our currency.

This means that, while foreign exchange received from the international capital market and earnings from our exports were declining, and due to our strong appetite for foreign goods, the cost of imports continued to rise, and foreign debt obligations also continued to increase, the value of our currency steadily depreciated. This has made living conditions hard for both the Government and individuals. Because the price of fuel continues to rise, the cost of goods and services has become expensive, such that in the month of October 2022, inflation reached forty point four percent (40.4% in October 2022).

We have gone through very difficult trials, and the Government knows the trouble that this situation has brought upon you. The Government expresses deep gratitude to farmers

INTRODUCTION

1. The 2023 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the Government's performance in 2022 and the revenue the Government expects to receive to advance its policies and programs in 2023. The theme of this Citizens' Budget is "Working together to restore strength to the National Economy through Growth and Commercialization and to ensure stability".

2. In the past years, it has almost been the case that with every new Budget we prepare, we introduce new development projects in it while ignoring ongoing ones. Not only does this situation put a strain on our finances, but it has also caused the nation to continue paying debts on development projects, which has greatly increased the cost of these projects because the postponed payments also attract interest that keeps rising.

3. In the year 2023, the Government will make new plans and activities, but we will ensure that it is our priority to complete all ongoing development projects.

Financial Plan

18. In order to sustain the revenue mobilization efforts, the Government will present before this Parliament for consideration and approval, revenue measures equivalent to one point thirty-five percent (1.35 percent of GDP). Some of these measures are as follows:

• The Electronic Transfer Levy will be reduced from one point five percent (1.5%) to one percent (1%) and the daily threshold removed.
