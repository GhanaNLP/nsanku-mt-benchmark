If it is done through proverbs, someone gets a wife and life.

THERE were three men, and they wanted to trade. They went to the northern grasslands to buy mats and various goods. They made their nephew carry some of them for them to sell. Then they arrived at a certain big town. Whenever that town’s Queen Mother's daughter was offered to anyone, she said she would not marry. When these strangers went there, the Queen Mother’s daughter was undergoing her puberty rites. The Queen Mother had bought two cloths and kept them for her daughter. When her daughter came out, she saw the three men. She said to her mother, "Today I have seen the one I desire, so slaughter a sheep for me to go and present to them." When the food had been cooked, the maiden went and took a whistling-leaf, cotton, cola nuts, and groundnuts, placed them on the food, and went to present it to the men. Then she said, "Whoever can explain the meaning, him will I marry." So the food was taken to them. The men uncovered it and said, "If it were not the Queen Mother's daughter who brought this food, we would have questioned her conduct." The men's nephew said, "If not for the respect and fear I have for you, I would have been able to explain the meaning." They said, "Explain it." The young man said, "The maiden says her mind has become as pure and white as cotton; and as for us who have arrived, if she does not get one of us, whom will she marry? The meaning of the whistling-leaf is that the day will come when she will miss him and whistle for him; the cola nut, its meaning is that the day will come when she will give it to him to chew; the groundnuts, their meaning is that she wants you to plant groundnuts for her, so that every year, when she goes to look beneath them, she will find some to harvest." And the Queen Mother’s daughter married the men’s nephew. When she married him, the Queen Mother's daughter asked him, "What is your name?" He said, "My name is Kwaku Susufuo." The maiden also said, "Mine too is Akua Susufuo." She married him properly. The next morning, the three uncles set off and went back to their own town. Kwaku Susufuo remained behind in his wife's town. He spent a year there, and whenever he got up, he would play marbles with his peers there. One day while they were playing, he won two points at once, and the people refused to let him have them and wanted to beat him. He turned around and looked behind him, and he saw a child sitting there; he took a stone and gave it to the child to take to his wife. When his wife received the stone, she said to her mother, "My husband wants to go to his hometown, for he says when a bird shows its underparts, it receives a stone." And the Queen Mother said, "If he is going, go with him." Indeed, she went with him. They arrived at Kwaku Susufuo's uncles' place. Later on, they said to him, "Kwaku Susufuo, take some cord so that we may go into the forest to set traps, so that we may get doves to serve your wife as a guest." But when they went, it turned out the elders had plotted to kill him. We went with Kwaku Susufuo; we reached the way; we bound him, and we told him, "We will kill you." And he told us, "I went to my wife’s town, I have acquired much gold, if you do not let me show you and I die, you will not find it." We asked him, "Where is the gold?" He said, "Go to my wife, go and tell her that I have bought meat and they say it is ten pereguan, and she should look into the sheepskin bag; if she looks into it and does not find it, she should look into the Krampan bag; if she does not find it, she should search into the knife-cut." And we came and told his wife the news. And she also said, "It is at the chief’s house, so let us go and get it." She went with us to the chief’s house; and the child told the news to the chief, saying, "The sheepskin bag, he says they have bound him; as for the Krampan bag, he says a vulture is going to eat his flesh; as for the knife-cut, he says they are going to cut his throat." Right there the chief beat the gong-gong so that they went there and rescued Kwaku Susufuo. And they brought him back. He caused the three elders to be killed. That is why they say, "Through proverbs someone gained a child to marry; and then he also gained his life."

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
