INTRODUCTION

Whereas recognition of the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family is the foundation of freedom, justice and peace in the world,

Whereas disregard and contempt for human rights have resulted in barbarous acts which have outraged the conscience of mankind, and the advent of a world in which human beings shall enjoy freedom of speech and belief and freedom from fear and want has been proclaimed as the highest aspiration of the common people,

Whereas it is essential, if man is not to be compelled to have recourse, as a last resort, to rebellion against tyranny and oppression, that human rights should be protected by the rule of law,

Whereas it is essential to promote the development of friendly relations between nations,

Whereas the peoples of the United Nations have in the Charter reaffirmed their faith in fundamental human rights, in the dignity and worth of the human person and in the equal rights of men and women, and have determined to promote social progress and better standards of life in larger freedom,

Whereas Member States have pledged themselves to achieve, in co-operation with the United Nations, the promotion of universal respect for and observance of human rights and fundamental freedoms,

Whereas a common understanding of these rights and freedoms is of the greatest importance for the full realization of this pledge,

the executive committee of the group

declare that:

It is expected that:



Order 1.

All human beings are equal. And everyone is created with a mind and wisdom. Therefore, everyone must love one another, respect one another, and honor one another.

Commandment 2.

The statement in this declaration of welfare is available to everyone without any discrimination. It does not matter whether a person is black or white, a woman or a man. The language someone speaks, the church someone attends, the political party someone belongs to, where someone comes from, the property they have, their birth, and the like do not prevent anyone from this arrangement.

Furthermore, no distinction shall be made on the basis of the country or territory to which a person belongs, whether that country is independent or under the jurisdiction of another country.

Order 3.

Everyone has the right to live in peace and freedom, free from any bodily harm.

Commandment 4.

No one should enslave anyone. The buying and selling of slaves is a trade that must be completely abolished.

Commandment 5.

No one should in any way subject any human being to torture, or cruelly punish or humiliate someone.

Commandment 6.

Everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law.

Commandment 7.

All people are equal before the law. The law protects all people equally without any discrimination. Under the law, there is no favoritism.

Commandment 8.

If someone violates any of these laws against another person, that person has the right to take legal action against them in accordance with the law.

Commandment 9.

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary arrest, detention or exile.

Commandment 10.

If someone is accused of something and taken to court, those who will judge the case must administer fair justice, without showing bias. The judges must be people who have no personal interest in the case at all.

Commandment 11.

If someone is accused, arrested as a result, and the matter cannot be investigated to determine the truth in it, the person is presumed innocent of that crime unless it is legally investigated and proven that he is guilty. Even if he is taken to court, he has the right to seek any assistance he can obtain to defend himself.

No one has the right, based on national or international law, to impose an improper punishment on someone for a wrong the person committed at a time when that law did not exist. And if it happens that a punishment already existed before a new punishment that is heavier than the former one comes into effect, the punishment that already existed at the time the person committed the wrong is the one that should be imposed on him.

Commandment 12.

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, his home, or his family affairs. No one shall defame anyone's reputation. The law protects every person so that nothing can hinder their well-being.

Commandment 13.

Everyone has the right to freedom of movement and residence within the borders of each state.

Everyone has the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country.

Order 14.

Everyone has the right to seek and to enjoy in other countries asylum from persecution.

If someone commits a non-political crime or acts contrary to the principles of the United Nations, that person does not have the right to be protected by this law from that crime.

Commandment 15.

Everyone has the right to seek asylum in another country.

No one can arbitrarily or deceitfully change someone's nationality. Also, if anyone wishes to change their nationality, it is their right to do so without any impediment.

Commandment 16.

Men and women of full age have the right to marry and to found a family, without any limitation due to race, nationality or religion, and they are entitled to equal rights as to marriage, during marriage and at its dissolution.

For the marriage to be valid, both partners must consent and agree to marry each other.

The family unit, which is based on father, mother, and children, is the foundation of social relations, and it is the duty of the community to protect them.

Commandment 17.

Everyone has the right to own property alone as well as in association with others.

No one shall be arbitrarily deprived of his property through intimidation or unlawful acts.

Commandment 18.

Everyone has the right to follow their own conscience, think their own thoughts, and attend any church they wish. This power allows a person to change their religion and belief publicly or privately without any hindrance, and regardless of whether they are among many people or alone.

Commandment 19.

Everyone has the right to express their opinion on something without any hindrance. Furthermore, everyone can also use any means to broadcast something to show their opinion or can seek and obtain information about something, regardless of whether they are a citizen or a foreigner.

Commandment 20.

Everyone has the right to join a peaceful group. There is also the freedom for people to meet together peacefully.

No one should force anyone to join a group they do not want to.

Article 21.

It is the duty of every person to have a say when their nation is being governed or discussed. The person can participate directly or take part through the means of transparent elections.

Every person has the right to an equal share of whatever their country's government does.

What the citizens desire is what their government ought to do. They carry out this responsibility through elections that are free from fraud, where one's vote is secret from another, and where everyone who is eighteen years old can vote.

Commandment 22.

It is the duty of every nation to protect every citizen. It must, through national effort or international cooperation that the nation has made with another nation, ensure that every citizen finds happiness and well-being in accordance with that nation's economy, resources, and culture that enable a person to live a fulfilled life.

Commandment 23.

It is everyone's duty to work. Also, there should be no obstacle to whatever work anyone wishes to do. The law must protect workers from exploitation and ensure that they have comfort in their work.

The appropriate remuneration should be paid for the same work done by a person without any discrimination.

It is every person's right to engage in any work of their choice, to receive a reward and an income that can enable them, their spouse, and their children to live happily as human beings, and if necessary, for the government to assist them in some way to protect them from distress.

Everyone has the right to form and to join trade unions for the protection of his interests.

Commandment 24.

Everyone has the right to rest and leisure if they work, and also reasonable limitation of working hours. Periodically, the person is granted paid holidays.

Commandment 25.

Everyone has the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and well-being of himself, his partner, and his children, who constitute the household. The things that bring comfort to the family or household include food, clothing, housing, medical care, and all necessary social services; and even in the event of unemployment, sickness, disability, widowhood, old age, or other lack of livelihood in circumstances beyond his control, he has the right to security and an adequate living.

Mothers, and especially nursing mothers, and all children are entitled to special care and assistance. All children, whether born in wedlock or out of wedlock, shall enjoy the same social protection without any discrimination.

Article 26.

Everyone has the right to education. Education in childhood shall be free, at least in the elementary and fundamental stages. Elementary education shall be compulsory. Technical and professional education shall be made generally available and higher education shall be equally accessible to all on the basis of merit.

The main benefit of education is that a person will learn, achieve progress in their life, understand their responsibilities, and fully comprehend their freedom. When that happens, it will foster understanding, patience, and friendship among nations, races, and all diverse religious beliefs, and it will help fulfill the goal of the United Nations to bring peace to all parts of the world.

It is the very first responsibility of all parents to determine the kind of education they want their children to receive in the future.

Commandment 27.

It is the duty of every person to take pleasure in their culture, to participate in all its activities, and to also benefit from science.

It is the responsibility of the nation to protect the work of anyone who has used their own intellect to write or create something and put their name on it, so that their labor is not in vain.

Commandment 28.

It is the duty of every person to live peacefully with joy and delight in their country or in another country, so that what has been declared in the United Nations Charter will be fulfilled.

Commandment 29.

It is the duty of every person to use their strength and wisdom to help improve the place where they live, because that is where has made them who they are.

Although every person ought to be self-governing and do what brings them peace and comfort, in order that no one obtains excessive pleasure that brings trouble to their peers, everyone must obey the laws of their nation or the group they belong to, so that a democratic life may protect human well-being.

In no case may these rights and freedoms be exercised contrary to the purposes and principles of the United Nations.

Commandment 30

This Declaration was not made with respect to any particular nation or group of people. For this reason, no one should rely on this Declaration to do anything against the goodwill concerning human rights and freedom that the United Nations had in making this declaration.
