HUMAN WELFARE STATEMENT

INTRODUCTION

Whereas recognition of the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family is the foundation of freedom, justice and peace in the world,

Whereas disregard and contempt for human rights have resulted in barbarous acts which have outraged the conscience of mankind, and the advent of a world in which human beings shall enjoy freedom of speech and belief and freedom from fear and want has been proclaimed as the highest aspiration of the common people,

Whereas it is essential, if man is not to be compelled to have recourse, as a last resort, to rebellion against tyranny and oppression, that human rights should be protected by the rule of law,

Whereas it is essential to promote the development of friendly relations between nations,

Whereas the peoples of the United Nations have in the Charter reaffirmed their faith in fundamental human rights, in the dignity and worth of the human person and in the equal rights of men and women, and have determined to promote social progress and better standards of life in larger freedom,

Whereas Member States have pledged themselves to achieve, in co-operation with the United Nations, the promotion of universal respect for and observance of human rights and fundamental freedoms,

Whereas a common understanding of these rights and freedoms is of the greatest importance for the full realization of this pledge,

the general council of the group

confessed that:

It is expected that:

This Universal Declaration of Human Rights now proclaimed will be a common standard of achievement for all peoples and all nations, to the end that every individual and every organ of society, keeping this Declaration constantly in mind, shall strive by teaching and education to promote respect for these rights and freedoms and by progressive measures, national and international, to secure their universal and effective recognition and observance, both among the peoples of Member States themselves and among the peoples of territories under their jurisdiction.

Command 1.

All human beings are equal. And everyone was created with a mind and wisdom. Therefore, everyone must love, respect, and honor one another.

Commandment 2.

This statement on welfare is for everyone without any discrimination. It does not matter whether someone is black or white, female or male. The language someone speaks, the religion they practice, the political party they belong to, where someone comes from, the property they own, their birth, and the like do not prevent anyone from this arrangement.

Furthermore, no distinction shall be made on the basis of the country to which a person belongs, whether that country is independent or under the sovereignty of another country.

Commandment 3.

Everyone has the right to live in peace and freedom without any harm coming to their body.

Commandment 4.

No one should enslave anyone. The buying and selling of slaves is a trade that must be completely abolished.

Commandment 5.

No one should in any way subject any human being to torture, or cruelly punish someone and humiliate them.

Commandment 6.

Everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law.

Commandment 7.

All human beings are equal before the law. The law protects all people equally without any discrimination. Under the law, no one is favored.

Commandment 8.

If someone violates any of these laws against another person, that person has the right to take legal action against them and deal with them in accordance with the law.

Commandment 9.

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary arrest, detention or exile.

Commandment 10.

If someone is accused of something and the person is taken to court, those who will judge the case must judge righteously, without turning a deaf ear. The judges must be people who have no involvement in the case at all.

Article 11.

If someone is accused, arrested for it, and the matter has not yet been investigated to find the truth in it, the person is presumed innocent of that crime, unless it is proven through legal means that he is guilty. Even if he is taken to court, he has the right to seek any help he can get to defend himself.

No one has the right, based on national or international law, to impose an improper punishment on someone for a wrong that the person committed at a time when that law did not exist. And if it happens that a punishment already existed before a new, harsher punishment came into effect, the punishment that was already in place at the time the person committed that wrong is the one that must be used to punish them.

Commandment 12.

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, his home, or his family affairs. No one shall defame another person's name. The law protects everyone so that nothing can hinder their well-being.

Commandment 13.

Everyone has the right to freedom of movement and residence within the borders of each state.

Everyone has the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country.

Order 14.

If someone's compatriots are persecuting them and seeking to destroy them, it is their right to flee to a place of refuge in another country and seek asylum there.

This right may not be invoked in the case of prosecutions genuinely arising from non-political crimes or from acts contrary to the purposes and principles of the United Nations.

Section 15.

Everyone has the right to go to another country and become a citizen there.

No one can arbitrarily, through deceitful means, change someone's nationality. Also, if someone wants to change their nationality, it is their right to change it without any hindrance.

Commandment 16.

Men and women of full age have the right to marry and to found a family, without any limitation due to race, nationality or religion, and they are entitled to equal rights as to marriage and its dissolution.

For the marriage to be valid, both partners must consent and agree to marry each other.

The family, which is the foundation of father, mother, and children, is the fundamental basis of kinship, and it is the duty of society to protect them.

Commandment 17.

Everyone has the right to own property alone as well as in association with others.

No one shall be arbitrarily deprived of his property through intimidation or unlawful acts.

Order 18.

Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion; this right includes freedom to change his religion or belief, and freedom, either alone or in community with others and in public or private, without any hindrance.

Commandment 19.

It is everyone's duty to be able to express their opinion about something without any hindrance. Furthermore, everyone can use any means to publicize something to show their opinion, or can seek and obtain opinions about something, regardless of whether they are a citizen or a foreigner.

Commandment 20.

Everyone has the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and association. People also have the freedom to meet together peacefully without doing any harm.

No one should force anyone to join a group they do not want to.

Commandment 21.

It is the duty of every person to participate when their country is being governed or discussed. The person can participate directly or through free and fair elections.

Everyone has the right to receive an equal share of whatever their country's government does.

What the people want is what their government must do. They carry out this responsibility through transparent elections, where one does not see another's vote, and where everyone who has reached eighteen years of age votes.

Commandment 22.

It is the duty of every nation to protect every citizen. It must, through national effort or international cooperation with another nation, ensure that every citizen achieves happiness and well-being in accordance with the state's economy, resources, and culture that enable a person to become whole.

Order 23.

It is the duty of every human being to work. There should also be no obstacle to whatever work anyone wishes to do. The law must protect workers from exploitation and ensure that they find comfort in their work.

Equal pay for equal work must be paid without any discrimination.

It is every person's responsibility to do whatever work they desire, receive its proceeds and remuneration that can enable them, their partner, and their children to live happily as human beings, and in the worst-case scenario, the government supports them in some way to protect them from hardship.

Everyone has the right to form or join trade unions for the protection of their work or themselves.

Commandment 24.

Everyone has the right to rest and leisure, including reasonable limitation of working hours and periodic holidays with pay.

Commandment 25.

Everyone has the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and well-being of himself, his partner, and his children, who constitute the household. The things that bring comfort to the family or household include food, clothing, housing, medical care, and all necessary social services, and even in the event of unemployment, disability, widowhood, old age, or any other lack of livelihood in circumstances beyond his control, he has the right to security.

Mothers, especially those nursing children, and all children must be given special care and assistance. A child born in wedlock and, with all due respect, a child born out of wedlock are all children who need proper care without any discrimination.

Article 26.

Everyone has the right to education. Education shall be free, at least in the elementary and fundamental stages. Elementary education shall be compulsory. Technical and professional education shall be made generally available and higher education shall be equally accessible to all on the basis of merit.

The main benefit of education is for a person to learn, achieve progress in life, understand their responsibilities, and properly understand their freedom. When it happens this way, it will foster understanding, tolerance, and friendship among all nations, races, and various religious beliefs, and help fulfill the goal of the United Nations to bring peace to all corners of the world.

It is the primary responsibility of all parents to decide on the particular education they wish for their children to have in the future.

Commandment 27.

Everyone has the right freely to participate in the cultural life of the community, to enjoy the arts and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits.

It is the right of every person who has created or written something from their own mind and put their name to it that the nation protects it for them so that their effort is not in vain.

Order 28.

Everyone has the duty to live in peace with happiness and joy in their country or in another country, so that what has been proclaimed in the United Nations Charter will be realized.

Commandment 29.

It is every person's responsibility to use their strength and wisdom to help their community develop, because that is where they became who they are today.

Although everyone ought to have self-control and do what will make them comfortable, in order that no one obtains excessive pleasure that brings trouble to their peers, everyone must obey the laws of their nation or community in which they belong, so that a democratic way of life will protect human welfare.

These rights and freedoms may in no case be exercised contrary to the purposes and principles of the United Nations.

Commandment 30

This Declaration was not made with any particular nation or group of people in mind. For this reason, no one should base upon this Declaration to do anything contrary to the good intentions regarding human rights and freedoms that the United Nations had in making this declaration.
