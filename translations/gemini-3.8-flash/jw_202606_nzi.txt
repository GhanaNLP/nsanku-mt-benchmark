I Have Served Jehovah for About 70 Years in Cuba

GUSTAVO JOSEPH'S STATEMENT

I WAS born in 1947 on a beautiful Caribbean island called Cuba. It is located between where the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet. I have two younger sisters. All five of us—me, my parents, and my two sisters—lived in a town called Esmeralda.

I remember that we had peace of mind in our village. We lived close to some of my father’s siblings and his parents. We did not lack food, and we were content.

When I was five years old, Brother Walton Jones began to study the Bible with my parents. He was an energetic evangelizer, and he used to walk for about ten hours to reach our village. Every time he came, many members of my family would gather at my grandmother and grandfather’s house, and they would spend many hours studying the Bible. My parents and my father's siblings, Pedro and Ela, enjoyed what they were learning, so before long they were baptized as Jehovah’s Witnesses. Ela is almost 100 years old, yet she is still a regular pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, Jehovah’s Witnesses could go out preaching and attend meetings in Cuba without anyone bothering them. We were well-known for carrying bags full of Bible-based literature as we went from house to house to preach. We used to walk a lot! I often remember how we served Jehovah in my youth during that “favorable time.” But a “difficult time” lay ahead of us.—2 Tim. 4:2.

WHEN DIFFICULT TIMES BEGAN

When I was almost five years old, my father and his brother went to a regional assembly in another town. Sadly, during that trip, they drank some water, and it caused them to contract typhoid. I remember that when they returned, all of my father's brother's hair had fallen out, but he recovered. However, my father died. He was only 32 years old.

When my father died, my mother made us move to live with her brother in a village called Lombillo. So we left behind my father's relatives and my grandparents, whom we loved dearly. But we continued to serve Jehovah.

On August 26, 1957, I was baptized in a small river near Lombillo. I was ten years old. But I did not know that in less than two years, the lives of Jehovah's Witnesses in Cuba would change completely. In 1959, the government was overthrown and a new government began to rule.

Which two covenants did the apostle Paul speak about in Galatians 4:24?

◼ After the apostle Paul spoke about the relationship between Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar, he also said: “These things may be taken as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, we understood that the two covenants were the Law covenant and the new covenant. However, further consideration of this scripture has made us realize that an adjustment is needed. It appears that Paul was referring to the Abrahamic covenant, and not the new covenant. Here are some reasons why he said so.*

Paul compared Hagar, who was a slave, to Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) Hagar therefore represented the Law covenant that was made at the foot of Mount Sinai in 1513 B.C.E. (Ex. 19:5, 6) No imperfect human could keep the Law perfectly, so it reminded the Jews that they were slaves to sin. This covenant was meant to help the Israelites identify the Messiah, the only human who could keep the Law perfectly. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, he made it possible for imperfect mankind to be redeemed from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) After Jesus fulfilled God's purpose, the Law was no longer needed.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul said that Hagar was different from Sarah, “the free woman” who had a child “through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise was the Abrahamic covenant. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) It is stated briefly in Genesis 22:18. Jehovah said: “And by means of your offspring [Abraham's offspring] all nations of the earth will obtain a blessing for themselves because you have listened to my voice.”

* This is an updated understanding of the matter discussed in The Watchtower, March 15, 2006, pp. 10-12.

That covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham shed much light on the promise that Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) We see in the account that the “offspring” that was spoken of would come through Abraham. Paul explained that this “offspring” primarily represents Jesus Christ. However, the 144,000 anointed ones who “belong to Christ,” and who will rule with Jesus in his Kingdom, are also part of this symbolic offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) Because of the promise God made to Abraham, people who accept Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings.

Why then did Paul use that illustration? He was showing that if the Jews in his day continued to keep the Mosaic Law, they would remain slaves like Hagar. However, if they accepted Abraham’s primary seed, who is Jesus Christ, they would become free like Sarah. They would be set completely free from the power of sin and death. (John 8:32-34) If they did not accept Jesus, it meant they did not understand that the Law covenant was leading them to Christ.

Many of the Galatians to whom Paul was writing were not Jews, so they had never kept the Law. However, some Jews insisted that those who were not Jews must keep the Law and get circumcised. Paul explained that it was not wise for these people to obey the Law that made them "slaves," because they already had "freedom" through Christ.—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

Nets Used for Catching Quail in Ancient Times

Perhaps people who read the Bible will remember that some of Jesus’ followers were fishermen. (Mark 1:16-20) They used nets to catch fish in the Sea of Galilee.

When making a fishing net, ropes are knotted together to form a mesh with many holes. If the net’s mesh sizes are small, it will catch small fish, and if they are large, it will catch large fish. If the net is a cast net, meaning it is small, lead weights or stones are tied around its mouth so that it can sink. And if the net is large, it has two edges. Therefore, lead weights or stones are tied to one of its edges so that it can sink, and then floats (corks) are tied to its other edge so that it can stay afloat.

Fishing nets were very expensive. If there were large rocks on the seabed or if they caught a lot of fish, the net could be damaged. (Luke 5:6) The Bible speaks of James and John who “were mending their nets.” They were likely doing this using a shuttle (needle) and twine. (Matt. 4:21) Perhaps every time they returned from fishing, they mended the nets. Luke also spoke of fishermen who ‘were washing their nets’ to remove weeds that were caught in them. (Luke 5:2) When they finished washing them, they spread them out to dry. They likely did this so that the nets would not rot. —Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus called the people who caught fish and told them, "I will make you fishers of men."
