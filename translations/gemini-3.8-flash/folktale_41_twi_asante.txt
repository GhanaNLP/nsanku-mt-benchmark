If you are going somewhere and your sibling/brother says they will go with you, do not go with them.

There lived a woman who had given birth to three children. The youngest among them was very sickly. His older siblings told their mother to give them money so they could go trading. The youngest also said he would go along. They said they would not go with him. Their mother said they should go with him. Then their mother gave the older ones two asuanu and a suru, and to the boy she gave an osua. The next morning, they set off. The older ones went ahead and left the boy behind. The boy set off and walked slowly along. The older ones met a man who was bringing a mudfish. He said to the older ones, "Buy it." They said, "Go on, and you will meet a boy; make him buy it, and if he refuses to buy it, beat him so that he buys it; and if he still refuses, take him back to his mother." The man indeed met the boy. He said, "Some older ones I met said you should buy this mudfish." The boy said, "If they who are the elders did not buy it, is it I who should buy it?" The man said, "They said if you do not buy it, I should beat you and take you back to your mother." The boy said, "How much is it?" The man said, "An osua!" And the boy paid the cost and took it. He went on and found the older ones in a certain town. They had cooked food but had not found any meat to put in it. The boy said, "Here is the mudfish that I bought." And they put it in the soup. When they were finished, the older ones gave him the head of the mudfish. When the boy cracked it open, he looked and saw that there was gold inside it, and he tied it into the corner of his cloth. The next morning the older ones set off, and as they went, they met a man with a rooster perched upon his things. When he met the older ones, he said, "Buy this rooster." They said, "Go on, and you will meet a boy; make him buy it, and if he does not buy it, beat him." He went and indeed met the boy. He said, "Your older brothers said you should buy this rooster, and if you do not buy it, I should beat you and take you to your mother." He said, "Here is what I have, take it." And the man also gave him the rooster. They reached a certain town. There were no roosters at all in that town, and dawn had broken. The next morning, the boy's rooster crowed. The chief of the town said, "Boy, give me the rooster so I may buy it." The boy said, "An osua and a suru." The chief paid the price. The next morning they set off again. As they were going, the older ones met a man holding a cat. He said, "Buy this cat." The older ones said, "Take it and go, and you will meet a boy; give it to him to buy. If he says he will not buy it, beat him, and take him back to his mother." The man went and met the boy. He said, "Your elders said you should buy this cat, and if you do not buy it, I should beat you and take you back to your mother." The boy said, "How much is it?" The man said, "An osua and a suru." The boy paid the price; and he went and found his older brothers in a certain town. In that place, whenever the chief slept, mice gnawed at his feet. When the boy took his cat there, he went to the chief’s house and caught all the mice that were there. The headman said, "Whosoever's cat this is, I will buy it." The child said, "It is mine." The headman said, "How much?" The child said, "Osua and suru." The headman weighed out the gold into his hand. When morning came, they set off. The elders were going; they met some thieves who had stolen the corpse of a certain great king. The thieves said, "Buy this corpse." The elders said, "Take it and go, and you will meet a certain child; give it to him to buy. If he says he will not buy it, beat him, and take him away to his mother." The thieves went, and they met the child; they said, "The elders said, 'Buy this corpse'." The child said, "Ah! As for a corpse, what shall I buy it to do, and I cannot carry it; whatever I do with it, it will not be well." The thieves said, "Your elders said if you do not buy it, we should beat you and take you to your mother." The child said, "How much?" They said, "Osua and suru." And the child paid the price, took the corpse, and put it in the bush. And he set out and arrived at a certain town, and went into a house to beg for food. The master of the house said, "There is none." The child said, "Grandfather, I beg you." The master of the house said, "There is none." The child said, "Grandfather, I beg you." The master of the house said, "Why does this child bother me so? Our King is dead, we have fasted; it is eight days today; we are even looking for his corpse and cannot find it." An old woman was sitting there, and she said, "Give him some." The woman gave him food and meat. The child finished eating. He said, "Master of the house, as for the King's corpse, I have seen it; yesterday as I was coming, some thieves gave it to me to buy, for asuanu and suru." The woman ran hastily and went to report the news to the elders. They came and took the child away, and he went and showed them the corpse. They went and brought it, and buried him properly. They said, "As for this, it is you who will rule as King." And the child ruled as King. Then his elder brothers heard news of him, and they came, and now they came to claim kinship with him. The child said, "Get away from here; I do not know you, go away." And he made his servants drive them away. That is why they say, "If you are going somewhere and your younger brother says he will go with you, go with him."
