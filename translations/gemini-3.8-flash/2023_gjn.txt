MONDAY

It is surprising that this year would begin with our children continuing to go to school in our country. Just as across the whole world, the terrible COVID-19 disease has divided us for three years since the beginning of 2022, causing our children to fall behind in their education and forcing us to send our children back to school and provide those who are not in school with the means to fight this terrible disease.

Just when we thought that we were turning the corner from the economic devastation that was inflicted by the COVID-19 pandemic on the world economy and had begun a modest recovery, another tragedy struck that shook our economic progress and destroyed the gains we had diligently made between 2017 and 2019. The foremost of these was the Russia-Ukraine war, which not only disrupted global supply chains throughout the year 2022, but also brought about huge increases in commodity prices. Consequently, it brought about an unprecedented tightening of monetary policy in developed economies, reduced capital inflows to developing economies, increased borrowing costs, and tightened external financing conditions leading to sharp depreciations of the cedi.

Therefore, while the other country's currency was rising against the currencies of its peers, the strength of the dollar against our other currencies, coupled with our delayed attempt to access other markets in this country, led to an increase in our debt payments and put pressure on our currency. This has brought about a rise in the cost of borrowing which has affected us all, the government and individuals alike. The rise in fuel prices led to an increase in transport and food prices, and inflation rose to 40.4%, which we had not seen in this country since October 2022.
