Just as the pied crow's neck will be white and its back will be black

A certain woman was called Ata, and she set off with her child to go fishing. When they arrived at the river, her child said, "Let us go to the source of the river." His old mother said, "Let us go downstream, for downstream is where there are crabs." Her child said, "As for me, I am going to the source of the river, for at the source of the river is where there are crabs." And his mother set off and went downstream. They dipped and dipped and dipped, and her child got some, and he came back, and he passed by to go look for his mother. When he arrived, his mother was sitting right on the surface of the river. The child said, "Ah! It is not another, it is not she, it is mother herself." And he lifted up his voice, weeping, and called his mother, saying:

I said it, but you didn't listen; I told you, but you didn't listen. I said, 'Mother, let's go upriver, for upriver is where the crabs are,'

And he said that he also went to the leech-infested stream, and it is in the leech-infested stream that there are leeches.

What should I do, oh! Mother Ata, mhm!

And the Hawk came; he said, "Child, this song of yours is sweet; sing it and let me remove your lice for you." And the Child began:

I said it, but you didn't listen; I told you, but you didn't listen. I said, 'Mother, let's go upriver, for upriver is where the crabs are,'

And he said that he also went to the leech-infested stream, and it is in the leech-infested stream that there are leeches.

What should I do, oh! Mother Ata, mhm!

And Osansa used his whisk to strike the surface of the river, and the river stood where it was. Then he departed. Then 'Pete Kokosakyi arrived, and he also said, "Sing the song so that I may listen and take out your lice for you." And the Child sang the song:

I said it, but you didn't listen; I told you, but you didn't listen. I said, 'Mother, let's go upriver, for upriver is where the crabs are,'

And he said that he also went to the leech-infested stream, and it is in the leech-infested stream that there are leeches.

What should I do, oh! Mother Ata, mhm!

And he too struck the surface of the river with the animal tail whisk, splash! And he too went. Then Crow also arrived. He said, "Child, why are you crying like this? Cry so that I may listen and pluck your eyes out for you." And the child sang the song:

I said it, but you didn't listen; I told you, but you didn't listen. I said, 'Mother, let's go upriver, for upriver is where the crabs are,'

And he said that he also went to the leech-infested stream, and it is in the leech-infested stream that there are leeches.

What should I do, oh! Mother Ata, mhm!

The Crow took out a medicine ball and threw it into the river, and struck the surface of the river with his tail, and the river divided into two; and he said, "Sing the song"; and the child sang. And he saw his mother's hair; and he smiled a little. He said, "Sing again," and he sang. And his mother emerged up to her waist. The Crow said, "Continue singing." And the child sang, and his mother emerged up to her feet. He said, "Sing again"; and he sang. His mother leapt out at once onto the bank. Ata said, "Crow, with what shall I thank you?" He said, "As for me, I do not want anything except black calico and white calico." Ata ran, went and bought them, and brought them to the Crow, and the Crow wrapped himself in the black cloth, and tied the white cloth around his neck. That is why when you see the Crow, his back is black, and his neck is white. And that was the gift he received from Ata.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some and eat, and take some and bring it back to me.
