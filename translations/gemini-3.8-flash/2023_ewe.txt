INTRODUCTION

It is an undeniable truth that this year has been a year in which economic matters troubled the nation more than other years. Like other nations of the world, the COVID-19 disease troubled us in the first three months of the year 2020. This caused the economy to no longer make steady progress. This caused the amount of revenue we earn to decrease, and it became necessary for us to spend money on unplanned things to curb the disease.

As we were emerging from the damages of the Covid-19 pandemic and began implementing measures that would bring about a turnaround, various obstacles emerged again. These obstacles derailed the economic successes we recorded from the Year 2017 to the Year 2019. The first obstacle is the war being waged between the countries of Russia and Ukraine. Subsequently, the delay in the approval of the revenue mobilization measures in the 2022 Budget Statement dampened confidence in the economic indicators. For the first time in ten years, the international capital market was closed to us. Raising funds domestically for development projects also became increasingly difficult, and the cost of borrowing also went up. All these led to an increase in our debt stock and its service, and the Ghana cedi depreciated sharply.

Even though foreign exchange support from official lending sources declined, our expenditure on imports increased. In addition, the increased cost of servicing our debts has further weakened the value of our currency. This has made life difficult for all of us, both the Government and the citizens. The increase in fuel prices has caused the cost of goods and many services to rise significantly to an inflation rate of 40.4% in October 2022.

These difficult times are a test we are going through as a nation, and the Government understands the hardships that these things have brought upon us.

PREFACE

1. The 2023 Budget is the work done by the Government in the year 2022 and the amount of money it expects to receive to carry out its plans and development projects. The theme of the 2023 Budget is: "Restoring Macroeconomic Stability, their Resilience and Sustainability through the Growth of Various Enterprises and Adding Value to Commodities."

2. In many past years, many new development projects were commenced while those already underway were abandoned. 3. In the year 2023, the Government will make plans for work on new development projects, but will ensure that all ongoing development projects are completed.

Tax collection arrangements

19. To ensure the country's economic stability, the Government submitted tax revenue measures whose yield would be equivalent to 1.35 percent of the country's gross domestic product to Parliament for consideration and approval. Some of these tax revenue measures include:

• Taxes paid on tobacco products will be reviewed to align with ECOWAS regulations, and the tax paid on hard liquor will be made higher than that paid on beer.

• The VAT rate will be increased by 2.5 percentage points from 12.5% to 15%. In addition to this, the VAT threshold will be reviewed to introduce key changes regarding VAT-exempt items.

• The tax known as NFSL will be changed to GSL with the intention of including all businesses.

• The tax known as "E-Levy" will be reduced from 1.5 percent to 1, and the daily threshold on which it is not paid will be eliminated.

• Some of the other revenue collection arrangements are:

○ The "5G Electromagnetic Spectrum" that is used in internet operations will be sold.

○ They will advance the issues regarding the rent tax paid on houses and premises.

○ Another arrangement beneficial to the nation regarding crude oil matters will be implemented at the oil field known as the Jubilee Field to benefit the nation more than before; and

○ The revised budget will not contain the expected debt relief in the first phase

Financial regulations

20. In addition to the tax collection measures, the Government implemented the following key financial measures to support the stability of the country's economy.

• The money paid to the vulnerable to reduce extreme poverty (LEAP) will be doubled for them from Ghana cedis 45 to Ghana cedis 90 every month, and the number of beneficiary households will be increased to exceed the 344,185 households currently receiving the money. These fiscal measures will cause the total amount paid under the LEAP Programme to increase from Ghana cedis 195.5 million in 2022 to Ghana cedis 395 million;

• The amount paid to Caterers under the School Feeding Programme has been increased by 138 million Ghana cedis.

• A further 30 percent reduction in the salaries of the President, Vice President, Ministers, Deputy Ministers, MMDCEs, and other political appointees in state institutions;

• Tax paid on specific funds will be reduced from 25 percent to 17.5 percent;

• All these special funds will be utilized through the use of the "GIFMIS" System, and it will be ensured that GIFMIS is used in all their receipts and expenditures. The work in this plan will be carried out before the end of the year 2023.

• The salaries of workers in State-owned enterprises will be adjusted every year so that they do not exceed the salaries paid to other public sector workers paid under the Single Spine Structure.
