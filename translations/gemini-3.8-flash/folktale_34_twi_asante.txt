We are not alike, not alike at all

If it is good, no one needs to explain it to anyone else

There was once a certain hunter. Whenever he arose, he would go into the forest to seek meat to kill and eat, and he would sell some. Then one day, he went into the forest; he heard a Wild Boar call his brother, saying, "Wild Boar Asamoa!" He replied, "Yes, my brother!" He said, "The time for work has arrived, so let us go to the blacksmith's shop by the riverside to forge iron tools, and let us sharpen their edges, so that whatever tree we cut, we shall be able to cut it down." When the Wild Boar called his brother, the hunter was close by, and he heard all the words. Then the Wild Boar's brother asked his brother the Wild Boar, saying, "And to which town shall we go to forge our iron tools?" He said, "We shall go to Nsuakyi." And he asked, "On which day?" He said, "Monday." The hunter heard all the words, and he set off and went home. And when he arrived home, he went and reported the matter to the Chief of Nsuakyi, telling him what he had heard when he went into the forest. And the hunter told the Chief, "Let the young men go and cut logs of wood and bring them, so that on Monday when the Wild Boars transform themselves and come, we may lay them between the logs." The young men went, cut the logs of wood, and brought them. The Chief went and spoke to the blacksmith who was in the town, causing him to forge many pairs of iron tongs for him. Then the blacksmith asked the Chief, saying, "As for these many pairs of tongs that you tell me to forge, what will you use them for?" And he reported the matter to him, saying, "A hunter went into the forest and has returned to report to me that on Monday certain beasts will transform themselves into human beings and come to your blacksmith's shop by the riverside to forge iron tools." The blacksmith ran and forged the tongs quickly. When the King had received all the logs of wood and the tongs, he had the herald beat the gong-gong, saying, "On Monday, whether female or male, let no one go anywhere." Monday arrived, and in the morning, an old woman said to the hunter, "Go and grind pepper, salt, and onions; and wherever the beasts cast off their skins, when you also go there, put the pepper inside them, so that if the beasts come here, and if we capture them, and if they flee to go and put on their skins, when the pepper burns them painfully, they will cast them off, and they will become human beings." The hunter went, and he went and lay in wait close by. Then he heard someone call, "Wild Boar Asamoa, Monday has arrived, let us go." And all of them came. They came and cast off their skins there. But as for one short-tailed beast among them, he went and hid his skin somewhere. And the hunter watched until the beasts departed. He went and took all the skins, and he placed the ground pepper inside them, and he took them and threw them into the river, so that the river carried them away. As for the short-tailed beast's skin that he had gone to hide, the hunter did not see it. The hunter returned home, and the King also went and called the people at the blacksmith's shop by the riverside, and brought them to his house. The people came to the palace and asked the King, "Why did you call us?" The King said, "You are my people, and you fled to live somewhere else; today you have returned, so I say you should come and I will not let you leave again." The animals told the King, "You have spoken, and we have heard; but we know that it was the creator's hunter who came and told you everything, so it does not matter, we will stay with you; and we know that as for what you are saying, ordinarily you would be lying; yet it does not matter; it is the hunter whom we thank. And we will stay with you; but what we forbid is this: that you reveal our origins, or that any of your subjects also reveals our origins; if so, we will destroy this town of yours and leave." When the King went to call them, the medicine man and some others fled. And the medicine man went and took his skin and left; but as for the rest, they returned. It was because they did not find their skins. The King agreed to the animals' terms, and the animals lived well with them. And the men who were in that town married some of the animals' children and had children with them. Then one day, some of the animals and some of the townsfolk were fighting. It was then that the townsman told the animal, "Get up from there, an animal like you from the wild pig group." As soon as he said that, all the animals grew furious, and they went to the King's palace and asked him, "The matter we spoke to you about, now that it has come to this, what are you going to do about it?" The King had the people who made the remarks summoned. The King examined the matter carefully, and judged that the animals should let it go and let it rest, for they had arrived a long time ago and no one had ever said such a thing to them before. But the animals said, "We will not listen." And the King said, "If you will not listen, do you think that what this man said was a lie; are you not wild pigs?" And the animals said, "Oh! We have heard." Then the animals joined with the Asuokyifuo. The animals destroyed the town, leaving only about ten people. And they fled and followed, letting the animals be vindicated. The animals listened, and they told the people, "An old matter is not a dwelling place, and if you are going to live with us, we hate insults to our origins. If you think about it and if you are going to speak, then show us the very leaves from which we came so that we may leave." And the people said, "We can never do that. The thing that we have done that brought ruin upon this nation, we will never do again." So they took the gong-gong and beat it, declaring, "No one should insult anyone's origins, for insulting one's origins destroys a town."

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some and eat, and take some and approve of me.
