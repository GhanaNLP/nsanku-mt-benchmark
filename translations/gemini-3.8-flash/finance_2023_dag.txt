This year ended with hardship, an unprecedented year in the economic history of this country. Like the rest of the world, the coronavirus pandemic hit in the course of the year 2020, which brought an economic downturn, leading to revenue shortfalls and debt that we incurred in preparing to fight the pandemic.

Just when we thought we were winning, finding relief in fighting the coronavirus, just as we began following our transition path, then many things came to confront us, wiping away at that time the successes we had achieved in the three major years from 2017 up to 2019. First, it was the Russia-Ukraine war, followed by the delay in passing the revenue measures of the 2022 budget, which brought about a setback. Once, for the first time in ten years, the doors of the International Capital Market were shut against us, domestic financing conditions tightened, borrowing increased, leading to heavy debt servicing, and the value of the cedi depreciated significantly.

Therefore, as external financial support from donor funding and our foreign earnings dwindled, and our spending on imports rose due to our strong appetite for foreign goods in this country, this led to an increase in our debt servicing costs and further weakened our Ghana Cedi. This has made life difficult for all of us, both the government and every single one of us. The increase in fuel prices led to high costs of goods and services, including inflation that reached 40.4% in October 2022.

Indeed, we are people who have been weighed, and the Government has seen it as a problem for all of us that brought this about. INTRODUCTION

1. The 2023 Citizens' Budget is a reflection of the Government's achievements in 2020 and its intentions for revenue mobilization and expenditure allocated for programs in 2022. The theme for this 2023 Citizens' Budget is, "Strengthening macro-economic stability and promoting inclusive growth with value addition".

2. In the past years, every new budget has brought new projects that start and leave old projects that are still being executed to be abandoned. This creates pressure on our expenditure channels and also makes us pay debts late, which increases the cost of projects, as unpaid debts accumulate interest over time.

3. In the year 2023, the Government will undertake new projects, but will also make efforts to complete existing projects that have already been initiated rather than abandoning them.

Ministry of Lands and Natural Resources

4. In the 2022 Mid-Year Budget Review, the Government set the following macroeconomic targets for 2022:

i. Real GDP growth of 3.7 percent;

The available report on the economic development of this country from the month of January to September in the year 2022 showed the recurring difficulties arising from both international and domestic sources. This has caused the depreciation of the currency, increased the prices of goods, made the servicing of the country's debt difficult, and caused fiscal vulnerabilities despite all the measures introduced by the Government to mitigate them:

i. Real GDP growth of 4.0 percent year-on-year for the first half of 2022 compared to 3.9 percent for the same period in 2021;

ii. Inflation increased to 40.4 percent in October 2022 from 37.2 percent in September and 33.9 percent in August, driven by increases in food and non-food prices;

2023 ANNUAL WORK PLAN DESCRIPTION

Tax Description

18. To support revenue mobilization, Government will submit to Parliament for approval, tax revenue measures equivalent to 1.35 percent of GDP. These include:

• To review the tax on cigarettes and tobacco in line with ECOWAS regulations, and to increase the tax on energy drinks.

• And increase the Value Added Tax (VAT) rate by two and a half (2.5) percent to the existing 12.5 percent, bringing it to 15 percent, and amend the laws that exempt certain goods from the VAT.

• And amend the law on the tax called "Convert the National Fiscal Stabilisation Levy (NFSL)" to become the tax named "Growth and Sustainability Levy (GSL)" in respect of all businesses.

• And reduce the mobile money fee which is “Reduce the Electronic Transfer Levy” from 1.5 percent to 1 percent, and remove the daily threshold limit rule.

• Other ways it has also described include

o Did not sell the "Electromagnetic Spectrum" at 5G size;

He strengthened or raised the rent tax laws;

It has not pursued what is called "Pursue Additional Oil Entitlement (AOE)" regarding the oil operations called the Jubilee Field; and

It reviewed the Income Tax so that it no longer covers import duty debts and instead strengthens the collection of duties that do not have debts.
