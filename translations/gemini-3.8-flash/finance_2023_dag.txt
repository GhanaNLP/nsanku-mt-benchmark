MINISTER OF FISHERIES

This year ended with the distress of an unprecedented year in the economic history of this country. Like the rest of the world, the coronavirus pandemic struck in the course of the year 2020, which brought an economic downturn, leading to revenue shortfalls and the debts we had prepared to safeguard our economy in fighting it.

Just as we thought we were turning the corner, recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic, and embarking on a path of structural transformation, a series of shocks hit us, eroding the tremendous gains we had made between 2017 and 2019. First was the Russia-Ukraine war, followed by the delay in the passage of the revenue measures in the 2022 budget, which caused a major setback. For the first time in a decade, the doors of the International Capital Market were shut against us, increasing domestic financing pressures and driving up borrowing costs, which led to higher debt service obligations and a sharp depreciation of the cedi.

Therefore, as international financial support from borrowings and foreign exchange earnings declined, and import bills increased due to our high demand for foreign goods over domestic ones, it increased our debt service payments and further weakened the Ghana Cedi. This has made life difficult for all of us, both the Government and every single one of us. The rise in fuel prices led to higher costs for many goods and services, alongside a rise in inflation that reached 40.4% in October 2022.

Indeed, we are among those who have seen the assessment, and the Government saw that it was a problem for all of us and brought this about. ii. Price inflation rose to 40.4 percent in the month of October 2022 from 37.2 percent in September and 33.9 percent in August, driven by increases in both food and non-food prices;

2023 ANNUAL WORK PLAN

Tax Assessment

18. To support resource mobilization for operations, the Government will submit tax policy measures estimated at 1.35 percent of GDP to Parliament for approval. These are:

• To review the tax on cigarettes and tobacco in line with the ECOWAS directive, and increase the tax on energy drinks.

• And increase the Value Added Tax (VAT) rate by two and a half (2.5) percentage points to the already existing 12.5 percent, making it 15 percent, and revise the list of goods and services that are exempt from the tax.

• And amend the law on the levy called "Convert the National Fiscal Stabilisation Levy (NFSL)" to become the levy known as the "Growth and Sustainability Levy (GSL)" covering all businesses.

• And reduce the mobile money fee which is “Reduce the Electronic Transfer Levy” from 1.5 percent to 1 percent, and remove its rule regarding the daily threshold limit.

• Other ways it has also described include:

He did not sell the “Electromagnetic Spectrum” at 5G capacity;

strengthen household or vehicle rental tax regulations;

Pursue Additional Oil Entitlement (AOE) in respect of the Jubilee Field operations; and

He reviewed the Income Tax so that it no longer covers the debt of exchanging goods, but rather strengthened the collection of taxes on the debt-free exchange of goods.
