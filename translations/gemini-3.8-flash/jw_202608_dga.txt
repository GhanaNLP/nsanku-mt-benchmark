What Did People Do at the City Gates of Israel in Ancient Times?

DO YOU KNOW why city gates were built in ancient times? Perhaps you would say that it was a place through which people entered the city. But that is not all, many things used to take place at the city gates. Because many people passed through there to enter the city, various activities were carried out there every day. If you look in the Bible, you will find that the Bible mentions city gates more than 50 times, showing also the things that used to take place at the city gates.

Many people used to gather at the city gate to conduct affairs. The Hebrew word rendered “gate” can mean several things. It does not refer only to the entrance where people entered the city. Rather, this word can also refer to places that people constructed at the city gate where they used to gather and transact their business. In some places at the city gates, wide open squares were constructed where many people could gather together.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants used to set up market stalls near the city gates. In the city of Jerusalem, there were some market stalls called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. Perhaps these things were sold at those places, which is why those places were called by those names.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People used to buy and sell things there, and some people also did their daily business there. The Bible says that Abraham made an agreement in front of many people at the city gate. In time, Boaz also did the same. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) In ancient times, if a stranger went to a place and did not find anywhere to sleep, he would go to the public square at the city gate to find a place to stay. (Judg. 19:15-21) Sometimes people also went to the city gate to hear the news.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

Judges used to listen to people's cases and judge in that place. They used to judge people in the presence of all the people who were there. (Deut. 21:18-20) Therefore, doing so made the judges usually judge by the gate in the sight of all the people

SOME THINGS THAT ARE IN THE BIBLE

One Tree That First Begins to Shed Leaves Before All Trees

The Bible mentions the names of more than 30 different trees. The almond tree is also among them. In the land of Israel, these trees blossom early. Therefore, from towards the end of the month of January, people see the very beautiful flowers of this tree. The flowers of this tree have a white color. For this reason, the Hebrew word used to name this tree means ‘the tree that blossoms before all others.’ So what is the connection between this tree and the one who made it?

The Hebrew expression used in Jeremiah 7:25 shows that Jehovah would get up early every morning and send his prophets to go and speak to his people. The March 15, 2011 Watchtower said: “Just as the almond tree blossoms early, in a sense Jehovah also ‘gets up early’ . . . to warn his people about the punishment that will come upon them because of their stubbornness. . . . he will not rest—he will get up early every day—until everything he has spoken comes to pass.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

Sometimes the Bible compares an elderly person’s white hair to the blossoms of the almond tree. (Eccl. 12:5) Because the almond tree was so beautiful, when Bezalel was making the lampstand for the sanctuary, he made cups shaped like the almond flowers that were on the branches of the sanctuary's lampstand.—Ex. 37:17-20.
