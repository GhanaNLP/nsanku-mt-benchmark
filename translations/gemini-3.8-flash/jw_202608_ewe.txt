What took place at the city gates in ancient Israel?

IN ANCIENT times, it was through the city gates that people entered into cities. But since many people passed through the gates, many things went on there every day. City gates are mentioned more than 50 times in the Bible, and various things that took place there are also described.

People gathered at the city gates. The Hebrew word used for gate meant more than just the gate of a city. It could also refer to the area within the city where booths or stalls were set up by the gates. In some places, it was an open square inside the city by the gate where people gathered.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants set up markets in areas near the city gates. In the case of some of the gates in Jerusalem, the gates were likely named after the kinds of goods sold there; for example, the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People went to the city gates to sell things, buy things, or do other business. Both Abraham and Boaz confirmed agreements they made with others before witnesses at city gates. (Genesis 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Some travelers stayed and slept in the public squares near the city gates if no one took them into a home. (Judges 19:15-21) Other people also went to the city gates to hear about recent events that were happening.—2 Samuel 18:24, 25.

Judges judged cases at the city gates. They judged cases in these places before the people so that they might hear what happened and how the judges settled the matter. (5 Moses 21:18-20) Since the cases were judged publicly, this prevented the judges from showing partiality, and they judged righteously. When the judges had passed judgment, the city elders executed it immediately. (5 Moses 25:2) Both Jehovah and the nation expected the judges at the city gates to act righteously, live a good life, and encourage others to do the same.—5 Moses 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Many things happen at the city gates every day

Jehovah’s faithful servants encouraged the Israelites at the city gates to trust in Jehovah. Prophets and others spoke Jehovah’s word at the city gates because they knew that people gathered there. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu spoke before all the people at the city gate that what Jehovah foretold about punishing the house of Ahab had come to pass. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) King Hezekiah gathered his military chiefs at the city gate and encouraged them to trust in Jehovah.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that “true wisdom” cries out and raises its voice “at the gates of the city.” (Prov. 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, city gates were where faithful men gave wise counsel and made sound decisions. Today, we are glad that Jehovah’s wisdom appears in the Bible and that his servants around the world are helping people study the Bible so they can find true wisdom.

The Tree That Wakes Up Early

One of the 30 different trees mentioned in the Bible is the almond tree. It is one of the trees that blossoms early after the winter season. The almond tree blossoms around the end of January. Its flowers are slightly reddish or white and are very beautiful to look at. It is very fitting that in Hebrew, the name given to the almond tree means “the one that awakens early.” What is notable about this tree and its Creator?

In the footnote of Jeremiah 7:25, Jehovah says that he gets up early to send his prophets to his people. The March 15, 2011 Watchtower states: ‘Just as the almond tree awakens early, so Jehovah will figuratively “get up early” to warn his people of the consequences of disobedience. And he will not stop—he will continue to “keep awake”—until his prophetic word comes to pass completely.’—Jer. 1:11, 12.

The Bible also compares the blossoms of the almond tree to the white hair of an elderly person. (Eccl. 12:5) Likely because of the beauty of these flowers, the cups on the golden lampstands in the temple were made in the shape of almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
