We say it shall not happen, it shall not happen oh

When the vulture speaks to the one who reveals evil, he understands it in proverbs.

'Bonekyerefo was there when they came and told him that his mother was dead. 'Bonekyerefo wept, wept, and wept, and he went and begged Kokosakyi, Akoko, Odwan, Okra, and 'Kraman to accompany him to his mother's funeral. All the animals set off, and they went with him. When they arrived and had finished the funeral rites, it was time for them to bathe. They went and said to 'Bonekyerefo, "Bathe so that we too may bathe." 'Bonekyerefo said, "As for me, while my mother lies in the earth, until I have completed forty days, I will never bathe." His friends said, "What kind of custom have we seen?" Whenever they woke up in the morning, their stomachs were churning with hunger, but as for 'Bonekyerefo, his body was strong and stout. One day, Kokosakyi said, "Ah! That 'Bonekyerefo, I know him; it must be his mother's corpse that he wakes up and goes to gnaw on." In the evening, Kokosakyi flew and perched on a tall tree by the cemetery; he looked, and there came 'Bonekyerefo, kokota! kokota! He came, and he went over the grave, dwiri! dwiri! and he dug up his mother's corpse, and went there, gnawing on it. Opete sat there. He said, "'Bonekyerefo, well done! We thank you with silent gratitude." 'Bonekyerefo said, "I beg you, do not mention this; whatever you tell me, I will immediately heed it for you." Opete flew off and returned home. Before long, 'Bonekyerefo arrived. Opete said to his friends, "Today, whether the room turns into stone, we will eat; and furthermore, we will go back to our town." The animals said, "Truly?" Opete said, "Just watch me." Opete said, "Come, let us go." They set out and went to 'Bonekyerefo's house. Opete said, "'Bonekyerefo, listen and let me tell you: today we want to eat, and you too must eat, and furthermore we will go back to our town today; now what do you say?" 'Bonekyerefo said, "I agree; I will eat so that you too may eat." Right away, 'Bonekyerefo went and caught three sheep, made a feast of them, and gave it to his friends to eat, and he too ate some. Then his friends set off and returned to their town. That is why they made a proverb of it, saying, "When Kokosakyi speaks to 'Bonekyerefo, he understands him through proverbs."

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
