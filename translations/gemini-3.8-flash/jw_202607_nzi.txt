What We Learn From the Gibeonites

"The inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were living among them."—JOSH. 10:1.

Forty years after the Israelites left Egypt, they arrived in the Promised Land. The Israelites conquered the cities of Jericho and Ai. But later, a surprising thing happened. A group of travelers who claimed to have come from far away came to them. Their message was that they wanted to make a peace covenant with God's people.

2 Those travelers were the Gibeonites. This was the first time the Gibeonites were mentioned in the Bible, but it was not the last. Various Bible accounts show that centuries later, the Gibeonites still had dealings with the Israelites. We can learn many things from their story, and it will help us to know much more about Jehovah God.

Settle the Problem Quickly!

HAS a brother done something that has deeply hurt you and strained your relationship? To resolve matters between you, you must (1) act quickly and (2) make up your mind to be friends again.*

Let us consider an example. If someone breaks a bone, it must be treated so that it will not cause the person pain. But if the person does not pay immediate attention to it, it will continue to cause pain. Even worse, it could cause the bone to become deformed, and he will no longer be able to do the things he used to do.

Just as a broken bone is treated quickly, in the same way, if a disagreement arises between you and a fellow Christian, you must settle it quickly. If you do not do so, your deteriorating friendship will cause you to continue to feel hurt. It will make you angry, and then your relationship will be completely broken. Jesus said: "Settle matters quickly with him." (Mat. 5:25) Therefore, speak kindly with your friend, but tell him what is causing you pain. Why is doing this sometimes difficult? Consider two things.

First, if you are not careful, you will be tempted to ignore the situation, perhaps because you feel uncomfortable talking about it.# Involve relatives

(Phil. 4:2) If they were humble, spoke from the heart, and forgave completely, they would become good friends again.

Second, perhaps you expect the person to admit their mistake. But you want to regain your friend, not to blame them. When a doctor is treating a broken bone, he focuses on how to heal it, not on whatever the person did that caused the bone to break. Therefore, when you are talking with your friend, do not be quick to look for who is at fault. Instead, remember how important your friendship is to you and focus your mind on what needs to be done to restore your relationship.

So talk to your brother, but make it your goal to settle the matter between you and become friends again. The Bible says “do not let the sun set while you are still angry.” (Eph. 4:26) Doing everything you can to settle the problem quickly will help you protect ‘the uniting bond of peace.’—Eph. 4:1-3.

Prepare an Encouraging Comment

When we attend meetings, we have the opportunity to encourage our brothers and sisters with our comments. (Heb. 10:24, 25) How, then, can we prepare comments that will truly encourage others?

• Pray about it. Ask Jehovah to help you give an answer that will honor him and encourage others.—Ps. 35:18.

• Do not just read your answer directly from the book. If you use your own words to explain your belief when giving an answer, it will help those who are listening to you.

• Do not give long answers. Short and clear answers usually give the best encouragement, and they also allow many people the opportunity to comment.—Prov. 10:19.

• Give constructive comments. Do not use your comment to counsel the congregation or an individual, nor to criticize the congregation or an individual.

Even if you raise your hand and are not called on, you can still encourage others. In what way? When they are giving an answer, listen carefully to them and commend them when they finish.—Prov. 10:21.
