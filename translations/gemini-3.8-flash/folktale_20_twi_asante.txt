HOW IT CAME ABOUT THAT KWAKU ANANSE'S BUTT BECAME VERY BIG WHILE HIS HEAD BECAME SMALL

It is said that a great famine occurred, and Kwaku Ananse decided to go search for meat and food for himself and his wife, Aso, to eat. When he went to a river, he met some people; but those people he met, with all due respect, were ghosts. When Ananse met the ghosts, they were standing in the river, fishing. Kwaku Ananse said, "My folks, can I come and fish too?" The ghosts said, "Come." Ananse went, and went to see that they were using their skulls to fish. The ghosts said to Ananse, "You have seen what we use to fish, can you let us remove yours so that you can fish too?" Ananse said, "I can, so remove it for me." The ghosts removed it for him indeed. Kwaku Ananse and the ghosts fished together. And as they were fishing, the ghosts raised a song, saying:

Ancestors, we look upon the river, we look upon the river with our heads, oh Ancestors, we look upon the river.

Ananse said, “This song is sweet, should I sing along?” The ghosts said, “Sing along.” And he began:

Ancestors, we look at the river, we look at the river with our heads, oh Ancestors, we look at the river.

When the Creator made things, did we look at the river with our heads?

Ancestors, behold the river. Today I have offered my life to the river.

Ancestors, look upon the river.

When Ananse finished singing, the ghosts said to him, "Look, we have obtained meat; as for you, take a full basket and go eat, take your calabash skull back, attach it, and go; but what we are telling you is that the very day you sing this song again, your calabash skull will burst open and fall off." Ananse said, "This abundant meat that you have given me, I like it, but as for the song, what would I sing it for?" The ghosts said, "All right, go." Ananse set off and left. The ghosts also prepared themselves and departed. Now, when the ghosts arrived there, they lifted it up:

Ancestors, we look at the river, we bow our heads toward the river, oh Ancestors, we look at the river.

And Ananse heard the song, and he too took it up:

Did the Creator create things for us to look at the river with our heads?

Ancestors, look upon the river.

As soon as he finished, his big head opened up and fell off. Ananse picked it up and hit him with it. He said,

"Ghosts! Oh, ghosts! My head has fallen off!" And the ghosts heard and said, "That is Ananse; he did not listen to what we told him and now he is calling us. Let us all go and hear what he will say." It was not long before Ananse came rushing along. He said, "Puo! Children of my father, my head has come off and fallen, so I beg you, whatever I have done to you, forgive me. I admit you are right, so please attach my head back on for me." The ghosts agreed, and they attached it back on for him. They told him, "Now, if you sing this song again and it falls off again, if you call us, we will not answer, so go." The ghosts set off and left. And as they were leaving, they were singing their song. Then Ananse sang it again, and his head detached and fell, kutukum! and bounced against his chest. He said, "Ghosts! Oh, ghosts!" Silence! He called and called and called, but all was silent! Now Ananse held it, but it would not fit, so he lifted it and stuck it onto, excuse the expression, his buttocks, and he dashed off along the roadside, sora! He said, "Road, save me, and in the future when I prosper, I will give you some." That is why you will see Ananse with a very tiny head and very huge buttocks; and it came from disobedience.

This is my folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away and bring some back.
