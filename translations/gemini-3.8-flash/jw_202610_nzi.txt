A NEW BOOK AND ADJUSTMENTS TO OUR MEETINGS

In April, it was announced in The Golden Age (now Awake!) that a new book entitled Deliverance would be released. This book was to replace the Studies in the Scriptures that was used to teach people in the preaching work. In order for the Bible Students to receive enough of the books, the brothers working at Bethel purchased a new printing press. It could print 22 books in just one minute. And the Bible Students gave a copy of the book to everyone they met in the preaching work.

For many years, the Bible Students studied Studies in the Scriptures at their meetings. But from May, 1926, onward, they began studying The Watch Tower. What was the result?

A congregation wrote a letter to Brother Rutherford. Here is an excerpt from their letter: “Greetings to you. Every morning we study the material that the organization has assigned for us to study. We are delighted that now all of us quickly understand any new explanation as it comes in its due time.”

New understanding helped the Bible Students to focus their attention on the preaching work. For example, an article entitled “Character or Covenant—Which?” explained that the primary work of true Christians is not to strive to develop a good character and speak eloquently to others. Another article entitled “Sacrifice and Service” emphasized that true Christians must be faithful to Jehovah to show their love for him. The article also encouraged the Bible Students to be zealous in witnessing and preach to as many people as possible.—Isa. 43:10, 12; Matt. 24:14.

Let Your Faith Rest on a Firm Foundation

WHEN you were studying the Bible with Jehovah’s Witnesses, what convinced you that you had found the “one faith” or the right way to worship God? (Eph. 4:4-6) Was it the love among Jehovah’s people that brought you into the truth? Was it their good conduct that impressed you? What has attracted many people to the truth is how we make them feel comfortable and truly love them, and that is understandable. For love and fine conduct are things that identify true Christians.—John 13:35; 1 Pet. 2:12.

And what if a fellow believer does something that upsets you or treats you in an unloving way? Or what if a brother commits a serious sin, a sin that is as shocking as what the man in the Corinthian congregation did, about whom Paul spoke? (1 Cor. 5:1) If our faith is based on the actions and words of others, it is not on a firm foundation, and it will not be strong. Do you have a firm foundation that will make your faith strong?

AN ANCHOR THAT STANDS FIRM

If you continue to strengthen your conviction that what you have learned from the Bible is the truth, it will make your faith rest on a solid foundation. How will this help you? Let us look at an example.

One day, many of Jesus' disciples stopped following him. What happened? A statement Jesus made offended them; he said they must eat his flesh and drink his blood. They responded: “This speech is hard.” That statement Jesus made troubled them so much that they decided not to follow him anymore.—Dwɔn 6:56-60, 66.

Then Jesus asked his apostles: “Do you also want to go?” Peter answered him: “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of everlasting life. We have believed and come to know that you are the Holy One of God.”—John 6:67-69.

What made Peter and the rest of the apostles different from those who left? Their faith was based on something solid, not on how they felt. Peter said: “We have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God.” So these faithful ones knew what they believed and why they believed it. Because they compared Jesus’ words and his actions with what they knew in the Scriptures, they fully believed that Jesus was the Messiah. That great confidence made them stand firm when some stumbled over Jesus’ words.

Imagine that you have boarded a vehicle going to a beautiful place far away. As the vehicle moves along, you will be very happy because you too are going there.

Jehovah Rewards Our Faith

Sister Emma used to work three days a week at a hotel, working nine hours each day. One day, she asked her manager for a day off to attend a circuit assembly. But her manager did not agree; instead, he said that from that time on, she would have to work five days a week. Then he said that if she could not follow the new arrangement, she should quit the job. Emma decided to quit the job so that she could attend the assembly, and she was confident that Jehovah would take care of her.

The day Emma went to the hotel to inform the manager that she was quitting the job, the man who oversees all the workers was there. When he heard the matter, he told Emma that he would handle it. However, two weeks later, Emma was dismissed. One day, she met the man who oversees all the workers again and told him that she had been dismissed. The man became angry that the manager had not done what he said and had dismissed Emma. He told Emma to return to work and even changed her working hours. She would work five days a week and work for four hours each day. That was better than before and gave her time to do spiritual things. Jehovah provided for her job need in an unexpected way.
