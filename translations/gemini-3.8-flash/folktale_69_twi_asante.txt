IF IT IS ABOUT BEING A SNAIL ON A TREE, IT LIES DOWN FLAT, BUT THE OKRA STANDS VERY TALL

Nwiadowa and Nkuruma lived in the same room; they swept rubbish to one side and slept on the other side. They did things together in that one room. Then Nwiadowa said to Nkuruma, "This room of ours is disgraceful, so let us go and dig a pit, and throw the rubbish into it." Nkuruma said, "I will not go." Nwiadowa set off and went to dig the pit. While he was digging, Nkuruma called out to him, and Nwiadowa answered. He said, "Lift the pit and bring it here so I can see it." Nwiadowa said, "Ah! Since the creation of the world, have you ever seen a person who lifts a pit?" Nkuruma said, "If you do not shut your mouth, I will come and beat you." Nwiadowa said, "Then take a sieve to fetch water and bring it for me to drink, and I will lift it and bring it to you." Nkuruma said, "You have taken me for a fool; living here in this world, does anyone fetch water with a sieve?" Nkuruma left there, went to the pit, and beat Nwiadowa. While he was beating him, God heard it, and He sent Vulture to go and bring the two people. The two people went. Nwiadowa told his side of the story; Nkuruma told his. Then God had the linguist declare Nkuruma guilty; Nwiadowa was declared innocent. That is why when Nwiadowa is planted, it lies on the ground; it is thanking God and his elders. But Nkuruma, for his part, stands very tall; he says he is going to fight with God because He gave an unfair judgment against him. This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some away and bring some back.
