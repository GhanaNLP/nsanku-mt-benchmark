If it is done, Ananse will become bald

It is said that there lived Kwaku Ananse and his wife Aso. One day they had returned from the farm; not long after, a messenger arrived. Ananse asked for his mission. He said, "It is nothing, but your father-in-law died yesterday in his sleep." Ananse said, "Aso, serious news." Aso put her hands on her head and wept bitterly. Ananse saw her off and allowed her to go. The next day, Ananse went and pleaded with Sheep, Cat, Dog, Fowl, and Goat, saying, "This is what has happened to me, so I beg you, come and accompany me to my father-in-law's funeral." All the people agreed. Ananse came home, sewed a hat, and wore mourning cloth. When Thursday arrived, he went and called the people he had pleaded with, and they all set off. They carried drums, guns, and drinks. Ananse reached the outskirts of his father-in-law's town; the guns were fired bang! bang! They brought the celebration home and sat at the funeral. He went and presented drinks to the mourners. When he finished, he went and contributed six peredwans of gold dust, an agopikyi, two pieces of cloth, a burial mat, a pillow, a sheep, and drinks. That was what Ananse used to bury his father-in-law. When evening cooled, they came and presented Ananse with two asuanu and nsano in return. The next day, all the people ate, and they came and told Ananse to eat. He said, "I, Kwaku, my father-in-law is dead; I will not eat for eight days. I will not eat, but look for something for my friends with whom I came, so that they may eat and leave." Then Aso went and found something, brought it to the men, and they ate. As for them, they left; and Ananse was left behind. While Ananse was there, by the fourth day, he was suffering unbearable hunger. In the house where he stayed, there were beans being cooked on the fire. Ananse looked this way and that, took off the hat, ladled some of the beans into it, and put it back on. Just then Aso arrived. He said, "Aso, I just remembered, the festival in Father's town that has not been held for a long time has arrived, so I am leaving right now." Aso said, "Oh, but how did this happen that you didn't tell me; besides, you came to the funeral and haven't eaten at all; please wait until tomorrow to go." Ananse said, "You don't know what has happened, which is why you say this." Aso went to call people to come and persuade Ananse to stop going. The people came. As soon as Ananse saw them, he held onto the hat, saying:

Father's town will be shaken before long.

Saworowa, we will shake it soon.

Saworowa, oh! Let's shake it continuously, let's shake it continuously.

Brass basin."

And the beans were burning his scalp badly. Ananse said, "I will never listen, I am going." Ananse set off on the way, and the people followed him. He said, "Turn back, for:

Now in Father's town, we shake it and stay long. Saworowa, we shake it and stay long.

Saworowa, oh! Let's shake it continuously, let's shake it continuously.

Brass basin."

Now as for Ananse, the thing had burnt him so badly that he fell down. When he tried to save himself, it did not work. He threw his hat and the beans away. The mother-in-law and the people booed him. Ananse jumped out onto the path, splash! He said, "Path, save me, and in the future if I become well-off, I will thank you." And the Path said, "I have heard." The Path looked after him and gave him medicine. That is why you will see Ananse with a bald head; it came from the rascality he committed at his in-law's funeral.

This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some to eat and let the rest return to me.
