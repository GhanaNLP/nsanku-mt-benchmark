DO YOU KNOW?

In ancient times, what was done at the city gates?

City gates were not merely places to enter the city. Many other things were also done there every day, so there was no leisure time at all. The Bible mentions city gates more than 50 times and lets us see the various activities that took place there.

People used to gather at the city gates. The Hebrew word translated “gate” does not mean only the doors that stood at the entrance of the city. It could also mean the area near the city gate where people could pitch tents. In some places, when you entered the city, there was an open square where people could gather.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants traded at the city gates. Some of the city gates in Jerusalem were likely named after the goods that were sold there. Examples are the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People traded at the city gate, and others also went there to transact other business. Both Abraham and Boaz legally made agreements with others at the city gate before witnesses. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Also, when travelers were on a journey and night fell upon them in a town and no one offered them a place to sleep, they slept in the public square, and the place they slept was near the city gate. (Judg. 19:15-21) In addition, people went to the city gate to hear news of events that had occurred.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

The judges heard cases at the city gate. When a case was brought before them, they handled it in the presence of everyone who was there so that everyone would hear the testimony of the witnesses and see how the matter was decided. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because the cases were judged in public, it helped the judges to treat everyone fairly and to resolve the matters properly.

There was no rest at all at the city gates, because every day people went to and fro there.

When the judges concluded a case, elders were there to see to it that the decision the judges had made was carried out right away. (Deut. 25:2) The judges who were at the city gates were expected to deal fairly with everyone and to encourage the people to lead good lives.—Deut. 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Men who faithfully served Jehovah went to the city gates to encourage the Israelites to put their trust in Jehovah. Because people often gathered at the city gates, prophets and others went there to make proclamations. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, it was at the city gate that King Jehu stood and told all the people that what Jehovah said would happen to Ahab’s household had come true. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) King Hezekiah also gathered his military commanders at one of the city gates and encouraged them to rely on Jehovah.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that ‘wisdom cries aloud and raises its voice at the city gates.’ (Prov. 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, men who faithfully served Jehovah gave counsel to people at the city gates, and they also rendered righteous judgment there. Today, Jehovah’s wisdom fills the Bible, and his servants all over this world are helping people study the Bible so that they may gain some of that wisdom.

A BIBLE STORY YOU WILL ENJOY

A Tree That Wakes Up Early

The almond tree is among about 30 trees mentioned in the Bible. As winter is passing, the almond tree is among the trees that blossom. Even before the month of January comes to an end, this tree has already blossomed. It can blossom with pink or white flowers. In the Hebrew language, what the name of this tree means is "one who is awake." Can this tree help us learn something about the Creator?

According to Jeremiah 7:25, Jehovah said of himself that every day he got up early and sent his prophets to his people. The March 15, 2011 Watchtower explained: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakes’ or blossoms early, so it is as if Jehovah ‘gets up early’ . . . to warn his people about what their disobedience will bring upon them. . . . Jehovah would not rest and would ‘keep awake’ until his prophecy was fulfilled.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

Sometimes the Bible compares the white hair of elderly men and women to the white blossoms of the almond tree. (Eccl. 12:5) Also, because its blossoms are very beautiful, Jehovah likely had the cups on the branches of the lampstand in the tabernacle made to resemble the blossoms of the almond tree.—Ex. 37:17-20.
