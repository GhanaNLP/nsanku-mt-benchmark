BIOGRAPHY

I Have Been Serving Jehovah in Cuba for Some 70 Years

AS GUSTAVO JOSEPH SAID IT

I was born in 1947 on the beautiful Caribbean island of Cuba. This island is where the warm waters of the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet. My parents had two daughters after me. The five of us lived in the village of Esmeralda.

I remember that we lived in peace in that small village. We were not far from my aunts, uncles, grandfather and grandmother, as well as some other relatives. We had enough to eat, and we were happy.

When I was about five years old, Brother Walton Jones began studying the Bible with my parents. He was a zealous evangelizer, and he would walk about ten hours to get to our village. As soon as he arrived, many members of my family gathered at my grandfather's house, and he discussed the Bible with them for many hours. My parents, my uncle Pedro, and my aunt Ela really enjoyed the study, and before long, they were baptized as Jehovah's Witnesses. My aunt Ela is nearly 100 years old now, but she is still a pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, Jehovah’s Witnesses were able to attend meetings and preach freely in Cuba. We were well-known for preaching from house to house and carrying bags containing precious Bible-study publications. We were also known for walking a lot! I still remember how happily we served Jehovah during those ‘favorable times.’ But “difficult times” were also ahead.—2 Tim. 4:2.

CRITICAL TIMES HAVE ARRIVED

My father and my uncle traveled to another part of the island to attend a small circuit assembly when I was about five years old. Sadly, they drank contaminated water, which caused them to contract severe typhoid. I remember that when they returned, my uncle had lost all his hair, but he survived. However, my father died. He was only 32 years old.

After my father's death, my mother decided that our family should move to live with her brother in the village of Lombillo. This meant that we would leave my grandfather, my grandmother, and other relatives whom we loved dearly. But we continued to serve Jehovah.

I was baptized on August 26, 1957, in a pond near Lombillo. At that time I was ten years old. I did not know at all that in just two years, things would change completely for Jehovah's Witnesses in Cuba. In 1959, the government was overthrown and the Communists came to power.

Which two covenants was the apostle Paul speaking about in Galatians 4:24?

■ After the apostle Paul spoke about the relationship between Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar, he said: “These things are a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, we said that these two covenants represent the Law covenant and the new covenant. However, after further examination, we have come to realize that an adjustment is needed. Paul's words likely do not refer to the new covenant, but rather to the covenant that God made with Abraham. Let us examine some of the reasons why Paul made those statements.*

Paul compared the slave girl Hagar to Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) Hagar corresponds to the Law covenant that God made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai in 1513 B.C.E. (Ex. 19:5, 6) No imperfect human could keep this Law perfectly, so the Law reminded the Jews that they were slaves to sin. It made them see that they needed the Messiah, the only one through whom God would bring salvation to humans. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, he opened the way for imperfect humans to be set free from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) Since Jesus fulfilled the purpose of the Law, the Law was no longer needed.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul showed the difference between Hagar and Sarah. He said that Sarah was a “free woman” who gave birth to a child “through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise was what God covenanted with Abraham. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) This covenant was briefly mentioned in Genesis 22:18: “All the nations of the earth will gain a blessing for themselves by means of your [Abraham's] offspring, because you have listened to my voice.”

The covenant God made with Abraham helped us to understand much about the promise Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Genesis 3:15) It helped us to see that the foretold “offspring” would come from Abraham's family line. Paul explained that Jesus Christ is the primary part of this “offspring.” However, the 144,000 anointed ones who “belong to Christ” are also part of the offspring, and they will rule as kings with Jesus in God’s Kingdom. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) All those who exercise faith in Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings because of the promise God made to Abraham.

Why did Paul use this ‘symbolic drama’? Paul showed that if the Jews of his day continued to cling stubbornly to the Mosaic Law, they would remain slaves like Hagar. But if they put faith in the primary part of Abraham’s “offspring,” Jesus Christ, they would be free people like Sarah. They would be freed from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) But if they did not believe in Jesus, they would discard the primary purpose of the Law covenant, which was to lead them to Christ.

Many of the Galatians to whom Paul wrote these words were not Jews and had never even been under the Law. But sadly, some of the Jews in the congregation were insisting that the people of the nations who became Christians should keep the Law and be circumcised. Therefore, Paul warned those who had become Christians not to become “slaves” to the Law, because by having faith in Christ, they had become ‘freemen’ through Christ.—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

Nets Used for Catching Animals in Ancient Times

Many Bible readers know that some of Jesus’ followers were fishermen. (Mark 1:16-20) They used nets when catching fish in the Sea of Galilee.

Ropes are woven and knotted together to form a net. The size of the mesh depends on the kind of animal to be caught with it. Often, lead or stones are attached to the edges of small nets so that they can sink into the water, but large nets are heavy and can easily sink into the water, so corks are attached to their edges to keep them afloat.

The nets used for fishing in those days were very expensive. Even so, these nets could tear when they caught on rocks in the water or when they were used to catch a large number of fish. (Luke 5:6) The Bible says of James and John that they were “mending their nets.” They likely did this using a needle and thread. (Matt. 4:21) Fishermen likely repaired their nets each time they returned from fishing. Luke also says that the fishermen were “washing their nets.” They did this in order to remove mud and other debris from them. (Luke 5:2) Then they spread the nets to dry so that they would not rot quickly.—Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus invited men who used nets to catch animals to become ‘fishers of men.’
