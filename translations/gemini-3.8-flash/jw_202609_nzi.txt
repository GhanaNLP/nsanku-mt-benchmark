How We Can Acquire “an Obedient Heart”

Give your servant an obedient heart.—1 KGS. 3:9.

PEOPLE have different reasons why they are obedient. For many people, they are obedient only when someone is watching them or if they fear being punished. In the worship of God, too, many people are obedient so that others will praise them, just as the religious leaders did in Jesus’ day. (Matt. 23:5) But as for us, we obey Jehovah because his guidance helps us. (Isa. 48:17, 18) That is a very good reason, but there is another even more important reason why we should obey Jehovah.

2 The main reason we obey Jehovah is that we love him very much. (Matt. 22:37, 38) Our love for him moves us to ‘become obedient from the heart.’ (Rom. 6:17) Love for Jehovah and “an obedient heart” move us to do everything we can to obey his laws. We obey at all times, not only when others are watching us or only when doing so brings us immediate benefits. However, because our heart is treacherous, at times it is difficult to remain obedient. Therefore, in this article, we will consider two Bible accounts that can help us build a desire to be obedient. First, we will consider the time when Jehovah delivered the Israelites from Egypt. After that, we will look at the fine example set by King Jehoshaphat of Judah.

A BIBLE STORY

Let the Peace of the Christ Control in Your Hearts

Colossians 3:15 speaks of “the peace of the Christ.” What does “the peace of the Christ” mean? It means the peace that a person has in their heart because they are a follower of Christ. When we have the evidence that Jehovah and his Son love us, it gives us this peace. —John 14:23, 27.

Apostle Paul encouraged the Christians in the Colossian congregation to let the peace of Christ rule in their hearts or let it guide what they should do. In what way? The Greek word that Paul translated as ‘rule’ is related to the word used for someone who acts as a referee when playing ball. This means that, just as the ball players obey the referee's decisions, in the same way we should let “the peace of Christ” guide what we should do.

For example, if we have a dispute with a fellow believer, we must let “the peace of Christ” guide us, as if allowing it to act as an umpire or referee so that we know what to do to continue living in peace and unity. To be able to do this, we may need to put our personal preferences aside or forgive others their wrongs. When we do this, it shows that we are letting “the peace of Christ” direct our hearts in what they should do.—Col. 3:12-14.
