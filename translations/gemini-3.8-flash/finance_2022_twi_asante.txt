THE MINISTER'S SPEECH

The adverse effects of the COVID-19 pandemic have affected the economy of every nation in the world, including Ghana. The agony that resulted in deaths, social distress, and job losses experienced by Ghanaians is not something the Government has forgotten at all. I say with pride that the steps this nation took regarding this pandemic were courageous and very sound decisions that nations all over the world have acknowledged as a great achievement. The Government also implemented various initiatives to support private businesses so that the citizens could get back on their feet.

Furthermore, in order to withstand the challenges and opportunities brought about by this pandemic, the Government, through the capable leadership of President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, swiftly instituted the Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (GhanaCARES) “Obaatan Pa” programme, with a budget of one hundred billion cedis (GH¢100 billion). This has brought genuine relief to our people and continues to bring revitalization to the nation.

It is a comfort to us that you continue to have faith in the Government while we continue to do everything necessary to free ourselves from this pandemic. What befell us during that period has shown our resilience as a nation capable of withstanding disasters.

The Holy Scripture gives us guidance at all times that we should follow in times like this in the book of Isaiah, chapter 9, verse 10, where it is written, “The bricks have fallen down, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the fig-mulberry trees have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place.” In the same way, the Government is determined to fulfill all its promises, and because of this, it will continue to make plans and carry out various activities that will enable us to recover all that we have lost, thereby improving the living conditions of the citizens.

One of the key objectives in this 2022 Budget and over the next three years is to build a nation where the private sector will take root to drive our economic growth, and where everyone will play their proper role, leading to an abundance of jobs, especially for the youth. Therefore, the Government's policy steps will be aimed at supporting the youth, thereby identifying the talents they possess and helping to ensure that everyone gets the opportunity to work in a way that creates wealth for all.

In order to encourage the youth and instill in them an entrepreneurial spirit to start their own businesses, in the year 2022, the Government will introduce an initiative dubbed the YouStart Programme, through which the youth will access loans to start, sustain, or expand their businesses.

In this 2022 Budget, we have made various plans to continue taking steps regarding the nation's current challenges, thereby enabling us to stand on our feet again after this pandemic. The government has set its sights on making effective arrangements and partnering with various groups to take steps that will provide youth with vocational and technical skills training and ensure that private entrepreneurship among the youth progresses. GOOD FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT STEPS IN THIS 2022 BUDGET

National Economic Policies

14. The government has put measures in place to stimulate industrialization and help produce quality goods that will find markets locally and internationally. For this reason, it has become necessary to implement the following policy reforms:

• In order to consolidate the benefits to the manufacturing sector and enable them to expand their operations, the Government will extend the zero-rating of VAT on locally manufactured textiles, which commenced in 2019, by two years;

• It has been two years since we implemented the foreign trade policy regarding imported goods, in which we gave foreign traders some relief. We will make changes to this policy to make it effective and to support the specific foreign traders whom this policy is intended to help;

• We will reduce the withholding tax on unprocessed gold sold by small-scale mining businesses from 3 percent to 1.5 percent (withholding tax rate reduction from 3 % to 1.5 %);

• From the beginning of January in the year 2022, the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) will assist the Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (MMDAs) to find a unified approach that will help them take steps regarding property rate collection arrangements so that they can collect it properly and also account for it well;

• The government will review the fees charged by Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) for their services, which will take effect from January 1, 2022. The review of fees charged by the MDAs will be based on the annual inflation rate;

• As soon as Parliament approves this Budget, the road toll we pay will immediately come to an end;

• The Electronic Transaction Levy ("Electronic Transaction Levy" or "E-Levy") will be introduced in order to widen the tax net and bring those in the informal sector into the pool of taxpayers in this country. This levy will cover mobile money transfers, bank transfers, merchant payments, and inward remittances, and it is a 1.75% tax to be borne by the sender, except for inward remittances where the recipient will pay the tax. This tax will take effect on February 1, 2022. Transactions that occur in a single day up to one hundred cedis (GH¢100) or less will be exempt from this tax.

Operational Financial Plan

15. In order to spend money on critical needs and help achieve fiscal consolidation, we will take steps on various expenditure rationalization measures in 2022 and the medium term:

a. The government will continue to protect the payroll of public sector workers, and in doing so, we will ensure that only public sector workers who have been legitimately employed have their names on the government payroll;

b. In the year 2022 and the near future, we will conduct an evaluation of the baseline duties of Public Sector workers to determine the number of staff that will be needed in various Government institutions so that we can have an optimal and efficient Public Sector workforce;

c. All other development projects mentioned in this 2022 Budget to be undertaken, as well as those in the Medium-Term Development Plans of Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs), Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (MMDAs), and State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), regardless of the source of funding, will be pursued in accordance with the law (L.I. 2411) in order to eliminate waste and help ensure that national development projects carried out by the Government for the citizens are executed effectively and profitably.

16. We will pursue the following economic measures to avoid deviating from our predetermined fiscal consolidation path in the near future:

• Only items approved by Public Entities in their Procurement Plans and published on the Public Procurement Authority (PPA) website will be used to award procurement contracts;

• Procurement requests made by any government agency in each year must be those for which funds have been allocated in the budget of that same year. Apart from this, we must know the source of funding for this procurement as well as the amount of money available for it;

• No government official has the right to sign any Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) that financially binds the Government of Ghana without obtaining approval from the Minister of Finance.

Ghana Covid-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (GhanaCARES) "Obaatan Pa" Programme

Glossary of Terms (Financial Planning Terms)

One District One Factory (1D1F): This involves various industries that will be established in districts to promote industrial development in Ghana. The objective of this is to increase jobs in this country so that Ghanaians across all parts of the country can secure employment.

Accountability: This stands for the account that the Government gives to the citizens regarding its operations, as well as reporting on the various steps it has taken. Financial matters and various properties that the Government oversees for the citizens are all included in this.

Allocation: This is the way national funds are distributed to the various government agencies and organizations for them to use for the citizens.

Alleviation: This is a way in which the effects of a problem are reduced.

Financial Plan (Budget): A financial plan represents the plan the Government has made regarding the way it will obtain money and what it will use that money for. For example, a family can make a financial plan to show the amount of money they have and the various things they need to use the money for in order of priority. It could be rent expenses, water and electricity bills, and food expenses. If it happens that they need more money, it will be necessary for the family to look for additional money or even go to take a loan.

Budget Deficit: This occurs when the government's expenditures exceed its actual revenue.

Capital Expenditure: This is money used for development projects such as road construction, school building construction, hospital construction, building reservoirs/dams, transport, water, heavy machinery, and the like.

Compensation of Employees: This is the employees' monthly salary, various allowances, social security contributions, travel allowances, and related payments made to government workers.

Development Partners: This stands for nations or organizations that assist developing countries to achieve their development goals. These can be various bodies such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank, and so on. It can also be individual countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States, and others.

End-year inflation: This is the level that the price of goods reaches in the month of December in a specific year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when the funds used for various operations exceed what was allocated in the budget approved by parliament.

"Free" Senior High School (FREE SHS): This Free Senior High School education is a new government policy introduced by President Nana Akufo-Addo's government in September 2017, ensuring that every JHS student who excels in their examination will gain admission to Senior High School to study without paying anything.

Grants: These are various financial assistances that Development Partners provide to support the Government, which the Government does not have to repay.

Grants to other Government Units: This represents funds that the law mandates to be paid into the District Assemblies Common Fund “DACF”, the “Road Fund”, the Ghana Education Trust Fund “GETFUND”, and the “Petroleum Related Fund”.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This refers to the total value of all goods and services produced in the country within a specific period.

Gross International Reserves: This is a measure by which we know the economic strength of a nation to be able to purchase goods from abroad at a specific time. This helps us to compare the treasury of one country to that of another to see what the nation's economic strength is like.

Inflation: This represents how the prices of various services and goods increase over a specific period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme - IPEP: This Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is one of the flagship programmes of the NPP Government, with the underlying objective of bringing development to rural areas, especially deprived communities, in order to help eradicate poverty and bridge the socio-economic disparity between rural and urban dwellers.

Macroeconomic targets: These are benchmarks used to indicate a country's economic performance. Examples of this include economic growth, inflation, broad employment growth, the performance of trade between this country and other countries, how the government's fiscal surplus or deficit is, and how the decisions that the Bank of Ghana will make will affect inflation in the country.

Micro And Small Business Development Programme - MSME: Micro and small businesses are private enterprises that can employ a few people and whose capital investment does not exceed a specific amount of money.

Nation Builders Corps- NABCo: This is a completely new initiative introduced by the Government of Ghana to provide employment opportunities for graduates of tertiary institutions in private or public sectors, enabling them to use their knowledge to solve various societal challenges, and to help them gain experience that will support the nation's economic development.

National Board for Small Scale Industries- (NBSSI): The National Board for Small Scale Industries is a non-profit state agency, under the Ministry of Trade and Industry, legally mandated to oversee the development of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs).

Non-Oil Real Gross Domestic Product (Non-Oil Real GDP): This is the total value of this country's goods and services excluding revenue from petroleum-related activities, which has been adjusted for inflation.

Non-tax revenue (Non Tax Revenue): This is government revenue generated from sources other than taxes. Examples include fees paid before obtaining a license to operate, and payments made for other things such as security deposits and interest.

Government Outstanding Obligations (Outstanding Obligations): Government outstanding obligations are monies that the Ministry of Finance must pay to the various Government Agencies, as well as outstanding arrears from 2016 that the law mandates the Government to pay into Statutory Funds.

Primary balance: This is the remaining amount of money left after the Government has spent all its expected revenue, excluding loan interest payments (meaning, loan payments by the Government are not included). And the way this is calculated is by measuring it as a percentage of the Gross Domestic Product.

Projected Growth Rate: This is how Ghana's economy is expected to grow in a specific year.

Government Revenue (Revenue): This is the total amount of money the government receives from its various activities within the country and abroad.

Rearing for Food and Jobs (RFJ): This is a new development program introduced by the Government of Ghana through which the livestock farming business will flourish and become profitable so that meat from livestock will be abundant for the citizenry to consume and completely reduce the importation of meat into this country, and thereby create abundant jobs for the citizenry

Social Benefits: This is money that the Government pays on behalf of the citizens to water, petrol, and electricity companies to cushion Ghanaians.

Subsidy: This is a financial support that the Government provides to citizens to ease some of the burden off them and bring them relief.

Surveillance: This is a way by which we keep track of something and observe how it is.

Transparency: This is when the Government allows the citizens to see what is going on and hear all reports periodically.

Use of Goods and Services: This is the total amount of money the Government pays for its operations and the services it provides to the public.

Value Added Tax (VAT): This is a tax that is levied on goods at each stage of production.
