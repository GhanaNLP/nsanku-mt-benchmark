THE MINISTER'S SPEECH

The negative effects of the COVID-19 pandemic have affected the economy of every nation in the world, including Ghana. The Government has not at all forgotten the distress leading to death, the social hardships, and the job losses that Ghanaians experienced. I say with pride that the steps this nation took regarding this pandemic were courageous and highly effective decisions, recognized and commended by the entire world. The Government also implemented various initiatives to support private businesses so that the citizens could get back on their feet.

Furthermore, in order to address the challenges and seize the opportunities brought about by this pandemic, the Government, through the visionary leadership of President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, swiftly instituted the Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (GhanaCARES) “Obaatan Pa” Programme, with a funding of one hundred billion cedis (GH¢100 billion). This has brought true relief to our people and continues to bring revitalization to the nation.

It is a source of comfort to us that you continue to have faith in the Government while we continue to do everything necessary to free ourselves from this pandemic. Through what befell us during that period, we have seen our resilience as a nation capable of standing up to disaster.

The Holy Scriptures give us guidance at all times that we should follow in times like these in the book of Isaiah, chapter 9, verse 10, where it is written, “The bricks have fallen down, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the fig-mulberry trees have been cut down, but we will replace them with cedars.” In the same way, the Government is determined to fulfill all its promises, and because of this, it will continue to make plans and undertake various activities that will enable us to recover all that we have lost, and thereby improve the lives of the citizens.

One of the primary objectives in this 2022 Budget and over the next three years is to build a nation where private enterprise will be anchored to ensure the success of our economic progress, where everyone will play their role appropriately, leading to an abundance of jobs, particularly for the youth. For this reason, the Government's policy interventions will support the youth, thereby identifying their God-given talents and helping everyone gain the opportunity to work, in order to promote wealth creation for all.

In order to encourage the youth and foster an entrepreneurial spirit to start their own businesses, in the year 2022, the Government will initiate a program dubbed the YouStart Initiative, which will provide loans to the youth to start their businesses and sustain or expand them.

In this 2022 Budget Statement, we have made various plans to continue taking steps regarding the nation's current challenges, thereby enabling us to stand on our feet again after this pandemic. The government has resolved to make effective arrangements and collaborate with various groups to take steps that will provide youth with vocational and technical training and ensure the promotion of entrepreneurship among the youth. KEY FISCAL POLICY MEASURES IN THIS 2022 BUDGET

National Economic Policies

14. The government has put various measures in place to stimulate industrial activities and support the production of quality goods that will find markets locally and abroad. Therefore, it has become necessary that we make the following policy changes:

• To consolidate the gains made by the local industries and enable them to expand their operations, the Government will extend the zero-rating of VAT on locally manufactured textiles, which commenced in 2019, by another two years;

• Two years after implementing the foreign trade policy concerning imported goods, in which we slightly relieved the pressure on foreign traders, we will make adjustments to this policy to make it complete and to support the specific foreign traders whom this policy is intended to help;

• We will reduce the withholding tax on unprocessed gold sold by small-scale mining enterprises from three percent to one and a half percent (withholding tax rate reduction from 3 % to 1.5 %);

• Beginning in January 2022, the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) will assist the Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (MMDAs) to explore a common platform that will help them take steps regarding property rate collection arrangements so that they can collect them effectively and also properly account for them;

• The government will make adjustments to the fees charged by Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs) for their services, which will take effect from January 1, 2022. The adjustments to the fees charged by the MDAs will depend on the annual inflation rate;

• As soon as Parliament approves this Financial Policy, road tolls that we pay will end immediately;

• We will introduce the Electronic Transaction Levy ("E-Levy") to widen the tax net and rope in the informal sector to be part of the taxpayers in this country. This levy will cover mobile money transfers, bank transfers, merchant payments, and inward remittances, and the levy on it is one point seven five percent (1.75%) to be borne by the sender, except in the case of inward remittances where the receiver will pay this levy. This levy will take effect from 1st February 2022. Transactions that occur in a single day up to one hundred cedis (GH¢100) or less will not be affected by this levy.

Financial Regulations Relating to Operations

15. In order to spend money on what is truly important and help achieve fiscal discipline, we will take steps on various program-based budgeting arrangements in 2022 and the medium term ahead of us:

a. The Government will continue to protect the government payroll system, and in this regard, we will ensure that only public sector workers who have been legitimately employed have their names on the Government payroll;

b. In the year 2022 and in the near future, we will conduct a Civil Service functional review to determine the number of staff that will be needed in various government institutions in order to achieve an optimal and effective Civil Service workforce;

c. All other development projects mentioned in this 2022 Budget Statement to be executed, and all steps regarding those in the Medium-Term Development Plans of Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs), Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (MMDAs), and State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), irrespective of the source of funding for the project, will be taken through the legal framework of (L.I. 2411) in order to eliminate waste and help ensure that public development services provided to the citizenry are fulfilled productively.

16. We will take the following financial steps so that we do not miss the fiscal consolidation path we have set in the short term ahead of us:

• Only items approved by Public Entities in their Procurement Plans and published on the Public Procurement Authority (PPA) website will be processed for the award of procurement contracts;

• Procurement requests made by every government entity in each year must be those for which funds have been allocated in the budget for that same year. Apart from this, the source of funding for such procurement must be known, as well as the total amount of money available for the procurement;

• No public official has the right to sign any Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) that binds the Government of Ghana financially without obtaining approval from the Minister for Finance.

POST-COVID-19 PANDEMIC ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

Ghana Covid-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (Ghanacares) “Obaatan Pa” Programme

17. To achieve our goal of a Ghana Beyond Aid and to recover from the COVID-19 pandemic, the Government of Ghana initiated the Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (GhanaCARES) “Obaatan Pa” Programme, with a funding envelope of one hundred billion cedis (GH¢100 billion). Modern Methods of Farming and Food Import Substitution

Glossary of Terms (Financial Planning Terms)

One District One Factory (1D1F): This is a variety of industries that will be established in the district to promote industrial development in Ghana. The aim of this is to increase jobs in this country and ensure that Ghanaians across all parts of the nation find employment.

Accountability: This stands for the accounts the Government gives to the citizens regarding its operations and reporting on the various steps it has taken. Financial matters and the various assets the Government manages for the citizens are all part of this.

Allocation: This is the method used to share the nation's funds among the various government departments and agencies so they can use them to serve the public.

Alleviation: This is a way through which the effects of a problem are reduced.

Budget: A budget represents a plan made by the government regarding how it will raise money and what it will use that money for. For example, a family can prepare a budget to outline the amount of money they have and the various essential things they need to spend that money on in order of priority. (It could be rent expenses, water and electricity bills, as well as food expenses). If it turns out that they need more money, it will become necessary for the family to find additional funds or even go and take a loan.

Budget deficit: This happens when the money the Government uses for its operations exceeds what the Government actually received.

Capital Expenditure: This is money used for developmental projects such as road construction, school building construction, hospital construction, dam construction, transportation, water, heavy machinery, and the like.

Compensation of Employees: This is employees' monthly salary, allowances, social security contributions, transport allowances, and other related payments made to government workers.

Development Partners: This stands for countries or organizations that help developing countries reach their development goals. This can be various groups such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank, and so on. It can also be individual countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States of America, and others.

End Year Inflation: This is the level that the prices of goods reach in the month of December in a specific year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when the funds spent on various activities exceed the amount allocated in the budget approved by parliament.

Free Senior High School (FREE SHS): This Free Senior High School is a new Government policy introduced by President Nana Akufo-Addo's government in September 2017, ensuring that every JHS student who excels in the examination will gain admission to Senior High School to study free of charge.

Grants: These are various financial assistance that Development Partners give to support the Government which the Government will not repay.

Grants to other Government Units: This represents funds that the law mandates to be paid into the District Assemblies Common Fund "DACF", the Road Fund, the Ghana Education Trust Fund "GETFUND", and the Petroleum Related Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This refers to the total value of all goods and services produced in the country within a specific period.

Gross International Reserves: This is a measure by which we determine the financial capacity of a country to purchase goods from abroad at a specific time. This helps to compare the reserves of one country with those of another to see the financial strength of that country.

Inflation: This represents how the prices of goods and services rise over a specific period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme - IPEP: This Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is one of the flagship programmes in the NPP Government's policies whose underlying objective is to bring infrastructure to rural areas, especially impoverished communities, thereby helping to eradicate poverty and change the disparities in living standards between rural and urban dwellers.

Macroeconomic targets: These are benchmarks used to indicate a country's economic performance. Examples of this are economic growth, inflation, continuous employment growth, the performance of trade between this country and other nations, the status of the government's fiscal surplus or deficit, and how decisions made by the Bank of Ghana will affect inflation in the country.

Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Programme - MSME (Micro And Small Business Development Programme): Small businesses are private enterprises that can employ a few people, where the capital on which the business is established does not exceed a specific amount of money.

Nation Builders Corps - NABCo: This is a brand-new initiative introduced by the Government of Ghana through which young men and women who have completed tertiary education will gain employment in private or public sector institutions and use their knowledge to help solve various social challenges, thereby helping them gain experience that will support the nation's economic development.

National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI): The National Board for Small Scale Industries is a non-profit public institution under the Ministry of Trade and Industry, legally mandated to promote the development of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs).

Non-Oil Real Gross Domestic Product (Non-Oil Real GDP): This is the total value of all goods and services produced in the country, excluding revenue from oil-related activities, adjusted for inflation.

Non Tax Revenue: This is government money received that does not come from taxes. Some of this includes the money a person pays before obtaining a permit or license to operate, and money paid on other things such as deposits and rent.

Outstanding Obligations: Outstanding obligations are funds that the Ministry of Finance must pay to the various Government Ministries, Departments and Agencies, as well as outstanding debt since 2016 that the law requires the Government to pay into the Statutory Funds.

Primary balance: This is the remaining amount of money left after the Government has spent all its expected revenue, excluding loan interest payments (that is, loans to be repaid by the Government are not included). And the way this is calculated is by measuring it as a percentage of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

Projected Growth Rate: This is how Ghana's economy is expected to grow in a specific year.

Government Revenue (Revenue): This is the total amount of money the Government receives from its various activities within the country and abroad.

Rearing for Food and Jobs - RFJ: This is a new development program introduced by the Government of Ghana through which the livestock farming business will be modernized and made profitable, so that meat from livestock will become abundant for citizens to eat and we can drastically reduce meat imports into this country, and thereby create abundant jobs that the citizens

will get some to do so that the lives of everyone involved in the vegetable trade will also improve.

Social Benefits: This is the money that the Government pays on behalf of the citizens to water, petrol, and electricity companies to relieve the burden on Ghanaians.

Subsidy: This is a financial support that the Government provides to citizens to ease some of their burden and grant them relief.

Surveillance: This is a way by which we keep an eye on something and observe how it is.

Transparency: This means that the Government will let the citizens know what is going on and they will hear all reports from time to time.

Use of Goods and Services: This is the total amount of money that the Government pays for the operations it carries out and the services it renders to the citizens.

Value Added Tax (VAT): This is a tax placed on various goods at every stage of production.
