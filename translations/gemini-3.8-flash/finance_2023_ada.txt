INTRODUCTION

There is no doubt that this year has been a year in which our country's economic system faced severe difficulties more than in past years. As it happened across all nations, the deadly Covid-19 disease also hit us shortly after we entered the year 2020. This crisis greatly disrupted our national economic planning, which weakened our economic structures, and treating this new disease, Covid-19, for which we were unprepared, was added to our existing health burdens.

While we thought we were coming out of this terrible disease through our interventions, other disruptions emerged that derailed our fiscal consolidation efforts from 2017 to 2019. The first was the war between Russia and Ukraine, and subsequent delays in approvals for the 2022 budget also weakened investor confidence. Consequently, the international markets shut their doors to Ghana, placing severe stress on the domestic financial sector. This drove inflation to historic highs, widened our fiscal deficit, weakened the cedi, and eroded its purchasing power.

Furthermore, the financial support we received from international trade decreased. In addition, our high debt burden and the inability to service our external debts continued to weaken our currency. This made living conditions and the prices of all goods very expensive for both the government and all citizens. Petrol and diesel prices rose drastically, which drove up the prices of food items and all other commodities, pushing inflation to 40.4 percent in the month of October in the year 2022.

We all have no doubt or hesitation that the Government sympathizes with everyone over the severe trouble and hardship that this crisis has brought upon everyone. As the Government, we want to extend our gratitude and
