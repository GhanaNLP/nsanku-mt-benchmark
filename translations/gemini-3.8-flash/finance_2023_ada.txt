INTRODUCTION

There is no doubt that this year has been a year in which our country's financial planning faced serious problems more than in previous years. As happened in all nations, the terrible Covid-19 disease also affected us just as we entered the year 2020. This problem greatly depressed our national economic planning, and this weakened our national economic plans, and the treatment of this new disease, Covid-19, for which we were not prepared, added to our illnesses.

Just as we thought we were emerging from the grip of this terrible disease through our mitigation measures, other external factors hit us, derailing all our fiscal consolidation efforts made between 2017 and 2019. The first was the outbreak of the Russia-Ukraine war, and subsequently, the delays in reaching an agreement on the 2022 national budget and financial plan eroded investor confidence or lowered credit ratings for our financial operations. Consequently, the international capital market, from which Ghana also borrowed, closed its doors to us, putting severe pressure on the domestic financial system. This drove inflation to record highs, worsened our debt distress, and dealt a heavy blow to our cedi, causing it to depreciate sharply.

Furthermore, the support and foreign exchange reserves we gain from international trade declined. In addition, our high reliance on imported goods, coupled with our inability to access the international capital market, continued to weaken our currency. This made the living conditions and prices of all goods extremely difficult for the Government and all citizens. The prices of petrol and diesel shot up drastically, which affected food prices and the prices of all other goods and services, pushing inflation to 40.4 percent in October 2022.

All of us have entered into some temptation or trial, and the Government sympathizes with everyone concerning the severe trouble and hardship that this situation has brought upon everyone. As the Government, we want to bring thanks and

Financial Allocation Plans

19. In order to properly implement these planned budget programs, the Government will submit to Parliament for consideration and approval, revenue mobilization measures capable of raising revenue of about 1.35 percent of GDP. Some of these measures include:

• Review or reform of cigarette and tobacco policies to align with ECOWAS regulations, and an increase or introduction of higher taxes on hard liquor compared to beer taxes.

• The standard rate of Value Added Tax (VAT) was increased by 2.5 percentage points from the previous rate of 12.5% to 15%. In addition, the Value Added Tax (VAT) base was widened to allow for major structural changes and some relief.

• The "National Fiscal Stabilisation Levy (NFSL)" has been transformed into the "Growth and Sustainability Levy (GSL)" to ensure it reaches everywhere.

• The Monetary Policy Rate will be reduced from 1.5 percent to 1 percent, which will allow the daily cash reserve requirement to be maintained.

• Some of the other arrangements are:
