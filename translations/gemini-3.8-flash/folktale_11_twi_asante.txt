KWATIMA was there and he said, "I am going to look for a piece of land to build a house on." And Leopard also thought about the very same thing. Then Kwatima set out and went to see the land he wanted; and he cleared it so that he could build a house. And Leopard also went. He said, "Well! The town I intend to build, I haven't even started it yet, and the spirits have already begun it." And he too cleared some, and then he left. When day broke, Kwatima also went. When he arrived there, he said, "Well! The spirits will truly help me, the land has already been cleared." And he too swept it, and he left. Then Leopard came. He said, "Ah, as for this matter, I am afraid of it." And he too went into the forest; he went and cut down trees and brought them and placed them there. And Kwatima also came, and he too looked here. He also went into the forest to cut vines, brought them and laid them there, and left. Leopard also came. He too looked here, tied things up, and left. Kwatima also came, dug holes, erected the wooden posts in them, and left. And Leopard also came, worked around it, and left. And Kwatima also came, removed thatch, brought it and placed it there, and left. And Leopard also came, stamped clay, and used it to plaster; and he too left. And Kwatima also came, roofed the house, and left. And Leopard also came, framed it, and left. And Kwatima also came, dug white clay and smeared it on, and went home. When he went, he said to his child, "Today I have finished the house, so if Adae falls on Sunday, on Monday we will go and hold the housewarming, so find someone who has a gun so that we can go and fire it in the house." And Leopard also came. He said, "Ah! I have nothing more to say, except that I am going to prepare myself and come back." Then he went, went and told his child, "Run swiftly and go tell the ghost hunter that if Adae falls on Sunday, on Monday I will hold a housewarming, so let him come and fire a gun for me." And his child went, and told him. He said, "I have heard." Then on Dapaa day Kwatima gathered his things and put them in the house, and he came back. Leopard also gathered his things and went. When day broke, it was Akwasidae, and Kwatima went to bid farewell to his people with whom he lived; and Leopard also went to bid farewell. When day broke, it was Monday, and Kwatima and his child went; they went and fired a gun in the house. And Leopard's child heard the gun; he said, "Today things are not right; today it is the spirits that are firing guns." And he too came; and the Ghost Hunter also went into the room there, and he made the gun sound out: boom! Leopard came to see Kwatima and his child sitting there. He said, "What is your news?" Kwatima also said, "What is your news too?" Leopard said, "If you have no news, I too have none." And Kwatima said, "Then this is how the two of us will live together." Then they lived there. Whenever Leopard got up, he went hunting, and brought meat. Every day. But as for Kwatima, he never brought any. One day the champion went into the forest; he hunted and hunted, in vain! He returned home and said, "Kwatima, if today you do not go into the forest and bring meat, what I will do to you, you will not like." Kwatima set out, went into the forest, and brought back a bushbuck and dropped it there. When Leopard looked at the animal, he was exceedingly amazed. When day broke, Kwatima went and brought some more again. The Leopard said, "Eh!" He told his child, saying, "Hey! I am going to the forest, both I and Kwatima, and that young duiker that I brought you the other day, when we go, call Kwatima's child and ask him if his father catches meat when he goes to the forest, and you also show this young duiker to him." And the people went. The Leopard's child said, "Kwatima's child, come, let us go play." He came; he said, "This is the meat my father catches when he goes to the forest." Then he leaped and caught the young duiker, and struck it in the throat. Kwatima's child said, "You too go and bring a very large stone." And the Leopard's child went and brought one. He placed it there. Kwatima's child went away from him and came back, and struck the stone with his forehead, *pim!* and it shattered into pieces. The Leopard's child said, "Eh!" Then their fathers returned from the forest, and the Leopard's child told his father the news; and Kwatima's child also told him the news. Now, while they were there, something stuck to Kwatima's foot, and as he tried to wipe it off, the Leopard thought he was moving behind him to kill him.

Immediately, Leopard fell upon Kwatima; he wanted to catch him. And Kwatima took to his heels, running with all his might. He came to a certain pond nearby, in which Python lay. Kwatima said, "Father Python, I flee to you for refuge, for Leopard will not leave me alone." Then Python said, "You, fellow, have done well, so skin me." Kwatima also tried his best and skinned him. That is how Kwatima came to be named Odwanini (Ram). And it was because of the Python (Onini) that he went to skin.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some and eat, and take some and bring it back to me.
