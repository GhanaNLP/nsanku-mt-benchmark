MINISTER OF FINANCE'S SPEECH

The devastating impact of COVID-19 has affected all economies, including Ghana. The hardships Ghanaians have faced due to the loss of lives and the decline in business and economic activities are not lost on the government. I say with pride that how we fought the pandemic was a collective effort of shared determination, and it earned the admiration of the whole world. The government also took measures to revive struggling businesses and restore our lives to normalcy.

Furthermore, regarding the announcement and the impact of the pandemic, the leader of the Government, His Excellency the President, Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, acted swiftly to establish the GH¢100 billion COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises programme (the GhanaCARES “Obatanpa” programme). This brought great relief in the blink of an eye to our people and revitalized our country.

We thank you for the trust you have placed in the Government, especially during this time when we are making every effort to work to overcome this pandemic. Throughout this period, our effort to stand together as one nation has been tested. The book of God continues to give us protection in times like this. As the Prophet Isaiah said, “The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the fig trees have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place” (Isaiah 9:10). The Government remains committed to fulfilling all the promises it made, and for that reason, we will continue with policies and programs that will beautify all the places that were neglected and improve the living conditions of the people.

One major goal in this 2022 budget, and the three years that follow, is to initiate a transformed nation that can ensure continuous, non-discriminatory economic growth, and also bring good jobs for everyone, especially the youth. All government policies will focus on supporting young people to expand their knowledge and demonstrate their skills, bringing progress and wealth creation to everyone.

To enable the youth to develop good ideas and acquire entrepreneurial skills, the Government will roll out the YouStart programme in 2022 to provide funding support for new businesses, to establish or expand their businesses.

In the 2022 Budget, we set out measures to continue addressing the economic difficulties that exist and enable a strong recovery post-pandemic. The government pledged to take comprehensive steps to reach everyone, provide skills training, and boost entrepreneurship among the youth. In line with the President's vision of Ghana Beyond Aid, it aims to encourage every Ghanaian, especially the youth, to embrace good attitudes, think deeply, and actively engage in business that will contribute to nation-building.

I encourage all of us to help build the Ghana we all want.

May God bless it!!!!

Ken Ofori-Atta

Minister for Finance

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT IN THE YEAR 2021

The big log of mahogany

3. The current information regarding the economic performance for the year 2021 follows below:

• Total GDP grew by 3.5% in the first half of 2021 compared to the same period in 2020, representing a 0.7% GDP growth in 2020;

• Non-oil GDP growth was 4.8% in the first half of 2021, compared to -1.1% in the same period of 2020;

• Inflation at the end of September was 10.6%, compared to 10.4% in the same period of 2020;

• The expenditure exceeding the budget estimate amounted to 7.7% of GDP, whereas the GDP estimate was 7.4%;

• The revised budget was a deficit of 2.0% of GDP, compared to the projected deficit of 1.7% of GDP;

• The country's debt (as a percentage of GDP) stood at 77.7% at the end of September 2021, rising from 76.1% in December 2020 to reach that level;

• Gross International Reserves (GIR) at the end of August 2021 stood at US$11.44 billion, sufficient to cover 5.2 months of imports of goods and services, compared with US$8.62 billion which could cover 4.1 months of imports at the end of December 2020; and

• Foreign exchange rates have remained stable over the past nine months of 2021. Indeed, the Ghana cedi depreciated by 1.7% against the US dollar in September 2021, compared to 2020, when it stood at 2.9% around the same time.

2022 BUDGET REVENUE MOBILIZATION STRATEGY

Tax collection procedures

14. The government shall seek ways to revive business enterprises and strengthen fair competition and cooperation between them and foreign entities. For this reason, it is mandatory for the Government to adopt the following measures below:

• Enhance business efficiency and expand the business environment. Government will extend the VAT zero-rating support that was on the textile manufacturing industry in 2019 for another two years;

• After two and a half years, the demand on imported goods will be reviewed to strengthen it and ensure continued oversight;

• Reduced the withholding tax on gold sold by small-scale miners from 3% to 1.5%;

• Starting in 2022, the Government will link its efforts with the Ghana Revenue Authority to assist MMDAs in the effective collection of property rates and fair revenue utilization;

• By January 1st 2022, the Government will review the fees charged by MDAs. The fees they charge will depend on the rate of inflation;

• When parliament approves this budget, no one will pay road tolls anymore.

• The government will place an Electronic Transaction Levy or E-Levy on all electronic transactions, which will broaden the tax net to include everyone. This levy will cover mobile money payments, bank transfers, merchant payments, and inward remittances, with a tax rate of 1.75% to be borne by the sender, except for inward remittances which will be borne by the recipient. This will take effect from February, 2022. This tax does not cover transactions of GH¢100 or less.

• No government worker should enter into an agreement on behalf of the Government that will cause the Government to incur expenditure unless the Minister responsible for Government finance has granted permission.

Objectives of the scouting movement

15. To ensure fiscal discipline and consolidate economic gains, fiscal policy will focus on revenue mobilization and expenditure management in the 2022 fiscal year and the medium term:

a. Government will continue to monitor and protect the integrity of the payroll, ensuring that only verified government workers who have followed the right process are on the payroll;

b. In this year 2022 and up to the middle of the year, there will be an investigation into Government workers, which will prevent deceit in the recruitment of workers into Government service, with the aim of promoting truthfulness among those entering Government service;

c. All new projects to be executed in the year 2022, as well as all those in the Medium-Term Development Plans of MDAs, MMDAs, and SOEs, will adhere to L. I. 2411 to enhance Government works and procurement. This will not depend on the source of funding for the projects.

16. The following expenditure control measures will also be pursued to protect against slippages from the medium-term fiscal consolidation path:

• Only items that appear in the plans of the MDAs and are also on the PPA website will be awarded contracts;

• All activities that procurement entities intend to undertake must be accompanied by the annual expenditure budget. In addition to this, all these activities must follow the appropriate funding sources, indicating that the funds for carrying out the work are available;

• No government official shall sign documents that would commit the Government of Ghana into debt without approval or without the authorization of the Minister responsible for finance.

ECONOMIC RECOVERY POST COVID-19

GhanaCARES "Obatanpa" Program

17. To consolidate Ghana's overall self-reliance beyond COVID-19, the Government launched the GH¢100 billion Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support programme (the GhanaCARES “Obatanpa” programme). This is designed to strengthen and elevate our country's economic revitalization and support the private sector.

18. The following are the successes achieved by the program in the year 2021 and the goals for the year 2022.

Adoption of new agricultural practices and food import substitution

19. About 579,662 people, including about 547,126 farmers as well as about 32,536 commercial farmers, registered as October 2021 farmers to strengthen fairness in Government support for the agricultural sector.

20. The government brought together actors in the poultry business and rice farming, as well as soybean and tomato farmers, to understand the challenges in these agricultural sectors, which led to a support program called the Interest Rate Subsidy (IRS) for agribusinesses to alleviate their financial difficulties. This IRS will help reduce the interest rate on borrowed funds by 50%, which will assist them in acquiring machinery and expanding their business operations.

21. In the adoption of new agricultural practices, the following are the pathways that the Government will strengthen in the year 2022:

• establish the Ghana Agricultural Land Information Bank (GhLIB) to help in accessing land for cultivation, especially for commercial farming;

• it discourages our youth from agriculture in addition to the use of social media; and

• Have agricultural and livestock business and investments that will be spent on the disposal/marketing of the animals, and sell its information to the youth.

EMPLOYMENT, SECTOR DEVELOPMENT AND COMMERCE

33. As the period of the Ghana Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (2018-2021) has passed, Ghana has entered the Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (MTNDPF) (2022-2025). Meanings and explanation of keywords (Budget Terms)

1D1F (One District, One Factory): The construction of warehouses and factories will lead this country into large-scale industrialization, which brings great development to this nation. Its objective is to create jobs for Ghanaians, across all corners and sectors of this country.

Spending money through the path of justice: This is when the government works openly and allows all the citizens of this country to know every secret of the work, its success, and all its challenges. This can be money or how the government manages all the country's resources.

Quota: It is the method followed to ensure that resources reach all Government offices and departments.

Alleviation: This is a way they follow to reduce the severity of a problem.

Budget: A budget is a way that shows where money will come from and what the money will be spent on. For example, a family (household) can have their budget to show how much money they have, and show how they will spend it on their rent, electricity and water bills, and food. If the money will be insufficient, the family will discuss finding additional money or borrowing money.

Budget deficit: This occurs when government expenditure exceeds the tax revenue collected.

Capital Expenditure: This is the money spent by the Government on major projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, vehicles, water, and machinery.

Government workers' compensation of employees: This is the salaries and allowances of workers that follow monthly pay, such as taxes and monies paid to retired Government workers.

Development Partners (DPs): This refers to countries or organizations that support the government to fulfill national development goals. Examples of DPs include the global financial institution we call the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, and African

End of year inflation: This is the rise in the prices of goods every year in the month of December.

Excess expenditure: This happens when the Government spends beyond the budget amount approved by Parliament.

FREE SHS: Free SHS is a policy of the Government of Ghana introduced by President Nana Akufo-Addo during his tenure in September 2017, to allow every student who qualifies to attend SHS to attend for free without paying a penny.

Grant: This is money given to the Government by international development partners which is not to be repaid.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is the goods and services that are in a country at a given time, and what their value is.

Gross International Reserves: This measures the value of a country's foreign exchange or its ability to purchase goods or services internationally within a given period. It also shows the value of the country's currency in comparison to the value of international currencies.

Inflation: The rate at which the prices of goods and services rise or increase over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is a good initiative introduced by the NPP Government to eradicate poverty and reduce disparities between urban and rural areas.
