We are not alike, not alike at all

A pufferfish that was ungrateful to its river sleeps in shallow water

It is said that for Okawa, Opitire, Bonko, and all the river animals, a severe drought occurred, and all the water dried up, causing all the animals to suffer. They were lying on the sand. Then they said, "As for this suffering we are experiencing, let us go to God and weep before Him so that He will give us water." All the animals set off and went. And Okawa began, saying:

"Is it not me, Oka’ Abena, the one who has marched here in thousands and returned here in thousands?

I have leaped and stood on the razor's edge, I have cried out and called you.

I have stubbed my little toe.

Aberefi Sempa my wife, Abuya.”

And it did not rain. And Mudfish had also come, saying:

Am I not Opitire Abena, the one who bustles to and fro?

I have leaped and stood on the razor's edge, I have cried out and called you.

I have stubbed my little toe.

Aberefi Sempa my wife, Abuya.”

And rain did not fall. And Bonko also came, saying:

Is it not I, Bonko Abena, who have marched back and forth thousands of times?

I have jumped and stood on the crossbeam, I have wept and called out to you. I have pulled the corn cob from under my foot.

Aberefi Sempa my wife, Abuya.”

And the rain did not fall. All the animals came to cry, and it was left with the Dolphin (or Porpoise/Water-beetle). He said, "Allow me to come and cry some." And they said, "Get away, we have cried and it did not work, and what about you, what will it do?" He said, "Allow me to cry." And they beat him, saying they had already told him not to cry. And Apupuo said, "I will cry." And they said, "Cry!" And he raised his voice:

"Is it not me, Apupuo Abena, who has pushed here and pushed there?

I have leaped and stood on the razor's edge, I have cried out and called you.

I have stubbed my little toe.

Aberefi Sempa my wife, Abuya.”

And God thundered, rumble! All the animals said, "Go on, go on." And it cried again, and God clouded over pitch-black! Then the animals lifted it up onto their shoulders, saying, "Go on again." And it raised it like that. Then God poured down heavily, splash! splash! splash! splash! and all the water came out. Then when this animal arose, it jumped into the water, splash! All the animals went in. When the mudfish said it was going in, they touched it, and it slept at the edge. That is why they say, "The mudfish that made its river, sleeps at the edge of the water."

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
