Jehovah has also helped me to see that despite my limitations, he can still use me. Therefore, I constantly ask him to help me to do his work. It is still hard for me to speak in public, but Jehovah helps me to encourage the brothers and sisters when I visit them.

Through all this, Claudine was a tremendous help and had a lot of patience with me. She supported me in handling any congregation assignments we were given and was thrilled to teach people about Jehovah. She adapted well to the tropical heat and to the various challenges of traveling oversight.

The first meeting I attended made me realize that Jehovah is our Creator and truly cares for his people. He also loves me very much. Jehovah helped me to understand that I need to respect those in authority and also control myself. I stopped making bad friends, which helped me to follow Jehovah’s teachings. I am just looking forward to the time when we will live forever, when Jehovah will give us everything we desire.—Mr. 10:29, 30.

Did you know?

What kind of work did people do in ancient Israel?

They used to do different kinds of work in this land

PEOPLE used to enter the city through gates. Because many people used to go through these gates, they performed various activities there. The Bible mentions city gates more than 50 times and shows the activities that were carried out there.

People used to sit and converse inside the city gates. The Hebrew word translated as city gate does not refer only to the city’s entrance gate. It also refers to the entire area around the gates where they used to build roofed places to sit. Some gates had a large area where many people could gather.—Ne. 8:1.

Merchants used to sell their goods near the city gates. Some of Jerusalem's gates were called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. It could be that they sold fish and sheep near these gates. That is why they were called by those names.—Zep. 1:10; John 5:2.

People bought and sold goods at city gates and also conducted other business. Abraham made an agreement before witnesses at a city gate. Boaz did the same at a city gate. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) When travelers visited another city and no one provided them a place to lodge, they would sleep at the square near the city gate. (Judg. 19:15-21) People would also go and sit at city gates to listen to news.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

The judges used to sit at the city gate to judge matters. People would be there listening to how they judged matters. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because people were there, it helped the judges to judge fairly. When the judges finished judging, the officers would do what the judges said. (Deut. 25:2) The judges were to judge fairly and also help the people do what was right.—Deut. 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Men who worshipped Jehovah in truth used to encourage the Israelites at the city gate to rely on Jehovah. Because people were at the city gate, prophets and other people would go there to speak to them. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu went to the entrance of the gate and told all the people that what Jehovah spoke against Ahab’s house had come true. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) King Hezekiah gathered the military leaders at the city gate and encouraged them to rely on Jehovah to help them.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that “true wisdom” cries out loudly “in the public square.” (Prov. 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, men who worshiped Jehovah in truth used to counsel people and help them make good decisions. Today, the Bible helps us to understand how wise Jehovah is. Jehovah's people also use the Bible to help people come to know Jehovah's wisdom.

THINGS IN THE BIBLE

How to Worship in this Way

The almond tree is among the many trees mentioned in the Bible. This tree was among the trees that blossomed toward the end of winter in the land of Israel. The flowers of the almond tree are white or somewhat pinkish. It also begins to blossom toward the end of January. In the Hebrew language, the meaning of the almond tree is a tree that "wakes early." How can we compare this tree with its Creator?

The figurative language used in Jeremiah 7:25 shows that Jehovah acted quickly to send his prophets to go and speak to his people. The March 15, 2011, Watchtower says: “Jehovah compared himself to an almond tree. This tree blooms early. In the very same way, Jehovah tells his people that if they do not do his will, disaster will come upon them. He made them know that everything he spoke would come to pass.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

Sometimes, the Bible compares the gray hair of older people to the white blossoms of the almond tree. (Eccl. 12:5) When Bezalel made the lampstand that was in the tent, he made its cups shaped like almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
