HOW THE ISRAELITES ACTED REGARDING THE INSTRUCTIONS THAT JEHOVAH GAVE THEM

3 At midnight before Jehovah delivered the Israelites from Egypt, Jehovah brought the tenth plague upon the Egyptians. Because Jehovah wanted to protect the Israelites, before he brought this plague upon the Egyptians, he told them to do something very important. He told them to take a male sheep or a male goat, kill it, and smear its blood on their doorposts. (Ex. 12:5, 7) When the angel Jehovah sent to kill the firstborn of humans and animals saw a house marked with blood on the doorposts, he would not kill the firstborn in that house. But the Israelites may have been a little afraid to carry out what Jehovah told them. The point was, the Egyptians gave special honor to male sheep. Therefore, the Israelites might have thought that if the Egyptians saw them killing these animals, they would become angry with them and even kill them. (Ex. 8:25-27) But the Israelites realized that it was very important to obey Jehovah, and therefore, ‘they went and did just as Jehovah had commanded.’ (Ex. 12:28) Jehovah also blessed them; he did not allow the angel to kill their firstborn.

WHAT WE LEARN FROM THE BAD EXAMPLE THAT THE ISRAELITES SET

6 The account of the Israelites shows us that we should examine ourselves to see whether we truly know why we obey Jehovah and those he has chosen to take the lead. Furthermore, we could ask ourselves, “Do I obey Jehovah even when others do not or when they are angry that I am doing so?” When the Israelites were leaving Egypt, Jehovah made them “find favor in the eyes of the Egyptians,” and the Egyptians gave them many things. AN ACCOUNT RECORDED IN THE BIBLE

Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts

The expression “the peace of the Christ” is mentioned in Colossians 3:15. What is “the peace of the Christ”? When a person becomes a disciple of Christ, he gains peace of mind and remains calm. When this happens, then he has obtained “the peace of the Christ.” We gain this peace when we come to know that Jehovah and his Son love us very much. —John 14:23, 27.

The apostle Paul encouraged the Christians in the Colossian congregation to let this peace we are talking about rule in their hearts or inner persons. How could they do that? In ancient Greece, during athletic contests, there was an umpire who saw to it that the contestants obeyed the rules of the contest, and he also awarded the prize to the winners. The Greek word Paul used, which is translated into Ga as “rule,” is related to the word used for this umpire. This shows us that just as in an athletic contest, whatever the umpire supervising the contest said was what the contestants had to follow, in the same way we should let “the peace of the Christ” control how we live.

For example, if we do not agree with a fellow Christian on a matter, we must let “the peace of the Christ” act as “judge” in the matter. If we have “the peace of the Christ,” it will not be difficult for us to see what we must do so that peace and unity will prevail between us and our brothers. In order for us to do this, at times, it will require that we give up what we prefer or that we forgive others. But if we do so, it shows that we are letting “the peace of the Christ” rule in our hearts. —Col. 3:12-14.
