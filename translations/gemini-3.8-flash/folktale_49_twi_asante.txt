Two rival wives cannot share one hearth

THEY say there were two rival wives; one had a child, and the other had none. They both lived in the same house; they shared one fire. One day, the one who had the child set off to go to a funeral, and as she was leaving, she gave her child to her rival wife, saying, "As for this, I will not be long, so take this child; if you look and the dew clears, take him and put him into it, and if the heat of the sun becomes intense, lift him up and go and put him in the shade." The rival wife said, "I have heard." She set off and went, and when the dew cleared, she took the child and went and put him into it. And the woman went and sat by the fire; she was cooking her food. Then the sun shone, and the child remained lying in it. And the sun shone intensely, and the child began to cry out:

Father is Animosuo, Cinema.

Did mother not tell you?

Cinema.

Dew will fall early, Sienima.

Adwoa Ananewa, Cinema.

I will manage on my own.

Cinema.

Korororodo, Cinema. Korororodo, Cinema.”

And the child's left hand had melted, and he lifted it:

"Father is our Honor, Cinema.

Did Mother not tell you that?

Cinema.

Dew will fall early, Sienima.

Adwoa Ananewa, Cinema.

I will relieve myself.

Cinema.

Deep silence, Cinema.

Clear and wide, Cinema.”

And the child's right hand was healed; and he lifted it up:

"Father is our Honor, Cinema.

Did Mother not tell you that?

Cinema.

Dew will fall early, Sienima.

Adwoa Ananewa, Cinema.

I will relieve myself.

Cinema.

Deep silence, Cinema.

Clear and wide, Cinema.”

And the woman did not answer. And suddenly, when she turned her eyes to look at the child, the child had turned into a mudfish. The woman lifted it up and put it into the soup; and she finished cooking the food. She took some of the food and set it aside for the child's mother on the veranda. Before long, the child's mother returned from washing clothes. She said, "Where is my child?" The woman said, "I have taken him to his father; go and take your food from the veranda and eat." The woman went and took the food; she uncovered the food and said, "Where did you get this fresh mudfish?" She said, "You went to wash clothes, and I bought it." The woman was eating; as she was eating, she said, "Where is my child?" Her co-wife said, "I have told you that he is with his father." When the woman finished eating, she went to her husband and said, "Give me my child, for I have not seen him today." The father said, "I have not seen him." The woman said, "Why are you treating me like a child playing with a sheep's tail?" The woman came to her co-wife; she told her, "I do not like this; why, am I not a child, that you play with me?" Her co-wife said, "Do you believe that I would go and find money from somewhere to buy the mudfish that you just ate? As for your child, it was raining and I put him there; when the sun shone, he said I should take him out, and I did not answer him; I looked, and he had turned into a mudfish; and I also used him to cook the food; and that is the whole story." The child's mother said, "I swear a great oath, from this very day, light your fire, and let me also light mine." That is why when two co-wives live in a house, this one has her own hearth, and that one has her own hearth.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
