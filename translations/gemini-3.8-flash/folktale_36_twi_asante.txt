How Kwaku Ananse came to marry Aso

There was a certain man born there, whose name was Akwasi Ninkunfuo (Akwasi the Jealous), and his wife was Aso, and he did not want anyone to see Aso, or anyone to speak to her. Therefore, he went and built a village and lived in it with Aso. Nobody went to that village. He, Akwasi Ninkunfuo, was a hunchback. For this reason, he knew that if he lived in town with her, someone would take her. Nyankopon (God) said to the young men, "Akwasi Ninkunfuo has been married to Aso for a very long time; he has had no child with her, therefore whoever can go and take Aso and make her pregnant, let him have her." All the young men made efforts to get her, but no one could. When Kwaku Ananse was there, he said, "I can go to Akwasi Ninkunfuo's village." Nyame said, "Truly, can you?" Ananse said, "If you will give me what I am looking for." Nyame said, "What?" He said, "Gunpowder and bullets." And Nyame gave them to him. Then Ananse took the gunpowder and the bullets to the villages, saying, "Nyankonpon says I should bring you this gunpowder and bullets, so that you kill meat, and the day I come here, I will come and collect it." He gave the gunpowder and the bullets to many villages until it was finished. All the people got some of the meat. One day Ananse went and wove a carrying-basket. Its length was about from here to there. Ananse took it to the villages where he had given them the gunpowder and bullets and went to collect all the meat they had killed. Father Ananse took the meat and carried the basket on his head, and set off on the road to Akwasi Ninkunfuo's village. He reached the stream from which Akwasi and his wife drank, and took some of the meat and threw it inside. Ananse made an effort and entered Akwasi Ninkunfuo's courtyard with the basket. And Aso saw him. She said, "Akwasi, come and look at something coming into the house here, what is it?" Ananse said, "It is Nyankonpon who sent me, and I am tired, and I will sleep here." Akwasi Ninkunfuo said, "I have heard, my master's servant." Aso said to Ananse, "Father man, some of your meat has fallen down in the courtyard." Ananse said, "Oh, if you have a dog, let it go take it and eat." Then Aso went and took it and gave it to her husband. Then Ananse said, "Mother, put food on the fire for me." Aso put some on, and Ananse said, "Mother, is it fufu that you are cooking, or mashed plantain?" Aso said, "Fufu." Ananse said, "Then it is too small; go and get a big cooking pot." Aso went and got a big one, and Ananse said, "Come and take meat." Then there were forty thighs of big animals. He said, "Take all this and put it in, and if you had a bigger pot, I would have given you meat to eat until your teeth fell out." When Aso finished the food, she dished it up, put it on the table, and drew water and set it there. Then Aso took hers to the fireside, and the men went and sat at the table. And they began to eat. They ate, and ate,

Kwaku Ananse said, “This fufu, there is no salt in it.” Akwasi told Aso, “Bring some.” But Ananse said, “No, a child is eating, and you tell them to get up and bring salt; get up and bring it yourself.” Akwasi got up, and Ananse looked into his pocket, took out a purgative, and put it into the fufu. Then he called Akwasi, saying, “Come, for I have brought some of the salt.” When Akwasi came, Ananse said, “Oh, I won't eat anymore, I am full.” Akwasi was eating, knowing nothing about it; he just kept on eating. When they finished eating, Akwasi said, “Friend, we live here with you, but we do not know your name.” Ananse said, “My name is, ‘Get-up-and-take-Aso’.” Akwasi said, “I have heard, Aso, have you heard this man’s name?” Aso said, “Yes, I have heard.” Akwasi got up and went to prepare one bedroom nicely.

He said, “Arise-and-eat-Aso, here is your bedroom, go and sleep there.” Ananse said, “I am the bather of the soul of the Supreme Being, I sleep in an open hall; ever since my mother and father gave birth to me, I have never slept in an enclosed room.” Akwasi said, “Where then will you sleep?” He said, “I would have slept in this open hall, but if it happens like that, then you and your God would have become equal, and it means that when I sleep in God's hall, I do not sleep in anyone else's hall in addition; therefore, it is right in front of this room in which you sleep that I will sleep.” The man took out a mat and laid it there for him. Akwasi and his wife went to sleep, and Ananse also slept there. Ananse lay there and set his face in quiet contemplation. As Ananse lay there, he took his lute, saying:

Akuamoa Ananse, today we will do something today.

Here is God, Nsia's child Ananse, today we will do something today.

God’s soul-washer Ananse, today I have seen something.”

Then he put down the sepirewa harp, laid it aside, and went to sleep. As he lay there, he heard Akwasi the Jealous Man calling him, "Father Man." Silence! "Father Man." Silence! Akwasi the Jealous Man was dying; the medicine had affected him, yet he called, "Father Man." Silence! Then he said, "Rise-and-go-eat-Aso." He replied, "Mm! Mm! Mm!" He said, "Relieve me." Ananse took his place, and Akwasi went elsewhere. Then Ananse got up and went into the room. He said, "Aso, have you heard what your husband said?" She asked, "What did he say?" He said, "He said I should get up and come lie with you." Aso said, "You lie." But she yielded to him, and he returned to sleep. That night, Akwasi got up nine times; Ananse also went to Aso nine times. When morning came, Ananse set off and left. About two months later, Aso’s belly had grown big. Akwasi asked her, "Why has your belly become so large, since you are sick, and I, who live with you, have not fathered a child?" Aso said, "Have you forgotten that man who came here, whom you told, ‘Rise-and-go-eat-Aso,’ and whenever that happened, he came and took me and made me pregnant?" Akwasi the Jealous Man said, "Get up, let me take you to him." They went to Nyankonpon’s town. Along the way, Aso gave birth. They arrived at Nyame’s town and reported the matter to Nyame, saying, "A servant of yours whom you sent came to sleep in my house, and he took Aso and made her pregnant." Nyankonpon said, "All my servants are roofing a house; go and point out the person." They went, and Ananse was sitting at the top. Aso said, "There he is." Then Ananse ran and sat in the middle. And Aso pointed, saying, "There he is." Then Ananse fell down from above to the ground. That day was a Friday. Ananse said, "I am purifying my soul, and you pointed your finger at me, causing me to fall down so that red clay has covered me." At that moment, the court attendants seized Akwasi the Jealous Man, and he slaughtered a sheep. When Akwasi the Jealous Man had finished slaughtering the sheep, he said to Nyame, "Here is the child; let Ananse take it." Ananse took Aso, but as for the child, it was killed, its flesh cut into pieces, and distributed widely. That is how jealousy came into the nation.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
