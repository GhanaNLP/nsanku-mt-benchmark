In the very same way, a person who is conscious of his spiritual need makes an effort to ask God to help him, and when He gives the guidance, he also listens. That person is also ready to accept the guidance that Jehovah gives to those who love Him.

13 Paul also said that we should let our speech be seasoned with salt. Paul did not mean that we should hide the truth or compromise it. Rather, he was showing that the words we speak should be tasty like food seasoned with salt so that, in a sense, when the listeners ‘taste’ it, it will be pleasing to them. (Job 12:11) But doing so is not easy. When it comes to actual food, we may think that if a certain food tastes good to us, it will taste good to others as well. In the same way, we might think that everyone likes the way we speak. But that is not the case. For example, because of their background, some people speak bluntly even when speaking with those older than them. But in the eyes of others, if someone speaks that way, they see him as disrespectful. Paul said that we should know how ‘we ought to answer each person.’ Therefore, we should not speak based on our background or upbringing; rather, the words we speak should be pleasing to our listeners.

15 Jesus’ example shows us that when someone knows the truth, it does not mean that he should tell people everything he knows all at once. In what way can we imitate Jesus? We must consider people's circumstances. Consider again the man whose family enjoys celebrating Christmas and Easter. We know that these celebrations originate from pagan religion, and God is not pleased with them. But imagine that you started studying the Bible with this man just a week or two before Christmas was celebrated. If you explain to him what the Bible says about celebrations that come from pagan religion, and you expect him to stop celebrating Christmas immediately, would that mean you are teaching him the truth with patience? Although some Bible students apply what they are learning quickly, there are others who take time before they are able to make changes. If we tell our Bible students what they need to know at the right time, that will be able to help them make changes.—Read Proverbs 25:11.

THINGS THAT WILL HELP YOU

Picture Bible Stories in Your Mind

The Bible is full of educational historical accounts. When we visualize the things that happened in the Bible in our mind, it will help us understand what we read very well, and we will benefit greatly from it. Consider some things that will help you to be able to do so.

• Read the text aloud. Doing so will help you to visualize the events clearly. If you are reading the Bible as a family, you can assign the characters whose stories you are reading to one another. That will make the account seem as if it is really happening.

• Put yourself in the situation. Try to feel the way the people you are reading about felt. For example, ask yourself: ‘Why did the person speak or act this way? If I were in that situation, how would I have felt?’

• Draw how things happened. When you draw Bible stories, it will help you visualize the events clearly, and you will never forget them, even if what you drew is not neat.
