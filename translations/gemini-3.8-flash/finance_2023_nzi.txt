About

This year will be one year that we will never forget for having brought immense hardship into the country's economic situation. The Coronavirus pandemic emerged in the country in the first four months of the year 2020 just as it happened everywhere in the world, which caused our trade and business activities to decline, leading to a decrease in the revenue the country was supposed to earn, and unforeseen debts were incurred that were not planned for in the budget, due to the fight against the pandemic.

We began our financial program at a time when we thought we were recovering from the various difficulties brought on by the coronavirus pandemic, but other challenges came upon us during that same period. This led to the loss of all the revenues we had gained from the year 2017 to 2019. Chief among these challenges were the Russia-Ukraine war and also certain key measures we took regarding revenue mobilization in the program, which were not approved on time. This drastically reduced our capacity to mobilize revenue. The international capital market closed its doors to us, our domestic revenue mobilization conditions became strained, our outstanding debt increased, driving up the cost of borrowing, and as a result, our cedi depreciated sharply.

Therefore, when external financial support from credit markets and foreign exchange earnings declined, import costs rose due to the country's high dependence on imported goods, and debt service payments continued to rise, the value of our Ghana currency depreciated. This has resulted in difficult living conditions for all of us, both the government and every individual. The rise in fuel prices, which led to price increases for many goods, has driven inflation to over 40.4 percent in October 2022.

YEAR 2023 AND BUDGET ESTIMATES MID-YEAR FISCAL POLICY REVIEW

Budgeting

19. The government will submit revenue mobilization measures that will collectively yield 1.35 percent of our GDP to Parliament for review and approval. This will support our revenue mobilization drive. Some of these measures include:

• The excise tax rates on cigarettes and other tobacco products will be revised to align with ECOWAS directives, and the excise tax on spirits will be increased above that on beer.

• VAT will be increased from 12.5 percent to 15 percent. Also, changes will be made to items that are VAT-exempt.

• The taxes collected at workplaces every month will be changed to be collected on a regular basis at the end.

• The tax charged on any amount of money withdrawn or deposited will be reduced from 1.5% to 1%. Also, the limit on the amount of money you can withdraw daily will be increased.
