WHEN THE EKUONA CLAN ARRIVED IN THE NATION

There was a certain girl called Abena; whenever her mother and her father offered her for marriage, she said, "No." And she told her father, "The day I see the man I love, on that day, when I come, if you are taking snuff, I will take the snuffbox and strike your forehead with it." The animal, the Buffalo, heard these words; he transformed himself into a human being and set out for the girl's town. When Abena saw him, she ran, and her father was taking snuff. She took the snuffbox and struck his forehead with it. On that day, the village elders told her father, "Our master's daughter has found the one she loves." So they went and brought the man home. The girl said, "This is the one I love." The man also agreed, and he married her. After spending three days there, he said he was going to his hometown, and the girl said she would go with him. The Queen Mother gathered things together, along with twenty-four people to accompany them. They set off and arrived at the outskirts of a certain town, and a rooster crowed. The people asked, "Have we arrived home?" The girl's husband said, "I have married a new bride; let me take her home first, and when she gets ready, she will come home to greet you." Then they set out and went on. They reached the foot of a certain giant silk-cotton tree. When they reached the foot of the silk-cotton tree, the man stopped; he said, "Abena, now the time has come, and this is how I am." Abena said, "How are you?" The man said, "I go pa! and I become gyiren!" and he turned into a buffalo. All the people said, "Eh! Eh, what did he say? He said he goes pa! and he becomes gyiren!" And all the people turned into buffaloes. Only one single person remained, and he fled back home. He went and reported to the chief the matter he had seen. The chief and the Queen Mother summoned a gathering; he reported to them how they had gone, and concluded, "He said he goes pa! and he becomes gyiren!" and he too became a Buffalo. Whenever someone tried to speak of it, they turned into a Buffalo. All the people of the town turned into Buffaloes, until only one old woman was left. Later on, a certain medicine man came to that town. He said, "Ah! Such a huge town, are there no people in it?" Absolute silence! Then he spoke again. Now an old woman said, "I am here." The medicine man said, "For what reason are you the only one living in this town?" The old woman said, "Now that you have come, go away so that I too may live in my place." The medicine man said, "You are a witch; it is you who have devoured all the people of this town, so I will kill you right now unless you tell." The old woman said, "If I must speak, go get a rope, come tie me up, and bind me to the fig tree." The medicine man came and tied her up, and bound her to the fig tree. Now, the old woman reported the whole matter to him and concluded, "He said he goes pa! and he becomes gyiren!" And the rope broke; the little old woman turned into a Buffalo and off she went. At this, the medicine man said, "So it was not the old woman who killed these people." Then he went and plucked herbs, crushed them, put them into a brass basin, and poured water into it; he dipped his whisk into it; and he made his medicine-carrier carry it. He set out into the forest. He looked and saw the buffaloes standing around, so he dipped the whisk into the medicine and sprinkled it over them, and they turned back into human beings again. Only the real Buffalo remained. And people came to the house. That is to say, the Ekuona clan came into the town; and it was because of Abena's marriage that she had entered into.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
