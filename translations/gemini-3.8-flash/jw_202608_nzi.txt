In ancient times, what was done at the city gates?

The city gates were not merely passageways to go through. Because many people passed there, it allowed for many different activities to take place there every day. The Bible mentions city gates more than 50 times, and it talks about the things that were done there.

People gathered there. The Hebrew word translated “gate” does not mean only the gate at the entrance to the city. It can also refer to the city gate area where various markets were set up. In some places, just inside the gate, there was a large open square where many people could gather.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants traded there. Some of the city gates of Jerusalem were called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. Perhaps they were named that way because of the things that were sold there.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People did business there and conducted their own other affairs there. Abraham and Boaz and others made agreements before witnesses at the city gates. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) If travelers did not find anyone to take them home in the evening, they slept in the public square. (Judg. 19:15-21) People also went to the city gates to hear the news of what was happening.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

Judges used to try cases there. They handled cases in front of the people gathered there so that they would hear what had happened and the outcome. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because many people were there, it helped the judges to render fair judgment. If the judges decided

Faithful ones encouraged the Israelites who gathered at the city gates to trust in Jehovah. A Tree That Bears Early Figs

The almond tree is among the some 30 different trees mentioned by name in the Bible. When winter ends, it is among the first trees to bud. By the end of January, people can see it producing pink or white flowers. The Hebrew name for the almond tree means "the tree that awakens early," and it fits it well. Does what this tree does resemble what its Creator does?

If you look at the context of Jeremiah 7:25, Jehovah spoke of himself as getting up early and sending his prophets. The March 1, 2011, Watchtower stated: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakes’ early, so Jehovah figuratively ‘kept getting up early’ to send his prophets to warn his people about the consequences of disobedience. He would not rest—he ‘would keep awake’—until his prophetic word was fulfilled.” —Jer. 1:11, 12.

Also, the Bible compares the gray hair of older people to white almond blossoms. (Eccl. 12:5) And likely because its blossoms are so beautiful, when making the furnishings of the tabernacle, the cups on the lampstand that held oil were made like almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
