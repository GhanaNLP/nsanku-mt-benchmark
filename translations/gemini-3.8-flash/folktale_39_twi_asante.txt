ELEPHANT was there, and said he wanted someone to play the game of akoto-wisie with him. All the animals said, "We cannot." Kwaku Ananse was there and said, "As for me, I can, but what I say to you is that if it is you who will kill me, you must bury me in a cave." Elephant said, "I have heard, and I will come and strike you for seven days, and you too will strike me for seven days." Kwaku Ananse went and dug up a yam, put it in the fire and roasted it, and went and cleared a farm by the side of the path. Whenever Ananse weeded, he took a piece of the yam and went to peel it at the farm. He looked and saw that Royal Antelope was passing by. But talk of his hunger, for a great famine had come. As he was passing by, Ananse threw the yam into the path. At once Royal Antelope picked it up. He said, "Father Ananse, with such a famine having come, is this what you bring to the farm?" Ananse said, "There is no shortage of food in my house at all." Royal Antelope said, "I will go with you to get some." Ananse arrived home with him; he told Royal Antelope, "There is a certain man who brings me food at night; so go and sleep there in the doorway, and when he comes he will say, 'Asikoto,' and you too respond, 'I receive it with my head.' And when you say that, he will drop some of the food there, and you take it." Royal Antelope said, "I thank you, Aku; truly Father Ananse, I have never seen a person like you before." Royal Antelope went and slept in the doorway. It was not long before Elephant arrived. He said, "Asikoto." Royal Antelope said, "I receive it with my head," and Elephant struck him, bam! and Royal Antelope died. Elephant left. When day broke in the morning, Ananse took Royal Antelope and gave him to Aso, who made soup for him. Then Ananse went to Elephant to greet him good morning. Elephant said, "Are you well?" Ananse said, "I am well." Ananse set off and came to his house. He took some of the yam and went to the farm again. He looked and saw Maxwell's Duiker passing by, and he threw the yam down. Maxwell's Duiker said, "Eh! Father Ananse, with such a famine having come, is this what you bring to the farm?" Ananse said, "If you want some, come so that we may go to my house and I will give you some." Maxwell's Duiker went with Ananse. Ananse said, "Sleep here in the doorway, and tonight my servant will bring food, and when he arrives here, he will say, 'Asikoto,' and you too say, 'I receive it with my head'." Maxwell's Duiker said, "I thank you, Aku, that you would do this." Maxwell's Duiker went and slept there. In the night Elephant came. He said, "Asikoto." Maxwell's Duiker said, "I receive it with my head." And he struck Maxwell's Duiker, and he fell, thud! And Elephant left. When day broke in the morning, Ananse took Maxwell's Duiker and gave him to Aso, and he set off and went to Elephant's house to greet him good morning. Elephant said, "Okuamoa, are you well?" Okuamoa said, "Very well!" And they conversed a little, and Ananse asked for permission to leave and came to his house. Then Ananse did the very same thing to Bushbuck and Black Duiker, Red River Hog Asamoa, Bongo, and Buffalo. Then it was Ananse's turn to go and strike Elephant. And he went to a blacksmith and forged a large iron peg; and he forged a crowbar to go with it. At night he set off and went to Elephant's house. Ananse said, "Asikoto!" Elephant said, "I receive it with my head." Ananse placed the iron peg on the crown of his head, and struck it with the crowbar, bam! bam! and it sank in. And Ananse came to his house. When morning came, Elephant told his wife, "I have a headache," and his wife said, "For shame, this is disgraceful; even Ananse was beaten for seven days, yet his head did not ache, and he did not die, and you are the one saying this; go and greet Ananse, just as he also did." Elephant braced himself and went; he reached Ananse's place. Ananse said, "Mighty one, are you well?" Elephant said, "Oh! I am well." Ananse said, "Good news." Ananse went and tapped the palm wine again. In the cool of the evening, he set out and went to Elephant. He said, "Asikoto." Elephant said, "I receive it with my head." Ananse placed the palm wine there and struck it with the iron bar, pam! pam! Ananse returned to his house. Now, while he was there, a messenger came and said, "Ananse, the elders of the Elephant family say I should come and tell you that your friend Elephant lay down to sleep and did not wake up; therefore come and split open a rock and put him inside it." Ananse said, "No, that is not how we arranged it. We said that whoever died, the other one would cook him and eat him." The messenger went and gave the report to Elephant's relatives. They said, "Go and bring him right now." The messengers came to Ananse and said, "They say come right now." Ananse said, "Wait for me." Ananse went into the bush, cut a tree, and carved it into a wooden figure of a man; and he carved a gun and placed it in its hand, gave it to Aso, and said, "Take this ahead and place it near where they told me to go and split the rock, and stand there too, and when I come, call out to me." Aso picked it up and went. Ananse also set out with the messengers and went. Ananse arrived before the Elephants, and they put a mattock, a hoe, an axe, and a knife into his hands. They said, "Get to work." And they led Ananse to a very large rock. They said, "Split it open, dig a pit, and put Elephant inside it." Ananse went there, pe! pe! pe! While Ananse was there, he called out, "Yes, oh!" He said, "Elephants, have you heard someone calling me?" The Elephants said, "Be quiet, you are looking for a way to run away." Ananse said, "I have heard," and he went back to his work, pe! pe! pe! Then he responded again, "Yes, oh!" He said, "Someone is calling me." The people said, "We will not let you go." So it went on, seven times. Then at last they said, "Go and listen, and come back right away; if you run away, we will catch you." Ananse went to where the wooden figure was. Then Ananse came running, kiri! kiri! kiri! The Elephants said, "Ananse, what is the matter?" He said, "Eh! you must leave, for there is a hunter standing over there; and he says I should move aside so that he does not shoot you with his gun. That is why he kept calling me and calling me to move aside." And when the Elephants looked toward the bush, they saw that indeed someone was standing there, and they took to flight. Ananse said, "Father hunter, kill them for me." The Elephants ran away; that was to the plains. And they left behind the dead Elephant, and Ananse took him and ate him. That is how the Elephants came to live in the plains, whereas formerly the Elephants lived in the forest.

This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some to eat and let the rest return to me.
