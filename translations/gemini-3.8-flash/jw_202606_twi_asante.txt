I Have Served Jehovah for About 70 Years in Cuba

GUSTAVO JOSEPH'S STATEMENT

I was born in the year 1947 on the beautiful island of Cuba. Where the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet, that is where Cuba is located. I was the eldest child, and my parents had two girls in addition to me. All five of us lived in a village; it was called Esmeralda.

I remember very well that there was peace in our village. We lived there in the village with some of our relatives; my grandparents, my father's brothers, and his sisters. As for what we would eat, we got it, and we were also happy.

When I was about five years old, my parents began studying the Bible with Walton Jones. Brother Walton was really zealous for preaching; he would travel for about ten hours to come to our village to preach. Whenever he came, many family members would gather at my grandfather’s house, and we would converse about the Bible with him for many hours. My parents, my father’s brother Pedro, and my aunt Ela were very pleased with what they were learning, and before long they were baptized to become Jehovah’s Witnesses. Now Ela is approaching 100 years old, yet she is still a pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, Jehovah’s Witnesses in Cuba had the freedom to worship Jehovah without hindrance. What people really knew us for was going to people’s homes to preach the good news; when we went, we carried our bags full of literature. As for walking, we did a lot of it! Whenever I remember the zeal with which we served Jehovah in my youth, I feel very happy. Those were good times, but bad times were also looming ahead.—2 Tim. 4:2.

After my father died, my mother decided that our family should go and live with her brother; he lived in a village called Lombillo. We loved our grandparents very much, but we had to leave them and the rest of the family behind. When we went, we did not stop serving Jehovah.

Looking at Galatians 4:24, which two covenants was the apostle Paul talking about?

■ The apostle Paul spoke about the relationship that existed between Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar. Afterward, he stated: “These things stand as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24.) Previously, we understood the two covenants to be the Law covenant and the new covenant. However, after reexamining this matter, we have seen the need to make an adjustment. It is likely that Paul was speaking about the Abrahamic covenant and not the new covenant. Let us consider briefly the reasons why we have made this adjustment.*

Paul said that the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai in 1513 B.C.E. corresponds to Hagar. (Gal. 4:25; Ex. 19:5, 6) No sinful human could keep that Law perfectly. So the Law reminded the Israelites that they were slaves to sin. Furthermore, this covenant could help the nation of Israel to identify the Messiah, because the Messiah was the only one who could keep the Law perfectly. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, sinful humans could then be freed from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) Once Jesus fulfilled God's purpose, the Law was no longer needed.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul also referred to Sarah as a “free woman,” and as for her son, he “was born through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise, that was the Abrahamic covenant. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) It was Jehovah and Abraham who made that covenant; it is recorded in Genesis 22:18. Jehovah said to Abraham: “And by means of your offspring all nations of the earth will obtain a blessing for themselves because you have listened to my voice.”

The Abrahamic covenant added to the promise Jehovah made in Eden. (Gen. 3:15) This covenant made it clear that the “offspring” he spoke about would come from Abraham. Paul explained that Jesus Christ is the primary “offspring.” However, “those who belong to Christ,” numbering 144,000 who will rule with him in his Kingdom, are also part of that offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) Because of the promise Jehovah made to Abraham, those who accept Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings.

Why did Paul say that “all these things stand for something”? He wanted to show that if the Jews of his day insisted on relying strictly on the Law, then they were like Hagar; they would remain slaves forever. But if they accepted Jesus Christ, who is the primary “offspring” of Abraham, they would become like Sarah, free forever. They would gain true freedom from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) However, if they did not accept Jesus, it meant they did not understand the Law covenant, because the purpose of the Law covenant was to help them recognize the Christ.

Many of the Galatians to whom Paul was writing this letter were not Jews, so the Law did not apply to them or bind them. Yet some of the Jews strongly insisted that the non-Jewish Christians must observe the Law. That included the law concerning circumcision. Paul explained that it was not wise at all for them to continue observing a law that had made them “slaves,” because Christ had given them “freedom.”—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

A BIBLE TRUTH

Fishing Nets Used in Ancient Times

The Bible makes us understand that some of Jesus’ followers were fishermen. (Mar. 1:16-20) They used nets to catch fish in the Sea of Galilee.

At that time, if someone wanted to make a fishing net, he would use strong ropes to weave something like a spiderweb with holes in it. If he wanted to catch small fish, he would make the holes in the net small. If he also wanted to catch big fish, he would make the holes in the net large. If small nets were to be used to catch fish, they attached something heavy like a stone or iron to it so that when it was cast into the water, it could sink to the bottom. But if the nets were large, they tied heavy things like stones or iron along the bottom. That made one part of the net sink to the bottom of the water; and they also attached something to it to make the other part of the net float on the surface of the water.

Rocks on the seabed could tear the nets. Sometimes, too, catching a large number of fish could cause them to tear. (Luke 5:6) Nets were very expensive. So almost every time the fishermen returned from the sea, they had to mend their nets. The Bible shows that James and John were “mending their nets”; they likely did this with needles and thread. (Matt. 4:21) Also, Luke states that the fishermen were “washing their nets” to remove debris and dirt. (Luke 5:2) Afterward, the nets were dried to keep them from rotting.—Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus invited men who knew how to fish with a net to become "fishers of men."
