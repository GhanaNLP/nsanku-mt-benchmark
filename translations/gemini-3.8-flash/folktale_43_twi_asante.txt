We are not alike, not alike at all

If only a fly would land on the dog, he would strike it hard! Hard!

A certain hunter had four dogs; one was named Ahuahua; another was named Ntaferentafere; another was named Abopo; and the other was named Amenekom. He used to hunt with his dogs. Whenever he went into the forest, he went with them. One day he went into the forest and saw a cola nut tree; and it had borne fruit so abundantly!

And he returned home to tell his wife the news, saying, "A certain cola nut tree that I have gone to see has borne so much fruit, extraordinary!" His wife said, "Go and pluck it, for you know we don't even have a single cola nut to chew, nor do we have any to sell." The hunter set out on the way, leaving the dogs at home, and went to where the cola tree was. And he climbed up and plucked some of the cola nuts. Then he heard nearby, "Twa! be! kukuru! kukuru! hu! pi!" This was Sasabonsam. He said, "Who has plucked my cola nuts like this?" The hunter said, "It is I!" Sasabonsam said, "For this thing you have done, I will kill you right now." The hunter said, "If you will kill me, let me call my dogs first." He said, "Call them, but if on the third time they do not come, then I will kill you." And the hunter raised his voice:

Vain boastings confront me, Flattery confronts me. Threatening confronts me, Swallowing me up confronts me.

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

And the dogs heard him clearly in the house, and they ran to the outskirts of the town. And the hunter raised his voice again:

Trouble surrounds me, flattery surrounds me.

Hardship surrounds me, poverty surrounds me.

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Now the dogs had heard; they were running fast at top speed, charging wildly! Sasabonsam said, "Call again, now it is the third time." The hunter raised his voice loudly, saying:

Trouble surrounds me, flattery surrounds me.

Hardship surrounds me, poverty surrounds me.

Please, please, please,

Swamp!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

Alas, alas, alas, Otekyem!

His mouth had not finished speaking when the dogs appeared. The hunter said, “Ahuahua, here is Sasabonsam, scent him out.” And Ahuahua scented him out. He said, “Ntaferentafere, lick him.” He said, “Abopo, tie him up.” And Abopo tied up his throat; and Sasabonsam fell down, kum! And Amenekom took him and swallowed him. The hunter and the dogs gathered the cola nuts and came home. When the hunter arrived, he cooked a bowl full of groundnut soup and set it down by the dogs; he drew water and put it there, and went to his wife to eat. When he finished eating, he went outside and sat under the shade tree. Then the hunter’s servant, the fly, went and saw the food, lifted it, and placed it on the scaffold. The dogs came from outside, and they were hungry. Ntaferentafere said to the others, “Have you seen what we have done for our master, yet he has not even given us food to eat, so let us go and kill him.” They said, “You are not lying, you are not lying.” Ntaferentafere went outside and licked the hunter, and the hunter played with him; and Ntaferentafere went away. Then Ahuahua also came; and he scented the hunter; and he also went away. When Abopo came, he tied up his throat, and Amenekom also came and swallowed him. Now the dogs came home; they looked about the open room, and they saw soup dripping there. They said, “Fly, where is the food the hunter left for us, has he eaten it?” The fly said, “I have not seen it at all.” When Ahuahua raised his eyes, he saw that the food was sitting up there. He said, “Father! we have killed our master for nothing, for look, here is the food he set down for us, and it was none other than the fly that took it and placed it there.” The fly took off from there and flew away. That is why, if a dog is sitting down and a fly settles on him, he seeks to catch it.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
