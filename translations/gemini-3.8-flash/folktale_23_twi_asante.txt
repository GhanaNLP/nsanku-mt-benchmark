We are not alike, not alike at all

IF THIS IS DONE, SICKNESS IN THE NATION WILL BE REDUCED

KWAKU ANANSE was there, and he went to Nyankonpon and said, “Nana, take your sheep, Kra Kwame, and give it to me to kill and eat, and I will go and bring you a beautiful woman.” Nyankonpon gave him the sheep, and Ananse set off, came to his village, and killed the sheep and ate it. Ananse went to a certain village. In that village, there was not a single man; they were all women. Ananse married all the women and lived there with them. One day, a hunter came there. When the hunter went back, he went and told Nyankonpon, “Ananse, that sheep of yours that he took, he went and killed it and gave it to some women to eat, and he has married them.” Nyankonpon said, “Is it true?” The hunter said, “Nana, it is true!” Nyankonpon dispatched hunters to go to the village and bring all the women who were there. When the hunters went, they met the women and took all of them, leaving behind only one woman who was sick, and brought them to Nyankonpon. Ananse said, “You who are left behind, what can I do with you? You cannot do anything for me.” The sick woman said, “Go and bring a calabash bottle.” Ananse went and brought a calabash bottle. She said, “Wash my body and put it into this calabash bottle!” Ananse washed her body and put it into the calabash bottle. This woman became very beautiful; her like was not in the nation. Ananse married his thing. Then the hunter came again and saw the woman. He went and told Nyankonpon, “Ananse is making a fool of you; it was ugly women he brought to you, but as for the beautiful one, he has kept her.” Nyankonpon dispatched hunters to go to Ananse and bring the woman to him. They went and told Ananse. He said, “And as for me, does he not want me?” The hunters said, “Nyankonpon said that it is the woman we should bring.” Ananse said, “There she is sitting, take her away.” Then Ananse went and took the calabash bottle into which he had put the woman’s sickness, covered it tightly with a piece of cloth, gave it to his son Ntikuma, and Ananse shouted, saying:

Y'odende dende den, Y'odende den.

Aso Ya, oh!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

You are being greedy for nothing!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

Your chimpanzee arm!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

Is this Yaa?

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

Your bowleg!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

You are as sour as unripe plantain!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.

Your feet are broad like a slave's!

Y'odende dende den, Y'odende den. Your head is like a cow!

Y’odende dende den, Y’odende den.”

Ntikuma said: “A beautiful child, a beautiful child.”

Then Afudotwedotwe and Nyiwankonfwea danced. Crow ran off and told Nyankonpon, "This play of Ananse's is something that befits you, not Ananse." Immediately Nyankonpon sent messengers to Ananse, telling him to bring his play. Ananse said, "This play of mine is played in the harem, and if Nyankonpon agrees, then I will bring it." They went and told Nyankonpon. He said, "It does not matter, let him bring it." Ananse took it to the harem, and Nyankonpon came and danced, and all his wives came and danced. Then it was left to the sick woman. Because she had seen that it was the gourd containing her sickness that Ananse had stretched out, she said she would not dance. Now, however, Nyankonpon commanded her; so she came; and as she was about to dance, Ananse lifted up the gourd and struck the child with it, and the sickness went, splash! That is how cancer, stomachache, headache, leprosy, guinea worm, smallpox, yaws, epilepsy, dysentery, and madness came into the world. Formerly there was no sickness in the world; it was God who caused Ananse to go and bring sickness into the world.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some away and bring some back.
