How to Listen to Jehovah With All Our Heart

‘Become obedient from the heart.’—ROM. 6:17.

THERE are some who, if they obey an elder or someone in authority, do so because of what they will gain from him. Others, too, obey only when they see that people are watching them, or when they might face legal consequences. For example, the religious leaders in Jesus’ day pretended to keep God's law so that they might receive praise from men. Today, some do the same. (Matt. 23:5) But as for us, one reason we obey Jehovah is that the guidance he gives us helps us in every way. (Isa. 48:17, 18) Yet there is another reason, the very main reason why we obey Jehovah.

2 The primary reason we obey Jehovah is that we love him very much. (Matt. 22:37, 38) It is because of the love we have for him that we ‘obey from the heart.’ (Rom. 6:17) So whether we understand Jehovah’s direction or not, whether someone is watching us or no one is around, we follow it because we love him. But the fact is, our heart is deceitful, so it is not always easy for us to follow Jehovah’s direction. In this study, we will look at two Bible examples that will help us to obey Jehovah from the heart at all times. We will first consider how the Israelites acted when Jehovah delivered them from Egypt. After that, we will consider a fine example set by King Jehoshaphat of Judah.

A BIBLE STORY

Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts

Colossians chapter 3 verse 15 speaks about the “peace of Christ.” What kind of peace is it? It is the inner calm that one receives because he is a disciple of Christ. What gives him this inner calm is that he knows that Jehovah and his Son, Jesus, love him.—John 14:23, 27.

Apostle Paul encouraged the brothers in the Colossian congregation to let this peace rule in their hearts or govern their hearts. How were they to do it? The Greek word Paul used is similar to the word used to describe a referee officiating in a competition. Just as a referee has authority over a game, so also we must let the “peace of Christ” have control over our minds and our hearts.

For example, if a dispute arises between us and a fellow Christian, we should let the “peace of Christ” guide us in what we should do. If we let the “peace of Christ” rule in our hearts, it will help us know what to do so that peace and unity will prevail between us and our brothers. For us to be able to do this, we need to put aside our own preferences and feelings, and ensure that we do not let anything destroy the relationship between us and our brothers. If we do this, it means that we have let the peace of Christ rule in our hearts.—Col. 3:12-14.
