IF THE DEEDS OF GOD ARE PERFORMED, THEY WILL BE CALLED TALES

KWAKU ANANSE went to Nyankonpon so that he would let him buy the Sky-God's stories. Nyame said, "Will you be able to buy them?" Ananse said, "Ei! I can." Nyame said, "Great towns like Kokofu, Bekwai, Asumengya have come and were not able to buy them, and will you, Kwafwiaban, be able to?" Ananse said, "What is the price paid for them?" Nyame said, "They are not bought for anything except Onini the Python, Osebo the Leopard, Mmoatia the Fairies, and Mmoboro the Hornets." Ananse said, "I will bring all these things, and I will add my old mother, Nsia, to them." Nyame said, "Go and bring them." Ananse came home and informed his mother, saying, "I want to buy the Sky-God's stories, and Nyame says I should bring Onini, Osebo, Mmoatia, and Mmoboro, and I said I would add you to them and give them to Nyankonpon." Then Ananse asked his wife, Aso, "How shall we get Onini?" And Aso told him, "Just go and cut a palm-branch and cut a climbing-vine with it, and bring them." And Ananse brought them. Then Aso said, "Take them to the riverside." And as Ananse was taking it there, he said, "It is longer than him; it is not longer than him; you lie, it is longer than him." Ananse said, "There he lies over there." Onini said, "What is it?" Ananse said, "Is it not Aso who is arguing with me, that this palm-branch is longer than you, and I say she lies." Onini said, "Come, come and measure me." He placed the palm-branch beside Onini. He said, "Stretch yourself out." And Onini stretched himself out, and Ananse laid the vine over him, tie! tie! tie! until he reached the head. Ananse said, "Fool, I am taking you to receive the Sky-God's stories." Ananse took him and gave him to Nyame. Nyame said, "I have received it; there remains what remains." Ananse returned and came to inform his wife, saying, "There remain the Mmoboro." His wife said, "Look for a gourd, put water in it, and take it along." Ananse went into the forest and saw the Mmoboro hanging there, and he poured out some of the water and sprinkled it over the Mmoboro. Ananse poured what remained over himself and cut a plantain leaf to cover his head. Then he said to the Mmoboro, "As it is raining like this, if you were to come and enter this gourd of mine, wouldn't this rain stop beating you; don't you see that I have covered myself with a plantain leaf?" And the Mmoboro said, "We thank you, Aku; we thank you, Aku." All the Mmoboro flew into the gourd, fom! Father Ananse corked the mouth. He said, "Fools, I have caught you; I am taking you to receive the Sky-God's stories." He took the Mmoboro and gave them to Nyame. Nyame said, "I have received it; there remains what remains." Ananse came back again and told his wife, saying, "There remains Osebo." Aso said, "Go and dig a pit." Ananse said, "Stop, for I understand." Then Ananse went to look for Osebo's trail and dug a very deep pit, covered it, and came home. The next morning, when Ananse went to look, there was Osebo lying in the pit. Ananse said, "Father's child! Mother's child! although I have told you not to drink alcohol, now truly you have gone to drink and gotten drunk, which is why you have fallen into the pit; if I say I will take you out, tomorrow when you see me, or if it is any child of mine, you will go and catch them." Osebo said, "Oh! I cannot." Ananse went and cut two sticks, placed one here, and placed one there. He said, "Put one of your paws here, and put your other paw here as well." And Osebo placed them there. As he stretched out, Ananse lifted a knife and swung it, gao into his head! Osebo fell down into the pit, fom! Ananse took a ladder, set it in the pit, and went to take Osebo out. When he took Osebo out and brought him up, he said, "Fool, I am taking you to buy the Sky-god's stories." He carried Osebo and gave him to Nyame. Nyame said, "I have received it; there remains what remains." Ananse came and carved an Akua Ba, collected rubber-tree sap and smeared it all over the Akua Ba; he mashed boiled yam, put some in the hands of the Akua Ba, and mashed some into an earthen bowl; he tied a string around the waist of the Akua Ba, and went and placed it beneath an odum tree where the mmoatia play. And one mmoatia came. It said, "Akua, may I eat some of this mashed yam?" And Ananse pulled the string, and the Akua Ba nodded its head. The mmoatia said to its sibling, "She says I may eat some." The sibling said, "Eat some, then." And it finished eating the food, and thanked her. It thanked her, but she did not answer. The mmoatia told its sibling, "When I thank her, she does not answer." The mmoatia's sibling said to it, "Slap her." And it struck her, pa! And its hand stuck to it. It said to its sibling, "My hand is stuck to it." The sibling said, "Use the one that is left and slap her again." And it struck her with it, pa! and that one too got stuck. And the mmoatia said to its sibling, "Both of my hands are stuck to it." The sibling said, "Push her with your belly." It pushed her, and its belly got stuck to it. Then Ananse came out and tied it up; he said, "Fool, I have caught you, I am taking you to buy the Sky-god's stories." And he took it home. Then Ananse said to his mother, Ya Nsia, "Rise up, let us go, so that I may add you to this mmoatia to go and buy the Sky-god's stories." He took them and went to Nyame. He said, "Nyame, here is the mmoatia, and here also is my old woman whom I spoke of." Nyame then called Kontire and Akwamu, Adonten, Gyase, Oyoko, Ankobea, and Kyidom. And he laid it before them, saying, "Great chiefs have come and could not buy the Sky-god's stories, but Kwaku Ananse has been able to buy them; I have received Mmoboro from him; I have received Mmoatia from him; I have received Osebo from him; I have received Onini from him; Ananse himself has added his mother to it; here are all the things lying down." He said, "Praise him." They said, "Eee!" Nyankonpon said, "Kwaku Ananse, from this very day, I give you the Sky-god's stories, completely! completely! completely! Let them no longer be called the Sky-god's stories, but let them be called Ananse stories." This my story that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
