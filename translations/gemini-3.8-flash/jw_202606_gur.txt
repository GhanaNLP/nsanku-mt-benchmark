I Have Served Jehovah in Cuba for About 70 Years

WHAT GUSTAVO JOSEPH SAID

I WAS born in 1947, on the island of Cuba. Cuba is located where the Caribbean Sea meets the Atlantic Ocean. I am the eldest child of my parents and have two younger brothers. My brothers, our parents, and I lived in the town of Esmeralda.

I remember that the soup we used to cook was delicious. We used to cook and taste it with many of our family members. We used to eat to our fill and also be happy.

When I was about five years old, Walton Jones was studying the Bible with my parents. He was zealous in the preaching work and would travel about ten hours to get to where we lived. Whenever he arrived, many people from our household would gather at my grandfather's house, and they would discuss Bible topics with him for many hours. My parents, my father's brother Pedro, and my aunt Ela loved what they were learning. It was not long before they were baptized as Jehovah's Witnesses. Ela is now about 100 years old and is still serving as a pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, Jehovah’s Witnesses worshipped Jehovah in Cuba without being harassed by the government. Because Jehovah’s Witnesses preached from house to house, the people of Cuba knew us very well. We used to have many books and magazines to give them. We also travelled on foot. My heart is filled with joy whenever I remember how Jehovah helped us “in every season.” At times, things were good, but later on, things became difficult for us.—2 Tim. 4:2.

Things were very difficult for us in Cuba

When I was about five years old, my father and one of his brothers went to a meeting in a town in Cuba. The sad thing was that on their way, they drank bad water and contracted typhoid fever. I remember that when they returned, the sickness caused my father's brother to become delirious, but he survived. But my father died. He was only 32 years old.

When my father died, my mother let us go and live with one of her younger sisters in the village of Lombillo. While there, we loved our family very much, yet we left them, our grandfather, and our grandmother to go. Despite all that, I, my mother, and my younger siblings continued to serve Jehovah.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS BY READERS

According to Galatians 4:24, what do the two covenants that the apostle Paul spoke about represent?

◼ The apostle Paul spoke about the situation of Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar. He said: ‘These things stand as a symbolic drama; for the two women mean two covenants.’ (Gal. 4:22-24) We understood that the two covenants were the Law covenant and the new covenant. But we have reexamined this matter and seen that an adjustment is needed. It appears that Paul was speaking about the Abrahamic covenant and not the new covenant. Let us examine the reason why he said that.*

Paul said that the Law covenant, which was established at Mount Sinai in 1513 B.C.E., corresponded to Hagar. (Gal. 4:25; Ex. 19:5, 6) Because the Jews were imperfect, they could not keep the Law completely. This helped them realize that they were sinners. This Law covenant helped the Israelites identify who the Messiah was, because only he could keep the Law completely. Since the Messiah was pure and died for mankind, he would rescue them from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) After Jesus died for us, the Law was no longer in force.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul explained that Hagar corresponds to Sarah. Sarah is “the free woman who bore her child through the promise.” (Ga. 4:23) This promise is the Abrahamic covenant. (Ga. 3:29; 4:28, 30) Jehovah spoke about this promise as recorded in Genesis 22:18. It says there: “Through your offspring [Abraham's descendant], all nations of the earth will be blessed, because you have listened to my voice.”

The Abrahamic covenant sheds clearer light on what Jehovah said in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) It helps us understand that the ‘offspring’ spoken of would be an offspring of Abraham. Paul explained that this ‘offspring’ refers to Jesus Christ. And the 144,000 who “belong to Christ” and will rule with him are also included as part of the offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) When the covenant God made with Abraham goes into effect, those who obey Jesus and belong to him will receive many blessings.

Why did Paul give this illustration? He gave this illustration to show that if the Jews of his time continued to follow the Law, they would be slaves just as Hagar was a slave. But if they believed in Jesus, who was Abraham’s true "offspring," they would be free like Sarah. That would save them from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) If they did not accept Jesus, that would show that they did not understand the purpose of the Law covenant. The reason the Law existed was to help them recognize the Christ.

Paul wrote these things to the Galatians, and most of them were not Jews. They also had not attempted to follow the Law. In the congregation, some Jews wanted those who were not Jews to follow the Law and be circumcised. Paul was saying that it would not be wise for them to do that. It was no longer necessary for them to follow the Law. They were already "free" because they believed in Jesus.—Ga. 5:1, 10-14.

THINGS IN THE BIBLE

Traditional Fish Traps Used In The Past

If you read the Bible, you will learn that Jesus' disciples were fishermen. (Mr. 1:16-20) They used to catch fish with nets in the Sea of Galilee.

If a person wanted to weave a fishing net, they would take cords or ropes to weave it so that it had meshes. Often, they would make the cords or the ropes from flax yarn. When fishermen wanted to catch small fish, they used nets with narrow meshes, and if they wanted to catch many or large fish, they used nets with wide meshes. They would attach heavy weights to the bottom of the small nets so that they could sink into the water. They would also attach heavy objects to the large nets so they could sink into the water, and they would add things like floats so that it would not sink completely.

Fishing nets were expensive. Rocks in the sea could tear the nets. Also, if they caught a lot of fish, the nets could tear. (Lu. 5:6) The Bible speaks of James and John that they were there “mending their nets.” They probably used needle and thread to mend them. (Mt. 4:21) Whenever fishermen finished fishing, they probably mended their nets. Luke showed that the fishermen were “washing their nets” to remove all the dirt from them. (Lu. 5:2) After that, the fishermen would spread their nets to dry. They probably did that so the nets would not rot.—Eze. 47:10.

Jesus told the men who were catching fish that they should be "fishers who will catch people," and that they should come and follow him.
