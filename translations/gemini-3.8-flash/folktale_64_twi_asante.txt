HOW ANANSE WENT UNDERWATER

OH, ANANSE! He married the daughter of Nyankonpon's mother, Nsia, and he told Nyame, "The day your mother Nsia dies, announce the funeral to me." Three days later, they came to tell him that Nyankonpon's mother, Nsia, was dead. Ananse’s relatives were Berekuo, Kwakuo, and Odwan. And Ananse said to them, "They have come to announce a funeral to me, so come and accompany me." Then Ananse sent word to Nyankonpon that he was coming to the funeral. And Nyankonpon also held an assembly just as an Omanhene would sit in state. And Ananse said, "I will come in the evening." Ananse folded a sack and put Berekuo, Kwakuo, and Odwan inside it, tied it around his waist, and wrapped his cloth over it. And he told the animals, "When I arrive before Nyankonpon, you must cry." And when he arrived before Nyankonpon, Berekuo struck up:

Kurukusu, ku! ku! ku!

Kurukusu, ku! ku! ku! it is."

And the monkey lifted it up:

"Kakum!"

And the Lamb lifted it up:

"Me!"

Kwaku Ananse wept. All the people said, "You have done well; one person with three cries." When it was over, God went and sought a nice house for him to stay in. The next morning God cooked delicious food and gave it to Ananse. When Kwaku Ananse saw the food, he said, "I will not give any to Berekuo, I will not give any to Kwakuo, I will not give any to Odwan, for I am the one who fasted." And Ananse ate it; he gave none to his relatives. And as he stayed there with them, God said, "We will observe the eighth-day celebration." But Odwan said, "Hunger is killing me; I will not go." Kwakuo and Berekuo said the same. So God went and sat in state, and he sent word to Ananse that he should come and perform the beautiful things he had performed that day, so that he and his elders might sit together. Ananse said, "I am sick." The next morning God said to Ananse, "You have disgraced me." And Berekuo, Kwakuo, and Odwan said, "You also starved us, so we are severing our family ties with you." Then Kwakuo and Berekuo went into the forest, and Odwan stayed at home; and Ananse jumped and went to stick to the ceiling out of shame.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
