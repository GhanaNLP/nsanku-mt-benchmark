What Can Help You to Have an Obedient Heart That Listens to Jehovah?

“Give your servant an understanding heart.”—1 KINGS 3:9.

Many people obey laws and commandments only because they do not want to be punished or because they know that people are watching them. When it comes to obeying God, some people do so just to make a name for themselves, like the religious leaders did in Jesus' day. (Matt. 23:5) But why do we obey God? One reason is that we know that everything he tells us to do is for our own good. (Isa. 48:17, 18) That is a good thought we have, but we also have an even more important reason why we should obey God.

2 Because we love Jehovah very much, we are ready to listen to him. (Matt. 22:37, 38) The love we have for God helps us to “obey from the heart.” (Rom. 6:17) Therefore, we apply Jehovah’s counsel even when people are not watching us or even if we do not understand why we should apply that counsel. But because we are imperfect, at times we may think that it is not easy to do these things. In this study, we will see two Bible accounts that will help us to always be ready from our hearts to listen to Jehovah. First, we will see what the Israelites did when God saved them from the hands of the Egyptians. Second, we will see what we can learn from King Jehoshaphat’s good example.

BIBLE WORDS

Let the Peace of Christ Rule in Your Hearts

Colossians 3:15 speaks about "the peace of the Christ." What is "the peace of the Christ"? It represents the inner peace of heart a person has when he becomes a disciple of Jesus. When that person realizes that Jehovah and his Son love him, he enjoys this peace.—John 14:23, 27.

The apostle Paul encouraged the members of the Colossian congregation to let this peace rule in their hearts or guide their conduct. But how could they do that? The Greek word Paul used for “rule” refers to an umpire or referee who oversees games or sports competitions so that they can be conducted properly to the very end. Likewise, it is only “the peace of Christ” that should guide us in what we ought to do so that everything will go very well for us.

For example, if a misunderstanding arises between us and our brother, we should let “the peace of Christ rule” and guide us on what we ought to do. Therefore, if we have “the peace of Christ,” it will easily direct us on what to do so that unity and peace will exist between us and our brothers. Sometimes it may require that we overlook certain things, sacrificing some of our personal desires in order to forgive others. But if we put forth every effort to do so, it shows that we are letting “the peace of Christ” rule in our hearts.—Col. 3:12-14.
