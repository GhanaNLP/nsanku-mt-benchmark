I Left Mother and Father So That Jehovah Would Train Me

THE WORK KEN KIUCHI DOES

When I went to Brooklyn Bethel in New York, U.S.A., I was assigned to work in the bindery where printed sheets were gathered and made into books. On my first day there, I was quite frightened. This was because the machines made a lot of noise, paper dust flew all over the air, and the smell of the petroleum used to clean the machines filled the entire place.

I enjoyed many things during my time at Bethel. One thing that touched my heart deeply was the young brothers and sisters who worked at that time. They did the very same work every day. Moreover, people did not praise them or reward them for what they did. Even so, whenever you saw them, they were happy. Looking at that, you would even say that hardships do not trouble them. When it is said that someone humbles himself and serves God faithfully, it was at that very time that the youth truly demonstrated it.

Over the years, Jehovah has trained me to become a more humble person. However, even with all his training, as someone who was a former Buddhist, I still didn’t know the true God.

FOUR TRUTHS GOD HAS SAID ABOUT TODAY'S WORLD

I was born in Chicago, Illinois, U.S.A. There were four children, and I was the eldest. My mother and my father moved from Japan to live in America, because they thought that life would be good for them there, and their children would also get a good education so that their future would be better.

HAS someone done something to hurt you so deeply that you feel resentful and it has ruined your relationship? If so, try to make peace with the person today. As you do, focus on the peace you wish to restore to the relationship instead.*

Let us consider this example. If someone's leg breaks and they go to the doctor, they can take care of it so that the leg heals. But if they do not go and just leave it alone, it can hurt them very much. If it is not treated well, they may not even be able to walk again.

Just as we quickly go to the doctor for treatment when our bone is broken, in the same way, when we realize that our relationship with a brother or sister is damaged, we must quickly mend matters with them. If we do not do so, it will cause us to harbor resentment against that person. This will cause our relationship with them to be completely ruined. However, Jesus said: “Settle matters quickly with him.” (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, go to your friend and tell them what they said about you, and when you are talking with them, try to be humble. But sometimes it is often very hard for us to do that, why? Let us look at two things that make us say so.

First, you may not want to talk about what happened at all. If that happens, it can be difficult to go to the person so that you can settle matters between yourselves. Something similar happened to Euodia and Syntyche. They were Christians during the time of the apostles. A problem arose between them that they were not quick to settle. So they kept living with it until the apostle Paul heard about it, even while he was in Rome. For this reason, he urged them to do everything they could to bring peace between them. (Phil. 4:2) If they humbled themselves, each speaking from the heart about how they felt, and forgave each other, it would enable them to become friends again.

Second, you might think that the fault was not yours. Therefore, it is rather your friend who should come to you. But know that what is important is that things become good between you and your friend, and not who is guilty or who is innocent. If someone breaks a bone and a doctor examines him, what the doctor focuses on is how the person will be healed. It is not asking questions about how the person got injured. In the same way, when you go to the person, it is not about finding out who is to blame. Rather, what matters to you is to settle matters between you so that there will be peace.

The Bible advises us, “Do not let the sun set while you are still angry.” (Eph. 4:26) Therefore, if you have a matter with a brother, talk it over with him quickly so that your relationship will not be spoiled. If you are able to do so, you will help promote “peace” in the congregation.—Eph. 4:1-3.

How to Give Encouraging Comments?

When we go to meetings, we have the opportunity to give comments that encourage our brothers and sisters. (Heb. 10:24, 25) Before we go to meetings, what can we do to enable us to give such comments when we attend?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask him to help you so that when you attend meetings, you are able to give comments that praise him, and also encourage the brothers and sisters.—Ps. 35:18.

• Make an effort not to read your answer directly from the book. If you do not read your answer from the book, but rather answer in your own words based on how you understand the matter, it will greatly encourage others.

• Keep your comments brief. Comments that are brief and simple are very encouraging. They also give others a chance to participate.—Prov. 10:19.

• Let your answer encourage others. Try not to use your answer to embarrass anyone or insult them.

Even if you raise your hand and you are not called on, you can still encourage your brothers and sisters. How can you do that? You can listen carefully to the answers they give, and after the meeting, you can let them know the specific thing you appreciated about their comment.—Prov. 10:21.
