BEGINNING

1. The 2021 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the Government's achievements in 2020 and its plans for revenue collection and projected expenditure for the year 2021. The theme of the 2021 Budget is "Completion, Consolidation and Continuation". This theme is appropriate because in past years, every new budget introduced many new projects to be started, diverting funds from ongoing works. This did not expand our revenue streams, but rather resulted in accumulated debt and delayed payments to many contractors who had completed their work. This caused the cost of every project to increase, as delayed payments for completed works led to interest charges on claims.

2. In the year 2021, the Government of Ghana intended to adopt a new path. Its intention was to put an end to the habit where a government begins a project without completing it before stepping down from office, and another person assumes office and abandons those old projects to also start their own new projects. The government promised the people that it would complete the projects that had been left halfway.

TAX COLLECTION METHODS

10. The government introduced tax collection measures in the 2021 Budget to ease development pathways and alleviate the hardships brought by COVID-19. The tax collection measures are as follows:

• COVID-19 Health Levy, a 1% increase in the National Health Insurance Levy and a 1% increase in the VAT rate;

• Sanitation and Pollution Levy (SPL) of ten pesewas (10 pesewas) on each liter of petrol or diesel under ESLA (Energy SEctor Levy ACT), to ensure that sanitation is maintained well to promote good living conditions and reduce premature deaths associated with poor sanitation;

• Energy Sector Recovery Levy (Delta levy) of twenty pesewas (20 pesewas) per litre on petrol/diesel under ESLA, to help pay the debts of the Power Purchase Agreement (Power Purchase Agreement) resulting from the plants standing idle or operating below their full capacity;

• a tourism development levy of 5% on gross profit, to support promotional activities in the tourism sector. This will be reviewed in 2024;

• The Minister for Finance and the Minister for Local Government shall jointly inspect the casino operating areas, consult with the relevant stakeholders in this country, and come up with multiple measures that will help improve the tax collection mechanisms in the casino operating sector; and

COVID-19 Pandemic Response

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that struck the world in late 2019 is one whose effects have surpassed all domestic and commercial activities in the world's economy. It has also disrupted economic affairs, prevents and restricts the lack of profits, and weakens economic growth. In addition, about 2.6 million people have lost their lives across the world.

14. In this Ghana, within a week, eleven (11) people were infected with this disease, and it rose up to 781 in the last two weeks of July 2020, and declined to 104 by the end of December 2020. And in the year 2021, new modes of infection emerged, and we also saw a figure of 99 in the first week of January, up to 880 by the week of February 5th.

15. In terms of measures to prevent and protect against this disease, the government has placed restrictions on Ghanaians and specific sectors of the economy such as hospitality, agriculture, health, travel, manufacturing, construction, finance, as well as education.

16. Transport workers, drivers and conductors of trotro and small informal transport groups also suffered during the restrictions on human movement. But although this restriction on public movement was necessary in order to prevent the disease, it also reduced the movement and availability of many goods.

17. When comparing us with our peers in this West African region, the bold measures taken by the Government of Ghana to prevent and curb the spread of the virus have been praised internationally, and this encourages us to do even more.

BUDGET KEYWORDS

1D1F (One District One Factory): The construction of factories and workplaces is what will usher this country into large-scale industrialization, which is great development for our country. Its aim is to provide jobs for Ghanaians across every part of this country.

The study agreed that: This is when the government carries out its work openly, allowing the citizens of this country to know all the secrets, successes, and challenges of the work. This can be money or any state resource that the government manages.

Allocation: it is the process through which resources are made to reach workplaces such as ministries and government agencies that we call MDAs.

Mitigation: it is a way followed to reduce the harm of a problem.

BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): An acronym used to bring together areas of knowledge that encompass both science and mathematics. This term is used in the academic field when selecting an academic program for a school to promote scientific knowledge.

Budget: A budget is a way that shows where money will come from and what that money will be spent on. For example, a family (household) can make their budget to show how much money they have, and show how they will spend it on rent, electricity and water bills, and food. If the money will not be enough, the family will discuss seeking more or taking a loan.

Budget deficit: this happens when the Government's expenditure exceeds the taxes collected.

Capital expenditure: it is the money the Government spends on major projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, culverts, vehicles, water, and machines.

Payment of workers' debts: This includes workers' salaries and all allowances that accompany monthly salaries. Or retirement benefits.

Development Partners (DPs): This refers to countries or organizations that support the Government to fulfill national development goals. Examples of DPs include global bodies such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, and African Development Bank (AfDB), or bilateral relations between countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, as well as the United States of America (USA).

End-of-year clothing sale: A clothing sale that takes place in December, the last month of the year.

Excess Expenditure: It occurs when the Government spends money beyond the approved budget. The Members of Parliament must approve it.

ESLA : Energy Sector Levies Act.

Evacuation: The return of people or their removal from a place.

FREE SHS: Free SHS is a policy that the Government of Ghana introduced during the tenure of President Nana Akufo-Addo in September 2017, which allows any student qualified for SHS to attend without paying a pesewa.

Fiscal Responsibility Act: This is a law that directs how the Government can balance expenditure and debt.

Grants: This is assistance that financial institutions give to the Government, which does not need to be repaid.

Grants to Government Units: This consists of monies the Government must pay into operational sectors such as the District Assemblies Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund, and the National Health Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is the value of goods and services within a country over a specific period.

Gross International Reserves: This measures the value of a country's foreign exchange reserves or its ability to purchase goods or services internationally within a given time. It also shows the value of a country's currency compared to foreign currencies.

Inflation: How the prices of goods and services increase over a period of time.

IPEP (Poverty Eradication Initiative): The Poverty Eradication Initiative is a programme introduced by the NPP Government to eradicate poverty and reduce disparities between urban and rural areas. Its aim is to provide rural communities with their basic needs to lift them out of poverty.

Loan Repayment: These are monies paid to countries, banks, or financial institutions that lent money to the Government to carry out development projects. These lenders can be Ghanaians or foreigners.

Macroeconomic targets: This is a measure used to assess a country's economy. Examples include economic growth, prices of goods and services, and changes in the workforce

MSME (Micro And Small Buisness Developmet Programme): MSME is a business where they can employ a small number of people, and their revenue also has a limit and will not exceed that limit.

Miscellaneous Provision: This is a section of the contract that also has parts, and all the matters contained within it are difficult to recall.

Mitigating Measures: This is an action taken to reduce or eliminate the potential hardship that would have existed.

NABCo (Nation Builders Corps): NABCO is an initiative introduced by the Government of Ghana to create good jobs for unemployed university graduates, in order to reduce their social hardships and also provide them with skills that will enable them to work to strengthen the country's economy.

NBSSI (SMALL SCALE ENTERPRISES): Small Scale Enterprises are non-profit ventures under the country's Ministry of Trade, aimed at promoting the growth and development of small businesses known as SMEs.

Non-Oil Real GDP: It is the measurement of all economic activities of a country, excluding the petroleum business.

Non-Tax Revenue: The monies the government obtains that are not collected as taxes. Examples include monies people pay to obtain licenses, fees people pay for using government facilities, as well as profits generated from government enterprises.

Outstanding Obligations: The mandatory funds that the Ministry of Finance must disburse to MDAs, as well as the existing 2016 arrears to be paid to Government departments.

Primary balance : It is the difference between government tax revenues and non-interest expenditures (debt payment is excluded from this). This is calculated as a percentage of GDP.

PFMRD (Public Financial Management Reforms Project): The objective of the PFMRD is to improve budget implementation, financial control, and enhance the transparency of Government expenditures. The project is designed to strengthen financial compliance, ensure equitable resource allocation, promote efficient operational delivery, and ensure the effective enforcement of financial regulations.

PFI: Private Finance Initiative.

Tax: It is all the money that the Government gets locally or from abroad.

RFJ (Rearing for Food and Jobs): A new project of the Government of Ghana that will enhance the value of livestock rearing, ensure the constant availability of young livestock, and create many jobs that will become the occupation of many people.

Social Benefits: The money that the government pays to electricity and water companies, as well as on fuel, which is paid on behalf of people who use it for their needs.

Transparency: This is when the government provides information about how it carries out its work so that citizens know how things are going.

TLM (Teaching and Learning Materials): These are all things such as living and non-living things, and humans and non-humans, that a teacher can use in teaching and learning to help achieve their objective.

Use of Goods and Services: The money government spends on paying people who work for it or on behalf of the public.

Value Added Tax (VAT): VAT is a tax that the Government places on goods and services at the place of their production or execution.
