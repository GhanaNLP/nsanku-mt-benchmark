We are not alike, not alike at all

There is no foolish child in the nation

There was a certain woman who had given birth to four children. The eldest among them was exceedingly foolish. He did not know how to do anything at all, so his siblings said, “We cannot live with him, therefore he must leave our house.” And pardon the expression, the fool went and built a village and lived in it. His village was nothing but sand. And he carved a swing, playing on it. The mother and her remaining children lived together. One day, she got up, intending to go to the farm, and saw a child sitting in a tree hollow. She said, “Who is that?” The child did not answer her. The woman went there to examine him properly; she took the child by the hand, saying, “Let us go home.” And the child got up, followed her, and came home. The woman said to her children, “Look at the beautiful child I have found, and give me a knife so that I may shave his hair.” The woman did not know that the child was a mmoatia (fairy/dwarf), which was why his hair had grown like that. And she took a knife and shaved off all the hair from his head and threw it away. The next morning, when they gave him food to eat, he refused to eat, and they asked him, “Why?” He said, “If you do not want trouble, put my hair back on my head.” The woman and her children went and fetched resin, applied it to the mmoatia’s scalp, and stuck the hair back onto it. The mmoatia ran his hand over it, and it tore off and fell away. In the morning, “Give me my hair.” In the evening, “Give me my hair.” By now they had become emaciated and grown thin. Then one day, the foolish son decided to go and visit his mother; he arrived to find his mother and his siblings all emaciated. He asked, “Why have you become so emaciated like this?” His mother was about to tell him the reason why they were emaciated, but her children said, “Do not tell him; what wisdom does this fool possess?” His mother said, “I will speak.” She told him; he said, “Oh! Bring the mmoatia and let us go to my village.” His mother and his siblings took the mmoatia and his hair and set off for the fool's village. When they arrived at his village, he said to the mmoatia, “Go sit on my swing and play for a while.” He went and sat on it, and he swung him, and he raised a song, saying:

"Mother gave birth to me as a fool's child, Hurotom.

Father gave birth to me as a fool's child, Hurotom.

Mother gave birth to me as a daredevil, Hurotom. Mother gave birth to me as a daredevil, Hurotom.

Mother gave birth to me 'Fool's child, Hurotom.

Mother gave birth to me as a brave person, Hurotom."

The fairy played games until tired, and the fool's child said to him, "Come, let us go and retrieve your hair." He arrived home with him; he said, "Stick my hair back on for me so that I may go." The fool's child also said, "You too, cover up my sand that you have trampled upon before I stick your hair back on." The fairy went there; when he covered this part, he trampled upon it again. Now, no matter how the fairy tried to cover it, it would not be covered. He said, "Father, I do not want any trouble; I forgive you on behalf of your mother, so you too forgive me, and let me go." The fool's child said, "Go, I too have accepted that." Now, his mother knelt down and said, "Thank you, thank you, for there is no foolish child in the nation." That is why it is said that if you have children, open your eyes and take good care of them, for you do not know which one will take care of you in the future when you are in need.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
