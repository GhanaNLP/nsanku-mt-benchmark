MINISTER FOR FINANCE'S SPEECH / STATEMENT

The damage of COVID-19 has affected all economies, including Ghana. The difficulties that Ghanaians have faced, brought about by the loss of lives and the collapse of the economy and businesses, have not been lost on the government. I boast and say that the way we fought the pandemic was with courage and unity, which the whole world admired. The government also took steps to revive businesses that had collapsed and to restore our lives back to normal.

Furthermore, regarding the announcement and impact of the pandemic, the leader of the Government, His Excellency the President, Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, acted swiftly to establish the GH¢100 billion COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises programme, (the GhanaCARES “Paɣ’ suŋ” programme). This has brought immense relief in the blink of an eye to our people and is reinvigorating our country.

We appreciate the trust you have placed in the Government, even as we strive and work to overcome this pandemic. Throughout this time, our effort to stand together as one nation is a test. God's book continues to give us protection in times like this. As the Prophet Isaiah said, "The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stones; the fig trees have been felled, but we will replace them with cedars" (Isaiah 9:10). The Government remains committed to fulfilling all the promises it has made, and for that reason, we will continue with road projects that will improve all the areas that were neglected, and advance the lives of the people.

One of the key objectives in this 2022 budget and the three years that follow is to initiate an economic transformation that will ensure sustained, non-discriminatory economic growth, and also bring about decent jobs for everyone, especially the youth. The intention of all government policy measures will focus on youth support and expanding their knowledge, demonstrating their skills, and bringing about development and prosperity to everyone.

In order to enable young people to properly unleash their potential and acquire life skills, the Government will roll out the YouStart programme in the course of 2022 to provide financial support to new businesses, to establish or expand their businesses.

In the 2022 Budget, we set out measures to continue addressing existing economic challenges so that it emerges stronger in the post-pandemic period. The government has pledged to adopt broad-based, inclusive measures to provide skills training and promote entrepreneurship among the youth. In line with the President's vision of Ghana Beyond Aid, we want to encourage every Ghanaian, especially the youth, to embrace good attitudes, think deeply, and engage in business that will contribute to nation-building.

I encourage all of us to help build the Ghana we all want.

May God bless it!!!!

Ken Ofori-Atta

Minister for Finance

ECONOMIC EFFORTS IN THE YEAR 2021

EFFORTS OF BIG LOGS

3. The available information regarding the economic performance for the year 2021 is as follows:

• The total GDP increased by 3.5% in the first half of the year 2021 compared to the same period in 2020, representing a 0.7% GDP growth in the year 2020;

• Non-oil GDP growth was 4.8% in the first half of 2021, compared to 1.1% in the same period of 2020;

• Inflation at the end of September was 10.6%, compared to 10.4% in the same period of 2020;

• The expenditure beyond what was budgeted for amounted to 7.7% of GDP, although it was estimated at 7.4% of the GDP;

• The revised deficit was 2.0% of GDP, compared to the projected deficit of 1.7% of GDP;

• The country's debt (as a percentage of GDP) rose to 77.7% at the end of September 2021, having started at 76.1% in December 2020 and increased to that level;

• Gross International Reserves at the end of August 2021 stood at US$11.44 billion, providing cover for 5.2 months of imports of goods and services, compared to US$8.62 billion which could cover 4.1 months of imports at the end of December 2020; and

• The foreign exchange market remained relatively stable over the first nine months of 2021. Indeed, the Ghana cedi depreciated by 1.7% against the US dollar in September 2021, compared with a depreciation of 2.9% in the corresponding period of 2020.

2022 BUDGET REVENUE MOBILIZATION PROCESS

Tax collection process steps

14. The government will find ways to revive commercial businesses and strengthen competition and rivalry between them and outsiders. For this reason, it is necessary for the government to adopt the following measures:

• Support business development and expand industrial business operations. The government will extend the VAT relief that was placed on fabric weaving factories in 2019 by an additional two years;

• After two and a half years, the demand for imported goods will be reviewed to strengthen it and ensure our continued attention to it;

• Reduce the withholding tax on gold sold by small-scale gold miners from 3% to 1.5%;

• Starting from 2022, the Government will partner with the Ghana Revenue Authority to assist MMDAs in the efficient collection of property rates and fair revenue distribution;

• By January 1st, 2022, the Government will review the fees charged by MDAs. The fees they charge will be in line with the rate of inflation;

• Once Parliament approves this budget, no one will pay road tolls anymore.

• The government will introduce an Electronic Transaction Levy or E-Levy on all electronic financial transactions, which will broaden the tax base to include everyone. This levy will cover mobile money payments, bank transfers, bank cash withdrawals, and all merchant payments, with a tax rate of 1.75% to be borne by the sender, except for inward remittances where the tax will be paid by the recipient. This will take effect from February, 2022. This tax does not apply to transactions of GH¢100 or less.

• A government employee should not commit the Government to any contract that will incur expenditure unless authorization is given by the Minister responsible for Government expenditure.

Aims of the scout movement

15. In order to oversee expenditures and strengthen the economy, expenditure measures will be sought and implemented in the 2022 economy, as well as regarding the mid-year revenue issues:

a. The Government will continue to monitor and protect the integrity of the payroll, ensuring that only genuine, verified Government workers are on the payroll;

b. Within the year 2022 up to the middle of the year, there will be an investigation of government workers to ensure transparency in the recruitment of workers into government service, with the aim of promoting integrity among those entering government service;

c. All new projects to be executed in the year 2022, and all those contained in the Medium-Term Development Plans of MDAs, MMDAs, and SOEs, will comply with L.I. 2411 to strengthen the procurement of government works and goods. This will be irrespective of the source of funding for the project.

16. The following expenditure control measures will also be pursued to prevent deviations from the medium-term fiscal consolidation path:

• Only items that appear in the procurement plans of the MDAs and are also on the PPA website shall be awarded on contract;

• All the activities that procurement entities wish to undertake must be supported by that year's expenditure budget. In addition, all these activities must be backed by the appropriate source of funding, indicating that funds are available to carry out the work;

• No government official shall sign documents that incur debt for the government of Ghana without approval or without the authorization of the Minister responsible for Finance.

ECONOMIC RECOVERY AFTER COVID-19

GhanaCARES “Obaatan Pa” Program

17. To strengthen Ghana's overall self-reliance, even beyond COVID-19, the Government launched the GH¢100 billion Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support programme (the GhanaCARES “Obaatan pa” programme). This is to strengthen and accelerate the development of our country's economy and support the private sector.

18. The following are the successes achieved by the program in the year 2021 and the objectives for the year 2022.

Adopting new farming methods and changing food importation into the country

19. About 579,662 people, including about 547,126 farmers as well as about 32,536 commercial farmers, have registered as of October 2021 farmers, in order to promote equity in Government support to the agricultural sector.

20. The government engaged stakeholders in poultry farming, rice and soya production, as well as tomato farming to understand the challenges in these agricultural sectors, which led to a support policy called the Interest Rate Subsidy (IRS) for agribusinesses to alleviate their financial challenges. This IRS will help reduce the interest on loans by 50%, enabling them to acquire machinery and expand their work economically.

21. Under the adoption of new agricultural practices, the following are the strategies the Government will strengthen in the year 2022:

• establish the Ghana Agricultural Land Information Bank (GhLIB) to help in acquiring land for farming, especially commercial agriculture;

• it has discouraged our youth from agriculture as well as the use of social media; and

• have agribusiness that involves livestock and resources to be invested in the disposal of those animals and to market its information to the youth.

Tourism, arts and culture promotion

22. Under the tourism, arts, and culture sector economic revitalization in GhanaCARES, the Government targeted about three hundred (300) operators in tourist destinations across the country, dubbed the National Tourism Destination Single Window, to enhance and improve tourism operations.

Manufacturing and expanding international trade

24. Under Government's 10-Point Industrialization Agenda, strategic anchor industries were introduced to reduce imports and expand fertilizer production to boost export-oriented industries. • Commercial legal reform (and the digitalization of the Registrar General's Department Acceleration office);

• Establish an initiative that will encourage industries across all sectors to strengthen their ties and collaborate with Technical Universities; and

• expand Ghana's domestic medicine manufacturing, and establish partnerships with pharmaceutical manufacturing plants to strengthen the production of Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (API)

Internet and e-commerce attraction and rapid advancement of e-commerce

25. The government has made efforts towards Ghana's digital economy. Because of this, the Government has provided photo identity cards to more than 15 million Ghanaians, and has also placed about 7.5 million homes into the National Digital Address System.

DEVELOPMENT SECTORS AND SOCIAL LIFE

33. Following the expiration of the Ghana Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (2018-2021), Ghana has transitioned into the Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (MTNDPF) (2022-2025). This new policy framework is derived from the Long-Term National Development Framework (Ghana@100), the Coordinated Programme of Economic and Social Development Policies (2017-2024), the Ghana Beyond Aid Agenda and Strategy, as well as the Ghana COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support Programme (GhanaCARES). These policy frameworks are also aligned with the UN Agenda 2030 (SDGs) and the African Union Agenda 2063.

34. This section outlines the Government’s efforts in some sectors of the economy in the year 2021, as well as the programs and projects that the Government will undertake in this year 2022.

RECRUITMENT METHODS FOR WORKERS

35. The government's efforts to reduce unemployment have led to the creation of pathways and programs such as the Ghana Productive Safety Net Project (GPSNP). Under this work, the Government will:

i. sought employment for 33, 952 poor persons across 80 MMDAs, with wages amounting to GH¢ 53.43 million; and

ii. used an amount of GH¢5.82 million to distribute to households in about 128 communities across 41 MMDAs in the five Northern Regions, for them to start or strengthen their businesses.

36. In the Government's intention to attract the youth into business so as to reduce youth migration to the south, and also to provide the youth with a good living, the Government undertook cocoa farm rehabilitation, replanting, mass spraying, fertilizer application, hand pollination, cocoa pruning, and the irrigation of cocoa farms in the cocoa growing areas, which provided employment to about 130,000 people.

Meaning and explanation of keywords (Budget Terms)

1D1F (One District, One Factory): The construction of factories and workplaces is what will move this country into industrialization, which is great development for this country. Its aim is to provide jobs for Ghanaians, across all parts and corners of the country.

Spending money through the path of justice: This is when the government works openly and allows all the citizens of this country to know every secret of the work, as well as its successes and challenges. This can be money or how the government manages all the country's resources.

Allocation: It is the means through which resources reach government offices and all departments.

Alleviation: This is a way they follow to reduce the severity of a problem.

Budget: A budget is a way that shows where money will come from and what that money will be spent on. For example, a family (household) can have their budget to show how much money they have, and show how they will spend it on their rent, electricity and water bills, and food. If the money is not enough, the family will discuss finding additional money or borrowing money.

Budget deficit: This happens when the government's expenditure exceeds the taxes it collects.

Capital Expenditure: This is money spent by the government on major projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, vehicles, water, and machinery.

Compensation for public sector workers: This is workers' salaries and all allowances attached to the monthly pay, such as taxes and amounts paid to retired public sector workers.

Development Partners (DPs): This refers to countries or organizations that support the Government to fulfill the country's development goals. Examples of DPs include the global financial institution we call the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, and African

End of year inflation: This is how prices of goods rise every year in the month of Christmas.

Excess expenditure: This happens when the Government spends beyond the budget amount approved by parliamentarians.

FREE SHS: Free SHS is a policy of the Government of Ghana introduced during the tenure of President Nana Akufo-Addo in September 2017, to enable every student who qualifies to attend SHS to attend for free without paying a penny.

Support: This is money given to the Government by international development partners which is not meant to be repaid.

Government Statutory Funds: This refers to monies that the law mandates the Government to pay into statutory funds such as the District Assembly Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund, as well as the National Health Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is the goods and services produced within a country at a given time, and their monetary value.

Gross International Reserves: This measures the value or capacity of a country's reserve funds regarding how much goods or services it can purchase internationally at a given time. It also shows the value of a country's currency relative to the value of international currencies.

Inflation (The general price increase of goods and services): The rate at which the prices of goods and services rise or increase over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is a good initiative introduced by the NPP Government to eradicate poverty and reduce the disparity between urban and rural areas. NABCo (Nation Builders Corps): NABCO is an initiative introduced by the Government of Ghana to provide decent jobs for unemployed university graduates, in order to reduce hardships in their lives, and also to equip them with skills to work and strengthen the nation's economic development pathways.
