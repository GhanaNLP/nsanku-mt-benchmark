CHILD KWASI CAT

THE LITTLE BOY Kwasi Gyinamoa had eight sisters, none of whom had a husband, and they set out to go somewhere to find husbands. The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "I will go with you." They said, "We will not go with you." The eldest among them said, "Let him go with us, so that if we are on the way and a thorn pricks us, he can pull it out for us." They set out and went with the little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa. They came upon a certain river, and part flowed as white clay; part flowed as red ochre; and part also flowed as charcoal. They said, "Ah! How will we be able to cross this river?" The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa struck it with a cow tail whisk so that the waters parted into two, and they passed through it and went on. They came upon a certain town; the town was very large. The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "A silent town, are there no people in it?" Then a bird said, "There are people in the house over there, so go on." When they went, an old woman was resting her head on her feet, searching for lice in it. When she saw the little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa, she lifted her head and put it back in place; she said, "My grandchildren, why have you come here where nobody comes?" The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "My eight sisters have not found husbands to marry, and they said they were going to look for husbands to marry." The old grandmother said, "Oh! It is well, I too have eight children." The old grandmother sent her grandchild to go and call her children. When they came, this one said, "This is my husband," and that one said, "This is my husband." When the marriage was concluded, the old woman went and took a medicine called "dokum" and placed it around the necks of her children. And each went and slept with the other. And the little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa also slept with the old woman. When night fell, the old woman sharpened her knife to go and kill the children. The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "Eh! Eh! Eh!" The old woman said, "What is it?" The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "If I were in my hometown, when I am lying down unable to sleep like this, mother would cut a piece of yam and put it in the fire for me, and she would take a sieve to go fetch water and bring it for me to drink." The old grandmother set out and went to the riverside, and the little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa went and stripped the dokum from the necks of the old woman's children and put them on his sisters, and woke his sisters up. The old woman came back from the riverside; she said, "When I fetch the water, it does not draw." The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "Go take swamp mud, tear leaves and put them in it, and use that to fetch it." The old woman went and did so, and she fetched water into it; and it worked. She brought it back and took the yam out of the fire and gave it to the little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa to eat, and he drank the water on top of it. Then he lay down there and went to sleep. The old grandmother got up; she said, "Here I come!" She sharpened her knife and entered the room with it; she touched the necks of the children with her hand, and whichever had the dokum on its neck, she pierced with her knife. In that way, she killed the eight children. She did not know that it was her very own children she had killed. At the break of day, early in the morning, the old grandmother set out and went to the farm. The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa also told his sisters, and they ran away. The old grandmother came back from the farm. The little boy Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "Old grandmother! Old grandmother! If you were not a fool, would you have killed your own children?" It is I and my siblings who are going." Old Lady said, "It is nothing, he who knows you loves you." She went, and Old Lady also transformed herself into a young maiden, and she took an adenkum gourd rattle and went to Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa's town. She said, "The person who can dance properly to this adenkum is the one I will marry." All the people who were in the town danced; they could not. Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa danced to it. She said, "I have married you." Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "As for me, I will not marry you." And it was his uncle who married her. He went and slept with her, and she plucked out both of his eyes and took them away. The young maiden said, "Oh Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa, it is your uncle's two eyes that I have plucked out and am carrying away." Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "Take them and go, for he who knows you loves you." When day broke, they said, "Akokoa, go and retrieve your uncle's eyes." He said, "I will not go." They pleaded and pleaded, and he set off. And Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa knew that a certain grandchild of Old Lady was pregnant, so he also took a calabash and placed it upside down over his belly, wrapped a cloth over it, and set off to Old Lady's town. He arrived at the crossroads, and he wept. Old Lady said, "Why?" He said, "My husband and I were coming; when he reached the mountain, he fell down and both of his eyes fell out and were lost." Old Lady said, "It is well, that scoundrel Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa's uncle's left eye is this one, and the right one is this. When you go, you say, 'my eye kuru, my eye kyiren'." As soon as Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa got hold of them, he said, "Oh Old Lady, it is my uncle's eyes that you came to pluck out that I am carrying away." Old Lady said, "It is nothing, he who knows you loves you." Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa came home, and he placed the eyes back into his uncle's eye sockets. He said, "My eye kuru, my eye kyiren," and his uncle's eyes became completely clear. When day broke, all the children went to bathe in the stream. Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "I will not bathe." They said, "Why are you acting so restlessly?" When they said that, he became angry, and he bathed. As soon as he jumped into the water, Old Lady seized him tightly! She said, "Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa, I have caught you," and she took him to her town. And Old Lady went and took a basket and covered him with it, and she placed palm nuts on the fire. She said, "I am going to the Gyabu nation; I am going to the Sorono nation, and I will come to kill Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa." She set off and left, and Akokoa gathered gravel and chewed it pupru! pupru! Old Lady's grandchild said, "What are you chewing?" He said, "Tiger nuts." She said, "Give me some to chew." He said, "Come and open for me." She said, "You will run away." He said, "No, I will not run away." She opened for him and he came out; Old Lady's grandchild was also pounding palm nuts. Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa took a pestle and struck Old Lady's grandchild, killing her. And he stripped off Old Lady's grandchild's skin, and covered himself with it. Old Lady came; she said, "Why are you so dirty?" He said, "It was Akokoa Kwasi Gyinamoa that I killed; I have put him on the fire." In Old Lady's great joy, she began to dance, saying:

Child, you have killed Child Kwasi the Cat, No, you will remove some.

And she finished cooking the food; we sat there eating. Grandmother Aberewa scooped the soup slurping! slurping! The child Kwasi Gyinamoa said, "It is your grandchild’s head soup that you are ladling." Grandmother Aberewa said, "What did you say?" He said, "I said there is no salt in this soup." Grandmother Aberewa said, "Go and bring some." And he went and brought some. When they finished eating, Grandmother Aberewa said, "Now I have killed the child Kwasi Gyinamoa, my grandchild come." He came and she went to fetch gold and adorned him beautifully. Then Grandmother Aberewa looked at his face here. She said, "Ah! this child, your face looks like the child Kwasi Gyinamoa." The child said, "Grandmother, why is it that you know the child Kwasi Gyinamoa has come to kill all eight of my siblings, and so you are hurting me?" Grandmother Aberewa said, "Shut your mouth." Then the child defecated in the house. His grandmother swept it up. She said, "Take it to go and throw it on the rubbish dump." And the child Kwasi Gyinamoa carried it. When he reached the rubbish dump, he stripped off all the gold the old woman had used to adorn him, and tied it up. Then he said, "Grandmother Aberewa! Grandmother Aberewa! It is I, the child Kwasi Gyinamoa, I have come to kill your grandchild again, and now too I have taken your gold." Grandmother Aberewa said, "I said your face looked like the child Kwasi Gyinamoa; as for this, wherever you will go, you and I will go together." And the two of them burst into a run, rushing! rushing! rushing! The child Kwasi Gyinamoa reached his town, and he gave the gold to his family, and he set off on the road and went to hide in the roadside, and Grandmother Aberewa also went there. That is why when you walk along the path, you hear the roadside rustle "pe!" It is Grandmother Aberewa chasing the child Kwasi Gyinamoa.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
