STATEMENT FROM THE MINISTER FOR FINANCE

Ghanaians voted massively in December 2020 for our President H.E. Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo again to continue his good works. The suffering that Ghanaians experienced under this government is no joke.

The spending of this our 2021 budget will revive our national economy, support businesses that are falling, and demonstrate an encouragement that will help build a better Ghana firmly after COVID-19. This national budget will serve as an anchor for every plan we have made that will help transform this nation, build the nation, and preserve all the nation's businesses.

We have reiterated all financial matters based on this again in this first phase, that is, through the good measures that have been put in place, the major expected targets were announced, some fundraising activities were unveiled, various ways of generating revenue were outlined, support for private businesses was addressed, as well as plans for managing crisis situations. By the end of 2019, we made efforts to transform the country's economic affairs through the realization of certain major goals to which the whole world bore witness, and then COVID-19 emerged. The way this disease disrupted economies was felt deeply all over the world, not just in Ghana.

The outbreak of the deadly COVID-19 disease and the severe troubles it caused have disrupted our work and everyday life. The authorities have clearly recognized the enormous hardship this has brought upon the citizens and have made arrangements to help revive all activities once again. There is still hope because we have seen various ways through which things are being tackled. The authorities have sacrificed themselves to ensure that our citizens remain healthy, and have therefore put measures in place to vaccinate Ghanaians so that everyone stays healthy and we can rebuild the nation.

Even in these difficult times, we have partnered with you for development activities, and we have instituted major policies that will help rebuild and stabilize our finances once again. About 1.2 million Ghanaians have gained complete access to secondary school education, we restructured the entire banking system so that customers of these banks could receive all their deposited funds. In addition, a different automotive policy has been implemented, which has enabled us to see the very first vehicles assembled here in our markets and on our streets.

We emphasize that indeed we went through some challenges at a time when we expected progress. The government has resolved to put certain measures in place to help stabilize things and support businesses to stand firm. One of these was that we invested an amount of up to GH¢750 million into certain business activities in August 2020, and this has helped large and small businesses such as Micro, Small scale, Medium-Sized Enterprises (MSMES) and others as well.

We are opening our arms and inviting everyone to join us so that we can continue to contribute appropriate ideas to develop the heritage/nation for the benefit of all. That is, we all pledge that we will help with all our heart and strength to serve Ghana.

God bless you all.

Long live Ghana!

Ken Ofori-Atta, Minister for Finance

PROBLEMS BROUGHT BY THE COVID-19 DISEASE

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that occurred globally at the end of the year 2019 brought many huge problems to homes, businesses, and markets all over the world. It disrupted the entire financial system, caused people to lose their jobs, resulted in indebtedness, and destroyed trade development matters. In addition, it killed more than 2.6 million people worldwide.

14. In Ghana, the number of confirmed cases increased weekly from 11 to 781 from the last week of March 2020, and then began to decline to 104 in the last week of August 2020. In 2021, another variant of the disease also surged, rising from 99 in the first week of January to 880 by the first week of February, on February 5.

15. In order to be able to reduce the spread of the virus, the Governors placed restrictions on Ghanaians, and doing this disrupted businesses, especially trade and financial matters, such as hospitality, agriculture, health, travel, crafts, land and construction, financial services, and all educational affairs.

16. Mechanics, drivers, and conductors in trotros, as well as all self-employed people who enjoy these aspects of life, were severely constrained because of the government's lockdown laws. Even though these restriction laws would help curb this viral infection, doing so also caused the trade of goods and services to decline drastically.

17. Compared to neighboring countries, the courage displayed by this government and its firm stance against the virus caused some foreign nations to support the government and encourage it, which led to a strict enforcement of the laws.

MEANING OF SOME WORDS

1D1F (One District One Factory): Industrialization in every district, which will make Ghana a manufacturing nation. The objective is for citizens to find jobs everywhere across Ghana.

Accountability: This is how Governors account to the citizens for what has been done, and it allows the results that come out of it to be seen. Money and other properties are included in the things that Governors oversee for the citizens' accountability matters.

Distribution: The ways by which things are distributed to Government offices for everyone to see some.

Intervention: Ways through which a problem is mitigated.

BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): a name used to call a group of certain knowledge fields. These words are used when discussing learning or education within the school system to help promote competitiveness in earth sciences and technological activities.

Budget: It is a plan that shows the money that will be needed, where it will be obtained from, and what the money will be used for. A family can write its budget to show the money the family has and how it will be used, for example: rent payment, savings, health, food, and others. If money is needed for the desired things, then the family looks for a loan, or reduces its needs, or looks for another way that will bring in money.

Budget deficit: This happens when the government spends more money than it receives in revenue.

Capital expenditure: This refers to the money spent on capital projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water supply, factories and their machinery.

Workers' compensation: It is the monthly pay of workers and other monies that go to workers, such as allowances, old-age compensation, and other monies given to workers.

Benefactors/Donors: They are foreign nations, or organizations that help developing nations to progress financially. Some of these donors are IMF, World

Foreign Exchange Reserves: This is used to measure the nation's funds that can be relied upon to acquire foreign goods at a certain time. It is used to back the currency circulating within the country.

Inflation: It refers to the rising cost of goods and the amount of things money can buy at a given time.
