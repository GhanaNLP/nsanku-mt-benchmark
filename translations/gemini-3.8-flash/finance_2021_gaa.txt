STATEMENT FROM THE MINISTER FOR FINANCE

Ghanaians voted again in the 2020 election for our President H.E. Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to continue his good works. The suffering Ghanaians experienced under these Governors is not a joke.

The implementation of our 2021 budget will revive our nation’s finances, support businesses that are declining, and demonstrate an encouragement that will help firmly build a better Ghana after COVID-19. This national budget will serve as a foundation for every decision we make that will help transform the face of this nation, build the nation, and protect all the country's sectors.

We restated all financial matters based on this again in this first phase, that is, through the good arrangements made, major expected targets were announced, some fundraising activities were exposed, various ways of apprehending certain things were shown, private business support, and arrangements for handling problem situations were also made. By the end of 2019, we made efforts to transform the country's finances through the indication of certain major targets which the whole world witnessed, and then COVID-19 emerged. How this disease destroyed the economy was deeply felt throughout the world and not only in Ghana.

The outbreak of the deadly COVID-19 disease brought severe difficulties that disrupted our work and everyday life. The government recognized well the great hardship this brought upon the citizens and made plans to help revive all businesses and activities. There is still hope because we have seen various ways in which things are being handled. The authorities have sacrificed to ensure that our citizens are healthy, which is why plans have been made to vaccinate Ghanaians all around so that everyone will be healthy and we can rebuild the nation.

Even during this difficult period, we have partnered with you for developmental initiatives, and we have instituted major policies that will help stabilize and rebuild the financial sector firmly once again. About 1.2 million Ghanaians have had complete access to secondary school education, we have cleaned up and reformed the operations of all banking institutions to ensure that customers of these banks recovered all their deposits. In addition, an automotive development policy has been established which has enabled us to see the very first vehicles assembled locally on the market and on our roads.

We emphasize that indeed we went through some challenges at a time when we were expecting progress. The government has resolved to put certain measures in place to help stabilize things and support businesses to stand firm. One of them is that we injected an amount of up to GH¢750 million into certain business activities in August 2020, and this has helped large and small businesses, namely Micro, Small scale, Medium-Sized Enterprises (MSMES) and others as well.

We are stretching out our hands and calling on everyone to join us so that we continue to invest good ideas into the wealth/nation to establish it so that everyone benefits. That is why we all promise to use all our hearts and all our strength to help and serve Ghana.

God bless you all.

Long live Ghana!

Ken Ofori-Atta, Minister of Finance

PROBLEMS BROUGHT BY THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that occurred worldwide at the end of the year 2019 brought many great problems to homes, workplaces, and the entire world's markets. It disrupted entire financial systems, caused people to lose their jobs, brought debt, and damaged economic growth. In addition, it killed more than 2.6 million people worldwide.

14. In Ghana, the number of confirmed cases increased weekly from 11 to 781 by the final week of March 2020, and then began to decrease to 104 in the final week of September 2020. In 2021, another variant of the disease also surged, rising from 99 in the first week of January to 880 by the first week of February 5.

15. In order to be able to reduce the spread of the virus, the Government imposed restrictions on Ghanaians, and doing this disrupted businesses, especially trade and financial matters, such as hospitality, agriculture, health, transportation, artisanal work, land and building construction, financial services, and education services.

16. Mechanics, drivers, and conductors in trotros, as well as all self-employed people who enjoy these aspects of life, were severely restricted because of the government's restrictive laws. Although these restrictive laws were meant to help contain the spread of this virus, doing so also caused the trading of goods and services to decline significantly.

17. Compared to neighboring countries, the boldness shown by these Governors and their firm stand against the virus prompted some foreign countries to back the Governors and encouraged them to tighten the laws significantly.

GLOSSARY OF SOME WORDS

1D1F (One District One Factory): Industrialization in every district, which will make Ghana an industrial nation. The objective is for citizens to find jobs everywhere in this Ghana

Accountability: This is the way Governments hold citizens accountable for things they have done, and it makes people see the outcome. Money and other properties are part of the things Governments supervise for the citizens' accountability matters.

Distribution: Ways in which goods are distributed to Government workplaces so that everyone gets some.

Relief: Ways in which a trouble is reduced.

BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): a name used to call a group of certain knowledge fields. These words are used when talking about study or education in the school curriculum to help foster competition in science and technological activities.

Budget: It is a plan that shows the money that will be needed, where it will be obtained, and what the money will be used for. A family can write its budget to show the money the family has and how it will be used, for example: paying house rent, utilities, food, and others. If money will be needed for the things desired, then the family either takes a loan, reduces its needs, or finds another way that will bring in money.

Budget deficit: This happens when the government spends more money than comes into the treasury.

Capital expenditure: This refers to money spent on capital projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water systems, workplaces and their machinery.

Remuneration of workers: It is the monthly payment of workers and other monies that go to the workers such as allowances, retirement benefits, and other monies given to workers.

Donors/Helpers: They are foreign nations, or organizations that help developing countries/nations to progress economically. Some of these donors/helpers are the IMF, World

Grant: These are funds that community development partners donate to the Governors. Those funds are not returned or paid back to the donors.

Statutory Funds : These are funds that are legally allocated to certain institutions. Some of these funds include District Assemblies’ Common Fund, (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund and National Health Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This means all the money that will be obtained for the value of the country's goods and services within a certain period.

Foreign Exchange Reserves: This measures a country's funds that can be relied upon to acquire foreign goods at a given time. It is used to back the currency circulating within the country.

Purchasing Power of Money: This refers to the prices of goods and the quantity of things that money can buy at a given time.

IPEP (Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is one of the NPP Government's initiatives aimed at eradicating poverty and completely bridging the gap between the poor and the rich, especially in villages and less developed areas, through the provision of developmental projects in these places.

Non-oil Gross Domestic Product (GDP): It is the total amount of money generated from national economic activities excluding oil revenue. It is used to measure the growth of wealth in the country.

Non-Tax Revenue: This is the money that the Government receives from things that are not taxed. Some of these are license fees, fees for services, profits, and others.
