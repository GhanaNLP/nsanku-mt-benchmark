About

This year will be a year we will never forget, as it brought great hardship to the country's economic situation. The Coronavirus pandemic broke out in the country during the first four months of the year 2020, as occurred everywhere else in the world, which caused our businesses to decline, reduced the revenue the country was supposed to earn, and incurred debts that had not been budgeted for in the financial estimates, in the fight against the pandemic.

We began our economic program at a time when we thought we were recovering from the various challenges brought upon us by the coronavirus pandemic, but other difficulties came upon us during that same period. It caused us to lose all the revenues we had gained between 2017 and 2019. These challenges, chief among them being the Russia-Ukraine war, as well as delays in the approval of key revenue-mobilization measures under the program, were not approved on time. This severely weakened our revenue mobilization capacity. The international capital markets were closed to us, our domestic revenue mobilization conditions tightened, our unpaid debt obligations increased, driving up the cost of our borrowing, and as a result, our cedi depreciated significantly.

Therefore, at a time when external financial assistance from lending groups and foreign exchange inflows declined, the cost of imports increased due to the country’s high dependence on imported goods, and debt service payments continued to rise, the value of our Ghana currency depreciated. This has imposed harsh living conditions on all of us, the Government and every individual alike. The rise in fuel prices, which has driven up the cost of many goods, has caused inflation to exceed 40.4 percent in the month of October in the year 2022.

YEAR 2023 AND THE FINANCIAL ARRANGEMENT BUDGET STATEMENT IN THE MID-YEAR

Budgeting

19. The government will present revenue mobilization measures totaling 1.35 percent of our revenue to Parliament for consideration and approval. This will support our revenue mobilization drive. Some of the measures include:

• The excise duty on cigarettes and tobacco products will be revised to align with that of ECOWAS, and the tax imposed on hard liquor will be higher than that imposed on beer.

• VAT will be increased from 12.5 percent to 15 percent. Changes will also be made to VAT-exempt items.

• The monthly tax collected at workplaces will be changed to a daily collection at the end of each day.

• The tax charged on all money withdrawn or deposited will be reduced from 1.5% to 1%. Also, the daily withdrawal limit will be granted.

• Other ways they might want to consider are:

○ 5G spectrum will be auctioned;

○ Efforts will be made to ensure that the property tax law is complied with;

○ They will explore getting additional benefits to add to what they already have from the Jubilee oil; and

○ Changes will be made to their taxes to remove penalties for things that could not be done and to account only for the things that were done.
