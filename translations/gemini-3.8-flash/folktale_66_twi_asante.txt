We are not alike, not alike at all

The Kokokyinaka bird does not borrow from the spider

ANANSE, Kokokyinaka came to him to ask for a loan of two asuanu. But Ananse himself did not have any, so he went to his friend Mosquito to borrow some and brought it to his friend Kokokyinaka. When the day Mosquito set had passed, Mosquito said to Ananse, “Go collect my money and bring it to me.” Ananse said, “Kokokyinaka is perched up in the silk-cotton tree, so go and collect your money.” Mosquito said, “How will I lay my hands on it; that creature flies.” Ananse said, “Go and butt against the big tree, you will see that the tree will break, and you will catch Kokokyinaka and collect your money.” Mosquito went and butted against the big tree. As it was about to break, Kokokyinaka took off from there, flutter, flutter, flutter! Then Mosquito came, and he said, “Well done, Ananse!” That is why people make the proverb, “If the creature Kokokyinaka had not taken a loan from Ananse, Mosquito would not have gone to butt against the big silk-cotton tree.”

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
