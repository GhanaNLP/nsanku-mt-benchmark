DO YOU KNOW?

What happened at the city gates mentioned in the Bible?

City gates were not merely places through which people entered a city. Because many people passed through these gates, many things were done there. City gates are mentioned many times in the Bible, and this helps us see what took place there.

People gathered at the city gates. When the Hebrew word translated "gate" is used, it does not refer only to the entrance into a city. It is also used for the place where people set up stalls, near the city gate. In some cities, there were large gathering places near the city gates where people could assemble.—Neh. 8:1.

Merchants set up markets near the city gates. Some gates in Jerusalem were called the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate. It is likely that they were given these names because of the things that were sold near these gates.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

People traded near the city gates and they also did other things there. Abraham made an agreement near a city gate in the presence of people. Boaz did the same thing too. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) When people were traveling and night fell upon them and no one gave them a place to stay, they spent the night in the public square near the city gate. (Judg. 19:15-21) Some people also went to the city gates to hear the news that had come. —2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

Judges tried cases near the city gates. They judged cases in the presence of the people so that the people would see the cases and how they were judged. (Deut. 21:18-20) Because the cases were tried in public, that caused the judges to see to it that they judged the cases well. When the judges settled a case, they saw to it that it was carried out immediately. (Deut. 25:2) It was expected that the judges who judged cases at the city gates would behave well and judge justly so that the citizens would also conduct themselves well.—Deut. 16:18-20; Job 29:7, 11-17.

Various things were done in the big towns

Faithful men of old went to the city gates to encourage the Israelites gathered there to trust in Jehovah. When people had matters they wanted others to know, they went to the city gates because they knew that many people gathered there. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For example, Jehu went to a city gate and told all the people that what Jehovah said against the house of Ahab had come to pass. (2 Ki. 10:8-10) King Hezekiah also gathered the military commanders at a city gate and encouraged them to rely on Jehovah for help.—2 Chron. 32:6-8.

The Bible says that “true wisdom” cries aloud or raises its voice “at the gates of the city.” (Proverbs 1:20, 21) In ancient Israel, faithful men gave good advice and judged matters properly at the city gates. Today, wise sayings from Jehovah are recorded in the Bible, and his servants all over the world are helping people to study the Bible and gain true wisdom.

A BIBLE STORY YOU CAN BELIEVE

An Early-Blooming Tree

About 30 trees are mentioned in the Bible. The almond tree is among these trees. It is one of the trees that blossoms early in Israel as winter is coming to an end. Toward the end of the month of January, it is seen blossoming beautifully. The color of its flowers is pink or white. The Israelites call the almond tree in their language “the early waker.” Is there something that the Creator of this tree does that is like how this tree blossoms early?

In the footnote to Jeremiah 7:25, it says that Jehovah gets up early in the morning and sends his prophets. The March 15, 2011, Watchtower stated: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakens’ early, so Jehovah figuratively ‘gets up early in the morning’ [to warn] his people about the consequences of disobedience. . . . He will not rest—‘he will keep watch’—until his prophetic word comes to pass.”—Jer. 1:11, 12.

The white blossoms of the almond tree are also compared in the Bible to gray hair in old age. (Eccl. 12:5) Because almond blossoms are beautiful, when Bezalel made the lampstand for the tabernacle, he shaped the cups on the branches of the lampstand like almond blossoms.—Ex. 37:17-20.
