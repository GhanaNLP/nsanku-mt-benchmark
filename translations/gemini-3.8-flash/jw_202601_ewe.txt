ALL OF US need certain basic things in life. That is how Jehovah made us. For example, in order to keep living, we need food, clothing, and shelter. (Read Matthew 5:3.) If we want to find true happiness in life, we must recognize this need and satisfy it.

He is dressed in rags, starving, and sitting on a street corner, yet no one drops anything in for him. Since he has no place to stay, the sun scorches him by day and the cold bites him at night. This person knows very well that for things to be well with him in life, he needs people to help him, which is why he is begging for assistance. In the same way, the one who knows that he needs spiritual things, or the one begging to be given the spirit, must also be helped. Such individuals will readily accept everything Jehovah provides to help those who love him and will make use of it.

10 Read Colossians 4:6. The apostle Paul admonished the Christians in Colossae to always let their words ‘be gracious.’ What does this mean? The Greek word translated here means that our words should be beneficial to people. But that is not all. It should be pleasant to hear, and we should say it in love.

11 When we are teaching the truth to people, we must follow Paul’s advice to make our words pleasant. The Bible itself compares the truth to a sharp sword that can pierce even to the dividing of soul and spirit. In other words, it can expose our feelings and our motives. (Heb. 4:12) However, if we do not use the Bible tactfully, we may end up just condemning people, which will cause offense. How might this happen?

12 Imagine this scenario: Suppose you are preaching and you meet a man who prays to images, and he loves celebrating Christmas and Easter with his family. What would you do? Would you read scriptures to show him that it is not wise to worship something that has no life, and that Christmas and Easter are pagan holidays? (Isa. 44:14-20; 2 Cor. 6:14-17) If you do that the first time you meet the person, you may have told him the truth, but you did not use God's Word tactfully at all.

A TIME TO SPEAK THE TRUTH

14 Whenever Jesus spoke to his followers, he used pleasing words, and he patiently taught them many things. (Mark 6:34) Yet, there was still much that his followers needed to learn. Jesus did not try to teach them everything he knew. He knew their limitations. And he also knew that it was not the right time for them to know certain truths. He even told them that there were some truths that were too much for them to bear at that time. (John 16:12) What do we learn from this?

Visualize Bible Events

Many events with lessons are in the Bible for us. When we visualize these events, we will understand them well and they will benefit us much more. Here are some of the things that can help you visualize Bible events.

• Read aloud. Reading the Bible aloud will help you to visualize the events better. If your family is reading the Bible together and you assign the roles of the Bible characters, it will help you visualize the event even better.

• Visualize how the people reacted. Try to see how the people in the story thought about things and their feelings. For example, ask yourself: ‘Why did the person speak or act that way? If I were in that situation, how would I think or feel?’

• Draw pictures. Drawing Bible events will help you visualize things and remember them easily, even if you are not a skilled artist.
