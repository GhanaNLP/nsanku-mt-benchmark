WHEN AKYEM-ABOADEE CAME INTO THE NATION AS A RESULT OF DOING

There was a certain maiden. Whenever any man said he loved her, she said, "I do not love you." Then the Leopard turned himself into a handsome young man, and came and married her, and took her to his town. He said, "I am a Leopard, and I came and married you." She said, "Father, I love you very much." And she bore him a male child, who was a human being. And then she also bore him a daughter. And her mother said, "I am going to seek the dwelling-place of this child of mine." And her mother went, and she hid her. Then her husband came; he said, "Has someone been here?" And she said, "No one has been here." He said, "What is making that rumbling noise up there, and also urine is dripping on me?" She said, "It is a sore!" When day broke in the morning, her husband went into the forest, and the daughter said to her mother, "Go, and when you reach the cross-roads, do not speak; when you reach the stream, do not speak, for if you speak, he will come and catch you." Then when she reached there, she did not listen, and she called out, "My in-law, oh! My in-law, oh!" And the Leopard came and caught her and went off, and his wife saw him, and she grew very sad, and she was weeping. Her husband said, "Why?" She said, "My stomach." When day broke, the daughter said, "I am going to my town." She mashed boiled yam and eggs, put it on her head, and took her two children; one went before her, and one went behind her. And whenever she broke off some of the mashed yam, she said, "Take and eat, sweet one's child." And she broke some off for the other one too, saying, "Take and eat, sweet one's child." Little did she know that her husband was following her. Then, as she was about to reach her town, the Leopard said, "Hear me! Since your mother came here and you did not show her to me, and you let me catch her, stand there and let me name these children of mine: the boy is called Ofori; the girl too is called Oforiwa." The Leopard brought out gold and gave it to his children, saying, "You are forbidden to eat any animal that the Leopard has caught; and when a Leopard is killed, take a sponge and white clay and put it on him; I bless you very, very much! You are of the Leopard Clan, the Aboadee." This story of mine that I have told, whether it be sweet, or whether it be not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
