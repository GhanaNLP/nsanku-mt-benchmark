WHEN SOMEONE DOES GOOD TO YOU, THANK THEM WITH GOOD

It is said that Eagle was there, and as he was walking about, he came across an Old Woman who had a sore on her leg. And Eagle said, “Oh! Look at this sore, as severe as this is, are you still able to walk?” The Old Woman said, “Well! Slowly, slowly.” Eagle said, “A human being like you, lest tomorrow comes, if I heal you, you will repay me with evil.” The Old Woman said, “Oh! I cannot do that.” Eagle said, “If you say you cannot, I have heard you.” He said, “Close your eyes and listen.” And the Old Woman closed her eyes and listened. Eagle said, “Look at your sore.” When the Old Woman looked, it was completely gone! Then he made her close them again; she listened, and when she looked, the entire land was cleared and cultivated. He said, “Close them again.” She closed them and listened, and houses were standing all around. Then he made her close them again. She listened, and when she looked, it was a massive town! Eagle said, “Old Woman, it is in your hands!” The Old Woman said, “I thank you.” Then the Old Woman said, “What should I give you in gratitude?” Eagle said, “I do not want anything, but as for me, that silk cotton tree standing there is what I want.” The Old Woman said, “What is that, after all? Take it.” So Eagle flew away and went to perch on it, and he wove a nest, laid two eggs, and placed them in it. Then he hatched the two eggs; and he went to look for something to bring for his children to eat. The Old Woman's grandchild was sitting beside her and said, “Ehe! Ehe!” The Old Woman asked, “What is it?” The child said, “I want to chew Eagle’s children.” The Old Woman said, “Where am I going to get Eagle’s children?” Then the grandchild cried again, “Ehe! Ehe!” The Old Woman asked, “What is it?” The child said, “I want to chew Eagle’s children; if I do not get some to chew, I will die.” The Old Woman said, “Ah! Should my grandchild die because of Eagle’s children? Rise up, take axes, fell the silk cotton tree, and bring Eagle’s children here.” The people went there, chopping vigorously! pinpin! pinpin! pinpin! It was just as the tree was about to fall to the ground that the eldest of Eagle’s children leaped and stood at the edge of the nest, raised its voice, and cried out to its mother, saying:

Oh, Sango Bird!

Oh, Sango Bird!

Sango Anoma, child of the Eagle!

Oh, Sango Bird!

When Sango goes to eat, come!

Oh, Sango Bird!

Welcome! Oh!

His mother heard that her child was crying; she flew, whoosh! She arrived and said, “Sanguri!” and the tree stood upright again, and all the people who were felling it sank into the ground! Then she gave the food to her children. And she bade them farewell, saying, “I am leaving; if the Old Woman comes to take you out, do not let her.” Then the Old Woman said, "Go and cut down the tree, and bring the young birds for my grandchild to eat." So they went there again, chop! chop! chop! As the tree was falling to the ground, the child came out and stood at the entrance of the cage, and called his mother, saying:

Oh, Sango Bird!

Sango the Bird, child of an Eagle!

Oh, Sango Bird!

Sango, go eat and come!

Oh, Sango Bird!

Welcome! Oh!

He cut it, cut it, cut it, cut it completely! Instantly, the tree crashed to the ground, crash! They took out the Eagle's children; they took one and gave it to the Old Woman, and the remaining one flew up and perched on a Wawa tree. One was roasted and given to the Old Woman's grandchild to eat with her roasted plantain. Not long after, the Eagle came. Upon arriving, the tree was broken down, and she saw that only one of her children was perched there; she asked what had happened. And that one also told her all its troubles. The Eagle set off and went to the Old Woman's house; when she arrived, the Old Woman's grandchild had finished eating her one child. She said, "Old Woman, I congratulate you." She left the Old Woman's house and went to the outskirts of the town. She said, "Sanguri!" and all the people perished; and she said again, "Sanguri!", and all the houses collapsed, instantly all the houses came crashing down! "Sanguri!" The town turned into a forest again. "Sanguri!" and the Old Woman's town was restored. And she said, "Old Woman, you see, that is why the elders say, if someone does you good, thank them with good, and do not thank them with evil."

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some and eat, and take some and bring it back to me.
