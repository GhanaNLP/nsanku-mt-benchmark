We are not alike, not alike at all

If it is done, the deities came home.

A certain hunter and his wife lived in a cottage. They were poor, and they were suffering. Whenever the hunter went into the forest, he got nothing! When he returned home, he would go and set traps in the water, and bring back mudfish and crabs to his wife. While they were there, his wife asked him, saying, “I believe that when you kill game you give it away, or you sell it to spend the money; therefore, if you go to the forest today and do not bring meat back, I will go back to my hometown.” The hunter set off into the forest. He walked and walked; he did not see any animal. He said, “Oh! What trouble I have seen.” Then, as he looked around, he saw a duiker wandering there. He said, “As for this duiker, I will kill you.” The hunter chased after it, running fast! The duiker turned into the hollow of a tree. It said, “Nana Tano, I flee to take refuge with you, for the hunter has caught me.” The hunter said, “As for me, I do not know what they call Tano, so come out and let me take you to my wife.” Tano stood there, and heard his voice speak, saying, “Hunter, leave the duiker and take me home, for great benefit will come to you through me.” The hunter stood there, trembling all over. He lifted him up and carried him. As he was bringing him back, he met Death. Death said, “Hunter, where are you taking my linguist?” The hunter said, “Father, my eyes are red with desperation, I was looking for meat to take to my wife; I went and saw a duiker, and when I was about to kill it, it said it was fleeing to take refuge with this thing; I said I knew nothing about it. This thing told me to take it home, for there is much benefit in it.” Death asked Tano, saying, “You are going home, and you know that whenever anyone falls sick, I come with my palanquin and set it down beside him, and no one can do anything about it; therefore, as you are going home, what I tell you is this: if someone falls sick, and I do not take him away, I will tell you so that you may take a pacification offering for me; and as for that pacification offering, it is only blood that I want, and you too may take the good meat.” The deity said, “Nana, I have heard, my lord.” The hunter brought Tano home. When Tano stood there, he sang a song, and they responded to it. At daybreak, whenever someone fell sick, they brought him to Tano. If he said, “You will die,” truly the person died. If he said, “You will not die, but bring this and that,” when they brought them, he said, “I am going to beg for life for you.” And because of the things brought to Tano, truly the person recovered his health. This is how it came to be known that deities are a good thing; so people would go to Tano to seek children from him.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
