When this is done, wisdom comes into the nation.

They say that Kwaku Ananse was there, and he swept up all the wisdom, gathered it together, and put it into a gourd. He said he would climb a tree and hang it up high, so that all wisdom would be gone from the earth. Then he set out to go, and when he reached the foot of the tree where he was going to hang it, he tied a rope around the gourd, hung it around his neck, and the gourd ended up in front of him, and he leaned against the tree to climb it. He climbed, climbed, climbed, and slide down! Then he tried again, but he climbed, climbed, climbed, slide down! And his son, Ntikuma, was standing there and said, "Oh! Aren't you ashamed, if only you had turned the gourd and put it on your back, you would have been able to climb."

He said, "Get away with your adult-like behavior." Then he tried to climb again, but failed completely! Then he looked down, and he placed the gourd on his back. This time when he began climbing, kra! kra! kra! he went smoothly up. When he reached the top of the tree, he said, "I, Kwaku Ananse, am ruined today; my little child, while I, being who I am, have gathered all wisdom together, yet some was left behind that I myself did not see, and my small child has taught it to me!" Then he held the gourd, and with great force, hurled it away, and it smashed to pieces! That is why everyone got some of the wisdom, and you who did not get there in time became (pardon the expression) a fool.

This folktale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some of it away and bring some back to me.
