YOU ARE AS SCARY AS A SPIDER

Ananse was living there, and Nyankonpon's mother Nsia fell ill. Then they went and got a herbalist to come and attend to her. When he came, he charged a deposit of one peredwan to treat her illness. But Ananse went and told Nyankonpon, “This fellow has taken your money for nothing, he cannot heal her; let me treat her for you, and if I am unable to heal her, I forfeit my head.” Then Ananse took two asuanu, and he told Nyankonpon, “If I am unable to heal her, I will give you a live lion.” The next day, Nyankonpon's mother Nsia passed away. They said, “Is that not Ananse?” Ananse left the place; he ran away. To cut a long story short, Ananse's very good friend was the Rat. He reported the matter to the Rat, saying, “This is what I have encountered, so help me.” The Rat said, “How should I help you?” He said, “I want you to go and dig a hole reaching right under Nyankonpon's dais.” The Rat said, “I will do it for you.” And he did it. The Rat came and reported to Ananse, saying, “I have finished.” Ananse went and called his son Ntikuma; he carved a horn for him, and he told him to go into the hole, and to go and sit beneath the dais, and when he saw that Nyankonpon had come to sit on the dais, he should blow the horn, saying:

If you kill Ananse, this nation will be ruined!

If you leave Ananse alone, the nation murmurs noisily!”

Ntikuma went and sat under the mound, and Ananse set off, saying that he himself was going to Nyankonpon. Upon his arrival, Nyankonpon's executioners caught him; they took him and made him stand before Nyankonpon. Then the linguist told him, “You, Kwaku Ananse, you told Nyankonpon that if his mother Nsia died, he should kill you; therefore Nsia too has passed away, so we are going to kill you.” Ananse said, “Allow me to speak my defense.” But the executioners seized him to go and kill him. Ntikuma raised his voice: "If you kill Ananse, this nation will be ruined!

If you spare Ananse, the whole nation speaks in turmoil!"

They say, "Listen, listen!" And the horn has blown again:

If you kill Ananse, this nation will be ruined!

If you spare Ananse, the whole nation speaks in turmoil!"

And the Kontirehene stood up and said to Nyankonpon, "You, Nyame, and Asase Yaa own this nation. Today you have captured Ananse to kill him. Asase Yaa says 'Release him'. Therefore, if you release him, it will be good." And Nyankonpon let the executioners release him. That is how it became a proverb, "You are fearful like Ananse."

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
