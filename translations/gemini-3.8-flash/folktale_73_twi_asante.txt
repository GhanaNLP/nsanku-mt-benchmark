One does not leave the elephant to throw stones at the sunbird

ANANSE lived there, and on the day of Akwasidae he went to greet Nyame, and Nyame gave him a lime. Ananse set off and went on his way. As he went, some women were in the water washing clothes. Ananse told them to take the lime and give it to their child. And they took it and gave it to the child. Ananse said, "Give me something, so that I may go." Then they drew water into a gourd bottle and gave it to him, and he took it and went on his way. As he went, some people were clearing a farm, and Ananse gave them the water to drink. And the people gave him a yam. And he went on, and he came upon a blacksmith chewing charcoal. And he gave him the yam, telling him to take it and eat it, for chewing charcoal like that was not good. Ananse said, "Give me something so that I may go." And he gave him a hoe, and he took it and went on his way. And he went and saw a person who was using his penis to stir palm nuts. And Ananse gave him the hoe, and he also gave Ananse palm oil, one pot. And he set off and went on his way. And he came upon a woman who had given birth to twins, and she was using her spittle to rub on them. And Kwaku Ananse gave the oil to the woman so that she might use it to rub on her children. And the woman gave him one child, and he set off with it, went on, and came upon another woman. She had no child and had taken a paddle and adorned it with beads. And Father Ananse gave the child to the woman. And the woman also gave him the paddle, and he set off with it and went on his way. And he came upon someone who was using their elbow to grind flour. And Ananse gave them the paddle, and that person also gave Ananse flour. And he took it and went on, and came upon the Elephant herd. And the Elephant herd was drinking mud. And Ananse said, "Take this flour and drink it, for drinking this mud is not good." And the Elephant herd took it, and they drank it. When they finished drinking, Ananse said,

"Give me something so that I can leave." And they said, "We have nothing, but this river lying here, all of us are going to jump across it, and whoever falls into it, you shall take." It happened that one Elephant was pregnant. And all the animals jumped, and the pregnant one fell into the water, and Ananse went and took her, and he took some thatch and tied the Elephant's foot, and held her. Then he saw a sunbird passing by. And Ananse said, "This bird is beautiful; I will go and catch it for my son Ntikuma." So Ananse followed the sunbird; he threw stones at it; but he did not get it. When he returned, the Elephant was gone. That is why they use it as a proverb: "One does not leave the back of an elephant to throw stones at a sunbird."

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
