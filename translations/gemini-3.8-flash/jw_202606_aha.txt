GUSTAVO JOSEPH'S OWN WORDS

I was born in the year 1947 on a small farm in Cuba. Cuba is located where the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet. I had two younger sisters. And we lived with our parents in a village in Esmeralda.

I remember that there was indeed peace in that town. We lived there with some of our family members. We had food to eat and a little money to support ourselves.

Shortly before I turned five years old, Brother Walton Jones began to study the Bible with my parents. He was an exceptionally zealous preacher. It took him about ten hours to walk to our village. Each time he came, many of our relatives would gather at my uncle's and aunt's house, and they would spend many hours studying the Bible. My parents, my Uncle Pedro, and my Aunt Ela enjoyed what they were learning, and before long they all became Jehovah’s Witnesses. Ela is now nearly 100 years old, yet she still serves as a pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, since the work of Jehovah’s Witnesses had not been banned in Cuba, we had the freedom to worship Jehovah. The people of Cuba knew Jehovah’s Witnesses very well. They were known for witnessing from house to house, and whenever you saw them, they had many Bible-based publications in their bags that they gave to people. We met frequently! Whenever I remember how we worshipped Jehovah with enthusiasm in my youth during that "favorable time," it gives me great joy. But "difficult times" were also on the way.—2 Tim. 4:2.

Ten years ago

When I was nearly five years old, my father and my uncle went to a meeting somewhere in the farm area. Sadly, they went and drank some water that had chemical poison in it, which made them sick. When they returned home, all the hair on my uncle’s head fell out, but he did not die. However, as for my father, he died. He was only 32 years old.

When my father died, my mother decided that we should go and live with her brother who lived in the village of Lombillo. This meant that we had to leave behind our relatives and our dear friends and neighbors whom we loved so much. However, my mother, my sisters, and I continued to serve Jehovah.

On August 26, 1957, I was baptized in a river near the town of Lombillo. I was ten years old. Within less than two years, things changed in the lives of Jehovah's Witnesses in Cuba. In 1959, the government was overthrown and the communist government came to power.

26 Month of June

The government at that time wanted to recruit many energetic young men. This made things very difficult for Jehovah’s Witnesses, because they would not go to war and would not get involved in political matters. For this reason, the government banned their work. We no longer had the freedom to worship Jehovah, and they put many of the brothers in prison. They often mistreated some of them and gave them very little food. At times, there was even blood in the food given to them, but as we know, the Bible says we should not eat blood.

Even though a ban had been placed on the work, we continued to meet together to worship Jehovah. (Heb. 10:25) We gathered secretly in various places in the country to hold meetings. I remember very well that one day, a brother allowed us to use his sheepfold to hold a meeting. We could not even remove the sheep from the pen or clean it up. Therefore, we held the meeting together with all the sheep!—Mic. 2:12.

We truly appreciated the hard work the brothers did to help us continue to receive spiritual food at that time. For example, the entire assembly program was recorded on cassettes and distributed to all the brothers in Cuba. Even so, only two brothers were assigned to prepare all the talks that would be given at the assembly. When they finished preparing, they delivered the talks and recorded them on cassette. Because the brothers recorded the talks at night, we sometimes heard the crowing of roosters and other strange noises in the background. If we held the meeting in a place without electricity, the brothers connected a generator to a stationary bicycle and had someone pedal the bicycle. When the bicycle was pedaled, it produced electricity from the machine attached to it. This enabled them to play the program for all of us to hear. Although we did not have the freedom to serve Jehovah or many Bible publications like the brothers in other lands, we still received what was necessary to remain close to Jehovah. Indeed, we were happy that we continued to serve Jehovah.—Neh. 8:10.

What two covenants did the apostle Paul speak about in Galatians 4:24?

◼ After the apostle Paul finished speaking about the relationship between Abraham, Sarah, and Hagar, he added: “These things may be taken in a symbolic sense; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, we understood that the two covenants were the Law covenant and the new covenant. But after we reexamined this matter more deeply, we came to see that an adjustment needed to be made. It would seem that Paul was not speaking about the new covenant, but rather about the Abrahamic covenant. Here are the reasons why that adjustment was made.*

Paul stated that the servant girl Hagar stands for Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) This means that the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai in the year 1513 B.C.E. corresponds to Hagar. (Ex. 19:5, 6) And there was no human who could keep the Law perfectly. Therefore, that law reminded the Israelites that they were slaves to sin. Also, this arrangement was to help the Israelites identify the Messiah. He is the only person who could keep the Law perfectly. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, he made it possible for imperfect humans to be set free from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) When Jesus fulfilled God's purpose, there was no longer a need for the Law.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul said that Sarah, “was a free woman and her child was born through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) And that promise is the Abrahamic covenant. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) It is spoken about in Genesis 22:18. It says: “Your

That covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham revealed much about the promise Jehovah made in Eden. (Gen. 3:15) It made it clear that the “offspring” spoken of would come through Abraham. Traditional Fishing Gears Used in the Olden Days

Did you know that in the Bible, some of Jesus' disciples were fishermen? (Mk. 1:16-20) And they used cast nets to catch fish in the Sea of Galilee.

And they used thread to weave it in a way that created spaces. They used nets with larger meshes to catch big fish, and they also used nets with small meshes to catch small fish. Furthermore, they tied stones or lead around the bottom of the nets so that they could sink into the water. Also, they used another type of net, and for that one, they attached corks or plastic around the top of the net so that it could float on the surface of the water.

The sea rocks and a great catch of fish could tear or damage the net. (Lk. 5:6) Yet the nets were very expensive. Therefore, it was very important for fishermen to mend their nets every time they used them for work. The Bible spoke about James and John when they were mending their nets. And it is likely that they were doing this with thread and a needle. (Mt. 4:21) Luke also spoke about fishermen who were “washing their nets” so that the weeds and mud on the net would come off. (Lk. 5:2) After that, the fishermen dried their nets so that they would not rot.—Eze. 47:10.

Jesus invited fishermen who used nets to catch fish to come and become "fishers of men."
