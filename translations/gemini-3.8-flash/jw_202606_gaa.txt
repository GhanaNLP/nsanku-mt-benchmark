10 How could it damage your friendship? If a brother or sister behaves differently from how you do, it might be difficult for you to become friends and even for your friendship to last. LIFE STORY

I Have Served Jehovah for About 70 Years in Cuba

THE ONE WHO PROPHESIED IS GUSTAVO JOSEPH

I was born in 1947 in Cuba, a beautiful island located where the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean meet. Three of us were born, me and my two sisters. I am the oldest. Together with our parents, making five of us, we lived in a village called Esmeralda.

I remember very well how pleasant and peaceful life in the village was. At that time, some of our relatives, including our aunts, uncles, grandfathers, and grandmothers, lived very close to us. We always had food and money to take care of ourselves, and this brought us great happiness.

When I was about five years old, a brother named Walton Jones started studying the Bible with my parents. He was very zealous and would walk about ten hours to our village to preach. Every time he came, many of my relatives would gather at my grandparents' house, and he would discuss Bible topics with them for many hours. My parents, my uncle Pedro, and my aunt Ela enjoyed what they were learning, and before long, they were baptized and became Jehovah's Witnesses. Currently, Ela is nearly 100 years old and continues to serve as a pioneer in Cuba.

At that time, the government did not oppose Jehovah’s Witnesses, so they preached and attended congregation meetings without interference. People in Cuba knew Jehovah’s Witnesses well because we went to their homes to preach. We always had the organization’s literature with us to give to them. Back then, we walked a lot! When I remember how we served Jehovah in those “favorable times” when I was younger, it brings me great joy. But it was not long before we faced “difficult times.”—2 Tim. 4:2.

Which two covenants did Paul speak of in Galatians 4:24?

■ The apostle Paul spoke about the relationship between Abraham and Sarah, and Abraham and Hagar. Afterward, he stated: “These things may be taken as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, we understood that the Law covenant and the new covenant were the two covenants Paul was speaking about. But as we studied these scriptures again, we now see that we must explain it in a different way. It seems that Paul was speaking about the Law covenant and the covenant God made with Abraham, rather than the Law covenant and the new covenant. Here are the reasons why Paul spoke about these two covenants.*

The Law covenant was made at Mount Sinai in the year 1513 B.C.E. Paul said that Hagar represented this Law covenant. (Ex. 19:5, 6; Gal. 4:25) The Jews were imperfect, therefore, they could not keep the Law perfectly. The Law covenant made them realize that they were slaves to sin, and that was one reason it was given. Another reason the Law covenant was given was to help the Jews recognize the Messiah when he came. The Messiah was a perfect human, and he was the only one who could keep the Law perfectly. When the Messiah sacrificed his perfect life, that opened the way for imperfect humans to be freed from sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) When Jesus fulfilled God's purpose, the Law covenant came to an end.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul also compared Hagar to Sarah, who was the “free woman” who gave birth “through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) The promise referred to is the covenant God made with Abraham. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) In Genesis 22:18, Jehovah spoke about this covenant. He said: “By means of your offspring [that is, Abraham’s offspring] all nations of the earth will obtain a blessing for themselves because you have listened to my voice.”

The covenant that God made with Abraham revealed more details about the promise that Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) It showed that the prophesied “offspring” would come through Abraham. Paul explained that Jesus is the primary part of this prophesied offspring. However, the Scriptures show that those who “belong to Christ,” that is, the 144,000 anointed ones who will rule with Jesus in his Kingdom, are also a secondary part of this offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) When God’s promise to Abraham is fulfilled, all who exercise faith in Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings.

What did Paul want to show by this “symbolic drama”? He made it clear that if the Jews of his day insisted that the Law must continue to be obeyed, then they would continue to be slaves like Hagar. But if they believed in Jesus Christ, who is the ‘offspring’ of Abraham, then they would be free like Sarah. Indeed, they would be completely free from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) But if they did not believe in Jesus, that would mean they did not understand the purpose of giving the Law covenant, because it was given to lead them to the Christ.

Many of the Christians in Galatia to whom Paul wrote this letter were not Jews, and therefore, they were not under the Law. But some Jews in the congregation strongly insisted that non-Jewish Christians must keep the Law, including the law of circumcision. Paul said that it was not wise for them to do that, because as soon as they believed in Christ, they were freed from under the Law and were no longer slaves to the Law.—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

A BIBLE STORY YOU CAN BELIEVE

The net with which people catch fish

Some of Jesus' disciples were fishermen. (Mar. 1:16-20) They caught fish with nets in the Sea of Galilee.

How were nets made in Jesus' day? Most often, nets were woven from ropes made from flax plants. If a fisherman wanted to catch small fish, he used nets with small meshes. But if he wanted to catch large fish, he used nets with large meshes. Often, lead weights or stones were attached to the nets with smaller meshes so that when cast into the water, they could sink to the bottom and catch fish. But the larger nets or drag nets were drawn into the water to catch fish. Weights were attached to the bottom of the dragnet so it could sink, and floats were attached to the top so that the entire net would not sink to the bottom.

Nets were very expensive, and they required careful handling so that they would not be ruined. Why? Because the stones in the water could tear them. Furthermore, if a large catch of fish was caught, they could tear. (Luke 5:6) The Bible says that James and John were ‘mending their nets.’ (Matt. 4:21) Often, fishermen mended their nets after returning from fishing. Luke also says that the fishermen were ‘washing their nets.’ (Luke 5:2) When the fishermen washed their nets, they spread them out so that they would not rot.—Ezek. 47:10.

Jesus invited some fishermen to become “fishers of men.”
