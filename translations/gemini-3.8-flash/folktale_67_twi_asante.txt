ANANSE SAID HE WAS GOING TO LOOK FOR A FOOL, BUT HE HIMSELF TURNED OUT TO BE THE FOOL

ANANSE was there, and he said he was looking for a fool to set fish-traps with him. He wandered, wandered, and wandered, but did not find one. Then he went and met Osansa (the Hawk). He said, "Let us go and set fish-traps." Osansa said, "I have meat to eat." And afterwards he went and met Anene (the Crow), and he said, "Let us go and set fish-traps." Anene said, "I have heard." Then Osansa said to Anene, "The fish-trap you are going to set with Ananse, do not go, for Ananse says he wants a fool so that he can set traps with him and take all the money." Anene said, "Never mind, for I will take all the money." Anene and Ananse set off into the forest to cut palm branches. Anene said to Ananse, "Give me the cutlass so that I cut them, and you take the tiredness." Ananse said, "Do you take me for a fool? Let me cut them, and you take the tiredness." Ananse was cutting, and Anene sat there singing accompaniment. When he finished cutting, they tied them up. Anene said, "Ananse, let me carry the trap materials, and you take the tiredness." Ananse said, "Get away, do you take me for a fool?" And he carried them. Anene followed behind, singing. When they came home, Anene said, "Let me weave the fish-trap, and you take the tiredness." Ananse said, "Never, let me weave it, and you take the tiredness." So Anene went and lay on his back, singing. Ananse said, "Fool, you think you are clever, but you are dying." When Ananse finished the fish-trap, Anene said, "Let me take the fish-trap to the river, and you take the tiredness." Ananse said, "No, I am taking it, and you take the tiredness." They arrived at the river; Anene said, "Father Ananse, there is a certain creature in the water, so let me step in and place this fish-trap inside, and if the creature bites me, you take its death." Ananse said, "Ah! You truly take me for a fool; I myself will go and set it in, and if it bites me, you take the death." Ananse went and set the trap inside, and they came home. The next day, they went to inspect the fish-trap, and it had caught two mudfish. Anene said, "Ananse, you take these two, and tomorrow when we come it will have caught four, and I will take them." Ananse said, "Ah! You fellow, you truly take me for a fool; you take these two, and tomorrow I will take the four." Anene took them. The next day, they went and it had caught four. Anene said, "You take these four, and tomorrow when we come it will have caught eight, and I will take them." Ananse said, "You take me for a fool; you take these four, and tomorrow I will take the eight." The next day they went, and it had caught eight. Anene said, "Take them, and tomorrow I will take sixteen." Ananse said, "Do you take me for a fool? You take the eight, and tomorrow I will take the sixteen." By this time, the fish-trap was ruined, and Anene said to Ananse, "Go and bring the worn-out fish-trap, for if you sell it, each one is worth two peredwan; so you, Father Ananse, take these fish and give me this worn-out fish-trap." Ananse said, "No, you go and sell the fish and keep the money, and I too will go and sell this worn-out fish-trap and keep the money." They carried their loads to the market. In no time, all of Anene’s fish were sold out; he got a great deal of money. But Ananse was left with his load. And Crow told him, "Take these dead nsoa fish and go hawking them, and people will buy them." Ananse picked them up; he called out, "I am selling dead nsoa fish!" The village chief heard him and said, "Where does this fellow come from? Go call him to come here." Ananse went. The chief said, "Do you believe that we who are here in this town are fools? While your companion is selling mudfish, did you not see, that you have come to sell dead nsoa fish to us?" The chief said, "Beat him." And they flogged him. Then Crow said, "Ananse, do you see? You are disgraced; you said you wanted a fool to work with, do you now see that it is you who are the fool?"
