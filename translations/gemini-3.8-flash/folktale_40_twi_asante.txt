Sit there and you will see

There once lived a woman and her child. Whenever she sent him on an errand, he would say, "I will not go." His mother would say, "Stay there and you will see." Every day she sent him, he said, "I will not go." And his mother told him, "Stay there and you will see." One day the child said, "Every day mother tells me, 'Stay there and you will see'; today indeed, I am going to look for 'I will see' and see it." And he set off. As he was going, an Avocado tree bore small fruit. The Avocado tree asked him, "Why does no one come here, yet you have come here?" The child said, "My mother, with whom I live, says, 'Stay there and you will see,' and I said I am coming to look for 'I will see' and see it." The Avocado tree said, "I am one, but the big one is ahead of me." The child set off again. As he went along, he saw that a Tall Flame met him. The Tall Flame asked him, "Why does no one take this path, yet you have taken it?" The child said, "Every day, mother says, 'I will see', and I said I am coming to look for 'I will see' and see it." The Tall Flame said, "I am one of them, but go forward." And the child set off. As he went along, Sparks of Fire were placed upon a potsherd, and were coming; they met him. The Spark of Fire said, "Why does no one come here, yet you have come here?" The child said, "When I am with my mother, she says, 'I will see', and I said I am coming to look for 'I will see' and see it." The Spark of Fire said, "I am one, but go forward, for the big one is over there." The child set off again. When he arrived, Parrots were plastering a house. The Parrots said, "As for this, we will get someone's blood to sprinkle on this house." Now the child reached there, and they said, "Why has no one come here, yet you have come here?" He said, "Every day that I am with my mother, she says, 'Stay there and you will see,' and I said I am coming to look for 'I will see' and see it." Then the Parrots seized the child, and they cut off his head, and they poured his blood into a brass basin; they sprinkled some on the house. Then the Parrots flew off and perched on a tall tree, and whenever one came, it dipped its tail into the blood, and its tail became red. All the Parrots dipped their tails into it, so that they made it a sign. Then one day, one of the Parrots said it was going to feed. And it went and met the child's mother. And the mother asked the Parrot, "My child, with whom I live, was as lazy as anything. And when I got up, I told him to stay there and he would see; excuse my saying so, in his foolishness he said he was going to look for 'I will see' and see it. It has been a week today since I have seen him, and I beg of you, if you have seen him, bring him to me." The Parrot said, "Oh! he came to our town, and we too, we killed him. It is some of his blood that we have used as a sign that is on my tail. Therefore, as you are going, go and tell all your people that whenever anyone tells someone, 'Stay there and you will see', they have placed a curse upon them."

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
