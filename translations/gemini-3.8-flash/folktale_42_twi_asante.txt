We are not alike, not alike at all

If it is possible, the leopard and the antelope will become friends

OKUROTWIAMANSA (the Leopard) lived there, and he said he wanted someone to form a partnership with him. None of all the animals was able to. Antelope was sitting there; he said, “Father Leopard, as for me, I will form a partnership with you, so what do you say?” Leopard said, “That is what I want.” And they formed a partnership. Leopard tapped a palm tree. When Antelope went and drank the wine, he became drunk; he could not get up, and he fell asleep under the palm tree. In the evening, Leopard decided to go and check his thing. When he arrived, there was Antelope, having drunk his wine and lying there completely drunk. Leopard said, “Well, you who said you would form a partnership with me. Even Elephant could not form a partnership with me without suffering.” Leopard went and cut palm fronds, wove a basket, put Antelope into it, and lifted it onto his head. On the way, Antelope sobered up midway along the path. He looked and saw that Leopard was carrying him. Antelope looked up, and a certain vine was hanging there. Antelope grasped it tightly, snap! Silently, Antelope defecated into the basket. Leopard knew nothing about it, and he carried it home. He said to his mother, “That old fool Antelope, who said he would form a partnership with me, I have brought him; put the palm nuts on the fire.” Leopard’s mother told her child, “The soup is ready, so bring him for me to kill him.” When Leopard looked into the basket, empty! He was gone, leaving only his dung. Leopard said, “Mother, he is gone, and only his dung is left; let us put it into the soup.” And they put it into the soup, finished the food, and sat there. The little old woman licked it thoroughly. She said, “Ah! Kwadwo, if this is his dung, what about his meat; then it will be sweeter than tiger nuts, so wherever he is lying, bring him!” Then Leopard set off and went to his palm tree. When he arrived, there was Antelope again, dead drunk and lying there. Leopard said, “Oh! Previously I was foolish, but this time I am going home to bring a wooden box.” Leopard went home, took out all his cloths and threw them aside, and took the box and the key to the palm tree. He took Antelope, put him into the box, locked it, lifted it, and carried it on his head. When he reached home, he said, “Mother, I have brought him; open the room so I can put him inside.” The little old woman opened it, and he put him inside. Then Leopard told his mother, “I am going to the North to look for onions, salt, and black pepper to bring, so that we can use Antelope to make soup and eat.” Leopard set off and went. When day broke, the little old woman was pounding palm nuts, and Antelope looked through a hole and saw the old woman. Antelope said, “Grandmother, you are pitiable, and if only you would open for me, I would come and pound it for you.” The old woman said, “You, Antelope, you will run away.” Antelope said, “May Aserampon, and the great Tano, and Dwomo, and Apeya, and Konkroma, and Little Tano, and Krakye Dente kill me if you open for me and I run away. If you wish, close all the doors.” The old woman said, “I have heard.” She closed the doors and let Antelope out. Antelope came, took hold of the pestle, and pounded the palm nuts. As the little old woman bent down to pick something up from the ground, Antelope struck the back of her head with the pestle, smash! and she died. Antelope skinned her, draped her skin over himself, cut up her meat, and put it on the fire. Before long, Kwadwo arrived. Adowa said, "Why have you stayed so long like this, I have seen an antelope and killed it; its meat is even on the fire right now." Osebo said, "Well done, Hunter!" He too added the things he had brought to the soup. They finished the food; when they sat down there and were eating it, Osebo took a bone. When he crunched it, it went, pu! pu! Adowa mumbled under his breath, "It is your mother's bone that you are crunching." Osebo said, "What did you say?" Adowa said, "I said, this antelope has grown old; its bones are hard." Osebo said, "Truly, even I cannot crunch it." Then Osebo slurped the soup, feao! feao! Adowa said, "Ei! It is the soup of your mother's head that you are slurping." Osebo said, "What did you say?" Adowa said, "I was saying, even its dung, let alone its meat, have you seen how delicious it is?" Osebo said, "Extraordinary, extraordinary, extraordinary indeed!" Now they finished eating. Adowa swept around the house; he told Osebo, "I am going to throw trash onto the rubbish dump." Osebo said, "Go, and which way should I take, am I coming?" Adowa reached the rubbish dump. He said, "Osebo! Osebo! Are you not a fool, are you not a beast, that I would have killed your mother and made soup for you to eat?" Osebo said, "Will I reach you there?" Adowa said, "You will bypass me." Osebo ran swiftly, yiridi! yiridi! and Adowa also took off, prada! prada! prada! Adowa reached his house, and he went into his room. The way he built it, there was no way in, except for a single path he had left there. Adowa went inside and slammed the door shut, bam! Now Osebo searched, but he did not see the single path through which Adowa went. Osebo set off and went to call all the animals, saying, "Come let us go catch Adowa for me, for the foolishness he has treated me with, unless you catch him for me before I tell you what happened." All the animals came to Adowa's house. Adowa heard that the animals were coming to the house to catch him, so he took pepper, salt, and onions and ground them ready. Now the animals stood around. They looked into each other's faces. Then Rat said, "Kwakuo (Monkey) has a long tail, so let him climb first, and let us use his tail to reach the house." Kwakuo climbed the house, kra! kra! kra! and he was up. He sat on the house, and as he bent down to look at Adowa, Adowa took the pepper he had ground, put it into his mouth, and spat it into Kwakuo's eyes. Then Kwakuo fell down. The animals came and saw that something was under his eyes. One of them tasted it by licking. He said, "Ye! Fellows come, the thing that Kwakuo has gone to bring." All the animals came. They licked it, licked it, licked it. And so Kwakuo's eye area became very hollowed out. Then they said, "Bush-pig, you also root around, root around! so that you can reach the house." Adowa heard this, and he went to sharpen his knife, namnamnam. Bush-pig rooted around, rooted around! and his snout emerged from the house. Then Adowa placed the knife at Bush-pig's snout and cut it. Bush-pig said, "Kokotee Asamoa, he is cutting my snout." The animals standing behind said, "Well done, Hunter; endure it and bring him out." Bush-pig pulled his head out; his snout was covered in blood. When the animals saw that Kokotee Asamoa's snout had been cut, they scattered from there, yii! And so it happened that Wild Boar's mouth became twisted. Now when the animals ran away, Leopard did not know what to do. He said, "Then I am not going anywhere; it is right here that I will lie down and die, unless I kill Antelope." When Antelope heard this, he took his mother's skin and covered himself with it, climbed over the roof, and came down behind it. Then he came to Leopard. He said, "Oh! Kwadwo, why have you become so lean? What is Antelope anyway? Let us go home and prepare ourselves and come back." They set off and went home. Antelope said to Leopard, "Bring out all your possessions and give them to me so that I may keep them safe." Leopard said, "May you be blessed!" And he brought out all his things and gave them to Antelope. As soon as Antelope got hold of them, he took to his heels and went to stand far off. He called, "Leopard!" He replied, "My brother!" Antelope said, "If you were not a fool and a beast, would I have killed your mother and made soup for you to eat? And now too, have I not deceived you and taken away your possessions?" Leopard said, "Shall I reach you?" Antelope said, "You will bypass me." Then Leopard took off in pursuit, dashing forward, and Antelope also took off running. He went inside his house and shut the door firmly, bam! Then Leopard stood there, furious; he wagged his tail rapidly, back and forth. He did not know what to say. Then he said to Antelope, "I swear by Thursday, come, let us be friends." Antelope said, "I will not come unless you let us drink the gods' oath." Leopard said, "I agree, for I cannot do anything to you." Antelope came, and he and Leopard drank the fetish oath. Antelope told him, "Never catch me; if you come across me caught in a trap, release me." Leopard said, "I have heard." Leopard also said, "Even the path that you walk on, I will wipe it with my paw so that you will know that I have no quarrel with you." And so, to this day, Leopard does not catch Antelope. Moreover, if a trap catches Antelope, he goes and releases him.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
