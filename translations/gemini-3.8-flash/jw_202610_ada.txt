THE PAST CENTURY

The English Watchtower of January 1, 1926, said, "It will not be long before God will glorify his name, and he will choose some people on the earth as his witnesses."

In 1926, Jehovah, through Jesus Christ, the head of the congregation, helped the Christians to understand that the preaching work was the most important work they needed to do. Therefore, “the faithful and discreet slave” produced new publications for God's people, made adjustments to how their meetings were conducted, and explained clearly what all Christians ought to do. (Matt. 24:45) Jesus helped them to obtain buildings and printing presses to produce literature so that they could carry out the preaching work.

NEW PUBLICATION AND CHANGES MADE TO CONGREGATION MEETINGS

In April, The Golden Age (now known as Awake!) announced that a new book entitled Deliverance would be produced. This book would replace the book Studies in the Scriptures, and this new book would become the main book to be used in the preaching work. In order to produce many books, the brothers at Bethel went and bought a new printing press, which could print 22 of this book in just one minute. The Bible Students gave this book to everyone they preached to.

For many years, the Bible Students studied the book Studies in the Scriptures at their meetings. But beginning in May, they will start studying the articles in The Watchtower. What brought about this change?

A congregation wrote a letter to Brother Rutherford, saying: “Every Sunday, we study the Watchtower articles that the congregation has arranged for us to study. We are happy that the light is shining for all of us, and we quickly understand the new adjustments that are made.”

“The shining light” or new changes that were made helped the Bible Students to make the preaching work a primary thing in their lives. For example, an article in the Watch Tower showed that an effort a Christian would make to change his conduct, and to show kindness and respect toward others is not enough, rather, he must preach. The Watch Tower continued, “Jehovah has commanded Christians to be his witnesses. Therefore, since all Christians love Jehovah, they must obey this command and be ready to preach.”

Another Watchtower article entitled “Sacrifice and Service” showed that true Christians demonstrate love for Jehovah through their faithful service to him. The Watchtower article encouraged the Bible Students to do everything they could to boldly preach the good news to more people.—Isa. 43:10, 12; Matt. 24:14.

These changes that were made greatly encouraged the brothers in the congregations. A few weeks later, the July 15, 1926 issue of the English Watch Tower mentioned a letter that a brother wrote. It said: “The Watch Tower articles that

CONVENTIONS GAVE GOD’S PEOPLE STRENGTH TO PREACH ZEALOUSLY

At the end of May, the Bible Students held a convention in London, England. At that time, all the printing establishments in Britain went on strike. Therefore, the brothers at Brooklyn Bethel spent many hours printing 117,000 copies of a booklet entitled The Standard for the People. This booklet was distributed to all who attended the convention.

On Saturday, May 29, the brothers immediately began using the tract. The program for that day said: “Let all Bible Students present preach the good news about Jesus and the Kingdom to the people. Therefore, get up early in the morning and go to the Alexandra Palace, and the brothers there will show you what to do.”

More than 100,000 books were distributed. The English Watchtower of July 15, 1926, said: “All the brothers were very happy, because they felt that they had obeyed the command that Jehovah gave to preach the good news.”

The next day, Brother Rutherford gave a public lecture at the Royal Albert Hall, explaining what would happen to the world powers. As soon as the doors were opened, 13,000 people filled the hall, and those who could not get inside listened to the lecture outside through various speakers.

In Brother Rutherford’s talk, he showed that the rulers were unsuccessful because they rejected Jehovah and his Kingdom. Brother Rutherford went on to speak against the League of Nations, showing that it would fail just as had been foretold.

Make Sure Your Faith Remains Strong

When Jehovah’s Witnesses were studying the Bible with you, what convinced you that you had found “one faith,” which is the way Jehovah wants to be worshipped? (Eph. 4:4-6) Did you draw close to Jehovah’s people because you noticed that there was love among them? Or were you pleased with them because of their good conduct? In fact, many people want to associate with Jehovah’s organization because we make them feel welcome and show them genuine love, and rightly so. For the love we show and our good conduct are what identify us as true Christians.—John 13:35; 1 Pet. 2:12.

But suppose a brother offends you, or he does not show love to you in some way. Or suppose a brother commits a serious sin, like what happened in the Corinthian congregation that the apostle Paul spoke about? (1 Cor. 5:1) If your faith is based only on how other people's actions make you feel, then it means your faith is not solid. In that case, what will help you to build your faith on a strong foundation?

WHAT WILL HELP YOU TO STRENGTHEN YOUR FAITH?

If you continue to reflect on the basic Bible teachings you have learned and gain strong conviction that they are the truth, it will greatly strengthen your faith. Here is an example of this.

At one time, many of Jesus’ followers stopped following him. What Jesus said about eating his flesh and drinking his blood stumbled them, and they said: “This speech is shocking; who can listen to it?” What Jesus said troubled them greatly, therefore, they stopped following him.—John 6:56-60, 66.

After that, Jesus asked the twelve disciples: “Do you also want to leave?” Simon Peter answered him: “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of everlasting life. We have believed and come to know that you are the Holy One of God.”—John 6:67-69.

What made Peter and the other apostles different from those who stumbled? Peter and the other apostles based their faith on a solid foundation, not on emotions. Peter said: “We have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God.” These faithful men knew what they believed and why they believed it. Because they compared Jesus' actions and each of his words with what they had learned from the Scriptures, they were convinced that Jesus was the Messiah. For this reason, their faith was firm, and they did not stop following Jesus when others stumbled and stopped following him.

What can we learn from this matter? The things we have come to learn and know are much more powerful than how we feel. Why do we say that? Because Bible teachings, such as God's name, the promises God has made, and the ransom sacrifice that we have come to learn from the Bible will never change. But our feelings can change. For example, people's actions or a few words from them can make us happy,

EXPERIENCE OF THINGS

Jehovah Blesses Us When We Trust in Him

Emma worked three days a week at a hotel, and these were the hours she worked each day. One day, she asked her supervisor for just one day off during the week so that she could attend the upcoming circuit assembly. Emma's supervisor did not agree to her request; instead, he told Emma that from that day on, she would have to work five days a week. It meant that if Emma did not do so, then she must quit the job. But Emma trusted in Jehovah and chose to quit the job, so she attended the circuit assembly.

When Emma wrote a letter that she wanted to quit her job, she also went to see the hotel manager himself there. When she told the manager what happened, the manager promised her that he would take care of the matter for her. But two weeks later, Emma was told that she had been dismissed from the job. Unexpectedly, Emma met the hotel manager again, and when she told him what happened, he was very angry that Emma's supervisor did not follow his instructions and dismissed Emma from the job. Because of this, he dealt with the matter immediately, and a different arrangement was made for Emma. Now, she would work five days a week, but she would work for only four hours each day. This new arrangement was very good for Emma, because she had more time to engage in the pioneer work and other activities in Jehovah's service. Jehovah provided for her needs even more than she had expected.
