There was a young girl named Kwaboaso. Whenever she was offered to anyone, she would say, "I do not want him." She was offered to a hunter, and she said, "Small fry! One whose body is dirty like this, I do not want him." One day, the child went to the farm, saying she was going to cut plantain. As soon as she struck the plantain with the cutlass, behold, mmoatia (little forest spirits) were sitting on the plantain; then they alighted and caught hold of Kwaboaso; they said, "Is it you that whenever you are offered to anyone, you say puff! puff! (expressing disgust)?" And the mmoatia held her, and they said:

Let us press it.

We are enduring, we are enduring at Kwaboaso.

Let us press it.

We are trying."

And the messenger who had been given Kwaboaso, when he heard the voice of Kwaboaso, said,

"I am going to see, for one should not repay evil with evil." He went, and Kwaboaso was there with the little forest spirits (mmoatia) gathered in great numbers. Then the hunter fired a gun at the spirits, and one fell dead. The elder among the spirits said, "He has drunk wine and become drunk; lay him down there for now." The hunter fired again, and another one fell. The elder of the spirits said, "The valiant one has drunk wine and become drunk; lay him down there." The hunter killed all the spirits, leaving only the elder. The elder called the hunter, saying, "Come." The hunter said, "I will not come." The elder of the spirits said, "Oh, come, for I will not do anything to you." The hunter went. The elder of the spirits said, "Look inside my room there; you will see all your gunpowder and bullets that you have shot. Take your things, and take Kwaboaso along with you. But before you go, go and cut some bananas, and as you go, pluck and throw them down, so that if the spirits wake up and try to catch you, by the time they gather these bananas one by one, you will have gone very, very far." And indeed, he went and cut the bananas, took Kwaboaso along with him, and whenever he reached a point along the way, he threw one down. In this manner, he continued until he reached home. Just before he reached the house, he looked and saw that the spirits were still coming. Then he threw down the one remaining banana, and the spirits went and picked it up and turned back. The hunter then took Kwaboaso and handed him over to his family. Then the hunter went to his house. Now, while the hunter was there, he saw messengers arrive before him, and he asked, "What is the matter?" The messengers said, "Kwaboaso says she fled to the Chief, saying that now she will marry you." The hunter said, "I thank the Chief, but as for the woman, I cannot marry her, for she does not please me in the least."

That is why the elders say, "Whoever you are given to, marry them, for you do not know who will rescue you when you are in need one day."

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
