How We Can Obey Jehovah From the Heart

“You became obedient from the heart.”—ROM. 6:17.

There are many people who obey the law only when they see that someone who has authority over them is watching them. Others also obey the law because they fear that if they do not follow it, they will be punished. There are still others who follow the law simply so that people will praise them. Those who are like that act like the religious leaders who lived in Jesus’ day. They claimed to obey God and follow his laws, but that was not the case at all. They only wanted people to think that they were very righteous. (Matt. 23:5) But as for us, we obey Jehovah because we know that if we apply what he teaches us in our lives, it will go well with us. (Isa. 48:17, 18) If we have this mindset and obey Jehovah because of it, that is good. But there is also an even more important reason why we obey Jehovah.

Because the Egyptians worshipped sheep, so they feared that if they sacrificed the sheep, the Egyptians might become very angry with them and harm them. A BIBLE STORY

Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts

Colossians 3:15 speaks of “the peace of Christ.” What does “the peace of Christ” mean? It means the inner calm and composure a person can have when he becomes a disciple of Christ. What gives such a person peace of mind is knowing that Jehovah and his Son Jesus love him, and this brings him peace.—John 14:23, 27.

The apostle Paul encouraged the Christians in the Colossian congregation to let the peace of Christ rule in their hearts, or to have control over how they truly live. How could they do this? The Greek word Paul used that has been translated as "rule" could be used to describe an umpire or referee in a game; because of the authority he has, whatever decision he makes is final. In the same way, whatever the "peace of Christ" that we desire to have directs us to do is precisely what we must do.

If a misunderstanding arises between us and our brother, we should let the “peace of Christ” guide us. If we do so, it will help us see what we can do to maintain peace and unity between us and our brothers. And to be able to do what it directs us to do, it may sometimes be necessary to set aside our personal preferences or forgive our brother’s fault. If we do that, then we are allowing the “peace of Christ” to rule in our hearts.—Col. 3:12-14.
