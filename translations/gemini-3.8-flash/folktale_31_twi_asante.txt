Just as we will see the cat lying on the mat, so too will the dog lie by the fireside.

It is said that there was a certain woman who, whenever she gave birth, the child would die; whenever she gave birth, it would die. So she set off to a certain fetish deity to ask for children. The fetish said, "I will give you one, but this child will only cause you debt; that is the work he will do for you, and one day he will pay the debt." Not two days, not three days passed, and the woman became pregnant. She gave birth—a child of folktales does not take long to grow—and the child grew up to become a fine young man. One day he was there and said, "Mother, give me money so that I may go to the Coast to buy salt." His mother said, "How much?" He said, "Two asuanu." His mother gave it to him, and he set off. As he was going, he met a man whose dog was very skinny. He said, "Give it to me to buy." The dog's owner said, "You cannot buy it." The child said, "How much?" The dog's owner said, "Two asuanu." He said, "What of it! Take the two asuanu." He took the dog and brought it home. When he arrived, his mother said, "Why, didn't you reach there?" He said, "I used the money to buy a dog." His mother said, "Oh!" Later on, about one month passed. He said, "Mother, give me money so that I may go trading." She said, "Indeed, you will take money to waste it again; how much do you want?" He said, "Two asuanu and a suru." She said, "Take it." And he set out on the trading journey. As he went, he met a man holding a cat. He said, "Man, give me this cat to buy." He replied, "When I sleep in my room, mice gnaw at my feet, that is why I bought it." He said, "I beg you, give it to me." He replied, "You cannot buy it." The child said, "How much will you take for it?" He said, "Two asuanu and a suru." He said, "Because of this? Take it." The child took the cat and brought it home. When he arrived home, he said, "Mother, here is what I went to buy!" She said, "Eh! Just as we said." The child stayed there. It was about forty days later that he said to his mother, "Give me money to go trading." His mother said, "All my money is finished; what is left is three asuasa. If I give this to you, and you go and do not use it to buy goods, then that will be the end of it." The child said, "I understand." At daybreak, he lifted his bag, and off he went! As he went, he met an Asante man holding a dove. He said, "Friend, give me your animal to buy." He replied, "I will not sell it, for I play with it." He said, "I will buy it." The bird's owner said, "I will not sell it, for I know what it will do for me." The child said, "Oh! Give it to me." He replied, "Can you buy it?" The child said, "How much?" He said, "Three asuasa." He said, "Because of this? Take it." He took the bird home. His mother said, "It is not well, is this what you have brought?" He said, "This is what I have brought." While the child was there, the Dove called him, saying, "Come!" When he went, the Dove told him, "I am a chief in my town, and I decided to travel, but a certain fellow caught me, and you, by your grace, you too have bought me. I beg of you, if you take me back to my town, we would thank you very much." The child said, "You are lying, you will fly away." The Dove said, "If you do not believe it, take a rope, tie it to my leg, and lead me along." The child took a rope, tied it to its leg, and followed it little by little until they reached the Dove's town. When they reached the outskirts of the town, children were playing marbles. When they saw him, they said, "Here is the chief! Here is the chief!" One went and told the Krontihene, and he seized him and cut his throat, saying, "You are making fun of our grief." Then another went again, and the same thing happened. Then the Akwamuhene said, "Gyaasehene, you yourself get up and go take a look." He went to look and came back. He said, "It is true!" Then they took a palanquin and regalia, went, and brought the chief home. He gave the whole town the news that he went on a journey and a certain fellow caught him, and that by the grace of this child who bought him, today he had brought him back. All the elders and children rose up and thanked the child. The Queen Mother gave a pot full of gold dust; all the elders gave pot after pot full. The chief himself looked at his hand, took off a ring, and gave it to him. And he said, "Take this ring, and whatever you want, this ring will do it for you." He said, "I have heard." And with the ring he set off to his town; and he took the gold and the ring to show to his mother. Now his mother said, "Welcome, Aku, welcome, Aku!" Previously, before he had the gold, whenever he came and called his mother, she would not answer him. Then he gave his mother the news, saying, "You see this gold and this ring; I am going to build a great town for us to live in." His mother said, "Be diligent." The child set out and went to stand in the forest; he took off the ring and threw it on the ground; he said, "Ring, clear all this place for me!" And all of that place was cleared. He said, "Sweep it!" And it was swept. He said, "Erect houses!" And it erected many houses. He said, "Ring, let people come and live in these houses!" And people came. He made his mother rule as queen mother; he himself ruled as chief. Yet he spoke with great dignity, and he was Ananse's good friend. One day, Kwaku Ananse set out and came to this child's town. He said, "Ah! Mother's child, father's child, you have become wealthy and you did not look for me, how did you manage to do this?" Then the child told him the whole story. Ananse said, "Oh! I am going to my town, but I will return." Ananse went to his town; he said to his niece, "I am going to send you to my friend; when you go, take this palm wine to him, and whatever he tells you, listen; but as for you, try gently so that you can get hold of the ring." The young girl set out and went to the man's town. The man told her, "As for this, I will not let you go today, but stay with me for three days before you leave." The young woman said, "I have heard." While the young woman was there with him, the man went to bathe; and he took off the ring and put it on the table; and the young woman took it; and she went and gave it to her uncle, Kwaku Ananse. When Ananse received the ring, he used it to build a very large town. And then, when the man looked for his ring, he could not find it. He heard that Ananse had built a very large town that was greater than his own. Then he went to consult a deity about it. The deity said, "Ananse's niece who came there was the one who took it and gave it to her uncle. Ananse too has consulted and found out that it is Cat and Dog whom you will send. Therefore, he has taken poison, treated meat with it, and placed it on the path, so that when the animals go and take some to chew, they cannot go anywhere again." The man came home, and he said to Cat and Dog, "The reason I bought you has now arrived; my thing is lost, and they say it is with Ananse, and it lies in his chest that stands in the middle room. And furthermore, they say he has mixed poison with mutton and placed it in the path, so when you get there, do not take it, but jump over it." Dog said, "Cat, have you heard? You are the one who will chew it." Cat said, "Get away, for you who are a little bit hungry will sniff around with your nose." Then an argument arose, and their master said, "It is alright, now go." It said, "Do not catch me, why?" He said, "My master's ring is lost, and it is said that it is lying in Ananse's box that stands in the middle, and if you can go and bring it to me, then I will let you go." The Mouse said, "I can." The Cat said, "If I let you go, and you go and do not bring it, what if you run away?" The Mouse said, "If you want, tie a rope around my waist." The Cat tied a rope around its waist. Then the Mouse went into Ananse's room, and it went and gnawed around the box that stood in the middle. It made a hole little by little, and it became big. It entered it, went and took the ring, and brought it to the Cat. When the Cat took hold of the ring, he ran with it and came upon the Dog, who was still lying where he had been lying. He said, "You are still lying here, and where is the meat?" The Dog said, "Ah! I didn't see, the sleeping people woke up and took it away; and where is the ring?" The Cat said, "Here it is." The Dog said, "The river that lies on the way, it is said to have flooded, and as for you, when you cross the river, you swim underneath, and it might fall out, so give it to me, for as for me, you know that I swim on the surface." The Cat said, "True, take it."

When they reached the river, the dog jumped into it. The cat did the same. Now the cat had crossed. When the dog also reached the middle of the river, he became tired, and as he tried to take a deep breath, the car fell out of his mouth into the water.

He crossed over to where Cat was. Cat said, "Where is the ring?" He said, "It fell into the water." Cat ran into the water; he looked and saw a huge catfish passing by. Then Cat caught it. The catfish said, "Why?" He said, "My ring just fell into this river, so if you do not want trouble, give me my thing right now. If you do not give it to me, I will kill you right now." Catfish said, "Let us go up to the dry land, and I will go get your thing for you." When he reached the dry land with him, the catfish vomited, and the ring fell out. Cat took it, brought it, and showed it to Dog. Dog said, "Father, I beg you, as you go, do not tell." Cat kept silent. When they arrived home, Cat gave an account to the master, saying, "Dog, as we were going, he ate the meat and could not walk. Again, when I went to fetch the ring and brought it back, Dog told me that since he crosses the river by walking on the surface, I should give the ring to him. I gave it to him, and he threw it into the river. Then, with great effort, I recovered it." All the people who were there said, "Praise Cat. Well done!" And the chief said, "As for you, Cat, whatever food I eat, I must put some into your earthenware dish. Whatever mat I sleep on, you must sleep on it too. As for you, Dog, unless it is the ashes of the hearth, that is where you will sleep when night falls. Nothing but beatings is what people will give you." That is why every day you will see Cat, and it is only on a good mat that he sleeps. Also, if you put food on the ground for him, he will not eat it. And as for Dog, every day you will see him sleeping out there, in the hearth ashes. Also, you will hear him being beaten here and there. He cries, "Koa!" And it came from the errand on which they were sent.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not, take some and eat, and take some and bring it back to me.
