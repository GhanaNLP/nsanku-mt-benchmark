Learn a Lesson from the Gibeonites

"The Gibeonites made peace with Israel and were living among them."​—JOSH. 10:1.

But later, something very surprising happened that they did not expect. Some people came to the Israelites and said that they came from a very distant land and wanted to make a peace treaty with them.

2 The people we are talking about are the Gibeonites. This is not the first time the Bible speaks about them, and the Bible speaks about them again later. The Bible shows that the Gibeonites and the Israelites continued to live together for many years. The Gibeonites displayed many good qualities that we can learn from. And what happened to them also helps us learn many things about Jehovah God.

Did the Gibeonites tell David that because he had not handled the matter quickly, he should pay them a large sum of money? My parents emigrated from Japan to Chicago because they thought that if they moved to America, life would be very good for them. They wanted their children to attend good schools so that it would be well with them in the future.

My parents were Buddhists who were very dedicated to their religion. In the village where my mother was raised, there was a Buddhist temple. My mother's father and her two brothers were Buddhist monks. When I was young, our family did not abandon the worship of Buddha at all. Whenever I came home from school, I would immediately burn incense for the Buddha idol that was placed in our home, and offer it food and water. And when it reached Sunday, we would go into the main Buddhist temple and worship an idol that was just like the one in our house, except that this one was large and made entirely of gold.

When I was about six years old, I remember looking at the moon shining over our house one night, and I asked myself, ‘When I die, what will happen to me?’ Buddhist worship had taught me that when I die, I would continue my life in a place where only spirits dwell. Because of this, I thought that one day when I die, I would become a spirit, and I would never again be happy as human beings are. This made me feel very sad, and right there I felt lonely, as if I had no helper at all.

When I was about 15 years old, I realized that there was no benefit in the things I was doing in Buddhism. I came to see that I had a need in my life. (Mt. 5:3) I had a belief that God is a living person who has power

They Resolved the Issue Quickly and Remained Friends!

Has a brother or sister done something to hurt you alone, damaging the friendship between you? If so, then you must do something about it quickly to help you become friends again. Doing so will help protect the unity that exists among God's people.*

To illustrate this, if someone's bone is broken, they must put it in a cast before it heals. But if they do not set the bone quickly, it will get worse, and sadly, it will not heal properly.

Just as someone with a broken bone must act quickly, if we have a dispute with a fellow Christian, we need to settle matters quickly between us. If we do not, we will continue to feel pain over the damaged friendship. It can make us angry, and it can completely ruin our relationship. Jesus said: "Settle matters quickly with him." (Mt. 5:25) Therefore, go to your friend and lovingly tell him or her how you feel. Doing this can be difficult, why? Consider two reasons why.

At first, instead of settling the matter, you may feel reluctant because you are anxious about discussing the situation.# That was how it was for two Christian women named Euodia and Syntyche. Each of them was angry with her companion. It seems that a long time passed without them settling their differences, because the apostle Paul even heard about it in Rome, which was far away from them. Therefore, he urged them to settle their differences. (Phil. 4:2) If they humbled themselves, spoke from their hearts about what was bothering each of them, and forgave each other their wrongs, they could become friends again.

Second, you might think that since it was the other person's fault, he or she is the one who should apologize. However, if peace is restored between you and your friend, it is much better than finding out who is right. When a doctor treats someone who has been poisoned, he focuses on how to make the person well rather than on what caused the person to be poisoned. So when you and your friend discuss the matter, do not focus on who caused the problem. Instead, think about your friend's feelings and see how you can make your friendship work.

If you have an issue with a brother or sister, why not make an effort today to speak with him or her and settle matters between you? We must ensure that we do not ‘let the sun set while we are still angry.’ (Eph. 4:26) If we take steps to settle the matter quickly, it will help us make an effort to ‘walk in peace.’—Eph. 4:1-3.

* If a friend does something against you and it is not a serious matter, you can go to him alone so that you settle it, and completely forgive his wrong. (Mt. 5:23, 24) If it is also a serious matter, you can apply the direction found in Matthew 18:15, 16, but if it does not work out well, you can seek help from the elders in the congregation. Preparation That Gives Strength

During our meetings, we have the opportunity to give comments to encourage our brothers and sisters. (Heb. 10:24, 25) How can we prepare comments that touch hearts and encourage others?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask Jehovah to help you give an answer that will glorify him and encourage others.—Ps. 35:18.

• Avoid just reading what is in this book. If you use your own words to give the answer, other people will benefit greatly and it will help them see how you have faith in Jehovah.

• Keep your comment brief. Brief and simple comments are very encouraging, and they help more people to participate in the study.—Prov. 10:19.

• Give an answer that strengthens. Do not use your answer to advise your brothers, scold them, or speak against them.

Even if you do not have the opportunity to give a talk, you can still encourage others. One way you can do this is by listening attentively when someone gives a talk, and afterwards, telling them what you appreciated about their talk.—Prov. 10:21.
