One scholar said: “To know about God is quite different from knowing Him well so as to be close friends with Him. We must also come to know his wonderful qualities.

He believed that no matter what happened, Jehovah would not forget him and would resurrect him because he had been faithful to Him.

THE ONE WHO PROPHESIED IS KEN KIUCHI

I will never forget my first day working in the bindery at Brooklyn Bethel in New York, U.S.A. When I entered the bindery, I was amazed at how the binding machine operated and how noisy it was. The paper had filled the air with dust, and the smell of the machine oil was everywhere.

Even though what they were doing was not visible to others, they still appreciated the work they were doing and were happy. Their example helped me see how to serve Jehovah with humility.

Over the past years, Jehovah has continued to train me so that I can serve him well. But before I get to that, let me tell you how I longed to find the truth when I was in Buddhism.

I SOUGHT JEHOVAH WHEN I WAS NOT YET BORN

I was born in Chicago, Illinois, in the U.S.A. I have three siblings, and I am the oldest. My parents were originally in Japan, but they thought that the United States would give them a better life, so they left and went there. Furthermore, they wanted their children to go to good schools and be successful in life.

My parents did not take Buddhism lightly at all. For example, in the house where my mother grew up, there was a Buddhist temple. Her two brothers and her father served as Buddhist priests. Because of this, as I was growing up, I also did not take Buddhism lightly at all. When I returned from school, the first thing I did was burn incense before our Buddha idol and give it rice and water. When Sunday came, we went to a Buddhist temple and worshipped the idol there. This idol was bigger than ours, and it was entirely covered with gold.

I remember that once, when I was about seven years old, I went and stood before the Buddha image in our house and asked myself, ‘When I die, where will I go?’ At that time, I learned that when I die I will become a spirit and go to live in some spirit realm. This made me realize that a time would come when my life as a human being would come to an end, and even if I became a spirit, I would no longer be able to do any of the things that humans do. This made me very sad, and I felt that I could do nothing about my life.

When I was about 15 years old, I felt that there was no benefit in the Buddhist rituals I was involved in. I realized that I was missing something. (Matt. 5:3) I believed that there is an almighty God, and I yearned to know him. I said to myself that the Bible would

When I was 17 years old, I remember that one day, I was taking a walk along the road with my dog when a man approached me. This man was called Wilson Bashou. When we finished talking, Wilson gave me a book, which was The Truth That Leads to Eternal Life. That small blue book completely changed my life.

Ten days later, I went to my very first congregation meeting at the Kingdom Hall. I will never forget how warmly the brothers and sisters welcomed me. From that day on, I never looked back. Later, Wilson and I began studying the Bible. I enjoyed studying the Bible so much that sometimes we would study for up to eight hours. This upset my parents very much, and they tried to make me stop studying. But as I continued to study, I became firmly convinced that this was the truth. Therefore, in 1983, I was baptized.

I LEFT UNIVERSITY SO THAT I COULD DO THE PIONEER WORK

When I was baptized, I was attending a prestigious university to become a doctor. My parents sacrificed many things so that I could go to this school. I could have continued until I finished, but I wanted to serve Jehovah with all my strength and all my time.

Once, my father told me, “If you stop going to the university, I will throw you out of the house!” This upset me and made me confused. I loved my parents very much, and I did not want to do anything that would break their hearts. Therefore, many times at night, I would go out into the school yard and pray to Jehovah, saying, “Jehovah, please help me to know what to do.” When I finally made up my mind that I would no longer go to the university, my father threw me out of the house. I called Brother Bashou and told him what had happened. He told me that I could come and stay with him. I packed all my belongings into one bag and took a vehicle to where he was. I felt as if a heavy load had been lifted off me. I saw that I had made a good decision.

I felt like a bird that had escaped from a cage! Now, I could serve Jehovah full-time without any distractions. Therefore, in 1984, I began the pioneer work.

While serving as a pioneer, Jehovah helped me to cultivate many good qualities. For example, he helped me not to become discouraged quickly when things were not going well. I LEARNED SOME IMPORTANT THINGS AT BROOKLYN BETHEL

After Wilson and I had lived together for two years, I was invited to serve at Brooklyn Bethel in 1985. As I mentioned earlier, I was assigned to work in the bindery. Once, while working with a machine, I did something that I should not have done. This caused several book covers to be ruined, and they all had to be thrown away. The brothers told me to go and tell our overseer what had happened. I went and explained what happened in detail to him, but there was something I failed to do. Therefore, our overseer kindly reminded me: “It is always good to apologize when you make a mistake.” So right at that moment, I learned that whenever I make a mistake, I need to admit it and apologize.

Furthermore, the way mature brothers who had served at Bethel for a long time conducted themselves taught me how to serve others well. I have never forgotten his humility and willing service. This reminded me of how Jesus served his apostles.—John 13:3-5.

Prepare yourselves quickly!

Has a brother done something to you that hurt you so much that your relationship is now ruined? Then you should do something about it immediately to repair your relationship, and you should do so with a good motive.*

When a friendship is damaged, it can be compared to a broken bone. If a bone breaks and it is taken to the hospital quickly to be set, it will gradually heal and the person's pain will end. But if it is not attended to early, it will continue to cause pain. And worst of all, it can leave the person disabled.

Just as a broken bone must be set quickly, so too when a problem arises between friends, they must do something about it quickly so that their relationship is not ruined. Otherwise, the problem that has arisen will intensify, and the friends will become so bitter toward each other that their friendship will be ruined. Jesus said: “Settle matters quickly with your adversary.” (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, talk with your friend in a gentle manner, and tell him how you feel. But perhaps, you will not find it easy to do that. Why? Here are two reasons why you might not find it easy to do that.

First, you might not do anything about the problem because you feel embarrassed to talk about it.# That was the case with two Christian women named Euodia and Syntyche. THINGS THAT CAN BE LEARNED

What Will Help You Give Answers That Encourage Others?

Whenever we attend a congregation meeting, we have the opportunity to encourage our brothers and sisters by giving comments. (Heb. 10:24, 25) What can we do to give encouraging comments?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask him to help you so that you can give an answer that will bring him glory and, in addition, encourage the brothers.—Psalm 35:18.

• Try not to read the answer directly from the publication. When you answer in your own words, the brothers will see how much faith you have in Jehovah, and they will benefit from it.

• Keep your comments brief. Often, simple and short answers are the most encouraging. Also, if we do not say everything contained in the material under discussion, others will also have an opportunity to comment.—Proverbs 10:19.

• Let your answer be one that builds up. Make an effort that when giving answers, you will not speak in front of the brothers, nor speak against them.

Even if you are not called on, you can still encourage the brothers and sisters. You can listen carefully to them when they speak, and later let them know what you appreciated about their comment.—Proverbs 10:21.
