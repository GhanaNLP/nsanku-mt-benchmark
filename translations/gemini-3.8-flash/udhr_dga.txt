UNIVERSAL DECLARATION OF HUMAN RIGHTS

Introduction

Whereas recognition of the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family is the foundation of freedom, justice and peace in the world.

Disregard and contempt for human rights have resulted in barbarous acts which have outraged the conscience of mankind, and the advent of a world in which human beings shall enjoy freedom of speech and belief and freedom from fear and want has been proclaimed as the highest aspiration of the common people.

Whereas it is essential, if man is not to be compelled to have recourse, as a last resort, to rebellion against tyranny and oppression, that human rights should be protected by the rule of law.

And it was right that the love of one another filled the whole world.

Whereas the peoples of the United Nations have in the Charter reaffirmed their faith in fundamental human rights, in the dignity and worth of the human person and in the equal rights of men and women and have determined to promote social progress and better standards of life in larger freedom,

All Member States have pledged themselves to achieve, in co-operation with the United Nations, the promotion of universal respect for and observance of human rights and fundamental freedoms.

So that everyone may know that these ways of freedom and human rights are the main thing in the fulfillment of this promise.

Therefore now,

THE WORLD AND HUMAN SOLIDARITY

announces:

that this UNIVERSAL DECLARATION OF HUMAN RIGHTS is a common standard of achievement for all peoples and all nations, to the end that every individual and every organ of society, keeping this Declaration constantly in mind, shall strive by teaching and education to promote respect for these rights and freedoms and by progressive measures, national and international, to secure their universal and effective recognition and observance, both among the peoples of Member States themselves and among the peoples of territories under their jurisdiction.

Chapter 1

All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. They are endowed with reason and conscience and should act towards one another in a spirit of brotherhood.

2 doors

Everyone is entitled to all the rights and freedoms set forth in this Declaration, without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status.

Furthermore, no distinction shall be made on the basis of the political, jurisdictional or international status of the country or territory to which a person belongs, whether it be independent, trust, non-self-governing or under any other limitation of sovereignty.

Room 3

Everyone has the right to life, liberty and security of person.

Door 4

No one has the right to hold another person in slavery or servitude. Slavery and the slave trade shall be prohibited in all their forms.

5 doors

No one shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.

Chapter 6

Everyone has the right to recognition everywhere as a person before the law.

Door 7

All are equal before the law and are entitled without any discrimination to equal protection of the law. All are entitled to equal protection against any discrimination in violation of this Declaration and against any incitement to such discrimination.

Door 8

When someone has a case before the court, the judges of that land must judge the case fairly, so that the truth is delivered to the person who has entered the place, in accordance with the law established in the land.

Chapter 9

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary arrest, detention or exile.

10 doors

Everyone charged with a penal offence has the right to be presumed innocent until proved guilty according to law in a public trial at which he has had all the guarantees necessary for his defence.

Door 11

Everyone who is arrested in connection with a matter and is to be tried has the right to be presumed innocent by the judges until they try him in public and find him guilty. In that trial, he must also be given the opportunity to seek everything that can help him defend himself.

No one shall be held guilty of any penal offence on account of any act or omission which did not constitute a penal offence, under national or international law, at the time when it was committed. Nor shall a heavier penalty be imposed than the one that was applicable at the time the penal offence was committed.

Door 12

No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, family, home or correspondence, nor to attacks upon his honour and reputation. Everyone has the right to the protection of the law against such interference or attacks.

Chapter 13

Everyone has the right to freedom of movement and residence within the borders of each state.

Everyone has the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country.

Room 14

Everyone has the right to leave a country where they are being persecuted or killed to go to other countries to find happiness.

This right does not exist if a person committed an offense that is genuinely being prosecuted, provided the offense is not of a political nature or contrary to the principles and purposes of the United Nations.

Chapter 15

Everyone has the right to a nationality.

No one has the right to say that someone should not stay in their country, or that they should not leave their country to go and live in another land.

Gate 16

Men and women of full age, without any limitation due to race, nationality or religion, have the right to marry and to found a family. They are entitled to equal rights as to marriage, during marriage and at its dissolution.

No one has the right to force people to marry; only those who are marrying shall freely agree and consent to marry each other.

A man, his wife, and his children are like seeds among the people of the town, and it is necessary for the townspeople and the elders of the town to take care of them.

Door 17

Everyone has the right to own property alone as well as in association with others.

Everyone has the right to own property alone as well as in association with others.

Doors 18

Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion; this right includes freedom to change his religion or belief, and freedom, either alone or in community with others and in public or private, to manifest his religion or belief in teaching, practice, worship and observance.

Doors 19

Everyone has the right to freedom of thought and expression; this right includes freedom to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive and impart information and ideas through any media and regardless of frontiers.

Room 20

Everyone has the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and association. Everyone also has the right to belong to any association.

No one can compel anyone to belong to an association.

Chapter 21

Everyone has the right to take part in the government of his country, directly or through freely chosen representatives.

Everyone has the right to freely participate in the cultural life of the community, to enjoy the arts and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits.

The authority of the government in the country shall proceed from the will of the people of the country. The way the people will follow for this to happen is to establish that periodically the people shall have elections without deceit in these elections; that everyone who is an adult has the right to choose the people who will lead them in the government of the country. During the election, no one has the right to see whom their fellow person wishes to vote for.

Door 22

Everyone, as a member of society, has the right to social security and is entitled to realization, through national effort and international co-operation and in accordance with the organization and resources of each State, of the economic, social and cultural rights indispensable for his dignity and the free development of his personality.

Door 23

Everyone has the right to work. He also has the right to free choice of employment. There is also the right to just and favourable conditions of work. There is also the right to protection against unemployment.

Everyone, without discrimination, has the right to equal pay for equal work.

Everyone who works has the right to just and favourable remuneration ensuring for himself and his family an existence worthy of human dignity.

Everyone has the right to form trade unions and to join the trade union of their choice for the protection of their interests.

Chapter 24

Everyone has the right to rest and leisure, including reasonable limitation of working hours and periodic holidays with pay.

Door 25

It is necessary for the community to ensure that a man, his wife, and their children live in accordance with their well-being, that they obtain food and clothing, and that their housing and health conditions are good. They also have the right that whenever they no longer have paid work, or if they are sick, or if something happens that prevents them from working, or if a spouse dies and leaves them alone, or if old age prevents them from working, or if anything happens beyond their control so that they can no longer provide food for themselves, the community in which they live must take care of them.

Motherhood and childhood are entitled to special care and assistance. All children, whether born in or out of wedlock, shall enjoy the same social protection.

Chapter 26

Everyone has the right to education. Education shall be free, at least in the elementary and fundamental stages. Elementary education shall be compulsory. Technical and professional education shall be made generally available and higher education shall be equally accessible to all on the basis of merit.

Education shall be directed to the full development of the human personality and to the strengthening of respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms. It shall promote understanding, tolerance and friendship among all nations, racial or religious groups, and shall further the activities of the United Nations for the maintenance of peace.

Parents have a prior right to choose the kind of education that shall be given to their children.

Chapter 27

Everyone has the right freely to participate in the cultural life of the community, to enjoy the arts and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits.

Everyone who uses their hands or their head to do work that brings benefits has the right to be protected by the country from being exploited or cheated in that work.

Chapter 28

It is necessary that these things that have been prepared and put here should be fulfilled, so that everywhere on earth every person living there may be independent and live in happiness.

Cain 29

Everyone has responsibilities toward their peers in the community where they live. This will help them find peace and well-being themselves.

Everyone who is free and enjoys their freedom has no obstacle to hinder this freedom unless the community has so established, and this law will also be a law that allows human beings to live in freedom, helping people do good deeds, promoting peace where people live together, ensuring that everyone lives well in their community, and giving them a voice in the affairs of developing their community.

No one has the right to engage in any activity or perform any act aimed at the destruction of any of the rights and freedoms set forth in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

30 rooms

Nothing in this Declaration may be interpreted as implying for any State, group or person any right to engage in any activity or to perform any act aimed at the destruction of any of the rights and freedoms set forth herein.
