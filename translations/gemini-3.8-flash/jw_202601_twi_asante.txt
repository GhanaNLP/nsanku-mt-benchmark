5 The Phoenician woman humbled herself. Why can we say that? Because when Jesus compared her to a little dog, she did not get angry. A little dog was an animal commonly found in the homes of non-Jews. If Jesus had compared you to a little dog, what would you have done? Would you have said that Jesus insulted you, so you no longer needed his help, and turned back? The Phoenician woman did not do that. Instead, she showed that she was humble. Apart from that, she also did something else. She continued to plead with Jesus to help her; she did not lose heart. What made her do that? It was because of the faith she had in Jesus. The truth is, Jesus saw that the woman's faith was very great, so it moved him to help her. Even though Jesus had earlier said that he had come only for "the lost sheep of the house of Israel," he still cast out the demon from the woman's daughter for her.

IMITATE PETER—STUDY GOD’S WORD CAREFULLY

7 Consider the apostle Peter. Peter was among the first Jews to recognize that Jesus was the Messiah. He realized that it was through Jesus that Jehovah was providing “sayings of everlasting life,” or spiritual food, to his people. (John 6:66-68) Before Jesus ascended to heaven, he commissioned Peter to ‘feed his little sheep.’ (John 21:17) Peter accepted that assignment and carried it out faithfully. Jehovah even inspired him to write two letters that are now part of the Bible. Yet through it all, Peter himself also needed to feed well spiritually, that is, to study God’s Word. Thus, Peter patiently studied the letters that God inspired the apostle Paul to write. He acknowledged that some of the things Paul wrote were “hard to understand.” (2 Pet. 3:15, 16) But Peter did not give up; he kept on studying. He believed that Jehovah would help him understand and apply the “solid food” that Jehovah had inspired Paul to write in his letters.—Read Hebrews 5:14–6:1.

7. What work did Jesus entrust to Peter, and what did he himself also need to do? (Hebrews 5:14–6:1)

8 Jehovah gave Peter some direction, and because he believed in Jehovah, he followed it. One day, while Peter was in Joppa, he received a vision. In the vision, an angel of God told Peter to kill and eat animals that the Mosaic Law forbade people to eat. Because Peter was a Jew, from his youth, all he had known was the Mosaic Law, so what the angel was saying did not make sense to him. Thus, when the angel said that, Peter said: “Not at all, Lord, because I have never eaten anything defiled and unclean.” After that, the angel told him: “Stop calling defiled the things God has cleansed.” (Acts 10:9-15) Then Peter understood what the angel was saying, and he changed his mind. How do we know that? Soon after he had the vision, three men came to him. They told him that their master Cornelius had sent them to call him to come, and that he wanted to see him. Previously, it was not something Peter would have ever done to go to the house of a non-Jew, because the Jews considered non-Jews to be unclean. (Acts 10:28, 29) But when Jehovah gave Peter new direction, he readily accepted it. (Prov. 4:18) So he preached the good news to Cornelius and his household, and they believed in Jesus, received holy spirit, and were baptized.—Acts 10:44-48.

Why We Need the Ransom

“Who will rescue me from the body undergoing this death?”—ROM. 7:24.

Suppose a building has collapsed, and someone is trapped inside who is not dead, but cannot get out. As you see it, what can he do? All he can do is shout for someone to rescue him.

2 We too, the situation each of us is in, it is as though a building has collapsed on us. Why do we say so? Because when Adam disobeyed Jehovah, he became a sinner. Later, he passed on his sin to all his children. Therefore, sin, which is like a house, has collapsed on all mankind, and we are trapped under it and cannot come out. STUDY ADVICE

Visualize Bible Events in Your Mind

Many of the events that happened in ancient times are recorded in the Bible, and we can learn a lot from them. When we read these accounts and truly want to understand them well, we must visualize them in our minds. The following suggestions can help you do that:

• Read aloud. Reading the Bible aloud will help you visualize it better in your mind. If you are reading the Bible with your family, you can assign each family member to read the parts of the different people you are reading about. Doing this can help you visualize it better in your minds.

• Visualize what was happening. Try to understand what the people you are reading about were thinking and how they were feeling. You can ask yourself: ‘Why did the person say that, or why did he do what he did? If that had happened to me, what would I have thought, or how would I have felt?’

• You can draw. If you visualize and draw the Bible account you are reading, it can help you remember it well. Even if what you have drawn is not nice, it can help you.
