IMITATE PETER—CONTINUE TO STUDY GOD’S WORD CAREFULLY

7 Let us consider the apostle Peter for a moment. He was among the first Jews to recognize that Jesus was the Messiah. He recognized that Jesus was the one through whom Jehovah gives “sayings of everlasting life” or spiritual food to His servants. (John 6:66-68) Before Jesus ascended to heaven, he entrusted a certain work to Peter. He told him to ‘feed his little sheep.’ (John 21:17) Peter accepted that work and taught Jesus’ followers. Jehovah even inspired him to write two letters that are part of the Bible today. Yet, after all that Peter did, he himself also needed to feed well, that is, to study God’s Word thoroughly. For example, Peter took time to study the letters that the apostle Paul wrote. He acknowledged that some of the things Paul wrote were “hard to understand.” (2 Pet. 3:15, 16) But Peter did not give up; instead, he continued to study. He believed that Jehovah would help him understand and apply the ‘solid food,’ that is, the deep truths that Jehovah inspired Paul to write in his letters. —Read Hebrews 5:14–6:1.

8 Peter trusted Jehovah, so he obeyed the guidance Jehovah gave him. Once, when Peter was in Joppa, he had a vision. In the vision, an angel of God told him to kill and eat animals that the Mosaic Law considered unclean. From his youth, he had never eaten such animals, so he was shocked by what the angel was telling him to do. As soon as the angel spoke, Peter said: “Not at all, Lord, because I have never eaten anything defiled and unclean.” After that, the angel told him: “Stop calling defiled the things God has cleansed.” (Acts 10:9-15) Peter understood what the angel meant, and he changed his mind. How do we know that? Soon after receiving the vision, three men came to him. They told him that their master, Cornelius, a Gentile, had sent them to call him to come, and that he wanted to speak with him. Formerly, Peter would never have set foot in a Gentile's house because the Jews considered the Gentiles to be unclean. (Acts 10:28, 29) But when Jehovah helped Peter see that from that time forward He wanted everyone to hear the good news, he accepted it immediately. (Prov. 4:18) So he preached the good news to Cornelius and his household, and they believed in Jesus. Afterward, they received holy spirit and were baptized, and this made Peter very happy.—Acts 10:44-48.

Why We Need the Atonement

Who will rescue me from the body that is taking me to this death?—ROM. 7:24.

LET US assume that a building has collapsed, and a man is trapped inside it but not dead. What can that man do? The only thing he can do is to shout to call people to come and rescue him.

2 Our situation has become like that of this man. Why do we say so? When Adam rebelled against his Creator, he sinned. Afterwards, he passed on that sin to his children. Therefore, in a sense, it is as if sin has overwhelmed all mankind and we are enslaved to it, unable to free ourselves. The apostle Paul spoke about the condition we are in within the letter he wrote to the Romans. (Read Romans 7:22-24.) He pleaded for someone to rescue him from his 'body that is leading him into this death.' Because Paul was born with sin, no matter what he did, he would inevitably die. (Rom. 6:23) We and Paul are all in the same boat. We, too, were born in sin, so we need someone to rescue us!

TIPS THAT CAN HELP YOU LEARN

Picture a Bible passage that you are reading

The Bible is full of history, and the important lessons in it are also very impactful. If we visualize what we are reading, it will help us understand it very well and we will also benefit greatly from it. Look at some things that can help you visualize what you are reading.

• Read aloud. Reading the Bible aloud can help you better visualize what you are reading. If you are reading the Bible together with your family, each of you can take the role of one of the characters you are reading about and read it as if that person were speaking. This will help you better visualize the account.

• Visualize what is happening. As for those you are reading about, try to see if you can understand what was going on in their minds and how they were feeling. Ask yourself: ‘What made the person say that or do that? If it were me, what would I have thought or how would I have reacted?’

• Draw. Even if you do not know how to draw and you try to draw something about what you are reading, it will help you visualize it, and it will also help you remember their story.
