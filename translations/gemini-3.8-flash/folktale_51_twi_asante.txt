If it were easy, the dove would have gone into the forest, leaving the fowl to come home

FOWL and Dove had the same father and the same mother. Then Fowl said to Dove, "Go to the savannah, and I too will go into the forest, and if you get food, then come and tell me." And they parted ways. And for three years Dove did not see her sibling. She said to her husband, "Let us go, accompany me so that I may go and look for my sibling." And the husband said, "You have spoken and I have heard, arise so that I may go and accompany you." And he set off with her and arrived at a junction. They saw a certain small village shrine there, and it was there that she met her sibling. And Fowl asked Dove, "You went to the savannah, did you get food to eat?" She said, "Where I went was nothing but guinea corn; and when I hatched about six chicks with me, and as we were about to gather them close to me, a hunter would fire a gun, boom! and kill my chicks, and if I were not wise, they would have killed me." And when she finished speaking thus, Fowl also said, "Where I went, I am an absolute royal." And Dove asked her, "You 'absolute royal', explain the meaning to me." And Fowl said, "The meaning of my 'royalty' is that, when my master spreads a fine mat, I go and sleep on it, and I defecate on it, and when my master is about to put meat into the soup, I pick out what I want; whatever food my master will eat, I go and take some, and if I eat like that, my master cannot take a gun to shoot me. That is the reason why I say I am an absolute royal." Fowl said, "Where you stayed is not good, so on Saturday Dapaa, bring your chicks and your husband, let us live in our home." Dove said, "I have heard, I am going, and on Saturday Dapaa I will come." Then a priest came to Fowl's town and shook bells. They asked him, "Nana, why?" He said, "Dente says, for two years now you have starved him, so go and tell the chief to bring four fowls and twelve eggs." Then the chief went and caught four fowls, took twelve eggs to mash eto, and took them to Dente's small shrine, slaughtered the four fowls upon it, and took their feathers, their heads, and their feet and stuck them to the small shrine; and he also smeared the eto on it. Then on that Dapaa day, Dove said she was going to meet her sibling. As she arrived, at Dente's small shrine, she saw her sibling's feathers, her head, her feet, her wings, her liver, and her intestines. Dove said, "As for this, I cannot go any further." And Dove flew off and perched on a silk-cotton tree and said:
