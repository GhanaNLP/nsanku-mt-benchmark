HOW DEITIES CAME INTO THE WORLD

There was a certain woman who gave birth; she had given birth to eleven children; and whenever she got up and cooked food, the children would eat it all. Their mother got nothing to eat. So she looked around, and set off to the farm and went and told the Silk-cotton tree, "I will send my eleven children to come under you here to pluck pumpkins; and when they come, drop eleven of your branches and kill my children." The Silk-cotton tree said, "I have heard, and I will do it for you." The mother set off for home; she told her children, "Go to the farm, under the Silk-cotton tree; there are pumpkins there, go pluck them and bring them." The children set off and went. They arrived at the Silk-cotton tree. The eleventh child said, "First, stop; Second, stop; Third, stop; Fourth, stop; Fifth, stop; Sixth, stop; Seventh, stop; Eighth, stop; Ninth, stop; Tenth, stop; I myself, the Eleventh, have stopped." The Eleventh said to them, "Do you not know the reason why Mother told us to go pluck pumpkins?" His siblings said, "No." He said, "She has told this Silk-cotton tree that when we go there, it should drop branches to strike us; therefore, all of you, cut sticks and throw them to strike into this Silk-cotton tree." They cut the sticks, and threw them striking into the Silk-cotton tree, pim! pen! pim! pen! The Silk-cotton tree thought the children had come. It broke off eleven of its branches and threw them down. The young Eleventh said, "Have you seen that if we had gone, the Silk-cotton tree would have killed us?" They went and plucked the pumpkins and brought them to their mother. She cooked them, oh! instantly the children had eaten them up. Their mother said, "Oh! as for this matter, I cannot bear it, so I will take these children to Nyankonpon (God)." The next morning, she went and reported to Nyankonpon, saying, "Certain children that I have borne, whenever I want to eat, I get nothing to eat; hunger is killing me; therefore I beg you, send for the children to be brought, and kill them, so that I may get something to eat." Nyankonpon said, "Is that true?" The woman said, "I speak with sincerity." Nyankonpon appointed messengers, and they went and dug a huge pit, lined it with stakes; he went and took a snake and a leopard and put them into the pit, and covered it. And now the messengers went and called the children. Just as they reached where the pit was, the Eleventh said, "First, stop; Second, stop; Third, stop; Fourth, stop; Fifth, stop; Sixth, stop; Seventh, stop; Eighth, stop; Ninth, stop; Tenth, stop; I myself, the little Eleventh, have stopped; do not go this way; do not go that way." His siblings said, "Why is the main road lying there, and yet we should go through the bush?" And as they were all going, they were holding round sticks. The Eleventh said, "Throw one of these sticks onto this road." They threw it there, and it broke through and fell in, yiriri! The Eleventh said, "Have you seen, if we had gone there, all of us would have died." The people took a detour and went to Nyankonpon. Nyankonpon had caused holes to be dug and stools placed over them, so that when the children sat on them, they would fall into the holes. The children arrived before Nyankonpon. God said, "The stools that are standing there, sit down on them." And 'Duko said, "Who are we that we can sit on such beautiful stools, so Nana, we will sit down here by the side." Then God looked at the children; he said, "These children, I will send them to Death's town." When day broke, he called the children; he said, "Go to Death and ask for the golden basin, the golden stool, the golden snuff-box, the golden stone, and the golden tail." 'Duko said, "You are our master, wherever you send us, we will go." God said, "Go then." The children set out and went to Death's town. They arrived there; Death said, "Why is it that nobody comes here, yet you have come here?" They said, "We were walking about and have come here." Death said, "Good!" Death also had ten children; including himself made eleven. When night fell, Death distributed the children one by one to his children, and he himself went to sleep with 'Duko. When night fell, Death ground his teeth, going to catch 'Duko. 'Duko said, "Death, I am not asleep." Death said, "When will you sleep?" 'Duko said, "If you give me the golden basin to drink from, then I would have slept." And Death went and brought it to him. After a little while, Death ground his teeth again, going to catch him. 'Duko said, "Death, I am not asleep." Death said, "When will you sleep?" 'Duko said, "If you went and brought me the golden snuff-box, then I would have slept." And Death went and brought it to him. After a little while again, Death was about to go catch 'Duko. 'Duko said, "I am not asleep." Death said, "When will you sleep?"

Duko said, "If you had gone to fetch the money tree for me and let me eat some, I would have been asleep by now."

Death went, fetched it, and brought it to him. After a short while, Death was going to catch him. 'Duko said, "Nana, I am not yet asleep." Death said, "Then when will you sleep?" 'Duko said, "Nana, if you had gone and fetched gold dust and brought it to me, I would have fallen asleep." And Death went and fetched it and came back. Now after a short while again, Death got up again. 'Duko said, "E, Nana, I say I am not asleep." Death said, "Then what day will you sleep?" 'Duko said, "If you had gone and taken a sieve, fetched water, and cooked food for me to eat, I would have fallen asleep." Death lifted the sieve and went to the river. When he drew the water, it sifted through and poured away. Then 'Duko said to his siblings, "Arise and run away." And they arose and ran away; and 'Duko went and cut plantain stems, came and placed them where his siblings used to sleep, and covered them with a cloth. And Death was at the river; he was drawing water. Then Death the Father called Death the Child, saying, "Oh Death!" He answered, "Ye Dwo." He said, "What are you doing?"

He said, "Alas! what a little child I have encountered; when I say I am going to fry him, he says, 'I am not asleep yet'; he has taken all my things, and now, he tells me to fetch water with a sieve." Death, the man, said, "Ah! you are small, but if you had plucked leaves and spread them inside the sieve, and fetched the water with it, wouldn't that have worked?" Death's child said, "Oh, true." He plucked leaves, put them in, fetched the water, and went back. 'Duko said, "Death, have you only just come? Cook the food." Death cooked the food; he resolved that he was going to kill 'Duko's siblings to cook food. When he went, he did not look carefully, and it was his own ten children that he killed. Early the next morning, Death had risen and was sitting by the fire. 'Duko said, "Grandfather, a tsetse fly is perched on your breast." Death said, "Take this fly-whisk lying here and kill it for me." 'Duko said, "Impossible! As you sit there, with a tsetse fly perched on you, and a golden fly-whisk is lying there, won't you let me go fetch it to kill it?" Death said, "Go into the room, bring it." 'Duko went and fetched it. He struck—missed, and he chased the fly away; he did not kill it. 'Duko said, "Ah! today, wherever this fly will sleep, I will sleep there with it." And 'Duko went into the room and took his bag containing the gold dust and the goods. He said, "Grandfather Death, unless I put the fly into this bag and bring it to you." 'Duko took off, running fast! He reached the crossroads; he said, "Grandfather Death! with all due respect, if you were not foolish, would I have come to take all your goods; the siblings I came with have found a way to go, and as for you, I have made you kill all your ten children? I am leaving." Death said, "You little child, wherever you sleep, I will sleep there with you!" 'Duko leaped and took off, running fast! fast! fast! and Death was also pursuing him. As 'Duko was going, he came upon his siblings waiting along the path. They were playing drums. 'Duko said, "Haven't you gone yet? Death is coming, so let us find somewhere to go." By then, Death had caught up with them. 'Duko took medicine and applied it to his siblings, and they went up into a silk-cotton tree. Then Death came and stood under the silk-cotton tree. He said, "It was right here that I saw these children, so where have they gone?" While 'Duko was up there, he said to his siblings, "I am going to urinate on him." His siblings said, "Eh, he wants to catch and bind us, and we have fled to sit up here, and you say, 'I am going to urinate on him'?" 'Duko did not listen, and he urinated on Death. Death said, "Aha! so here you are, today you will suffer!" Death said, "Child sitting over there, 'kyere-he-ne', 'kyere-he-ne'." And one of the children fell down; "'kyere-he-ne'", and two fell down. Now it was left with 'Duko. Death said, "Child, 'kyere-he-ne'," and 'Duko jumped and landed on the ground, with a thud. And Death went up into the silk-cotton tree. 'Duko said, "You huge fellow, you yourself, 'kyere-he-ne'." And Death also came down, thud! he fell. 'Duko went and plucked medicine, crushed it, and applied it to his siblings, and they arose. As 'Duko threw the medicine away, some touched Death, and Death woke up. He said, “You killed me, yet you have revived me; today I shall deal with you.” Then all of us took to our heels together, kiri! kiri! kiri! And Death pursued us. As we went along, a great river had flooded. When ’Duko and his brothers arrived there, they knew how to swim, and they swam across. Only ’Duko did not know how to swim. The children stood on the other side of the river; they had wept, wept, and wept; their mouths were swollen. ’Duko also turned into a stone. Death arrived at the river. He said, “As you children are standing there, if I could find a stone to throw and strike those swollen mouths of yours.” As Death looked down at the ground, he saw a stone lying there; he picked it up and threw it; as the stone went through the air, it said, “Wind, carry me and set me down on the other side of the river.” It landed on the other side of the river. ’Duko said, “Here I am!” Death said, “Ah, you child, hey! I have nothing to say to you, but what I will tell you is, go and stay at home and become a fetish priest, and whenever anyone I am coming to take comes to you, you must tell me; then if I wish, I will leave them for you; and whatever I desire, you must take it and give it to me.” That is how fetishes came into the world, and it came from the little child ’Duko. This folktale of mine which I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it to enjoy, and let some of it bring me praise.
