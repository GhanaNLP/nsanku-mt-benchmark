There was a certain Queen Mother who was childless; and she went to a certain fetish that he might grant her a child to bear. And he gave her one, and told her, saying, "As for this child, when you bear it, it must not eat any meat except sheep's liver, and even its meat it taboos." Now one day its mother was going to the farm, and she gave the sheep's liver to her slave-girl Kra, so that she might cook food with it for her child. But Kra took it and cooked the food, and went and took some real mutton and added it. The Queen Mother's child said to her, "Mother said I taboo real meat, and if you add some to it and I eat it, I shall die." Kra said, "I will add it, and if you will die, then die." And the Queen Mother's child said, "Shall I eat it and die?" Then it went into the room, took its mother's red silk cloth, and tied it on. And the child set out on the path, and Kra also followed behind it. Kra was afraid that if her mistress came upon her, she would get into trouble. And as they were going, the Queen Mother's child said, "Kra!" Kra said, "Yes!" It said, "If I send a message by you, will you deliver it?" Kra said: "I will deliver it, I will deliver it before I die; I will speak, I will speak before I die. Formerly when I went, it was yams that stood there; now that I have come, it is dried cassava that lies there, Kokotee Asamoa, Kuruti Amoa that lies there; Gyanoa gold, I have none, Boafo Denkye the Elder, today I have ended life today. He says, let us not kill a sheep for him to eat, let us not kill a fowl for him to eat. He says, 'Aku, good morning.' He says, 'Ya'ku.' He says, 'The Executioner says he bids you good morning.' He says, 'Y'abrawo.' He says, 'My brother, where are you going, that I may send a message by you?' He says, 'I am going to my grandfather's town, Sekyere Gyamase.' He says, 'What happened at grandfather's town Sekyere Gyamase that the drums did not sleep, Oboama's father, Agyei Ampon; did father go to war or did father go trading, that ripe plantains have been broken into the funeral celebration; two did not come, Ntim Sekyere, Kwakye did not come?' He says, 'Parrot Boama, if you have spoken, then cease, let me also speak some, and your eyes are clear in the water. Your grandfather Mpampinam, let us go and see, he goes and catches a huge royal antelope. Kwao Adu Dakowa, Bantama Mpedu Okyere, Adwoa Kese, Odum Abena and her talisman that walks in vain. Adwoa, Adwoa, grandchild of the river Kobi, Tontoroni Ya, Ode Nsee.' "

And the child met his old mother. His old mother said, "Is this my child I have met, yet I left him at home, and what has happened to him that he is running so fast?" The Queen told her servants who followed behind her to catch him for her. But they did not get him; and as he was running, he went and plunged into the water, and Kra also went and plunged into it too. When they plunged in, the Queen's child turned into a crab, with a red tail; and Kra also turned into a stone, with stripes across its face. The red tail of the crab was the Queen's child's silk cloth which was tied around him when he plunged into the water; and the stone also was the slave Kra, with stripes across his face. This tale of mine which I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away and bring some back.
