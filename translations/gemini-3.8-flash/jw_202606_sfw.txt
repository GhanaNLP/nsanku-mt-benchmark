I was born in 1947 in Cuba. It is an island between the Caribbean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, and the climate is very warm. I was the eldest child, and later two younger brothers were born after me. We all lived in Esmeralda, which was a village at that time.

His time in the village was very wonderful, because food was not scarce, and many of his family members were also living close to each other.

When I was five years old, my mother and my father began studying the Bible. The brother who studied with them was named Walton Jones. And he did not joke with his preaching at all. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Which two covenants did the apostle Paul speak about in Galatians 4:24?

◼ The apostle Paul spoke about the relationship between Abraham, his wife Sarah, and her servant Hagar. In this regard, he said: “These things stand as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24). Formerly, we understood that these two covenants were the Law covenant and the new covenant. But as we reconsidered this matter, we saw the need to make an adjustment in our understanding. It seems that the covenant Jehovah made with Abraham is what Paul was talking about, rather than the new covenant. Now, let us examine what caused Paul to make that statement.*

Paul spoke concerning Hagar that she corresponds to Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) Therefore, Hagar is the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai in the year 1513 B.C.E. (Before Christ Came). (Ex. 19:5, 6) No sinful human was able to keep that Law perfectly. This made the Jews realize that they were slaves to sin. That covenant would also help the Israelites to recognize the Messiah, who alone was the human that could keep the Mosaic Law perfectly. So when the Messiah offered his life as a sacrifice, he bought sinful humans out of the hands of sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) Because of this, the Law was no longer needed, because the purpose for which God gave that Law to the Israelites had been fulfilled by Jesus.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul compared Hagar to Sarah, who was a “free woman” and who gave birth ‘through a promise.’ (Gal. 4:23) That promise was the covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) Jehovah spoke about that covenant in Genesis 22:18, saying: “By means of your offspring all nations of the earth will gain a blessing for themselves because you have listened to my voice.”

That covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham helps us to better understand the promise Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) That arrangement made it clear that the promised “offspring” would come through Abraham. Paul explained that that “offspring” is Jesus Christ. However, those who ‘belong to Christ,’ the 144,000 people who will rule with Jesus in his Kingdom, also form a secondary part of that offspring. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) People who believe in Jesus and obey him will receive many blessings in fulfillment of the promise God made to Abraham.

So, why did Paul use this 'allegory'? He wanted the Jews in his day to realize that if they insisted on following the Law of Moses, like Hagar, they would remain slaves. However, if they accepted Jesus Christ, who is the principal "offspring" of Abraham, it would make them free like Sarah. This meant they would be freed from the slavery of sin and death. (John 8:32-34) Furthermore, if they did not accept Jesus, it meant they did not understand the real purpose for which the Law covenant was made. That purpose was to lead them to Christ.

The Galatians to whom Paul wrote this letter, many of them were formerly Gentiles. Therefore, they were not under the Law of Moses in any way. However, some of the Jewish Christians were saying that the Gentile Christians had to obey the Law of Moses. An example of this was that they should be circumcised. Therefore, Paul made them understand that because of Christ who came and accepted them, they had received “freedom.” Therefore, there was no need for them to follow that Law. Because if they did so, they would be putting themselves back into “slavery.”—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

PEOPLE AND THINGS THE BIBLE TALKS ABOUT

Fishing Net Used to Catch Fish

The Bible makes us understand that at one time, the work some of Jesus’ disciples did was going to the sea to catch fish. (Mark 1:16-20) And they did this on the Sea of Galilee, and they used nets to catch the fish.

And they take thin threads and thick threads to weave the net. Usually, they leave holes between it so that it will be able to catch the fish. If they want to catch big fish, they make the holes big. And if they also want to catch small fish, they make the holes small. As for the net that is small, they usually attach small lead or stones to its edges so that when it is cast into the water, it will be able to sink. As for the big nets too, they attach big leads to their bottom to make them heavy. Then they attach floats to the top so that when they cast the net, it will float on the surface of the water.

When the nets were cast, the sharp stones at the bottom of the sea could tear them. Also, when they caught an excessive amount of fish, it could cause them to do so. (Luke 5:6) Therefore, whenever they returned from the sea, it became necessary that they “mended their nets.” James and John, that is what the Bible says they were doing. (Matt. 4:21) It is likely that they used a needle and thread to mend them. Luke also reported about others that they were “washing their nets” so that they could remove the dirt that had gathered in them. (Luke 5:2) After that, they spread them out so they would not rot. (Ezek. 47:10) They did all this because, in those days, net ropes were very expensive.

Jesus told the men who were doing that work to follow him, and that they would "fish for people."
