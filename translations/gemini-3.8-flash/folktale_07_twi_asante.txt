FLY and Leopard were there, and Leopard said to Fly, “Let us go and find women to marry.” Fly said, “Yes!” And Leopard bathed and anointed himself, adorned himself with his gold, and tied a bundle of rags and loaded it onto Fly. Then they set off; they arrived at one town. And Fly said, “Mothers and Fathers, good evening.” They said, “Welcome, our good husband!” Leopard also arrived there and said, “Mothers and Fathers, good evening.” They said, “With your stinking mouth, do you bid us good evening?” They set off and reached another town. Fly said, “Mothers and Fathers, good evening.” And they responded to him just the same. Leopard also arrived there, and he greeted them, and they responded, saying, “With your stinking mouth, do you bid us good evening?” They set off; they reached along the path; Leopard said, “Look! Bring me the bundle of rags, and you too take this gold and adorn yourself with it, so that if it is because of the rags that the women like you, I too will see.” So he took it and adorned himself with it, and they set off and arrived at one town. Leopard went first, carrying the bundle of rags; he called out. They said, “Do not call out to us with your stinking mouth, even though you are carrying rags.” Then Fly arrived there; he said, “Mothers and Fathers, good evening.” And they responded, saying, “Welcome, look at this young man, oh! gold suits you like this, if they had not come to report to us, we would have escorted you on your way.” And they set off. As soon as they reached the path, Leopard said, “Hey! friend, stand there, strip off my gold for me right now!” When Fly had finished taking it off for him, Leopard looked along the path, plucked a rope, and seized Fly. He said, “I am tying you up, what do you have to say?” Fly said, “I have nothing to say.” Leopard said, “Today I have not even killed you yet, and you will go with me.” And Leopard seized him, tied him up, and bound him to a tree. Dokon’fa said, “I have heard; I will untie you.” He went there and untied him. As soon as he finished untying him, Leopard sprang forth! He said, “Why did you untie my human?” He replied, “I have untied him, so do whatever you will do.” He said, “As for me, I will not trade words with you; I will fight you with fists.” Dokon’fa said, “If we are going to fight, let us go into the bush and gather firewood, and bring it to light a fire.” So they went and gathered dry wood. Then he said, “Let us light it.” And Leopard lit it. They engaged in the fight, grappling vigorously, and Leopard threw Dokon’fa to the ground, bam! Dokon’fa said, “One cleanses oneself for one's wife's bath.” Then they met again. And Dokon’fa also threw Leopard, bam! Leopard said, “I too have cleansed myself for my wife's bath; as we meet now, whoever throws the other should finish him off once and for all.” Then they clashed violently, wrestling back and forth! And Dokon’fa lifted Leopard and threw him into the fire. Leopard cried, “I am coming out, I am coming out, I am coming out!” When they looked at his body, it was scorched and spotted all over! That is why you will see Leopard with spots all over his body; where the marks are, that is the burn; and where it is black, that is where the charcoal scorched him. And if you are walking along and you see half a loaf of kenkey lying discarded, you will notice that many flies settle on it; it is for no other reason than to give him thanks.
