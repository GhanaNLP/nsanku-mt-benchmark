We are not alike, not alike at all

ANY WAY YOU WILL SEE THAT THE LEOPARD HAS WEAKENED, AND THE HARE HAS PREVAILED OVER IT

IT IS SAID that Leopard and Leopardess, and Hare and Hare’s wife lived in a village. And a famine came. Then Leopardess said she was going to the stream. When she went, Hare’s wife was grinding corn. Leopardess said, “Ah! Mother, where did you get this?” Hare’s wife said, “My husband, it was a big path that lies behind the house here that he took when he was going to look for wild yams, and he went and got this corn, so take this and go boil it and eat.” She said, “Thank you, Aku.” And Leopardess took it home, and put it on the fire. Then her husband came, and asked her, and she said, “It was Hare’s wife who gave it to me, and she said it was a big path that lies behind the house there that her husband took and bought it and brought it.” Leopard said, “Then tomorrow I will go there very early in the morning.” And he set off and went. When he arrived, there was corn indeed. And he took his knife, cut some, made a basket, and gathered it into it. Now Hare was sitting in his house, and saw that Leopard had passed by into the farms. Right then, he too set off. When he reached the crossroads, he turned into a Duiker. One half of it was rotten; one half was not rotten. Then he went forward and turned into a Bushbuck. One half of it was rotten; one half was not rotten. Then he went forward and turned into a Bongo. One half of it was rotten; one half of it was not rotten. Then he went forward and turned into a Buffalo. That one was entirely fresh. Now, carrying the basket and corn, Leopard was coming along. When he came, he came upon Buffalo. Leopard said, “Before the famine fully came, these animals were dying like this.” And he turned back, and came upon Bongo; and he turned back, and came upon Bushbuck; and he turned back, and came upon Duiker. And he said, “Ah! If these animals are so plentiful, then let me go and collect them.” He threw the corn and the basket to the ground, and set off to go take the animals that were ahead. When he got there, they were not there. Now when he returned, the basket and the corn were not there. He set off, went home, and sat there quietly. Leopardess asked him, “Where is the corn?” He said, “When I went, I did not find any to buy.” Then Leopardess went to the stream, and went and met Hare’s wife. Leopardess said, “As for mine, yesterday my husband said when he went he did not find any of the corn to buy.” Hare’s wife said, “He lies; as for yesterday, what he went to take, it was my husband who beat him and took it from his hands.” Leopardess set off and went home. She told her husband, saying, “Well done! Well done! Well done! You said when you went you did not find any of the corn, yet it was Hare who beat you and took it from your hands.” Leopard said, “Who is Hare, tomorrow shall I go and fight with him with sticks?” Hare was sitting in his house and heard it. He told his wife, “Tomorrow Leopard will come here to fight with me, so I will meet him on the way, lest perhaps you be afraid.” When day broke, Hare set off. When he reached the outskirts of the town, he turned into a Royal Antelope, and was vomiting white clay, charcoal, and blood. Then he went forward, and turned into a Bushbuck, and was vomiting the same thing. He went forward and turned into a Buffalo. Leopard was coming along. Royal Antelope said, “Father Leopard, hold me, for I am dying.” Leopard said, “Why?” Royal Antelope said, “It was Hare that I went to argue with about a corn matter, and he beat me, and I am dying.” Leopard said, “Let me pass by.” And he went on. And he went and met Bushbuck. The Bushbuck said, “Leopard, hold me, for Rabbit and I have had a dispute over some corn.” Leopard asked, “Why?” Bushbuck said, “I went, and he beat me, and now I am dying.” Leopard said, “Let me pass.” When he went, he came upon the Buffalo. Buffalo said, “Father Leopard, hold me, for I am dying.” Leopard asked, “Why?” Buffalo said, “It was Rabbit; I went and had a dispute with him over some corn, and he beat me, and now I am dying.” Then Leopard looked around. He said, “Ah! If I go back to have this very same dispute over corn with him, what if he beats me too!” And Leopard took to his heels, running with all his might! Right then, Rabbit also set off after him. He chased him, and Leopard shouted loudly, “Little Leopard, run!” Leopard reached his house and entered his room, pushing his head hard against the door as he went in, and Rabbit seized both of his thighs. Then Leopard said, “Father Rabbit, let me go.” Rabbit said, “I will not let you go; I will beat you.” Leopard said, “I beg you, do not beat me, and let me swear by the deities that whatever path you take, I will come and scratch the ground there.” That is why, whichever path you take, what you will see where Leopard has clawed the ground is that Rabbit has passed that way.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
