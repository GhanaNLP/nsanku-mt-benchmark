THE ACT BY WHICH SOME BECAME BEAUTIFUL, WHILE OTHERS DID NOT BECOME BEAUTIFUL

They say there was a certain woman, and she went to an Oil Palm tree that stood in the water to seek childbirth. And when she went, the Palm tree said, "I will give you a child, but the child that I will give you, if it serves, it serves for nothing." She said, "I want it." In not two days, not three days, she became pregnant and gave birth. A spider-tale child does not take long to grow, so soon he had become an adult. And his mother told him, saying, "In this town, you have no taboo except the Palm tree alone, so never climb one." He said, "Mother, I have heard." One day, his mother left him and went to the farm. She went and called her lover, and she was playing with him, and he took hold of her cloth and it tore completely. And the woman said, "This cloth of mine that you have torn, go and find palm-fiber and bring it to me to stitch it." The man went and bought some and brought it. The woman said, "I do not want this, unless you go and find the Palm tree that stands in the water." And the young man set off to go and find some. As he approached, there was a Palm tree standing in the water. He climbed it rapidly, kra! kra! kra! until he reached the neck of the Palm tree. As he struck it with a knife, the Palm tree split into two, and the young man fell inside, and the Palm tree snapped back shut. Now he was there, and it was squeezing him tightly. Before long his mother came; when she reached the water, she looked and saw a reflection in it, and she said:

Whose spirit lies in this water like my child Akwasi Kwa-saman?

He said, "Mother, it is I."

He said, "Why is the palm tree pinching you?"

He says, "I am going to prepare a farm to give to my lover because Abe is putting pressure on me."

Mother said, "Squeeze this palm nut, squeeze it, squeeze it, my child Kwasaman.

Palm nut, squeeze it, squeeze it, squeeze it, palm nut, squeeze it.

And the mother went away. And the father also came and said:

Whose spirit is upon this water like my child Akwasi Kwasaman?

He said, "Father, it is I."

He said, "Why is the palm tree pinching you?"

He says, "I am going to prepare a farm to give to my lover because Abe is putting pressure on me."

Father says, "Press this palm nut, press it, my child Kwasaman.

Abe squeeze him, squeeze him, Abe squeeze him."

Then he went home and went to tell the Chief, and the Chief also called all the people; and they went and arrived under the Palm tree, and the Chief spoke up and said:

Who is it whose spirit lies in these waters like my grandfather Akwasi Kwasaman?

He said, "Nana, it's me." He said, "Why are you hard-pressed, Abe?"

He says, "I am going to prepare a farm to give to my lover because Abe is putting pressure on me."

Nana says, "Press this palm nut, press it, press it, My grandchild Kwasaman.

Abe squeeze him, squeeze him, Abe squeeze him."

And the chief fought, and the people sang that very song, saying, "Squeeze the oil palm fruit." Then his lover came, and she said:

Who is it whose reflection in this water resembles my lover Akwasi Kwasaman?

He said, "As for my lover."

He said, "Why did Abe squeeze you?"

He says, "I am going to fell a palm tree for you, for the palm tree is pressing hard on me."

The child said, "Palm tree, leave him, leave him, leave him, Palm tree, leave my lover, Akwasi Kwasaman, Palm tree, leave him, leave him, leave him, Palm tree, leave him."

He said this, and Abe's pride was slightly hurt, and his lover was also slightly irritated. Then he spoke up again, saying:

Who is it whose spirit lies in the depths of the water like my lover Akwasi Kwasaman?

He said, "As for my lover."

He said, "Why did Abe squeeze you?"

He says, "I am going to fell a palm tree for you, for the palm tree is pressing hard on me."

The child said, "Palm tree, leave him, leave him, leave him, Palm tree, leave my lover, Akwasi Kwasaman, Palm tree, leave him, leave him, leave him, Palm tree, leave him."

Now, her lover immediately went and clung to her chest, and it was melted; and it turned into shea butter. And the people who went there early took some and applied it to their faces, and they are the ones who are very beautiful; and those who did not get any, that is why they are not beautiful.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
