Imitate the Example of the Gibeonites

"The inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were living among them." —JOSH. 10:1.

Jehovah has helped the Israelites to completely conquer the cities of Jericho and Ai. But something unexpected happened. Some strangers appeared, claiming that they came from a distant land. Bible records show that the Gibeonites and the Israelites lived together for many hundreds of years. We can learn many good things from the Gibeonites, and we can observe Jehovah’s qualities in the way he dealt with them.

Be Quick to Settle Matters With Your Brother!

HAS a brother or sister offended you so deeply that the relationship between the two of you is no longer sweet? To be able to repair the relationship between you and your brother or sister so that you can continue to be friends, you must: (1) take action immediately, and (2) do this with a good motive.*

For example, suppose someone breaks a leg. The bone must be set so that it can heal properly and the pain will stop. But if the bone is not set quickly, the person will continue to suffer. What is worse, the bone may not mend properly and may not function well.

Just as it is urgent to attend to a broken bone quickly, so it is necessary that we quickly resolve misunderstandings between us and our fellow Christians. Otherwise, we will suffer pain because our friendship has been damaged. We may end up harboring anger toward each other, and our relationship could deteriorate completely. Jesus said: ‘Settle matters quickly with your adversary.’ (Mat. 5:25) Therefore, approach your friend and talk freely and honestly about the matter. Why might this be difficult? Consider these two reasons.

First: You may feel like ignoring the matter because going to the other person to talk about it is difficult for you.# Consider two Christians named Euodia and Syntyche. A disagreement likely arose between them and they could not resolve it. The disagreement seemed to have lasted for a long time because even though the apostle Paul was far away in Rome, he heard about the matter. He urged them to do everything they could to make peace. (Phil. 4:2) If they humbled themselves, talked freely about the matter, and forgave each other from the heart, they would continue to be friends.

Second: Perhaps it seems to you that the fault lies with the other person, so they should be the one to apologize. But the most important thing is to protect your friendship, not who is right or who is wrong. For example, if someone breaks a bone and a doctor is attending to them, what the doctor will be concerned about is how to make the person recover quickly, not necessarily to know in detail exactly what the person did before the bone broke. In the same way, when you are resolving the matter, you do not need to be anxious about who is wrong. Rather, let your focus be on your mutual care for each other and what you can do to repair your relationship.

Therefore, if there is a matter between you and a brother, try to speak with him today to settle the matter so that you can be friends again. “Do not let the sun set on your anger.” (Eph. 4:26) If you take these steps and quickly repair the relationship between you and your brother, then you will protect the “uniting bond of peace” that makes us one.—Eph. 4:1-3.

How to Make Your Comments Upbuilding
