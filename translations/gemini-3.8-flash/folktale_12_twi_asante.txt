HOW ASO WILL COME TO ASANTE

KWAKU ANANSE and his children, Tikonokono, Afudotwedotwe, and Nyiwankonfwea lived in a house together with Kotoko. Kotoko went and cleared a farm, and Kwaku Ananse begged him to give him a portion to weed. So he went and gave him a portion. While Kwaku Ananse and his children were weeding, Kotoko went home to eat. Then Kotoko returned; he took out his hoe, placed it on the ground, and raised a song, saying: "Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa." The hoe weeded a great deal. When they closed for the day and were leaving, Kotoko went and hid the hoe. And Kwaku Ananse saw it. He said, "This hoe that I have seen, very early tomorrow morning, I will come and take it to go and do my work." Truly, very, very early in the morning, Ananse went and took it; he took it to his farm. Ananse did not know how it was made to stop, and he began: "Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa, Kotoko saworowa, Gyensaworowa." The hoe weeded and weeded. It weeded until it reached the ocean. From there, it reached overseas, and the white people took it, looked at it, and made some like it. That is how hoes came in large quantities to the Ashanti nation. Formerly, it was Kotoko alone who had one. This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back to me.
