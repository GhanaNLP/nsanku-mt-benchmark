INTRODUCTION

1. The 2021 Citizens' Budget is a review of the Government's achievements in 2020 and its intentions for revenue mobilization and the projects projected to be undertaken in the 2021 year. The theme of the 2021 Budget is “Completion, Consolidation and Continuation”. This theme is appropriate because in past years, every new budget came with many new projects to be started, whose funding would come from ongoing projects. This does not align well with our revenue mobilization avenues, but rather results in debt accumulation, and many contractors who have completed their work are not paid on time. This caused the cost of every project to increase, as delayed payments for completed works led to interest charges on delayed payments.

2. In the year 2021, the Government of Ghana intended to adopt a new path. Its aim was to put an end to the culture of a government starting work without finishing it before leaving office, only for another to come into office and abandon those old projects to start their own new ones. The government made a promise to the people that it would complete all those abandoned projects.

METHODS OF TAX COLLECTION

10. The government introduced tax collection measures in the 2021 Budget to ease development initiatives and reduce the hardships brought by COVID-19. The tax collection measures are as follows:

• COVID-19 health levy, a one percent increase in the National Health Insurance Levy and a one percent increase in the VAT flat rate;

• Sanitation and Pollution Levy (SPL) of 10 pesewas on each liter of petrol or diesel under the ESLA (Energy Sector Levy Act), to ensure proper sanitation, promote good living conditions, and reduce the daily premature deaths resulting from poor sanitation;

• Energy Sector Recovery Levy (Delta levy) of twenty pesewas (20 pesewas) per litre on petrol/diesel under ESLA, to help pay the Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) debts incurred when the plants were standing idle or operating below capacity;

• tourism development levy of 5% on gross profit, to help pay for promotional work in the tourism sector. This is to be reviewed in the year 2024;

• The Minister for Finance and the Minister for Local Government shall jointly inspect the gambling areas and consult with stakeholders in this country to come up with several strategies that will help improve tax collection methods in the gambling areas; and

BUDGET TERMINOLOGY

1D1F (One District, One Factory): The construction of factories and offices will bring this country into a state of major industries, which is great development for our country. Its aim is to provide jobs for Ghanaians across every corner of this country.

Research confirms: This means the Government works openly and lets the citizens of this country know everything about the work, both its secrets, its successes, and all its challenges. This can be money or any state resource held by the Government.

Allocation: it is the process through which resources reach workplaces such as ministries and other government agencies known as MDAs.

Mitigation: it is a way followed to reduce the harm of a problem.

BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): A word used to bring together fields of knowledge that encompass both science and mathematics. This word functions in the educational sector when selecting an academic field for a school to promote scientific knowledge.

Budget: A budget is a way that shows where money will come from and what that money will be spent on. For example, a family (household) can make their budget to show how much money they have, and show how they will spend it on room rent, electricity and water bills, and food. If the money will not be enough, the family will discuss to find an addition or they will take a loan.

Budget deficit: this happens when the government's expenditure exceeds the taxes they collect.

Capital expenditure: it is the money spent by the government on major projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, vehicles, water, and machinery.

Workers' remuneration/compensation: This is the salaries of workers and all allowances that accompany monthly salaries. Or retirement benefits/pensions.

Development Partners (DPs): This refers to countries or organizations that support the Government to achieve national development goals. Examples of DPs include global institutions such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Bank, and African Development Bank (AfDB), or bilateral relations between countries such as Germany, United Kingdom, and the United States of America (USA).

Year-end clearance sale: A clearance sale that takes place at the end of the year in the month of December.

Fiscal Responsibility Act: This is a law that directs how the government can balance spending and tax revenue.

Grants to Government Units: This consists of statutory payments made by the Government into designated funds such as the District Assemblies Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund, and the National Health Fund.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is the value of goods and services produced in a country within a specific period.

Gross International Reserves: This measures the value or ability of a country's capital reserves to purchase goods or services internationally within a given period. It also shows the value of a country's currency relative to international currencies.

Inflation: How the prices of goods and services increase over a period of time.

IPEP (Poverty Eradication Initiative): The Poverty Eradication Initiative is a program introduced by the NPP Government to eradicate poverty and reduce disparities between cities and rural communities. Its aim is to provide rural communities with their needs to lift them out of poverty.

Debt Payment: This refers to monies paid to countries, banks, or financial institutions that lent money to the Government to undertake development projects. These creditors can be Ghanaians or foreigners.
