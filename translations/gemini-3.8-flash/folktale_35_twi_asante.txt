We are not alike, not alike at all

As the elders say, bedroom matters are not spoken of

It is said that Nyankonpon Kwame cleared a very large farm, and he planted okra, onions, beans, garden eggs, peppers, and pumpkins. The farm became overgrown with weeds, and stinging nettles took it over. Then Nyame beat the gong-gong announcing that his farm was overgrown, and whoever could weed it without scratching himself should come and take his daughter, Abena Nkroma, in marriage. When this person went, he scratched himself, and they hooted at him; when that person went, they hooted at him; all the people went; they could not do it. Then Kwaku Ananse said, "As for me, I can do it." However, Nyankonpon’s farm was beside a path, and it was along that path that people passed to go to the market every Friday. And Ananse, knowing this, went to weed only on Fridays. And as he weeded, when the people passing by said, "Father Ananse, well done with the work!", he replied, "Thank you, Aku!" They said, "This farm that no one can weed, is it you who are weeding it?" Ananse said, "Eh! It is because of that one girl that I am toiling like this. Her hand alone is like this." And that place itched him; and when he did that, the place that itched him was relieved. Another person passed by again and greeted him, "Well done with the work!" and he slapped the place that itched him with his hand. If it was his thigh, he said, "That one girl, it is said that her thigh, see how straight it is." Doing that, he finished weeding. Then he went and reported to Nyankonpon that he had finished weeding the farm. Nyankonpon asked the messenger, "Is it true that he has finished?" The messenger said, "Yes!" Nyame asked him, "Did he scratch himself?" He said, "No, he did not scratch himself." Nyame gave Abena Nkroma to Ananse in marriage. One night, Ananse went to sleep with his bride, and the bride asked him, "How was it that you were able to weed Father's farm like this, a farm that whenever anyone went there, he turned back, but as for you, you were able to weed it?" Then Ananse said, "Do you think I am a fool? When I was weeding and someone passed by and asked me, 'Ananse, this farm that no one can weed, is it you who are weeding it?' then whichever place itched me, I hit it with my hand, and I scratched it, and I told the person, your thigh is like a parrot’s thigh, and its beauty, and how smooth it is. That is how I was able to weed it." Then Abena Nkroma said, "Then tomorrow I will tell Father that you scratched yourself." Ananse told her, "Do not say it, for this is a bed-matter." Abena Nkroma said, "I do not know of any bed-matter, and I will say it." Abena Nkroma took her mat and went to sleep on the other side of the room. And Ananse was troubled, so he went and took his harp, and he played it, saying:

Abena Nkroma, this is not a problem.

Let us not treat it lightly.

He said, "No."

Someone's own has arrived, and another's is on the way.

Then Ananse got up and went to sleep. While Ananse was lying there, he got up. He said, "Abena Nkroma." Silence! Ananse said, "I have caught you." He took a calabash, went and fetched water, and came to pour it on Abena Nkroma's mat. Then Ananse went to sleep. While he was lying there, he said, "Go on! Abena Nkroma, what is this? You have wet the mat; shameless one, you are no good at all; when day breaks, I will tell everyone. Truly, that is why whenever anyone goes to the farm, he says, 'A child who wets the bed! I will not weed a bed-wetter's farm.'" Then Abena said to him, "I beg you, leave it alone and let it stay." Ananse said, "I will not leave it, for mine came first; you said you were going to tell your father; I said leave it; you said 'no'. So I too will not leave it." Then Abena Nkroma said, "Drop mine, and as for yours that I mentioned, I will drop it, for if you do not drop mine, I will be disgraced." And Ananse said, "I have heard, if you want us to treat it as matters of the mat, then the matter is ended." That is why the elders say, "Mat matters are not spoken of." This my folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet, or whether it is not sweet, take some and eat, and give some back to me.
