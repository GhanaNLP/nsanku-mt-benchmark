HOW THE SPIDER WALKS ON WATER QUICKLY

KWAKU ANANSE went and told Okraman, "I want to go with you to build a village and live there." Okraman said, "I too want that." Ananse said, "When Adae falls on Sunday, go get a rope on Monday, and I too will go get a rope so we can go. Furthermore, I will take a gourd and fetch water, and you too should fetch some, for perhaps where we are going there will be no water." Ananse fetched some of the water; Okraman, however, put honey into a gourd. As they were going, they got midway on the journey and grew tired. Ananse said, "Let us drink some of our water." Okraman said, "Take mine and drink." Ananse tasted some; he said, "Ah! Where does this come from?" Okraman said, "It is in my stomach." Ananse said, "I see!" As they were sitting there resting, Ananse said, "Dog, let us play a game." Okraman said, "What kind of game?" Ananse said,

"Tying-and-tying game; bind me first, and then I will also bind you, and when you hear me say 'Wui!', then you let go." The dog said, "No, you bind me first." Ananse said, "Get away, an elder is speaking to you and you are talking back?" The dog said, "Then leave it alone." Ananse said, "Come and bind me." The dog took a rope, put it on Ananse, and bound him. Then he picked him up, carried him on his head, intending to go and sell him to eat. When Ananse heard this, he raised a cry, saying:

Ananse Akuamoa, I did not get a good beast so that it would not sell me;

And this foolish dog is going to sell me out.

Who will I get to save me?

The way saves me!"

Ananse cried and cried. The dog carried him to the edge of a river. Just then, Crocodile heard Ananse crying. When he arrived, he asked the dog for news. Then the dog abandoned Ananse and ran away, and Crocodile untied Ananse. Then Ananse said, "Father, what should I use to thank you?" Crocodile said, "We do not want anything." Ananse said, "If you have children, tomorrow I will come and shave beautiful parting lines in their hair." Crocodile said, "I have heard." Ananse returned home; he said, "Aso, go and look for palm nuts and onions, for tomorrow I will go and kill Crocodile and bring him back so that we may eat soup." Aso went to look for some and came back, and Ananse sharpened his knife and mashed yam with palm oil, and took it to the river. He said, "Father Crocodile, river beast, I have come to praise you!" And he took the mashed yam and threw it into the water. Crocodile came, and as he tried to take the mashed yam to eat it, Ananse slashed at him with the knife, whoosh! but it missed him; and he swam away. Ananse returned home. Aso asked him, "Where is the beast?" Ananse said, "Get away from there; a person has just returned from a journey, he has not rested, and you are asking him questions." Aso said, "I see, you did not get him." Ananse said nothing at all. The next morning, Aso was going to the river. When she arrived, there was Crocodile lying on the bank, with large bluebottles swarming over him. Aso returned home and told her husband. Ananse said, "Now you see the kind of charm I have obtained; when I kill an animal, it is only on the next day that I go and collect it, and since you have seen it, well done!" Ananse cut a stick, took it to the river, and found Crocodile. He poked him with the stick, saying, "Crocodile, are you dead? Crocodile, are you dead?" and turned him over and over. But Crocodile was just lying there quietly; he was not dead. And just as Ananse reached out with his hand to touch him, Crocodile suddenly snapped shut around Ananse, snap! Ananse scrambled and struggled, flailing wildly, and managed to find a way to escape. That is why when Ananse walks on water he runs, because he is afraid that Crocodile will come and catch him again.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
