THE MINISTER'S STATEMENT

In the month of December 2020, Ghanaians voted again for President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to continue the good work he was doing and to do many more good things in addition to what he was doing. It is not something to undermine at all, the confidence that Ghanaians have had in this government.

This 2021 Budget is an entirely unique Budget because it will manage our economy well and help protect the citizens, stimulate businesses in distress, and then help us use our strength to set Ghana back on its path of development following the abatement of the COVID-19 pandemic. This Budget is based on our vision to transform this nation into a financially self-reliant country.

In the first four years, we made plans through various means to help this nation stand on its feet again financially. These various plans included those that would create abundant jobs, avenues through which we generate revenue for the national coffers, steps that have led to business growth, support for private businesses, and social development interventions provided to the vulnerable. By the end of 2019, Ghana was on a good path of economic development, as evidenced by all the factors that promote business and economic growth, and the global community also recognized us as a nation on the path of progress, and it was in the midst of this that this devastating COVID-19 pandemic struck us. Countries across the world, including Ghana, have all experienced the adverse effects of this devastating pandemic.

This Covid-19 pandemic has brought various hardships upon us and has fought against our businesses and daily lives. The government has recognized how this pandemic has brought adverse effects to the citizens, and for this reason, the government has made various plans to support businesses so that we can return to our normal state. There is hope because we have identified other ways of operating that will bring many opportunities. The government is determined to ensure that the citizens obtain good health, and for this reason, it has made plans for every Ghanaian to be vaccinated against this pandemic so that we will all have the health to work and lift the nation of Ghana to the level that we all desire.

We have worked together through all difficulties on our development journey, and we have taken bold steps to get this nation's economy back on track for progress. Currently, about 1.2 million Ghanaians have had the opportunity to attend Senior High School which will change their lives, we have also resolved the bad situation across the banking sector so that the majority of people have received their money that was tied up in all the collapsed banks. Furthermore, we have established an automobile assembly company in our nation and have seen the first vehicles produced by this company on the market and on our roads.

The truth is, we acknowledge that we have faced various challenges while continuing to strive to take steps toward our development goals. The government is very serious about putting measures in place to find solutions to these challenges so as to bring relief to businesses. For instance, in the month of May in the year 2020, we supported small, medium, and large enterprises and others with seven hundred and fifty million Ghana cedis (GH¢750 million) under the programme dubbed the “CAP-BuSS” Programme.

Now, more than ever, it is extremely necessary that we hold hands and unite to continue taking bold steps that will enable us to achieve the progress we desire for ourselves. At the very least, we have promised in the National Pledge that we will serve Ghana with all our strength and all our hearts.

May God bless us all.

Long live Ghana!

Ken Ofori-Atta Minister of Finance

INTRODUCTION

1. This 2021 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the activities undertaken by the Government and the total revenue the Government aims to mobilize to implement its policies and programmes in 2021. The theme for this Budget is "Completion, Consolidation and Continuation for Economic Recovery". The Budget carries this theme because in past times, each year's budget contained many new projects the Government wanted to undertake, while there were numerous ongoing projects that had not been fully completed. When this happens, not only does it strain our finances, but it also leaves us with significant debt, to the extent that we are unable to pay contractors who have completed their work on time. This leads to excessive cost overruns on projects because interest is paid on delayed payments for works that were due.

2. In this year 2021, the Government has decided to chart a new path. The objective of this new direction the Government wishes to take is to put an end to the culture of starting projects and abandoning them uncompleted, and therefore, the Government is determined to complete all unfinished projects.

12. This section shows the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the world and on Ghana in particular. Furthermore, it highlights the various measures the Government has taken to contain and mitigate these impacts.

The Effects of the COVID-19 Pandemic

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that struck the world towards the end of 2019 brought impacts on every household, every organization, and even on the economies of all nations worldwide. Economic activities were disrupted, people were laid off from jobs, citizens lost money, and various activities that were meant to bring income to the public collapsed. In addition to this, it has also been reported that over two million six hundred thousand (2.6 million) people worldwide have lost their lives.

Glossary of Terms (Financial Planning Terms)

One District One Factory (1D1F): This involves various industries that will be established in districts to promote industrial development in Ghana. The objective of this is to increase jobs in this country so that Ghanaians across all parts of the country can secure employment.

Accountability: This stands for the account that the Government gives to the citizens regarding its operations, as well as reporting on the various steps it has taken. Financial matters and various properties that the Government oversees for the citizens are all included in this.

Allocation: This is the way national funds are distributed to the various government agencies and organizations for them to use for the citizens.

Alleviation: This is a way in which the effects of a problem are reduced.

Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics - BSTEM (Basic Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): These are words used to represent these various subjects. These words are used when making educational plans and curriculum arrangements that help bring about educational development regarding these subjects.

Budget: A budget stands for a plan made by the government regarding the way it will acquire money and what it will use that money for. For example, a family can make a budget to show the amount of money they have and the various things they need to use the money for in order of priority. It could be rent expenses, water and electricity bills, and food expenses. If it happens that they need more money, it will be necessary for the family to look for additional funds or even go and take a loan.

Budget Deficit: This occurs when the government's expenditures exceed its actual revenue.

Capital Expenditure: This is money used for development projects such as road construction, school building construction, hospital construction, drainage construction, transportation, water, large machinery, and the like.

Compensation of Employees: This is the employees' monthly salary, various allowances, social security contributions, travel allowances, and related payments made to government workers.

Development Partners: This stands for countries or organizations that assist developing countries to achieve their development goals. This can be various bodies such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank, and the like. It can also be individual countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States, and others like them.

End Year Inflation: This is the level that the prices of goods reach in the month of December in a specific year.

Expenditure Overrun: This occurs when the funds used for various operations exceed what was allocated in the budget approved by parliament.

Energy Sector Levies Act -ESLA: (Energy Sector Levies Act).

Evacuation: This is the means used to move a crowd of people from one place to another.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This refers to the total value of all goods and services produced in the country within a specific period.
