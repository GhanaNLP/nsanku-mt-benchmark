FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS BY READERS OF OUR LITERATURE

From Galatians 4:24, the apostle Paul compared two covenants; what do these two covenants represent?

◼ The apostle Paul compared the relationship that Abraham had with Sarah and Hagar. That was where he said: “These things stand as a symbolic drama; for these women mean two covenants.” (Gal. 4:22-24) In the past, our publications explained the meaning of these matters to be that the two covenants were the new covenant and the Law covenant. However, as we examined the matter further, we came to see that it was necessary to adjust the explanation we previously gave regarding this matter. If you look at the matter carefully, you will see that Paul was not referring to the new covenant, but rather he was referring to the covenant that was between Abraham and God. Let us examine the matter a little further.*

Paul likened the slave girl Hagar to Mount Sinai. (Gal. 4:25) The matter of Hagar represented the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites in the year 1513 B.C.E. at Mount Sinai. (Ex. 19:5, 6) No imperfect human could follow this entire Law to the end without stumbling. Therefore, the Law kept reminding the Israelites that they had sin in them. However, this Law covenant was to help the Israelites identify the Messiah. The Messiah alone was the human who could keep the entire Law to the end without stumbling. When Jesus came and gave his life as a ransom, he opened the way for humans to gain freedom from the power of sin and death. (Gal. 3:19, 24, 25) When Jesus fulfilled God's will, the Law covenant that Jehovah made with the Israelites at Mount Sinai came to its end.—Rom. 10:4.

Paul contrasted Hagar with Sarah, who was a free woman. Sarah had her child “through a promise.” (Gal. 4:23) That promise was the Abrahamic covenant. (Gal. 3:29; 4:28, 30) Details of that covenant are found at Genesis 22:18, where it says that all the nations of the earth would through his [Abraham’s offspring

place to find blessing for themselves, because you have obeyed My word.'

The covenant that Jehovah made with Abraham helps us understand many things concerning the promise that Jehovah made in the garden of Eden. (Gen. 3:15) What Jehovah told Abraham helps us know that the ‘offspring’ referred to in Genesis 3:15 would be a descendant of Abraham. Therefore, Paul went on to say that this ‘offspring’ is Jesus Christ. Also, those who “belong to Christ” are included with Jesus to form the ‘offspring.’ Those who “belong to Christ” are the 144,000 who will go to heaven to join Jesus in ruling. (Gal. 3:16, 29; Rev. 14:1-3) All those who believe in Jesus and obey his commands will receive many blessings when God fulfills the promise he made to Abraham.

Why did Paul use this "symbolic drama"? He wanted to show that if the Israelites living in his day stubbornly insisted on following the Mosaic Law, they would be like Hagar, being slaves. But if they believed in Abraham’s “offspring,” who is Jesus Christ, they would be like Sarah, being free. They would also gain freedom from sin and death. (John 8:32-34) If they did not believe in Jesus, it would show that they did not understand why Jehovah had given them his Law through Moses. This is because the purpose of the Law was supposed to lead the Israelites to Christ.

Most of the Galatian people to whom Paul wrote this letter were not Israelites. Therefore, they did not practice the Law of Moses in their lives at all. However, some Israelites who were in the Judaism religion and had become Christians insisted that their fellow Christians in the congregation who were not Israelites must obey the Law of Moses. This meant that they had to be circumcised, just as the Law instructed the Israelites to do. But Paul said it made no sense for Christians to do so, because people no longer needed to be slaves to the Law of Moses. When Christians came to accept Christ, it allowed them to be free from following the Law.—Gal. 5:1, 10-14.

SOME THINGS THAT ARE IN THE BIBLE

How Traditional Wrestling Used To Be In Ancient Times

Today, many people know that Jesus' main followers were fishermen. They worked to take care of themselves. (Mark 1:16-20) They used to catch fish with their fishing nets on the Sea of Galilee.

What was used to make the fishing nets? Well, they used to weave ropes together like a net with holes in it. Therefore, if a fisherman wanted to catch small fish, he would use a net with small holes to catch them, but if he wanted to catch big fish, he would use a net with big holes to catch them. They used to tie objects like stones or irons to the bottom of the small fishing net, so that it could sink completely to the bottom of the water. But as for the large fishing net, they would attach heavy objects to it and also tie small sticks to the top, so that when it sank into the water, those small sticks would float on top of the water.

In ancient times, fishing nets were not cheap at all. But sometimes, the rocks in the water tore them, or when they caught a great number of fish, they would break. (Luke 5:6) The Bible says that James and John were “mending their nets,” perhaps using bone needles and thread to do so. (Matt. 4:21) This shows that fishermen likely took care of their nets after they finished fishing. Luke also wrote that “the fishermen had gone out and were washing their nets” to remove the dirt in them. (Luke 5:2) Afterward, the fishermen would spread their nets out to dry so that they would not rot.—Ezek. 47:10.

These men used to catch fish with fishnets, but Jesus called them to leave that work and come preach the good news.
