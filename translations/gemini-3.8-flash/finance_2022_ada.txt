INTRODUCTION

The COVID-19 pandemic brought challenges to the global economy, including Ghana. The various difficulties and troubles that this disease brought to the people of Ghana through death, social hardship, and the collapse of businesses placed a heavy burden on the Government. I am proud to say that the manner in which we combated this terrible disease showed a boldness and resilience that has been admired by the international community. The Government put in place policies and a comprehensive plan to cushion and support businesses and to restore our livelihoods.

Furthermore, in the face of difficulties and other matters that arose during this disease, the Government, led by Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, acted quickly to allocate funds amounting to one hundred (100) billion for the COVID-19 alleviation and revitalisation enterprise support programme, dubbed the Ghana CARES “Obaatan Pa” programme. This programme immediately brought relief and assistance to rescue citizens from distress and set the nation back on its feet.

We appreciate the trust and confidence you have in the President and the support you have given to his efforts to combat this disease. At this time, our endurance and our hope for national development have entered into a great test. As Isaiah said, “The bricks have fallen down, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will replace them with good cedars.” The President has resolved to fulfill his promises, will continue to execute his visions and plans so that he can sustain us and restore the lives of the citizens again.

One key objective among the objectives included in the 2022 budget over the next three years ahead of us is that the Government will undertake national transformation that will make the country a self-reliant nation capable of expanding or stabilizing the national economy. This will help create jobs for young men and young women. The Government will focus on laws and policies that will provide support to youth to enable them to pursue their natural talents and creativity that will bring economic growth and open up employment opportunities for everyone.

In order to enable job creation, resilience, and an entrepreneurial spirit among young men and women in the year 2022, the Government launched the YouStart initiative for youth to access capital or business funding to create their businesses or expand existing ones.

In the 2022 budget, we laid out pathways that will help overcome the challenges in the national economy, in order to strengthen it after the deadly COVID-19 pandemic. The government has committed itself to open initiatives that will assist and promote progress that will boost employment for young men and young women. As we pursue the President's vision of a Ghana Beyond Aid, I wish to put it before all Ghanaians, especially the youth, to enthusiastically participate in technical and vocational training programmes. I urge you to rise up and involve yourselves in initiatives that will develop the country into an industrialized nation.

I congratulate you, and I encourage all of us to establish our nation Ghana to become what we all desire.

FINANCIAL PLAN IMPLEMENTATION IN 2021

The country's financial planning development

3. Public opinions on how the national budget was implemented in 2021 are as follows:

• In absolute terms, the country's economic growth was not sustained as it fell to less than 3.5 percent in the first quarter of 2021, compared to an increase of 0.7 percent during the same period in 2020;

• The foreign exchange rates were stable for foreign currency transactions in the year 2021. Specifically, the Ghana cedi depreciated by about 1.7 percent against the US dollar at the end of December 2021, compared with a depreciation of about 2.9 percent by the cedi in the same period of the year 2020.

FINANCIAL PLAN FOR THE 2022 BUDGET

Financial Planning Programs

14. The government will introduce programmes that will change the structure and production of goods and services in the local and foreign markets. In doing this, it will be very necessary that we make these changes:

• In order to maximize the benefits and broaden performance and participation, the Government will extend by two years the policy relief on Value Added Tax (VAT) introduced in 2019 for the manufacturing of African textiles and garment fabrics;

• The progress of this work after two and a half years, the human trafficking test indicator used to identify victims to bring some relief, will be re-examined to function well so as to bring progress and change into the identification system;

• The tax on informal and small-scale businesses will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• Starting from January 2022, the Government will, through the Ghana Revenue Authority, assist the MMDAs to implement a centralized system for the assessment and collection of property rates;

• The government will review and adjust the fees charged by the MDAs starting from the first day of January 2022. A mechanism will be introduced so that these fees and charges will automatically increase or decrease in line with annual inflation;

• Street and lockup tolls will be abolished or removed immediately after the legislative assembly approves the budget.

• We will begin the collection of taxes on electronic commerce transactions, known as the “Electronic Transaction Levy” or “E-Levy”, in order to broaden the tax net and reach sectors that are largely outside government oversight. The transactions that this new tax will mainly cover are electronic money transfers and withdrawals, bank transfers, electronic money transfers and cash-out services, with the person sending or withdrawing the money paying a tax of 1.75 percent on the transaction values. The collection of these taxes will begin on the first day of January in the year 2022. This tax will not be charged or applied to money transactions up to one hundred cedis (GH¢100).

Financial Plan

15. In order to ensure that we understand the people's concerns well and grasp the financial program accordingly, we have laid out various strategies for this financial plan's implementation within the 2022 budget in the short term:

16. Strengthening or monitoring financial management or governance procedures so that procedures do not deviate from the established financial management rules:

• Permission will be granted only to individuals who have been included by the MDAs in their procurement plan and uploaded or published on the PPA website procurement plan;

• All procurement requisition or permission letters for the year must be based on the budget prepared for procurement and the annual procurement plan. In addition to this, the procurement processes must be prepared or managed based strictly on the funds available and the expected sources of funding;

• A head of an agency or official should not sign any MOU or agreement that will financially commit the Government without seeking permission from the senior civil servant responsible for the nation's financial planning.

EXPLANATION OF SPECIFIC TERMS (BUDGET TERMS)

One District One Factory 1D1F: The construction or opening of factories in the districts will bring a lot of work for the development of the nation of Ghana. It will help open various jobs across the entire country of Ghana. This will create diverse employment opportunities for all Ghanaian citizens.

Accountability: This is the way through which the Government accounts for all things and responsibilities entrusted into its hands to the citizens. Some of these things are: money, land and its resources, and everything entrusted into its hands.

Distribution of People: This is the arrangement through which the Government distributes different families to workplaces under the Government.

Humanitarian: This is the system through which the Government addresses the problems and various challenges of the citizens.

Budget: It is a plan that shows where money will come from and what it will be spent on. Example: A family can make such a plan to show how much money it has and how it will spend that money according to its needs. They will look at some of their specific needs, such as rent, water bills, electricity bills, food, and others. If this family needs extra money, they can work or go and borrow money for these planned activities.

Budget deficit (Deficit): This deficit occurs when the Government spends more money than the revenue it expects to receive.

Capital Expenditure of Government: This is the capital the Government puts into things like the construction of school buildings, roads, hospitals, bridges and dams, water, vehicles, and machinery.

Workers' Compensation or Pension: This is the payment that the Government pays to its workers and workers who have retired under the Government.

Development Partners (DPs): They are countries, organizations, and groups that provide aid to developing countries to carry out development projects that will bring improvement to life. These DPs can be multilateral organizations such as: the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank (AFDB), or countries like Germany, the United Kingdom, and the United States of America (USA).

Census at the End of the Year: The extent to which the census reached in the period before Christmas in the year under review.

Unauthorized Expenditure: This occurs when the Executive incurs expenditure above the amount approved by Parliament in the budget.

Free Senior High School Education: The free senior high school education without fee-paying is a new policy of the Government of Ghana introduced by President Nana Akufo-Addo's administration in September 2017. This policy allows JHS students who qualify for senior high school to attend freely without any fees.

Grants / Aid Funds: These are financial aids provided to the Government by donors or donor agencies to support national development work. These funds are free monies that do not require repayment or replacement.

Statutory Allocations: This is the legal allocation from the Government to the District Assemblies Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFund), Petroleum Fund, and the National Health Insurance Scheme.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is all that is produced within the country that constitutes the wealth of the country within a specific period or point in time.

Gross International Reserves (GIR): This refers to a country's financial strength held in foreign currencies within the balance of payments accounts over a specific period.

Human Trafficking: The trafficking of people and labor at a specific time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): This is a new or modern policy introduced by the NPP Government to reduce poverty and bring about balance or equality between the poor and the rich. Especially in the small towns through the provision of basic needs such as roads, water, and housing infrastructure.

Assessment of the Nation’s Economic Policies: This is the means through which the nation's economic performance is assessed to determine whether it has achieved the targets set for it. Some of the things this program looks at include: economic growth, inflation, various changes that occur in employment, growth in international trade, the success and failure of certain government programs, and some forecasts that the Bank of Ghana made regarding the growth of the nation's economy.

Micro and Small Business Development Programme (MSBDP): This refers to small businesses that employ a small number of people and whose business scale is very small.

Nation Builders Corps (NABCo): It is an initiative introduced by the Government of Ghana to provide jobs for graduates to address some social challenges in the country. This will also help train or equip them for the future so that it can boost or improve the nation's economy.

National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI): This agency is a government institution that oversees small businesses, whether formal or informal. This agency is under the Ministry of Trade and Industry, and its mandate is to oversee the survival and growth of these small businesses.

Non-Oil Economic Growth: This is the economic growth of the country that does not include petroleum-related activities. These reflect how the value of people increases or decreases.

Non-Tax Revenue: This is money that the Government receives that is not tax money. Some of them are fees paid to seek a permit or to request a license, fees charged for applying for documents from certain Government offices to do work.

Residual Debt: Residual debt is the outstanding debt commitments between the MDAs and the Ministry of Finance, as well as other debts that are due for payment.

Primary balance: Primary balance is the difference between the government's revenues and its non-interest expenditures (this does not include interest paid on debt). It can be measured as a percentage of the nation's gross domestic product.

Economic Growth: This shows how Ghana's economy will progress in a year.

Revenue to the Governor: This is all the money that the Governor receives from the state and external programs.

Rearing For Food and Jobs: This will bring good support to the animal rearing industry and will boost the business and reduce the importation of animals into the country. This will create jobs for various people who engage in animal rearing and will also bring improvement to their lives.

Public Interest: The government pays or subsidizes water, petrol and electricity power and some production facilities for the citizens or on behalf of the citizens.

Social Security or Welfare Money: This money is social security money that the Government provides to the citizens to help them escape the problems of poverty.



Workplace Transparency: This happens when the Government establishes measures for frequent reporting on the things it does.

Personnel Emoluments: This is the money that the Government pays for its programs for the citizens.

Value Added Tax (VAT): This is a tax that is levied on the stage-by-stage value added by people in the process of production.
