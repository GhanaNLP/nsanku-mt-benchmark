INTRODUCTION

The COVID-19 pandemic brought a crisis to the entire world's economy, of which Ghana is also a part. The various crises and troubles that this disease brought to Ghanaians through death, social hardships, and the destruction of businesses brought a great burden upon the Government. I am proud to say that the manner in which we combated this pandemic was a bold and resilient effort that pleased all nations. The Government laid down laws and comprehensive measures that were strict and supported businesses to restore our living conditions once again.

Furthermore, in the face of difficulties and other matters that arose concerning this sickness, the Government led by Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo acted quickly and provided funds amounting to one hundred (100) billion for the COVID-19 alleviation and revitilisation programme known as the Ghana CARES, “Obaatan Pa” programme. This programme brought relief immediately to deliver the citizens from distress and placed the nation back on its feet again.

We appreciate the faith you have in the President and the support you have given to the efforts he is making to defeat this disease. At this time, our perseverance and our hope for national development have entered into a great test. As Isaiah said, “The bricks have fallen down, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will replace them with good cedars.” The President has resolved to fulfill his promises, to continue to execute his purposes and plans so that he can set us on course and improve the living conditions of the citizens once again.

One of the key objectives set out in the 2022 budget for the next three years ahead of us is that the Government will undertake transformative changes in the country to make it an entrepreneurial nation that can support or build up the country's economic system. This will help create jobs for young men and young women. The Government will turn its attention to laws and policies that will provide support for youth so that they can pursue their natural talents and intellect, which will bring economic growth and open up employment opportunities for everyone.

In order to foster friendship, resilience, and an entrepreneurial spirit among the youth in the year 2022, the government launched the YouStart initiative to provide youth with funding or capital to establish or expand their businesses.

In the 2022 budget, we outlined measures to help overcome the challenges in the national economy and strengthen it in the aftermath of the devastating COVID-19 pandemic. The government committed itself to clear initiatives that will support broad-based progress and job creation for the youth. As we pursue the President's vision of a Ghana Beyond Aid, I wish to call upon all Ghanaians, especially the youth, to take an active interest in entrepreneurship. I urge you to rise up and engage in initiatives that will transform the country into an entrepreneurial nation.

I thank you and encourage all of us to establish our nation Ghana so that it becomes what we all desire.

FINANCIAL BUDGET PERFORMANCE IN 2021

National budget implementation

3. The following are the public consultations for the 2021 national budget:

• Overall, the country's economic growth slowed down and contracted by more than 3.5 percent in the first quarter of 2021, compared to an increase of 0.7 percent during the same period in 2020;

• The country's non-oil economy grew by 4.8 percent in the first half of 2021 compared to 1.1 percent in the same period in 2020;

• Domestic inflation pushed overall inflation to 10.6 percent at the end of the first quarter, compared to 10.4 percent recorded in the same period in 2020;

• Overall, the budget deficit reached 7.7 percent of the gross domestic product, higher than the projected 7.4 percent;

• Paper money reached 2.0 percent compared to the expected target of 1.7 percent;

• The country's debt reached 77.7 percent at the end of September in 2021 compared to 76.1 percent as it was at the end of December in 2020;

• The country’s foreign exchange reserves at the end of May reached US$ 11.44 billion. This could cover 5.2 months of imports compared to US$ 8.62 billion, which could cover 4.1 months of imports at the end of May in the year 2020;

• The exchange rate for foreign currencies stabilized for foreign currency transactions in the year 2021. Specifically, the Ghana cedi depreciated by about 1.7 percent against the US dollar by the end of December 2021, compared to a depreciation of about 2.9 percent in the same period in the year 2020.

FINANCIAL BUDGET ALLOCATION FOR THE 2022 BUDGET

Financial Planning Systems

14. The government will take measures that will reform the land and production systems in domestic and foreign markets. In doing this, it will be very necessary for us to make these changes:

• In order to bring benefits and develop the fabric and manufacturing industry, the Government will extend the VAT zero-rating policy introduced for the manufacture of African prints and textiles in 2019 for up to two years;

• The progress of this work after two and a half years, the human trafficking shelter which was established for survivors and brought some relief, will be reorganized so that it operates well to bring progress and change into the program plan;

• Taxes charged on small-scale mining concessions that are not regularized will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• Beginning in January 2022, the Government will assist MMDAs through the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) to implement a common platform for property rate collection and administration;

• The government will review and adjust the fees charged by MDAs starting from the first day of June 2022. A mechanism will be put in place so that these fees and charges will automatically increase or decrease in line with annual inflation;

• The street and lorry station tolls will be removed or stopped immediately after the budget is approved by parliament.

• We will introduce the collection of taxes on electronic transactions, known as the “Electronic Transaction Levy” or “E-Levy”, in order to widen the tax net to cover businesses in the informal sector. Those who will mainly be affected by this new tax are mobile money transfers and cash-outs, bank transfers, and merchant payments, where the sender or person withdrawing the money will pay a tax of 1.75 percent on the transaction value. The collection of these taxes will take effect from 1st January, 2022. This tax will not be charged on amounts up to one hundred cedis (GH¢100).

Financial Management Plan

15. In order to ensure that we understand the people's voices well regarding the financial work plan, we have rolled out several different channels for this financial planning work in the 2022 financial plan in the short term:

a. The Government shall continue to deduct into the workers' pension scheme so that only the names of people who are in Government employment will remain in the Government's master payroll;

16. Tightening or monitoring of financial administration or management systems to ensure that the systems do not deviate from the established financial management path:

• Access will be granted only to those who have been added by the MDAs to their procurement plan submitted or posted on the PPA website;

• All requests or permissions for the procurement of goods during the year must be based on their procurement budget and their procurement plan for the year. In addition to this, the procurement processes were carried out or managed based on the funds available to them and the actual expected sources of funds;

• An employer or senior official should not sign an MOU or an agreement that commits the Government financially without seeking permission from the senior civil servant responsible for the nation's financial planning.

DEFINITION OF KEY TERMS (BUDGET TERMS)

One District One Factory 1D1F: The establishment or opening of workplaces in the districts will bring numerous jobs for the development of Ghana. It will help open various jobs across the whole country of Ghana. This will open different jobs for all Ghanaian citizens.

Accountability to the People: This is the means by which the Government accounts to the citizens for all people and responsibilities entrusted into its hands. Some of these things are: money, land and its contents, and whatever else has been placed in its care.

Distribution of People: This is the arrangement through which the Government allocates various citizens to workplaces under the Government.

Human Rights / Person: This is the arrangement through which the Government relieves the problems and various difficulties of the citizens.

Budget: It is a plan that shows where money will come from and what it will be used for. For example: A family can set up this plan to show how much money it has and how it will use that money to meet its needs. They will look at some of their specific needs such as house rent, water bill, electricity bill, and food and other things. If this family will need extra money, they can work or borrow money for these plans.

Budget deficit: This debt occurs when the Government spends more money than the revenue expected from taxes.

Main Government Expenditure: This is the main money that the Government invests in things such as school buildings, roads, hospitals/clinics, electricity and bridges, water, and the acquisition of vehicles and machinery.

Compensation or Gratuity for Workers: This is the payment that the Government gives to its workers and workers who go on retirement under the Government.

Development Partners (DPs): These are countries, organizations, and groups that provide aid and support to developing countries to carry out developmental projects that will bring improvement to life. These DPs can be multilateral organizations such as: the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank (AFDB), or countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, and the USA.

Census at the End of the Year: The level that the population reached before Christmas in the year under review.

Unapproved Expenditure: This happens when the Governor spends money beyond what was put into the budget and approved by the Legislature.

Free Senior High School Education: Free senior high school education without paying fees is a new policy of the Government of Ghana that President Nana Akufo-Addo's administration introduced in September 2017. This policy freely permits JHS students who qualify to attend senior high school to attend freely without paying any fees.

Grants: These are financial aids provided to the Government by development partners or organizations that support national development efforts. These funds are outright gifts that do not require repayment or reimbursement.

Financial Support for Government Agencies: This is the statutory funds allocated by the government to Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Fund, and the National Health Insurance Scheme.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This is everything produced in the country that constitutes the nation's wealth within a certain period or a specific time.

Gross International Reserves (GIR): This indicates a country's financial strength in foreign currencies based on people's purchases and debt records for goods and services at a specific time.

Human Trafficking: The trafficking of people and labor at a specific time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP): This is a new policy or program introduced by the NPP Government to reduce poverty and bring about equity or fairness between the poor and the rich. Especially, in the small towns through programs providing essential needs such as roads, water, and housing.

National Budget Evaluation Tool: This is the means by which the performance of the national budget is monitored to see whether it is able to achieve its intended targets. Some of the things this system looks at include: economic growth, inflation, changes in employment generation, increase in trade with other countries, the progress and implementation of certain Government policies, and some projections made by the Bank of Ghana regarding the growth of the national economy.

Micro Small Business Development Program (MSBDP): This is small businesses that employ a few people and whose turnover is not very high.

Nation Builders Corps (NABCo): It is a program established by the Government of Ghana to address graduate unemployment to help resolve social problems. This will also help train them or equip them with skills for future endeavors to enhance and boost the nation's economy.

National Board for Small Scale Industries (NBSSI): This agency is a government institution that oversees small businesses that either employ a few people or do not expect large-scale operations. This agency is under the Ministry of Trade and Industry, and its mandate is to look after the survival and growth of these small enterprises.

Non-oil economic growth: This refers to the growth of the country's economy excluding the oil sector activities. These measure the extent to which the prices of things are expensive or cheap.

Non-Tax Revenue: This is money that the Government receives that is not tax money. Some are fees paid to seek a way or to request a permit, fees for signing documents with certain Government officials to perform work.

Debt Arrears: Debt arrears are contractual debt obligations between MDAs and the Ministry of Finance, as well as other obligations that are due.

Primary balance: Primary balance is the difference between the government's revenues and expenditures, excluding interest payments (this does not include debt service costs). It can be measured as a percentage of the country's gross domestic product (GDP).

Revenue of the Government: This is all the money that the Government receives from the country and external projects.

Public Utilities: The government pays fees or taxes on water, petrol and electricity power, and parts of the production of things for the citizens or on behalf of the citizens.

Allowance or Relief Fund: This money is an allowance that the Government gives to citizens to relieve poverty problems.

Facial Recognition: This system is the precise calculation of people's identity or the detailed inspection and identification of people with deep learning.

Openness of Operations: This continues if the Government sets up a procedure for prompt reporting on the things it is doing.

Public Service Funds: This is the money that the Government pays for its projects for the citizens.
