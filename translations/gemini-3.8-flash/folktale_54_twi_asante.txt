When Friendship Came into the Town

A hunter and his wife set out and went into the deep forest. Then Leopard went and caught the hunter and ate him, leaving behind his wife, who was pregnant. The woman went into the cornfield, where she met Leopard's cub, who asked her, "Mother, what are you looking for?" The woman said, "I came with my husband, and Leopard has killed him." Leopard's cub said, "I feel for you, so do not come here again; for if you come here and my father comes upon you, he will kill you." The woman did not listen, and she returned to that cornfield, and Leopard went and caught her and brought her home. When Leopard's cub saw the woman, he said to his father, "I am sick," because of the woman he had seen. Leopard said to his cub, "If you are sick, get up and go butcher the meat." He butchered it, and he saw that a child was lying in her womb, so he took it out and went to put it somewhere. His father asked him, "What have you gone to throw away?" The cub said, "What I went to throw away, if you saw it, you would not eat this meat." Leopard cooked the food. He told his cub to come and eat, but he answered him, "Because of what I have seen and my sickness, I believe that if I eat some, I will die." His father said, "You are not lying." The next morning, his father set out into the forest, and his cub went and brought the child home and bathed him. Then he went and got bananas; he cooked them, squeezed their juice, and gave it to the child to drink. He watched for the time his father would arrive, and then went to hide the child. The child stayed for about a year and grew up; the child of a folktale does not take long to grow up. The human child asked Leopard's cub, "What is your name?" He said, "My name is Aboa 'ba Kofi." He said, "As for me, my name is 'Nipa 'ba Kofi." Aboa 'ba Kofi said to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "I want to play with you, but I fear my father, and you too fear him; so one of these days I will ask him to teach me wrestling, and if he teaches me and I know how to do it, I will throw him down and hold him, and when you hear that I have got him, oh! then bring a knife and come cut off his head." His father came, and Aboa 'ba Kofi said to him, "Teach me wrestling." He said, "Tomorrow morning." The next morning, he and his father went to a place that had been swept clean, and his father leaped, and his cub leaped too, and his father went and lay on his back. His father said, "Leap and land on me." Aboa 'ba Kofi leaped, and landed on him, and Aboa 'ba Kofi struck him in the throat. He called to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, saying, "I have got him, so come!" He ran there with a knife and cut off his head. Aboa 'ba Kofi said to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "Now, you and I are even, so let us go bury him." They went and buried him, and then they returned home. Aboa 'ba Kofi said to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "I believe that you want to go to your hometown." 'Nipa 'ba Kofi said, "Yes, but I do not know the way." Aboa 'ba Kofi said, "As for me, I will go, seek the place, and return." He set off, and some people were at a rubbish dump, saying, "Truly, since this hunter and his wife went recently, no one has heard from them." Immediately, Aboa 'ba Kofi ran to tell 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "I have seen your hometown, and the people I saw were wearing cloth, so I am going to find some to bring to you." Aboa 'ba Kofi set off on the road, and some traders were passing by. He made a move toward them, and the traders threw down their goods and fled. Aboa 'ba Kofi took the cloth and brought it to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi. Then 'Nipa 'ba Kofi said, "Gun and gunpowder remain." Aboa 'ba Kofi went to seek some and brought it to him. Aboa 'ba Kofi asked 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "Will you be grateful to me?" 'Nipa 'ba Kofi said to him, "As I stand before you here, I cannot say right now." Aboa 'ba Kofi set off with 'Nipa 'ba Kofi to bring him to his hometown. When they reached the outskirts of town, Aboa 'ba Kofi said to 'Nipa 'ba Kofi, "Here is your hometown, and as you go, every single day I will bring you a carcass of meat; furthermore, when evening comes, I will come to you, so if you marry as you go, let your wife sleep on one side, and you also sleep on the other side." 'Nipa 'ba Kofi reached home, and he reported that he had gone into the forest with his mother and his father, but they had all died, and he had returned. The people offered him condolences. He fired guns and completed all the funeral rites. Whenever morning broke, Aboa 'ba Kofi brought meat. When evening came, he came to his side. And before long, he married. Whenever the woman went to sleep, he made her sleep elsewhere. When day broke, he would take a whole carcass of meat and give it to his wife. His wife saw that he never went into the bush yet always obtained meat, but she said nothing. One night as they were lying there, the woman reached out to touch her husband, and her hand touched something very smooth, while her husband was also lying there. The next morning, she went and reported to her people, saying, "My husband’s friend is a Leopard, and he is the one who gives him meat." Then the people took guns and lay in wait by the meat rack, and when evening fell, the Leopard came to put meat on the rack, and they shot him with a gun. Aboa 'ba Kofi said, "'Nipa 'ba Kofi, human beings have no gratitude." When 'Nipa 'ba Kofi heard this, he ran to carry him, brought him and laid him down there, and went to report to the chief how he had gone with his mother and his father, and all the troubles he had experienced. He said, "Therefore, since this animal is dead, I will go with him, for it was because of me that he died." Then he had two coffins made; and they put Aboa 'ba Kofi into one; and he also shot himself, and they put him into the other coffin. And they buried them. And they said, "Go and play your games of friendship." That is how friendship came into the world.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
