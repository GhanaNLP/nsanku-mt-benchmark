IF IT WERE DONE, WE WOULD NOT GET A BLAMELESS CHILD

It is said that a hunter and his son went to build a village, and they settled there, and the hunter killed animals, killed, killed, and killed. There were all kinds of animals among them. And with these animals, whenever he killed them and ate the meat, he would cut off their heads and set them aside.

As for one room, it was completely full of animal heads. But without speaking to him, whenever the hunter went into the bush, he would leave his child at home. And one day, as the child was sitting there, a great beast came into view, striding along! It said, "Good evening, my grandchild." It said, "Where is your father?" He said, "Father has gone to the bush." When the leopard looked, it saw the animal heads, so very many of them! It said, "Well! You have suffered." It said, "Bring the heads of all the animals for me to see." And the mighty beast stood there, wagging its tail like this, swish! swish! swish! Then the child lined up the animal heads there in a long row, about a hundred or more. And the leopard said, "Call out their names for me to hear." And standing over the child's neck, it said, "Call it out, what kind of animal is this?"

The Leopard said to him, "I have heard; I am going, tomorrow I will come, and when your father comes, ask him its name." His father came from the bush, and he said, "This is what I have experienced." His father said, "What is it?" He said, "When you left me here and went to the bush, Leopard came, and he made me collect all the animal skulls and made me call their names one by one, and when it reached his brother's, he told me to call the name, and I said he should let you come first, for taking that one out is terrifying." His father said, "Well! It is nothing, tomorrow too we shall see." Truly too, when day broke, his father went to the bush. The Leopard came again, and he made him gather the animal heads and place them there again; and he had him call the heads of the animals one by one. When he reached the Leopard's head again, he said, "Let my father come first; removing this one is truly terrifying!" Leopard said, "Well! As for tomorrow, if I come and you do not tell it to me, I will cut off your head as well." The child said, "I have heard." When evening fell, his father came, and he said, "Today, if tomorrow you do not prepare yourself as you should, then I will pack up and leave this town for you." His father said, "Why?" The child said, "The Leopard has come again, and he said that tomorrow, if I do not say it, what he will do to me, I don't know." His father said, "Well, I have heard." And so he went roaming to search for herbs and tree stumps to wash the inside of his gun. He loaded it and set it down there. At daybreak, he went and hid somewhere. Before long, he looked and saw that Leopard was steadily approaching. As soon as Leopard arrived at the house, he said to the hunter's son, "Today, what will happen will happen." The child said, "Oh! It is because you don't know." Leopard said, "Eh! I didn't hear, what did you say?" He said, "You said I should gather them, and what did I say?" The Leopard said, "Continue." The child brought the animal heads, gathering them up, saying, "Where should I put it?" Leopard said, "What did you say?" He said, "This animal's head, which I mentioned." The Leopard went and stood right over the child's neck, saying, "Continue, whose animal head is this one?" He said: "This is a duiker, duiker, duiker, Father was the one who killed the duiker, Father was the one who killed this duiker, when the gun fires, I offer Father condolences!" And he recited all the animals in that manner, until it reached Leopard's own. He said, "What about this one?" He said: "This is a leopard, leopard, leopard, Father Leopard, Father was the one who killed the leopard, when the gun fires, I offer Father condolences!"

The leopard said, "Ah! You have suffered; one of my ears has heard, one remains." And he asked him again, and he spoke. He said, "Both of my ears have heard, it remains for the little charm that sits on my forehead." And he whistled to call him, saying to him, "Draw closer." And the child leaped and landed. The leopard said, "Where are you going?" The child said, "I am passing between your legs!" The leopard said, "My grandchild, you are wise." He said, "What kind of beast is this here?" He said, "This is Leopard, Leopard, Leopard, Father Leopard! Father, who killed this Leopard, when the gun fires, I offer Father condolences!" As his words ended, just as the leopard sprang to seize him, the child let the gun weep—*tomm!*—and the leopard lay there flat—*tim!* Then his father arrived, and he came and cut up the leopard's meat, and he made an offering. That is why the elders say that one should not leave a child alone at the hearth, for if the hunter had done so, he would have let the leopard come and catch his child. This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away and bring some of it back to me.
