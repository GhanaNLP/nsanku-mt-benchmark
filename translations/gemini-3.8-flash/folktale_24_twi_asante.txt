How anger is expressed

A hunter went and borrowed gunpowder and two charges of shot, and he and his wife and his small child went into the bush to kill game. He went and built a hut, and he, his wife, and his child stayed there. Whenever day broke, he would go into the forest. If he saw an animal and shot at it, he would miss it. It was like that every day, for about two weeks (sixteen days). Then his wife said, "Since I get no meat to eat here, I am going to my hometown." When day broke and the hunter went into the bush, his wife and child also went to their hometown. Now, all he had left was a single charge. He was walking and talking to himself; the hunter's name was Gyekye, and his wife's name was Boniaye-kae-dabi. When the hunter wandered until he reached beneath an odum tree, the place was very beautiful. There, the hunter thought to himself, "If I go home, they will demand from me the money for this gunpowder, and I have none, so I will take this gun, shoot myself in the throat, and die." Right then, Something called out to him, saying, "Odomankoma's hunter." And the hunter said, "Father." And the Something said, "Why are your eyes so red (with sorrow)? If you do not have 'Go-tell-home' in your house, I would give you something." And the hunter said, "There is none in my house." Something said, "If there is none in your house, go to the hollow of the odum tree; two gongs are lying there, bring them." And the hunter went and brought them. Then Something said, "Go cut two saplings of the odwen tree, close your eyes, and run; take one and go plant it over there, and take one and pass by to plant it over there." When he came back, he said, "I have finished." Something said, "Take the big gong and strike it." And he struck it, and the forest turned into a town between the two saplings. And the odum tree stood in the middle. Then Something revealed itself as an Eagle. And it instructed the hunter that whenever any great matter arose, it should be judged under the odum tree. The hunter said, "Yes, I have heard." When the Eagle told him that, he received wives, elders, and royal servants. At that time, his wife Boniaye-kae-dabi heard word that he had become a chief, so she bought palm wine and set out with it to the chief’s town, with her child on her back. When she arrived, she gave her reason for coming, saying, "Your child says he brought wine to look upon your face." And he received the wine and gave it to all the people there to drink. Then he gave the child to the Gyasehene to take to the house. When the evening grew cool, the child bathed and went to the chief in his room. The child said to the chief, "You and I have endured rain and sun together; today you have found a place to stay, and was it not you who sent for me?" And when he said that—men and their foolishness—he could not reply. Right then, he seized the child

and slept with her. And the next morning, he went and took her from the Gyasehene's house and brought her to the chief's quarters. She stayed there until a time when a great matter arose.

The chief and all his elders went and sat under the odum tree. The child came and stood before his father, saw the eagle's chick up in the odum tree, and said, "Father, I want it." Then his father slapped his buttocks and pushed him on the back of his head, sending him home.

And one of the remaining children also came and stood before him again. When he too saw the child of the Eagle, he said, "Father, I want it." And he slapped his buttocks and pushed the back of his head so that he fell down, and as he was leaving, he was crying. And his mother said, "Why are you crying?" And he said, "Father beat me." And his mother said, "Eh!

He wouldn't look at you much, if it were his wife Boniaye-kae-dabi, he wouldn't look at her child."

And Boniaye-kae-dabi said, "As for this, I will not touch you lest you do something to me." As soon as he said that, the two of them joined together to do it. Then the servants said, "Because of the thing that is on this tree which is causing our father's wives to fight; we will cut it down." Right then they took axes, chop! chop! At that time the Eagle had gone wandering, and he left his children with the Old Woman and Tete-Akroma. When the people cut down the tree, it was falling to the ground

then the Lizard stretched out and said, "Pimpeao!" and the tree stood upright again. Then they went back there again. And the Lizard said to Tete-'Kroma, "Go and search for the Eagle so that she comes to look after her children, for as for me, my death has overtaken me." And right there the people went and took a gun, shot him, and killed him. Then Tete-'Kroma flew away to go and call the Eagle. As he went along, he wept, saying:

Chief! Greetings to you!

Nana, oh! Here are your children, oh!

And as he called in that manner, the Eagle did not hear; so he raised his voice, saying:

Sankuro oh, bird oh, Sankuro oh, bird Eagle.

Sankuro, bird, Sankuro, when you go feeding, return.

And Okodee heard it. And he came, and he said, "What is the matter?" And Tete-'Kroma said, "They have cut down the tree, taken away your children, and as for Oberewa, they have shot and killed her." Immediately, Okodee flew, swoosh, and came to perch on a tree at the edge of the town, and he lifted his eyes, and truly, they had cut down the tree. He said nothing. Then, as the tree broke and was falling to the ground, Okodee darted there and gathered his children. He took them and perched them on the tree where he had been staying. Then he came down to the ground and said in his mind, "Ah! Is that how humans are?" And he was deeply saddened. And he wept, saying:
