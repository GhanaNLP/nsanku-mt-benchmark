IF YOU ARE A MAN, DO NOT SAY THIS WIFE OF MINE IS THE ONE I LOVE VERY MUCH

OBEREKUO, Onwam, and Okusie were married to one man. And that man favored Okusie as his senior wife; but as for Oberekuo and Onwam, he did not care for them much at all. Whenever he went out and saw something beautiful, he would buy it for Adueku. Before long, not even two or three days later, illness struck their husband and caused him to die. When he died, his family members called the widows and said, "Come and wail." Oberekuo came and began to sing: "Du du, du du du du!

Ten ten!

Du du, du du du du!

Akuno is dead, Du du!

I won't ever eat, Du du!

And have bought Akuno.

Ten ten!"

The lesser animals said, "You have done well, you have done well; let the Toucan also come." The Toucan came and lifted it up:

Alas, alas, my husband is dead!

Limp, limp.

And my husband is dead, Kya, kya.

Akuno is dead.

Limp, limp.

I will never eat!

Limp, limp.

And I bought Akuno.

Snap, snap."

The villagers said, "You have done well, you have done well, Onwam; Rat, come, so that you and your husband may eat sweet things, sweet things." Rat said, "Do not touch me." And she came; and she raised it:

Sankuroro Dampofo!

Leprosy.

I am a rare male, Sankuroro Dampofo.

Once you are dead, you are dead!

Sleeping sickness."

And in the end, everyone took it as advice to themselves that, "In this nation, no one should say, 'This is my wife and that is my co-wife.' " And it was solely because of the Rat's crying, which he wept.

This folktale that I have told, whether it is sweet or whether it is not sweet, take some of it away, and bring some of it back.
