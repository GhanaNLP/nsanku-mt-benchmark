INCLUSION

The COVID-19 pandemic damaged the economies of all nations in the world, and Ghana was no exception. It weighed heavily on the Government that the disease brought suffering, death, and job destruction to the citizens. I am proud to say that we acted swiftly to address these challenges and worked on them with courage. Indeed, nations around the world appreciated these actions of ours. The Government has also put plans in place to revive businesses with the intention of restoring our livelihoods to the state they were in before.

In addition to this, in addressing the challenges and opportunities brought about by the disease, the Government, under the leadership of President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, began the implementation of the COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalization of Enterprises Support (CARES) Programme with an amount of 100 billion Ghana cedis. The implementation of this programme brought immediate relief to all citizens and is reviving the country's economy.

We thank you for the confidence you have in the Government as we are working hard to ensure the stability of our economic recovery. At this time, we are encountering many challenges in our efforts toward stability. Often, the Bible shows us the necessary steps during such times. Just as Isaiah 9:10 says, "The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stones; the fig trees have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place," so too is the Government firmly determined to restore things and fulfill its promises. For this reason, the Government will continue to implement various programs to restore everything that has been damaged and to improve the lives of the citizens.

One of the key objectives of the 2022 Budget is that from now until the next three years, we will promote entrepreneurship in the country with the aim of growing and stabilizing our economy. In addition, everyone must participate in the implementation of the economic program so that people, especially the youth, can find jobs. Therefore, the Government's programs will support the youth to demonstrate their potential and skills through the provision of equal opportunities for everyone, so that employment issues will be successful for all citizens.

To plant the spirit of entrepreneurship in the youth and to give them confidence, the Government will provide the youth with the opportunity to access funds to start or expand their businesses under the “YouStart” Initiative in the year 2022.

In the 2022 Budget, we planned to continue addressing urgent economic challenges with the aim of ensuring economic recovery in the post-COVID-19 era. The government is committed to implementing various programs to build the skills of the youth and promote entrepreneurship among them. As we strive to achieve the President's vision of 'Ghana Beyond Aid', I would like to urge all citizens to participate in these community projects and also to innovate. In addition to this, they should participate in promoting entrepreneurship for their sustainability in the country.

FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE IN THE YEAR 2021

Activities of Major Financial Institutions

3. The allocations regarding financial performance in 2021 are as follows:

• The country's total revenue increased by 3.5 percent in the first six months of 2021, compared to 0.7 percent in 2020;

• Non-oil Gross Domestic Product increased by 4.8 percent in the first six months of 2021, compared to 1.1 percent for the same period in 2020;

• By the end of September 2021, inflation was 10.6 percent, compared to 10.4 percent for 2020 over the same period;



• The amount of money the Government needs to add to the money it has in order to reach the expenditure amount is 2.0 percent of the Gross Domestic Product, while the target was 1.7 percent of the Gross Domestic Product;

• By the end of September 2021, the country's debt increased from 76.1 percent in 2020 to 77.7 percent in 2021;

• By the end of November, the Government's foreign reserves amounted to 11.44 billion US dollars. This amount covered 5.2 months of imports by the Government, while the foreign reserves held by the Government by the end of 2020 were 8.62 billion US dollars. This amount was equivalent to 4.1 months of imports by the Government; and

• The exchange rate of the Ghana cedi against major foreign currencies did not change in the first nine months of 2021. However, the Ghana cedi slightly depreciated against the US dollar by 1.7 percent at the end of September, compared to a depreciation of 2.9 percent in the same period in 2020.

Top Financial Technology Goals in 2022

9. The Government’s policies on the major macroeconomic indicators in 2022 are as follows:

REVENUE POLICY MEASURES IN THE 2022 BUDGET STATEMENT

Tax collection arrangements

14. The Government will implement various programs to revive commodity manufacturing industries and improve the quality of goods so that they can be sold both domestically and internationally. For this reason, it has become essential for the Government to introduce reforms in the following arrangements:

• To sustain the gains made and expand manufacturing, the Government will extend the VAT exemption granted to domestic textile manufacturers for another two years, as started in 2019;

• The debt restructuring program, under which an implementation period of two and a half years was proposed as an interim solution to the tariff payment challenges, will be reviewed. This will ensure that the debt restructuring of tariffs on imported goods benefits the nation and that the Government achieves its goals;

• Taxes paid by unlicensed gold miners for gold mining will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• From January 2022 onwards, the Government will assist the Ghana Revenue Authority to support the Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies (MMDAs) to collect and account for property taxes;

• The government will review the fees charged at public institutions for government services and their implementation will begin on January 1, 2022. The payment of these fees will be computerized so that they automatically adjust and correspond to the regular annual changes in the value of goods;

• Tolls paid by drivers for using highways and bridges will be abolished immediately after the budget is approved;

• The government will begin collecting a new tax called "E-Levy" on money transfers through banks and mobile phones with the intention of enabling those who do not pay taxes to do so. This tax covers money sent via mobile phones, merchant payments, and inbound remittances. The sender of any of these funds will pay a tax of 1.75 percent on the amount being sent, except for the recipient of inbound remittances, who will pay 1.75 percent of the money sent to them. The government will start collecting these taxes from January 1, 2022. This tax will not be paid on an amount that is 100 Ghana cedis or less than 100 Ghana cedis in a single day.

Financial Regulations

15. In order to anchor good financial governance, the Government will implement several reforms in 2022 and the following years:

a. The government will continue to ensure that only public sector workers recruited through approved processes are eligible to be paid;

b. The government will monitor the deployment of workers to workplaces in the year 2022 and the following few years with the aim of ensuring that only the appropriate number of workers are present in government workplaces;

c. New development projects announced in the 2022 Budget, as well as planned development projects of government agencies, Municipal, Metropolitan, and District Assemblies, will be monitored in accordance with the provisions of Regulation L.I 2411. This will ensure that the Government's development projects are properly prioritized and implemented.

16. The following proper financial management measures shall be implemented so as not to cause disruption in the operation of the sustainable financing scheme:

• Only undertake procurement and works planned by government agencies that have been approved and published on the Public Procurement Authority's website;

• Expenditure on procurement of equipment and execution of works by government agencies in a given year must be related to the budget prepared by the agency and approved for that year. Definition of Certain Financial Terms

The One District One Factory Initiative: Establishing factories and production facilities will enable the country to progress in manufacturing and economic matters. The goal of this initiative is to bring many employment opportunities into the country so that citizens can find work.

Accountability: It is the duty of the Government to account to the citizens for its actions, the nation's finances, and the use of the resources under its control.

Allocation of funds and resources: The plan by which the Government allocates funds and resources to government agencies.

Mitigating problems: How to reduce a problem or suffering.

Budget: A budget is a plan that the Government makes regarding where it will get money from and how it will use the money. For example, a family can make a plan for the amount of money it has and how it will use it (e.g. house rent, electricity bill, water bill, and money for food). If the family needs more money, it must borrow money or reduce its expenditures.

Budget deficit: This happens when the Government spends more money than it receives, or when the amount of money it spends is greater than the amount of money it receives.

Amount of money the Government uses for development projects: This is the amount of money the Government uses in carrying out major development projects such as constructing roads, building schools, building hospitals, constructing markets, addressing transportation issues, providing good drinking water, expanding roads, and others.

Compensation of Public Employees: Payment of salaries to Public Employees. This includes the salaries of public employees and other monies paid by the Government to public employees, pensions paid by the Government to retired public employees (Social Security), gratuities, and others.

Development Partners: These are countries and organizations that provide assistance to other countries by giving them money so they can achieve their development goals. Partners can be institutions or groups of certain countries. For example, the “International Monetary Fund” (IMF), the World Bank, African Development Bank

(AfDB) and others. Or the donor may be just a single country. For example, Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States of America (USA), and others.

Cost of Living at Year-End: How the prices of goods and services rise or fall frequently at the end of the year or in December.

Excessive expenditure: This occurs when the money spent by the Government exceeds the Budget approved by Parliament.

Free Senior High School Education: The Free Senior High School Policy, which the Government of Ghana introduced under the leadership of President Akufo Addo in September 2017, gave basic school students who were successful in the final examination the opportunity to attend Senior High School for free under the Free Senior High School Policy.

Funds given to the Government: These are various assistances that development Partners give to the Government for the execution of its development projects. Usually, the Government does not repay these funds.

Funds that the Government gives to government institutions: This is the amount of money that the Government puts into the District Assemblies’ Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Education Fund (Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Fund, and National Health Insurance Fund, among others.

Gross Domestic Product (National Aggregate of Goods): This is the total value of all goods and services produced within the country over a specific period of time.

Foreign Reserves of the Nation: This is the country's capacity in terms of foreign currencies at its disposal to purchase goods and procure workers from foreign countries at a specific time. It is also the country's capacity regarding the strength of its currency, when compared to those of other foreign countries.

Price of Goods: The way prices of goods and services consistently rise or fall over a period of time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme: This programme is one of the NPP Government's flagship programmes whose objective is to eradicate poverty and reduce inequality between the poor and the rich, especially through infrastructure development in small communities.

Macroeconomic Objectives: These are benchmarks used to measure economic activities. For example, economic growth, prices of goods, changes in employment issues, and trade with other nations. The government's economic performance or lack thereof, and other policies implemented by the Bank of Ghana that affect the financial situation in the country.

Small Enterprise Development Programme: Often, small enterprises are businesses that employ a few people and operate with a limited amount of capital.

Job Placement for the Unemployed: This is a program initiated by the Government of Ghana for graduates of tertiary institutions to address social issues in the public and private sectors to enable them to enhance their work skills for the nation's economic development.

Small Scale Industries Department: This is a non-profit government agency. This department is under the supervision of the Ministry of Trade and Industry. This department's mission is to promote the growth and development of small scale businesses.

Non-Oil Gross Domestic Product: All income earned by the country through other means. This is all the income the Government receives through its revenue-generating activities excluding the income it earns from oil; this also takes into account the value of goods.

Non-Tax Revenues: This is the Government's revenue that is not derived from taxes. For example, license fees and user fees paid for the use of public goods.

Government Arrears: This includes debts or amounts paid by the Government to government institutions through the Ministry of Finance, as well as arrears owed by the Government in the year 2016.

Surplus or Deficit: This is the net amount of money left if the Government subtracts the amount of money it has spent from the amount of money it has (excluding loans). Most often, this amount of money is measured as a percentage of the country's Gross Domestic Product.
