VISION

The COVID-19 pandemic damaged the economies of all nations worldwide, and the nation of Ghana was not exempt. It weighs heavily on the Government that the disease brought suffering, death, and disruption of jobs upon the citizens. I am proud to say that we took swift action to resolve these challenges and worked on them with courage. Indeed, the nations of the world expressed appreciation for these steps we took. The Government has once again put measures in place to revive jobs with the intention that our lives may return to the state they were in previously.

In addition, in responding to the challenges and opportunities brought about by the pandemic, the Government commenced the implementation of the COVID-19 Alleviation and Revitalisation of Enterprises Support (CARES) Obaatan pa Programme, with an amount of 100 billion Ghana cedis, under the leadership of President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo. The implementation of this programme has brought immediate relief to all citizens and is revitalising the nation's economy.

We thank you for having confidence in the Government as we work hard to ensure the stability of our economic recovery. During this period, we are facing many challenges in our efforts toward resilience. Often, the Bible shows us the necessary steps in times like these. Just as Isaiah 9:10 says, "The bricks have fallen down, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the fig trees have been felled, but we will replace them with cedars," likewise the Government is firmly resolved to restore things and fulfill its promises. For this reason, the Government will continue to implement various policies to restore everything that was ruined and improve the lives of the citizens.

One of the main objectives of the 2022 Budget is that from now until the next three years, we will promote entrepreneurship in the country with the aim of growing and stabilizing our economy. In addition, everyone must participate in economic activities so that people can find jobs, especially the youth. Therefore, the Government's programs will support the youth to showcase their potentials and skills by providing equal opportunities to all, so that employment matters will succeed for all citizens.

To instill the spirit of entrepreneurship in the youth and encourage them, the Government will provide young people with access to capital to start or expand their businesses under the “YouStart” Initiative in 2022.

In the 2022 Budget, we planned to continue addressing critical economic challenges with the aim of achieving economic stability in the post-COVID-19 era. The government committed to implementing various programs to build youth skills and promote entrepreneurship among the youth. As we strive to realize the President's vision of 'Ghana Beyond Aid', I wish to urge all citizens to participate in these initiatives and foster innovation. In addition, they must engage in promoting entrepreneurship for their sustainable livelihood in the country.

FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE IN THE YEAR 2021

Major Economic Activities

3. Accusations related to financial management in the year 2021 are as follows:

• The country's Gross Domestic Product increased by 3.5 percent in the first six months of 2021 compared to 0.7 percent in 2020;

• The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) increased by 4.8 percent in the first six months of 2021, compared to 1.1 percent in the same period of 2020;

• By the end of September 2021, the inflation rate was 10.6 percent, compared to 10.4 in 2020 over the same period;

• The fiscal deficit requested by the Government was 7.7 percent of the Gross Domestic Product, whereas the target pursued by the Government was 7.4 percent of the Gross Domestic Product;

• The budget deficit was 2.0 percent of GDP against a target of 1.7 percent of GDP;

• By the end of September 2021, the country's public debt increased from 76.1 percent in 2020 to 77.7 percent in 2021;

• By the end of October, the government's foreign exchange reserves stood at US$11.44 billion. This amount was equivalent to 5.2 months of imports of goods and services, compared with foreign exchange reserves of US$8.62 billion at the end of 2020, which was equivalent to 4.1 months of imports; and

• The exchange rate of the Ghana cedi against major currencies did not change in the first nine months of the Year 2021. However, the Ghana cedi slightly depreciated against the US dollar by 1.7 percent at the end of September, compared to a depreciation of 2.9 percent in the Year 2020 over the same period.

THE 2022 BUDGET AND ECONOMIC POLICIES

Goals of the Major Financial Theories in 2022

9. The Government’s plans for the main economic indicators in the Year 2022 are as follows:

• Gross domestic product will increase by 5.8%;

• The country's Gross Domestic Product increased by 5.9%;

• By the end of the year, the inflation rate will drop to 8%;

• The amount of money the Government will need in addition to the revenue it expects to receive before it can carry out development projects will be 7.4% of the country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP);

• The remaining funds for the Government before it pays the interest on the money it borrowed will be 0.1% of the gross domestic product; and

• The volume of the country's foreign exchange reserves shall not be less than the value of imports ordered by the Government over four (4) months.

MEASURES TAKEN ON FINANCIAL RESOURCES IN THE 2022 BUDGET

Tax collection systems

14. The Government will implement various programs to revive manufacturing industries and improve the quality of goods so that they can be sold both domestically and internationally. For this reason, it has become essential for the Government to make adjustments to the following programs:

• To sustain the gains made and expand domestic production, the Government will extend the VAT exemption granted to domestic textile manufacturers for a further two-year period, as was introduced in 2019;

• The debt restructuring program, under which implementation over two and a half years was considered as a period for resolving debt repayment problems, will be reviewed. This will ensure that debt restructuring for taxes on imported goods will benefit the country and allow the Government to achieve its objectives;

• The withholding tax paid by unlicensed gold miners for gold mining will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• From January 2022 onwards, the Government will assist the Ghana Revenue Authority to support Municipal, Metropolitan and District Assemblies to collect and account for property rates;

• The government will review the fees charged by government institutions for government services, and their implementation will begin on January 1, 2022. The payment of these fees will be computerized so that they will adjust automatically and correspond to the regular annual changes in inflation;

• The collection of tolls paid by drivers for using highways and bridges will cease as soon as the financial plan is approved;

• The government will begin collecting a new tax called the "E-Levy" on electronic money transfers made through banks and mobile phones, with the intention of widening the tax net to include people who are not paying taxes. This tax covers mobile money transfers, merchant payments, and inbound remittances. The person sending any of these funds will pay a 1.75 percent tax on the amount being transferred, except for recipients of inbound remittances who will be the ones to pay the 1.75 percent of the money sent to them. The government will begin collecting these taxes from February 1, 2022. This tax will not be paid on an amount of 100 Ghana cedis or less in a single day.

Financial Regulations

15. In order to stabilize the financial system and ensure sound financial management, the Government will implement several measures in the year 2022 and the subsequent years:

a. The government will continue to ensure that only government workers who were employed through approved channels are eligible to be paid;

b. The government will monitor the deployment of workers to workplaces in the year 2022 and subsequent years with the intention that only the appropriate number of workers are in government offices;

c. New development projects announced in the 2022 Budget, as well as planned development projects of Ministries, Departments and Agencies, and Metropolitan, Municipal, and District Assemblies (MMDAs), will be reviewed in accordance with the provisions of L.I. 2411. This will ensure that the Government's development projects are executed in an orderly and efficient manner.

VARIOUS WORKPLACES AND THEIR OPERATIONS

33. As the implementation of the National Development Agenda for 2018 to 2021 comes to an end, the implementation of another National Development Agenda from 2022 to 2025 will begin. This new development agenda is linked to the long-term National Development Plan to be implemented up to when Ghana turns 100 years (Ghana@100). The goals of this plan are also aligned with the United Nations Agenda 2030 (Sustainable Development Goals) and the African Union Agenda 2063.

Definition of Some Special Terms of the Financial System

One District One Factory Initiative: The establishment of workplaces and factories will enable the nation to progress in manufacturing and economic matters. The goal of this initiative is to attract many jobs into the country so that citizens can find employment.

Accountability: It is the duty of the Government to account to the citizens for its activities and the use of the nation's funds and resources under its control.

Allocation of funds and resources: The arrangement by which the Government allocates funds and resources to government agencies.

Reduction of problems: How to reduce a problem or suffering.

Budget: A budget is a plan made by the Government regarding where it will get money from and how it will use the money. For example, a family can make a plan regarding the amount of money it has and how it will use it. (e.g., rent, electricity bill, water bill, and money for food) If the family needs more money, it must borrow money or reduce its spending.

Budget deficit: This happens when the Government spends more money than it receives, or when the amount of money spent is greater than the amount of money received.

Amount of money the Government uses for development projects: This is the amount of money the Government uses in carrying out major development projects such as constructing motor roads, building school buildings, building hospitals, constructing bridges, managing transportation affairs, extending safe drinking water, paving roads, and others like them.

Government Workers' Compensation: Payment of Compensation to Government Workers. This includes salaries and other monies paid by the Government to government workers, benefits paid by the Government to retired government workers (Social Security), gratuities and others.

Development Partners: These are countries and organizations that provide assistance to other countries through funding so that they can achieve their development goals. Partners can be an institution or a group of certain countries. For example, the “International Monetary Fund” (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank

(AfDB) and others. Or the Contributor can be only one single country. For example, Germany, United Kingdom, United States of America (USA) and others.

Prices of Goods and Services at the End of the Year: How the prices of goods and services frequently rise or fall at the end of the year or in December.

Excessive spending: This happens when the money spent by the Government is more than the Budget approved by Parliament.

Free Senior High School Education: The Free Senior High School Policy implemented by the Government of Ghana under the leadership of President Akufo Addo in September 2017 provided the opportunity for basic school students who were successful in the final examination to attend senior high school for free under the Free Senior High School Policy.

Funds given to the Government: These are various supports that development partners provide to the Government for its development work. Most often, the Government does not repay these funds.

Government transfers to government agencies: This is the amount of money the Government puts into the District Assemblies' Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Fund, and the National Health Insurance Fund, among others.

Gross Domestic Product: This is the total value of all goods and services produced within a country over a specific period.

The Nation's Foreign Reserves: This is the country's ability in the foreign currency reserves it possesses to purchase goods and hire workers from foreign countries at a specific time. It is also the country's capability regarding the strength of its currency, when compared with that of other foreign nations.

Cost of Goods: The way the prices of goods and services constantly rise or fall over a period of time.

Infrastructure Development for Poverty Eradication Program: This program is one of the NPP Government's flagship initiatives aimed at eradicating poverty and promoting equity between the have-nots and the haves, especially through the implementation of development projects in rural communities.

Small Enterprise Development Program: Often, small enterprises are businesses that employ few people and operate with a small amount of capital.

Job Placement for the Unemployed: This is an initiative established by the Government of Ghana to provide employment for graduates from tertiary institutions in order to address social challenges in public and private institutions, to enable them to develop their work skills, and to advance the country's economic development.

Small Scale Industry Development Agency: This is a non-profit government agency. This agency is under the supervision of the Ministry of Trade and Industry. The mission of this agency is to promote the growth and development of small businesses.

Non-Oil Gross Domestic Product: Total earnings of the country through other means. This is the total revenue the government generates through its economic activities, excluding revenue derived from oil; this also takes into account the value of goods and services.

Non-Tax Revenues: This is the government's revenue from non-tax sources. For example, license fees and user charges for using public facilities.

Government Arrears: This includes the arrears or amount of money that the Government pays to government agencies through the Ministry of Finance, as well as the debts owed by the Government in the year 2016.

Surplus or Deficit: This is the amount of money or debt that remains when the Government subtracts the amount of money it spent from the amount of money it has (excluding borrowing). Often, this amount of money is measured as a percentage of the country's Gross Domestic Product.
