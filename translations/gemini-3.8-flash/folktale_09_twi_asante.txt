Just as the crab will get a cooking pot, so the old woman will also get crab soup

A certain child had been born as a child; whenever she sent him on an errand, he would say, “I will not go.” She would say, “Stay there, you and your elders.” Every day she sent him, he would say, “I will not go.” Then one day she said, “Go fetch water and bring it to me.” He said, “I will not go.” Then his Old Woman beat him, and she said, “Stay there and you will see.” Then the child said, “I am going to look for ‘I will see’ and find out.” And he set off on the road, and he ran on swiftly, yiridi! yiridi! yiridi! Now, he reached a certain town and looked, and there was an Old Woman sitting there. As soon as he saw her, he tried to turn back. The Old Woman said, “My grandchild, do not turn back, for I have seen you.” And the child went over; the Old Woman said, “Why are you so bathed in tears, like this?” He said, “It is Mother whom I live with, whenever she sends me, she says, ‘Stay there and you will see,’ and she beats me, so I said I was coming to look for ‘I will see’ and find out.” And the Old Woman said, “That is me.” She said, “Go behind the house and dig up yams and bring them; as you go, when you arrive there, whichever says, ‘Dig me, dig me, dig me,’ do not dig it; but whichever says, ‘Do not dig me, do not dig me,’ that is the one you must dig.” He said, “Alright!” Truly, he went there; some of the yams were saying, “Dig me, dig me, dig me”; some also were saying, “Do not dig me, do not dig me, do not dig me.” And those that said “Do not dig me” were the ones he went and dug. And he brought them; and the Old Woman told him, “Peel them and put them on the fire.” And he peeled them and put them on, and they cooked, and he took them off, and he placed them before the Old Woman. And she said, “Go to the back and look on the drying-frame, there is meat on it, search among it and take what pleases your appetite.” And the child took some and brought it, and he put it on the fire and made soup. When he finished, he placed it before the Old Woman. The Old Woman said, “Have you finished?” He said, “Grandmother, yes.” She said, “Come and pound the fufuo.” And the Old Woman went and lay flat on her back; she said, “Dish out the boiled yam into my nostrils and pound it.” The child said, “Good grief! Is it in a person’s nostrils that one pounds fufuo; is Mother crazy?” All this he said in his mind. He went and pounded it; he pounded and pounded it gently. The Old Woman said, “Pound it hard, if you do not pound it hard, your waist will break.” And he kept at it, pi! pi! pi! and he finished and served it, and took it to the Old Woman. The Old Woman said, “My grandchild, mention my name and take some and eat.” He said, “You are named Grandmother Old Woman.” She said, “Aha! He did not see it, so he did not eat.” And the Old Woman ate it all. For eight days she treated him like that every day. Then one day the child went to the stream to fetch water. A Crab saw him and said, “Why! Someone’s child, why are you so emaciated like this?” He said, “A certain Grandmother Old Woman whom I stay with, when I cook food for her, she says, ‘Mention my name and take some and eat; when I say, you are named Grandmother Old Woman,’ she says, ‘Aha! He did not see it, so he did not eat,’ and she has eaten it, that is why I am emaciated.” Then the Crab said, “She is named Aberewa Mosono.” The child said, “Alright.” And he carried his pot. The Crab said, “As you go, do not forget it.” And the child set off, all the way as he came he kept saying, “Aberewa Mosono, Mosono, Mosono.” As he reached the outskirts of town, he forgot it, and he stumbled and the pot broke, and he went home. The old woman said, "Where is the water pot?" He said, "When I was coming, I stumbled, and it broke." She said, "Take another one and go." And he took it and went; and the Crab said, "Have you come back now?" And he said, "The name that you told me, I forgot it, and I have come to ask." The Crab said, "Her name is Aberewa Mosono." And he said, "Yes." All the way going home he kept reciting it, and when he reached home, he set down his load. The old woman said, "Go dig up yams and bring them." He did the same thing again, and he brought the food and placed it before the old woman, and the old woman said, "Mention my name and take it and eat." He said, "Your name is Nana Aberewa." She said, "I say my real name." He said, "Your real name?" The old woman said, "Yes!" He said, "Your name is Aberewa Mosono." Nana Aberewa said, "Take it and eat." And the child took it and ate it, and the old woman set out on the road, and she said, "I am going to find the person who told you that my name is Mosono." And she went to the riverside, met Obonto Ya, and she said, "You, whose mother is a catfish, whose father is a catfish, did you tell my grandchild that my name is Mosono?" He said, "It was not me." The old woman looked to see Mudfish who was there, and she said, "You, whose mother, mother, mother is a mudfish, whose father, father, father is a mudfish, was it you who told my grandchild that my name is Mosono?" He said, "It was not me." She looked to see Crab who was sitting there. She said, "You, whose mother, mother, mother is a crab, whose father, father, father is a crab, was it you who told my grandchild that I, born in Accra, a royal child, my name is Mosono?" Crab said, "Yes! I was the one who told him, and why have you caused someone's child to become so thin like this?" And the old woman became angry, and the calabash that she was holding, she lifted it up and threw it to strike Crab. That is why Crab's back is hollowed like a pot. In the past, the hollow on Crab's back was not there. And Crab also scooped up white clay and also threw it to strike the old woman's forehead. That is why old women's foreheads are white. This spider tale that I have told, whether it is sweet, or whether it is not sweet, take some to eat, and let some come back to me.
