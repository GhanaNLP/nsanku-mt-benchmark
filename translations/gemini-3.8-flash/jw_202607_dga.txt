What Lessons Can We Learn From the Gibeonites?

‘The people of Gibeon had made peace with the Israelites and were living among them.’—JOSH. 10:1.

When the Israelites left Egypt and arrived at the Promised Land, it was 40 years. Jehovah helped them conquer the cities of Jericho and Ai, which were in the Promised Land. Therefore, some people from certain communities in the land of Canaan heard about how the Israelites were conquering people. So they made up their minds to come and say certain things to the Israelites. They came and told the Israelites that they had come to them from a land that was very far away. They said that they wanted to make friends with them rather than fight with them, because they knew that God was on their side.

2 These people were the Gibeonites. When you look into the Bible, this is the first time the Gibeonites are mentioned. However, later on, the Bible mentions them again. The Bible also shows how the Gibeonites and the Israelites lived together for many years. The Gibeonites displayed many good qualities that we can imitate. We can also learn about Jehovah’s good qualities from the things he did for the Gibeonites.

Strive to resolve misunderstandings between you and your sibling peacefully!

Has a brother or a sister done or said something that hurt you, and the issue keeps troubling you so that you are no longer friends? If that is the case, you should act quickly and do everything you can so that you can be friends again. If you do so, it will help maintain unity between you and your siblings.*

In this likeness, if someone's bone is broken and the person goes to have it set, the bone setter usually massages the bone very well. Little by little, the bone straightens and is no longer crooked. But if the bone setter does not massage the bone carefully, even if the bone heals, it will still remain crooked. If you also do not take good care of it, the bone will not straighten well again.

It is also the same when our friendship with a brother is damaged and we do not take steps quickly to resolve the matter. If we delay in resolving the issue quickly, our friendship will be damaged even further. If that happens, you will begin to harbor grudges in your heart and will no longer want to reconcile with that person. However, Jesus said: ‘Settle matters quickly with your adversary.’ (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, it is necessary that you take the initiative peacefully to discuss the matter with the person. Make an effort to go to him so that you can do so. But why might it be difficult for you to do so? Let us consider two of these reasons.

First, if you and a good friend have a misunderstanding, at the beginning you may not want to talk with them about the issue. Perhaps you don’t even know how to begin talking about it.# This same issue happened to some of the first Christians. Prepare yourself for the meeting so that your comments will be effective

Each of us has the opportunity to give a comment at the meeting to encourage our brothers and sisters. (Heb. 10:24, 25) How can you prepare yourself to comment in a way that builds up your brothers?

• Always pray to Jehovah. Pray that he will help you so that your answers will bring praise to his name and also encourage your fellow worshippers. —Ps. 35:18.

• Try to write your own words, do not just read your answers directly from our publications. If you express answers in your own words, your brothers and sisters will benefit greatly from it and also see how you have faith.

• Make your comments brief. When you keep your comments short, it helps your siblings to also get the opportunity to answer questions so that they can be involved in the meeting.—Eccl. 10:19.

• Take good care so that your speaking does not harm your sibling/fellow person in any way. When you are going to speak, be very careful not to let your speech mock or publicly expose the secrets of your sibling/fellow person.

Even if they do not call on you to comment, you can still encourage your brothers and sisters. You can listen attentively to their answers and then show your appreciation to them.—Prov. 10:21.
