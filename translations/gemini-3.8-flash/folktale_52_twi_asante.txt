IF WITCHCRAFT COMES INTO THE TOWN

A CERTAIN man, a hunter, and his wife lived in a village, and she became pregnant, but the man could not look after her well. So the child went to Nyankonpon and said, "I have come to make a pledge with you, that when I give birth, you will take my child in marriage." Nyankonpon also agreed. Then she went and made a pledge with Leopard as well, so that she might get meat to eat. Leopard also agreed. And she informed her husband about it. Whenever Leopard got meat, he brought some to his mother-in-law. And whenever Nyankonpon also got meat, he brought some to his mother-in-law. In this way, they cared for the child until she gave birth safely. When she gave birth, she bore a child, and she sent word to Nyankonpon saying, "Your mother-in-law has given birth to a child." Then she also went and told Leopard, "Your mother-in-law has given birth to a child." Then Leopard gathered things together and sent them to her to wipe away the child's birth-dirt. Nyankonpon also brought some as a congratulatory gift. They reared her until she grew up well and beautifully. Then she reached puberty, and they went and informed Nyankonpon. They also went and informed Leopard. Nyankonpon sent things to his wife to perform her puberty rites. Leopard also came and performed puberty rites for his wife; and he added a silver ring to it. And they completed doing all the things. But Leopard did not know that she was Nyankonpon's wife; and Nyankonpon also did not know that she was Leopard's wife. Later on, Leopard found out; and he said to his mother-in-law, "You have made a fool of me, therefore I will not do anything to you, but as for your daughter, the silver ring that I gave her, I am taking back my thing." He caught her hand and pulled it, and removed his ring, and went to his town with it. As for the girl, Nyankonpon sent word to her saying that his wife had grown up and that she should be brought to him. As she was going, they tailored a smock for her to wear, so that no one would see her hand. And they set off and went. When she bathed, she bathed at night. Now, the girl who lived with her was called "Kra", and Kra spied on her one day, and saw that her hand was cut off. Then she told her fellow wives, "The girl who has come, her hand is cut off, so when the Adae festival falls on a Sunday, on Monday you should show off your bodies so that Nyankonpon will offer her as a sacrifice." They informed the girl about it. The next morning she mashed yam and added eggs to it. She said, "I am going to my mother's place to prepare myself." When she went, she came upon a certain pool, and Python was lying in it. He asked her, "Why is it that no one comes here, yet you have come here?" And she told Python everything. Python said, "This is nothing at all; but what have you brought?" She said, "I have brought mashed yam and eggs." And Python accepted and ate it. Then Python opened his mouth wide and said to the girl, "Put your stump of a hand into my mouth." And she put it in. After a little while, he said, "Take it out!" She took it out, and the stump of her hand was gone, and it had become a whole hand. Then the girl asked him, "What will you charge me?" He said, "I will not charge you anything, but the child that you will have with Nyankonpon, bring it to me." Then Python turned behind him and brought out a golden mortar and a golden pestle and gave them to the girl, and she set off and went to Nyankonpon's town. The next morning Nyankonpon sat down and asked the women to come and display themselves before him. Then the girl took off the smock and showed her hand. All the people said, “Well done!” And she brought out a gold mortar and pestle, and they finished setting the table for the rice. God went and slept with her, and she became pregnant, and she bore a child. When forty days had passed, she took leave of her people, saying that she was going to her mother’s town. And she mashed a great deal of mashed yam, and one hundred eggs, and set out and arrived at Onini’s place. And she said to him, “Today I have come to thank you; here is the child.” And she added the eggs and the mashed yam to it. And Onini said, “I too, I will not kill this child, but call him Aniniwa, and do not let my name ever be lost.” And she thanked Onini; and she set out and came home. That is how the name Anini came into the nation.
