MESSAGE FROM THE PASTOR

The massive destruction brought by the deadly COVID-19 disease created a huge economic challenge everywhere, including Ghana. The hardships affected all citizens of Ghana through deaths, living conditions, and disrupted jobs, but the Government stood firm on its feet. I boast to say that the measures we took to contain it were courageous and decisive, and the whole world bore witness to this with gladness. The Government also enacted some policies that would help revive businesses so that livelihoods could be stabilized once again.

Furthermore, through the challenges and opportunities that emerged after this deadly disease, the Government, led by His Excellency Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo, acted quickly to establish a development programme, investing GH¢100 billion into interventions and initiatives to empower people financially and repair the damage caused by COVID-19, namely the GhanaCARES “Obaatan Pa” programme. This gave citizens immediate relief and comfort, and the nation's businesses began to stand on their feet again.

We appreciate your faith in these Governors even while efforts are being made to properly restore public services after the devastation of this terrible disease. In this time of our testing, it is our time of trial, and we are striving to emerge from this trouble. The Holy Scripture always guides us in times such as these. As the book of Isaiah testifies in chapter 9:10, “The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stone; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place” (Isaiah 9:10). The Governors have resolved to work to fulfill the promises. Therefore, efforts will be made to put good plans in place and enact laws that will support the governance of the nation to promote a good life.

One of the key priorities of the 2022 Budget to be implemented over the next three (3) years is to stabilize all businesses so as to support economic growth, empowerment, and ensure an all-inclusive, complete employment drive, especially for youth employment. Government policies will particularly strengthen the youth to help them realize their talents and capabilities, enabling them to empower themselves for the development of all.

To strengthen the entrepreneurial spirit of young men and women, in 2022 the Government will introduce an initiative called “YouStart” to help them access capital to start or expand their businesses.

In the 2022 budget, some measures have been outlined to continue addressing fiscal challenges and to robustly emerge from post-pandemic issues. The government is sacrificing and working relentlessly to strengthen activities so that young men and women can gain livelihoods and advancement in private enterprises. As we enter into our President's vision to develop the country through the "Ghana Beyond Aid" agenda, I appeal to all Ghanaians, especially the youth and women, to take interest, think deeply, and actively participate in national activities so as to reform and rebuild the nation's institutions.

I am asking everyone to get involved so that the nation can be built properly.

FINANCIAL PROVISIONS BILL ESTIMATES FOR THE YEAR 2022 BUDGET

Land search legal arrangements

14. The administrators will use certain arrangements, and this will promote businesses so that they can withstand competition in both domestic and international markets. Therefore, it is necessary to make the following laws:

• In order to bring benefits into the industry and enable greater production, the Government will extend the VAT exemption introduced in 2019 for textile manufacturers on locally manufactured fabrics in Ghana by another two years.

• If this practice continues after two and a half years, the safeguard regime will be used so that its exact cost and implications can be evaluated.;

• The withholding tax on unprocessed gold will be reduced from 3 percent to 1.5 percent;

• From April 2022, the Government, through the Ghana Revenue Authority, will assist the Municipal Assemblies (MMDAs) to enforce a comprehensive law that will help with the collection and accounting of property rates on houses and properties.

• The government will review taxes and fees charged for activities to be used by MDAs from the 1st of January 2022. Taxes and fees on goods to be collected will be based on the domestic inflation rate and collected automatically;

• The toll collected on all roads will no longer be collected after this budget is approved.

• The "Electronic Transaction Levy" or "E-Levy" will be introduced as a measure to assist in collecting taxes through digital transactions to open up the tax or revenue net into the informal sector. Transactions where this tax will be collected include mobile money transfers, bank transfers, remittances, and merchant payments, and that tax rate will reach 1.75%, which will be borne by the sender, except for merchant payments where the recipient will also pay the tax on it. The implementation of this will begin from the 1st of February in the year 2022. Transactions for which no payment is made include amounts not exceeding GH¢100 or less, per day, which are exempt from this tax payment.

Financial plans

15. In order to manage the debt and the state of financial affairs, several expenditure measures will be initiated in 2022 and over the specified period:

a. The Governors shall continue to maintain the workers' payroll books faithfully, so that only the well-known workers of the country's Governors are recorded in the books and only they are paid;

b. An action plan will be prepared to reform civil service performance in 2022 and within the scheduled time to help improve Civil Service operations and training institutions that are established for the advancement of the services;

c. All recruitments advertised to be carried out in the 2022 budget and those scheduled to be undertaken in the plans of MDAs, MMDAs, and SOEs will be processed through the law, L.I. 2411, without considering the source of remuneration, to determine the eligibility to execute them within the Civil Service.

16. Suspected money laundering activities will be revisited through the following ways in order to not compromise the set timeline:

• Only items mentioned and approved that are in the ‘MDAs’ Procurement Plans’ on the PPA ‘website’ will be allowed to be used in procurement;

• All items requested by Buyers in the offices to be purchased during the year should be included in the budget and approved. Not only these, but everything requested to be purchased must have a specific justification indicating where it will be obtained from and the available funds with which it will be bought;

• No government official should sign any document for approval that will commit the Government of Ghana to financial obligations unless the Minister for Finance is involved or has previously approved it.

PROGRESS AND CHALLENGES OF MAJOR ROAD WORKS

33. Because the period set for some documentation regarding the nation's overall development plan elapsed between (2018-2021), the year 2022 is the time to transform and reshape the nation under the 'Ghana Medium-Term National Development Policy Framework (MTNDPF)' 2022-2025. This new constitutional policy framework is anchored on the long-term period the entire nation has set for development initiatives, which is the 'Long-Term National Development Framework' (Ghana@100), planned development initiatives through economic and social policies from (2017-2024), the Ghana Beyond Aid Agenda and its strategic pathways, the COVID-19 alleviation and business revitalization, and supportive initiatives, which is the (GhanaCARES) program. These frameworks are designed to help ensure that the goals and visions set forth in the UN 2030 (SDGs) and the African Union Agenda 2063 are implemented.

MEANING OF SOME TERMS IN FINANCIAL PLANNING

1D1F (One District One Factory): Industrialization in every district, which will make Ghana a transformed nation. The aim is for the citizens to get jobs everywhere in Ghana

Accountability: This is the way Governors account to the citizens for the things that have been done, and it allows the results that come out to be seen. Money and other properties are included in the things that the Governors oversee for these citizens' accountability matters.

Sharing: How to share items with Everyone in the Government domain

Exemption: Ways of reducing hardship

Budget: It is a plan that shows the money that will be needed, where it will be obtained, and what the money will be used for. A family can write its budget to show the money the family has and how it will be used, for example: paying house rent, utilities, food, and others. If money will be needed for the things desired, then the family either takes a loan, reduces its needs, or finds another way that will bring in money.

Budget deficit: This occurs when the government spends more money than what goes into the treasury

Capital expenditure: This refers to money spent on capital projects such as roads, schools, hospitals, bridges, transport, water systems, workplaces and their machinery.

Employees' remuneration: It is the regular monthly payment to employees and other monies paid into the hands of employees such as allowances, pension pay, and other monies given to workers.

Donors: They are foreign countries, or institutions that support developing countries to progress in their financial situation. Some of these donors are IMF, World Bank, ADB, or countries that have partnered with Ghana for assistance. Some of these countries are Germany, USA, UK, etc.

Year-End Inflation: It is inflation in Afuabe.

Excess Expenditure: This happens when the Government spends more money than was allocated in the budget approved by Parliament.

Free Senior High School or FREE SHS: The Government of Ghana, led by Nana Akufo-Addo, introduced the Free SHS policy in September 2017 for students who have completed JHS and qualify to attend SHS without paying any school fees.

Grant: These are funds that community development partners donate to the Governors. Those funds are not returned or paid back to the donors.

Statutory Transfers to Governments : These are statutory funds deducted for certain institutions by law. Some of these funds are District Assemblies’ Common Fund, (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Related Fund and National Health Fund

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): This means the total value of money that will be obtained for the country's goods and services within a specific period.

Foreign Exchange Reserves: This measures a country's funds that can be relied upon to acquire foreign goods at a given time. It is used to back the currency circulating within the country.

Purchasing Power of Money: This refers to the prices of goods and the quantity of things that money can buy at a given time.

IPEP (Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme): The Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme is one of the NPP Government's initiatives aimed at eradicating poverty and completely bridging the gap between the poor and the rich, especially in villages and less developed areas, through the provision of developmental projects in these places.

Macroeconomic targets: This is a projection for the country's economic standing. Some indicators include economic growth and its changes, inflation, changes in employment, our trade development with other nations, the success or failure of the Government's budget, and the Bank of Ghana's objectives regarding money supply and its allocation in economic activities.

MSME (Micro And Small Buisness Developmet Programme): It refers to small businesses that employ few people and have accounting records or that show profits that do not reach a certain amount of money

NABCo (Nation Builders Corps): also known as NABCO, is an initiative introduced by the Government to engage unemployed graduates to help address social challenges in public and private sectors so that they gain work skills for future employment in this country and contribute to national development.

NBSSI (National Board for Small Scale Industries): National Board for Small Scale Industries: It is a body under the Ministry of Trade and Industry. Their work is to oversee the development of micro and small enterprises (MSEs)

Non-oil Gross Domestic Product (GDP): It is the total amount of money generated from national economic activities excluding oil revenue. It is used to measure the growth of wealth in the country.

Non-Tax Revenue: This is the money that the Government receives from things that are not taxed. Some of these are license fees, fees for services, profits, and others.

Other channels: Other channels are from other Ministries, Departments and Agencies (MDAs) to the Controller and Accountant-General's Department (CAGD) and other payments owed to some public funds.

Difference between gross income and gross expenditure: This is the difference between the gross revenue received by the country and its gross expenditure, excluding any borrowing. It does not include interest payments. This is used to assess all the revenue that the country will receive.

Projected growth: How Ghana's financial system will perform during the year.

Revenue: All the money a country obtains for its activities, including what is acquired from within the country and from abroad

RFJ (Rearing for Food and Jobs): This is an initiative and effort of the government to create competition in the livestock rearing business and enable us to get more animals to help reduce the importation of meat from abroad, and for people to get employment and development in livestock rearing.

Public assistance/subsidy : It is money paid by the Government to reduce the cost of certain things like electricity, water, and petrol for the citizens.

Subsidy: It is money paid by the Government to reduce the price of something so that the citizens can have relief.

Transparency: It is the timely provision of information by the Governors to the citizens regarding the things that are done.

Goods and services tax: It is the money that the Administrators pay for their activities and service work
