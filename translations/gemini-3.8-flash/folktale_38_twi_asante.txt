The elders say, "Whether it is your sibling, your husband, or anyone who is working, if they ask you for help, help them."

ANANSE told Aso, "Let us go and make a farm." Aso said, "I will not go." Ananse went to clear the land. When he returned, he said to her, "So truly you did not go?" Aso said, "A woman dislikes weeding." Ananse said, "Tomorrow then, let us go and burn the farm." Aso said, "A woman dislikes farm-burning; should I go for fire to burn me all over?" And Ananse went and returned. The next day, he said, "Aso, I beg you, accompany me so we can go and plant things." She said, "I said I will not go." Ananse said, "I have suffered." Then he went and planted everything nicely. Now as time went on, the crops bore fruit. One day Aso went to the farm. She came and said to Ananse, "Kwaku, the food in the farm is ready, so which day shall we go there?" Ananse said, "Oh! What a Muslim has told me, I do not understand myself at all." Aso said, "Why? Speak so that I may hear." Ananse said, "I will be right back." Ananse went to the farm, and as he stood there, he said, "I, Kwaku Ananse, being as I am, shall I sit by for a woman to take me for a fool?" Ananse took a knife, an axe, and a hoe, and dug a huge pit. He took all the food crops in the farm and put them inside, and then he returned home. He said, "Aso, come, let me tell you the news." Aso came. He said, "Ah! It was a Muslim whom I went to consult about my destiny; he said there are only eight days left for me to die, therefore as for that farm, I should not go into it, nor should you go into it, and when I die, that is where you should go and bury me. I have dug a huge pit lying there, and you should bury me inside it." The eight days arrived; Ananse suddenly died. People lifted Ananse up and went to bury him in his farm. They returned and held a very grand funeral. Aso also went into widowhood. On the eighth day of the mourning, Aso said, "Ah! Let me go and look at my husband's farm." When Aso arrived; the farm was completely cleared out! Aso said, "Eh! The farm my husband made and which I was hoping on, people have come and eaten all the things in it." And she wept and placed a curse, saying, "Kwaku Ananse's Ghost, all the things in your farm that you left behind for me, people have come and eaten them." Kwaku Ananse raised his voice:

Stop it! I have lost!

Stop it! The ear hates noise.

Stop it! I have suffered!

Stop it! The ear hates loud noise. Stop it! I have enjoyed my things!

Stop it! The ear hates insults.

Stop it! I have eaten my thing!

Now, when Aso stood there, she was afraid. She went home and called Rabbit to come so they could go and listen to something on her husband's farm. Rabbit set off with her. When Aso arrived at the farm, she began to weep again:

My husband is Kwaku Ananse, and he has died leaving me a farm.

And people have eaten food on the ground.

Kwaku Ananse's ghost, I kneel, I beg you, kill them for me.

Kwaku Ananse has lifted it:

Stop it! I have suffered!

Stop it! The ear hates insolence, stop it! I have eaten my thing!

Stop it! The ear hates insults, stop it! Let me enjoy my things!

Stop it! The ear hates insults.

Stop it! I have suffered! Stop it!

By the Creator's nature, have you seen a woman who does not help her husband?

Stop it! I have eaten my thing, oh! Stop it!

Hare said, "I have finished listening, so let us go home so I can tell you the news." He went home with Aso. Hare said, "Ananse said, he told you, 'let us go clear the bush'; you said you would not go; 'let us go burn it'; you said you would not go; 'let us go plant'; you said you would not go; so stop crying, for he has eaten his things, and, since the creation of the Creator, he has never seen a wife who does not help her husband; so stop crying, for he has eaten all his things." Therefore, it is said, "Whenever anyone is doing something and begs you, help him."
