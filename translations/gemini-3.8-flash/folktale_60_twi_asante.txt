We are not alike, not alike at all

IF YOU DO, "KURONTO" AND "BURU" WILL COME

HUNTER Kwaku and his wife Buruwa became poor, so they set off and established a village. Whenever Hunter Kwaku woke up and went into the forest, he killed a bush-cow. When he brought it home, he said, “This one is not good.” Then he went and sold it. Every day he killed one, it was like that. His wife did not see the money either, so she asked him, saying, “You do not bring money, and you do not bring meat, so what is happening?” He said, “If I go and it is not bought, it gets rotten and I throw it away.” One day, he went and killed one, and carried it onto the road, and his wife followed him, went to spy on him, and saw that he had sold the bush-cow, used the money to buy an earthen jug of palm wine, and drank it. Then his wife returned home. Not long after, Hunter Kwaku also arrived. When he reached home, he said, “Buruwa, good evening!” She replied, “Welcome, Bush-cow Jug.” He asked, “Why do you respond to my greeting like this?” She replied, “It fits you.” The hunter said nothing. As for him too, the next morning, his wife went to the farm and returned; she said, “Good evening!” He replied, “Welcome, Buru.” Buruwa said, “Why do you respond to my greeting like this?” The hunter said, “It fits you.” That is how it came about in the nation saying, “Welcome, Kuronto,” and “Welcome, Buru.” And it came from Hunter Kwaku and his wife Buruwa. This tale of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away and bring some back.
