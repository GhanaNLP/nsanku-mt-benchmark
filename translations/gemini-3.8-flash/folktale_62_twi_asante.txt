We are not alike, not alike at all

HOW DIVORCE CAME INTO THE NATION

There was a hunter called Kwasi Gyaba; he and his wife went to live in a hunting camp, and they hunted. Whenever Kwasi Gyaba went into the forest, he brought no meat home. He never brought any meat. Then one day, his wife asked him, saying, “I believe that when you kill game, you sell it and spend the money, or you give it away; therefore, today if you go into the forest and do not bring meat home, I will go to my mother, for I am tired of eating nothing but salt and vegetables.” The hunter Kwasi Gyaba set off into the forest. He looked and saw an antelope passing by. He said, “This antelope, wherever you will sleep, I will sleep there with you.” The antelope went and stood under a tree. He raised the gun—bang! From where did the python come, from where, but it coiled over the antelope; and a leopard also leaped upon it. The hunter stood by completely silent. He said, “Alas! I am in trouble.” The python said, “Father Hunter, come, for when one head intends something, another head intercepts it; so Hunter, come let us go to our town, and let us go and share this antelope, and we will not harm you.” The hunter took his gun and followed them. They went and shared the meat. The python said, “Father Hunter, you and I are allies; so sleep here today, and I will give you medicine; and when I drop this medicine into your ears, you will hear the speech of the grasses, the leaves, and all the animals. Therefore, tonight when you go to sleep, do not breathe.” The hunter went to sleep; he held his breath. The python came and dropped the medicine into his ears. When day broke, the Hunter heard the speech of all things clearly. He set off and came to his town. And when he arrived, he found that his mother-in-law had come to her child. Now, that mother-in-law of his was blind in her right eye. The hunter went and sat on the veranda. While speaking with them, he went and met his mother-in-law pounding corn. The hunter sat there quietly; and two chickens were there. One asked the other, “Where do you get this corn, while whenever I go, she pecks at me?” The other said, “Why, don’t you see that her right eye is blind, so if you take the corn and pass by the blind side, she will not see?” Kwasi Gyaba heard this; he turned his back, laughing heartily: he! he! he! His mother-in-law said, “Ah, my son-in-law, why are you laughing at me like this?” And Kwasi Gyaba lay on the ground, laughing and laughing. Right then and there, his mother-in-law swore God’s oath upon him that Kwasi Gyaba must go and explain the meaning of that laughter before God. God sent executioners, and they came and arrested Kwasi Gyaba. When he reached the outskirts of the town, he raised a song:

"And I will die crying out, oh Kwasi Gyaba!

And I will remain dead, oh, mother, weep for me.

Kwasi Mpuroko Gyaba, as he was going, said, "O dear, weep for me." Nyankonpon's elders said, "Listen to the song that the prisoner sang." Kwasi Gyaba was brought before Nyankonpon. The linguist stood up and said, "Kwasi Gyaba, your mother-in-law says you laughed at her; is it true, or is she lying?" Kwasi Gyaba said, "Nana, I have a certain medicine, but if I explain its meaning, I will die." Nyankonpon said, "I am angry with you, so I will not let you die." His mother-in-law said, "Nana, he is lying; he was actually laughing at me, so he must explain the meaning to me." The linguist said, "Kwasi Gyaba, have you heard?" Kwasi Gyaba said, "Then I ask for a pit to be dug, so that when I die, I will be put into it." Just as he finished speaking, a rooster and a hen arrived; and the rooster said to the hen, "Stand there so that I may mate with you." The hen said, "Why, right here in front of people?" The rooster said, "If you do not stand there, I swear an oath that you must take yourself away." Kwasi Gyaba heard this. Right then, he also said, "Nana Nyame, let them stop digging the pit, for I have something to say." Nyame said, "Speak!" Kwasi Gyaba said, "I swear an oath that this mother-in-law of mine should take her daughter away." All the elders said, "True, true, the matter is settled." Right then, Nyame made a law saying, "Anyone, if your companion runs away from you, swear an oath upon them, or if you act like this, swear an oath to divorce them." This is why divorce came into the nation. And it was from the rooster that it was learned.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
