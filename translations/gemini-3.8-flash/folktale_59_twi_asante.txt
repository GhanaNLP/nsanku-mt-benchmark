We are not alike, not alike at all

Even if a dog is well cared for, it will never give up stealing.

There was once a Dog, and because of his stealing, he could not find a woman to marry. So he set out to another town and found a woman there to marry. He lived there with his wife, and later his wife set off and came to her hometown. Then her husband sent word to her that he was coming to visit her. His father-in-law took a gun, went into the bush, killed an antelope, and kept it in wait for Dog. After about three days, Dog went there, and upon arriving, he saw that the antelope meat had been placed over the fire in the kitchen. At night, he went to sleep with his wife. When Dog got up to urinate, he saw the meat dripping fat into the fire. Dog went back to bed, put his head on the pillow, and kept looking at the meat. Soon, the aroma of the meat took hold of him; try as he might to restrain himself, it was to no avail. Oh! Then he got up, went to the meat, ate it all up, and came back to bed. The next morning, when his wife went to sweep and looked for the meat, not even a single piece remained there. His wife cried out, “Mother! Mother! Someone has eaten all the meat.” Dog heard this, became thoroughly ashamed, covered his head with his cloth, and lay there silently. Then he got up at once, took to the road, ran back to his own town, and reported that he had gone and eaten his father-in-law’s meat and had been disgraced; therefore, he was going to look for whoever had medicine for thievery so that they could give him some. The people said, “Hare has a very potent one.” Dog set out at a run and arrived at Hare’s town. He said, “Father Hare, I have fled to you for refuge; I have stolen and stolen and stolen, and they say you are the one who has the medicine for it, so please give me some, and whatever you charge, I will pay.” Hare said, “Indeed, I have some, but go look for pork and bring it.” Dog set out to another town, managed to get one thigh for one and a half pence, and brought it. Hare boiled it, cut it into pieces, chopped it up, ground pepper, salt, and onions, mixed them with it, and put it on the fire. When the fire touched the meat, as it cooked, one could hear it sizzling, s-s-s-s! And Dog looked on. Then Hare said, “Dog, you go sleep in one room, and I too will go sleep in one room, and tomorrow morning, I will take this meat and make the medicine for you.” Dog went to sleep. Hare also went to sleep. Then, as Dog lay there, the aroma of the meat filled his nostrils, and he could not remain quiet. Then he called out again. He said, “Father Hare.” Hare said, “Yes!” He said, “In this town of yours, there are mice here, so let me go fetch the meat and put it beside me.” Hare said, “Go get it! As for me, I only care that I get hold of it tomorrow morning to make the medicine for you.” And Dog went to fetch it. Then Dog lay there again. He said, “Father Hare.” He said, “Yes!” He said, “The mice will eat it, so I will put it under my pillow and sleep.” Hare said, “I only care that tomorrow when day breaks, I use it to make the medicine for you.” Dog lay there again; try as he might to restrain himself, it was to no avail. He said, “Father Hare, the mice have come under this pillow, so let me carry the meat on my head and stand until daybreak.” Hare said, “Shoo! I only care that when day breaks, I use it to make the medicine for you.” Dog took the meat, carried it on his head, and stood there. Then a little gravy from the meat dripped across his face, and he licked it. He said, “Oh! Father Hare, this meat, the mice are about to climb all over me and reach my head, so I am holding it in my mouth.” Hare said, “Oh no! I thought tomorrow morning I would use it to prepare medicine for you.” Now holding it in his mouth, he was pacing around the room; he was wagging his tail back and forth! He said, “Father Hare, let this meat be mine.” Hare said, “How can it be yours?” Dog said, “I say, rather than letting the mice eat it, let it be mine.” Hare said, “No, oh! Let tomorrow come.” Oh! Right then Dog swallowed it. Then he lay down there and went to sleep. Very early the next morning, Hare said, “Dog, bring the meat.” Dog said, “Oh! Father Hare, why, didn't you hear me yesterday when I said let it be mine?” Hare said, “So you have eaten it?” Dog said, “Yes! I have eaten it; I bought my own thing.” Hare said, “You Dog, your thievery will never cease from you.”

This folktale that I have told, whether it was sweet or whether it was not sweet, take some of it away and bring some of it back.
