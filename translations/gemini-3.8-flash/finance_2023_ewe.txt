INTRODUCTION

It is an indisputable truth that this year has been a year in which economic issues troubled the nation more than in other years. Like other countries of the world, the COVID-19 disease troubled us in the first three months of the year 2020. This caused the economy to no longer move forward properly. This caused the amount of money we earn to decrease and necessitated that we spend money on unplanned things to curb the disease.

As we were emerging from the devastation of the Covid-19 pandemic and began implementing programs that would bring transformation, various challenges emerged. These challenges undermined the economic successes we had achieved from 2017 to 2019. The first challenge was the war being fought between Russia and Ukraine. Subsequently, delays in approving the revenue measures in the 2022 Budget led to discouraging signals in the economy. For the first time in a decade, access to international capital markets was closed to us. Raising domestic revenue for development projects also became increasingly difficult, and the interest we pay on loans increased. All these factors led to an increase in the volume of our debt and interest payments, and the Ghana cedi depreciated sharply.

Although foreign currency inflows supporting us from lending sources have decreased, the amount of money we spend on imported goods has increased. In addition to this, the increase in expenditure on loan servicing has further depreciated the value of our currency. This has made the cost of living difficult for all of us, both the Government and the citizens. The increase in fuel prices caused the prices of goods and many services to rise significantly, with inflation reaching 40.4 percent in October 2022.

These difficult times are a test we are going through as a nation, and the Government understands the hardships that these things have brought upon us.

Tax collection systems

19. To ensure the country's economic stability, the Government submitted tax policy measures equivalent to 1.35 percent of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) to Parliament for consideration and approval. Some of these tax measures include:

• Customs duties on tobacco products will be reviewed to align with ECOWAS directives, with higher excise duties imposed on hard liquor than on beer.

• The VAT rate will be increased by 2.5 percentage points from 12.5 to 15 percent. In addition, the VAT threshold will be reviewed to introduce significant reforms regarding VAT-exempt items.

• The tax known as NFSL will be changed to GSL with the intention of covering all businesses.

• The tax known as "E-Levy" will be reduced from 1.5 percent to 1, and the daily threshold amount that was exempt from payment will be scrapped.
