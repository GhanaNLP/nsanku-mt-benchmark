IF RIGHTEOUSNESS CAME INTO THE NATION

NTIKUMA, when a great famine came, set off to go and seek food to bring to his father, Father Ananse. He set off and went to a certain town; he met an Old Woman. The Old Woman said to him, "My grandchild, why is it that nobody comes here, yet you have come here?" Ntikuma said, "It is because of hunger, and I have come to look for something to go and eat." The Old Woman said, "Go to my farm over there, and the yam that will say, 'Dig me up, dig me up,' do not dig it up, but the yam that will say, 'Do not dig me up, do not dig me up,' dig it up and bring it." Ntikuma went. Some of the yams said, "Dig me up, dig me up"; others also said, "Do not dig me up, do not dig me up." And Ntikuma dug up the ones that said, "Do not dig me up, do not dig me up." He brought them home; he peeled them. And the Old Woman said, "Go and throw away the good food, and take its peels, put them into a pot, and place it on the fire." Ntikuma peeled them and placed the peels on the fire. When it was cooked, he took it off the fire and poured it out, and it had all turned into good food. Ntikuma said, "I have finished, so give me meat to put into it." The Old Woman said, "Go and tell the Monkeys that are in the tree, 'The Old Woman says you should all drag your testicles on the ground.'" The Monkeys said, "If it were not for the Old Woman, we would have beaten you."

Nana Aberewa said, "As for me, it is not my problem, so you come and beat him." The animals got down to beat him. Nana Aberewa said, "My Mfamfinam Cutlass, cut off the heads of these animals for me." And Mfamfinam cut off the heads of the animals. Ntikuma gathered all the animals, dug up some yams to add to them, and set off home to his town. When Father Ananse saw Ntikuma's face, he said, "Welcome, firstborn." He said, "Come sit behind me." Ntikuma went and sat there. Then Ananse went and took one yam; he said, "As for this one, when I menstruate, I will eat it." Aso said, "Since the Creator created things, have you seen a man who menstruates?" When Ananse, his wife, and his children finished eating the yams, Father Ananse's own remained. The next day, Ananse said, "I have menstruated." And he went and sat behind the house, cooked the yam, and ate it until he was full. Ntikuma said, "I am going again." Ananse said, "I will go with you." Ntikuma said, "I will not go with you." When Ntikuma went to sleep, Ananse went and took a small bag, pierced holes in it, put ash into it, and tied it to the edge of Ntikuma's cloth. The next morning, very early, Ntikuma set off on the way. When day broke, Ananse looked for Ntikuma, did not see him, went and looked for the ash, saw it, and followed it. When he arrived, there sat Ntikuma and Nana Aberewa. Nana Aberewa said, "Father Man, what is the news?" Ananse said, "I am hungry, so give me something to eat." Nana Aberewa said, "As for something, where would I get it?" Ananse said, "You take me for a fool; even when my son Ntikuma came, you gave him some." Nana Aberewa said, "Go to the farm; the yam that says, 'Dig me up, dig me up,' do not dig it up, but the one that says, 'Do not dig me up, do not dig me up,' that is the one you should dig up." Ananse said, "Ah! foolish talk is what you are telling me; if a thing says it loves you, you say as for you, you hate it, have I ever heard the like?" And Ananse went to the farm. When he got there, some yams said, "Dig me up, dig me up," and some also said, "Do not dig me up, do not dig me up." And he dug up the ones that said, "Dig me up, dig me up," and brought them home. When he finished peeling them, Nana Aberewa said, "Go throw away the good food, and put its peels into the pot on the fire." Ananse said, "Ah! what kind of nonsense are you telling me; since the Creator created things, does one throw away the good food and take its peels?" Ananse took the good food, put it on the fire, and threw one of the peels on top. When the food was cooked, he uncovered it and all of it had turned to stones, and the one peel he had thrown on top had turned into good food. Father Ananse said, "Eh! so this old woman was not lying." And Ananse went and gathered the peels and placed them on the fire. When the food was cooked, he went to uncover it and it had turned into beautiful, soft yam! He said, "Nana Aberewa, as for this, give me meat to put on it." Nana Aberewa said, "Go look on that tree, there are some Humpbacks up there; when you go, say to them, 'You beasts'." Ananse said, "Look at this woman! I have no quarrel with these Humpbacks, so why should I go and insult them?" Nana Aberewa said, "Go, and if any trouble comes, it is my own problem." Ananse set off and went. He said, “You monkeys in the trees, Old Mother says you are beasts.” The beasts said, “Were it not on Old Mother's account, we would beat you.” Old Mother said, “Then come and beat him.” Ananse said, “Old Mother, that is not good.” Then the beasts fell upon him, about to beat Ananse. Old Mother said, “My Knife-That-Cuts-Cleanly, cut off their heads.” Tintin! Old Mother said, “Cool down!” But Ananse did not hear. Then Ananse gathered the meat and brought it home, and Old Mother stepped aside a little. Then Ananse stole the knife, put it in his pocket, and ran away with it to his town. When he reached his town, he said, “Ntikuma, because of the knife I have obtained, let us go into the bush.” So they set off, and they went and saw the Wild Hogs. Ananse said, “Ntikuma, go and insult them.” When Ntikuma went, he said, “Wild Hogs, you are dirty.” The Wild Hogs said, “Were it not that you are Kwaku Ananse’s child, we would come and beat you.” Kwaku Ananse said, “Come and beat him; he is no good at all, his insults are no joke.” When the Wild Hogs moved to beat him, Kwaku Ananse said, “Old Mother's Knife-That-Cuts-Cleanly, cut off their heads for me.” Oh! and the Knife fell upon them, and finished cutting off the heads of all the beasts. And then it went on cutting the trees. Kwaku Ananse said, “Ah! what is this you are doing? I said cut the heads of these beasts; you too have finished cutting them, and now what is this?” Then Ntikuma looked this way; he said, “Cool down!” Kwaku Ananse did not hear, but the Knife fell to the ground, and Ananse went and picked it up. They returned home. The next morning, Kwaku Ananse said they should go to Nyankonpon. Ntikuma said, “Father, on account of this knife, do not go.” Ananse said, “Get away from there!” Ntikuma said, “Go, then.” Ananse set off to Nyankonpon, went and told him, “Nana, today, if anyone opposes you, I alone, Kwaku Ananse, will cut off all their heads in a single day for you.” Nyankonpon said, “This is indeed worthy of thanks, but there is a great army standing at the edge of the town, so go!” Ananse said, “Bring me liquor and one sheep.” Immediately, they brought them. Ananse took to the road; Ananse asked the army, “Are you the ones who say you will fight Nyankonpon?” They said, “Yes! What are we saying?” Nyankonpon’s army was standing behind Ananse. Then Kwaku Ananse said to Old Mother's Knife-That-Cuts-Cleanly, “Cut off the heads of the army for me.” And the Knife cut off their heads—tan! tan!—completely. Ananse said, “It is enough, so stop.” Then the Knife turned on Nyankonpon’s army and was cutting them down. Ananse said, “Hey! This is Nyankonpon’s army, so stop,” but it kept cutting. Ananse said, “If you are angry, let it cool.” Oh! all of Nyankonpon’s army was wiped out! Now the Knife was flying through the air; it was coming toward Kwaku Ananse. He said, “Look here! Do you not know me? Do you not know me? I, Kwaku Ananse, am the one who came and took you from Old Mother.” Oh! and then Kwaku Ananse’s head, tan! Then the Knife went on and on; and wherever it struck, it turned into the leaves called “Etene,” whose edges are sharp, sharp, sharp.

This story of mine that I have told, whether it is sweet or not sweet, take some away, and bring some back.
