MESSAGE FROM THE MINISTER OF FINANCE

In December 2020, Ghanaians voted again for President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to continue his good work and others over the next four years. The confidence that the citizens have in this government is something that we will not take for granted at all.

The 2021 Budget is a significant budget that spoke about how we are restructuring our economy to protect citizens, revive livelihoods affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, and do our best to ensure that Ghana improves in the post-COVID-19 era. We built the Budget on our goal of bringing new transformations to the nation's development so that we can be financially self-reliant.

For the greater part of the past four years, we restored our economy through the implementation of several key programs: we promoted macroeconomic stability, began implementing tax enhancement programs, undertook various development projects, supported the private sector, and also promoted social interventions. By the end of 2019, we brought about many transformations in our economy. This related to the macroeconomic targets we achieved and the appreciation shown by other nations of the world for Ghana's progress during the period of the COVID-19 pandemic. This disease damaged economies across the entire world, and the nation of Ghana was no exception.

This COVID-19 disease has brought many challenges to our businesses and daily lives. However, the Government has recognized how the disease has affected us as a nation and has put in place various measures so that businesses do not collapse and things can be well restored. We have not lost hope at all, because we have discovered many new ways through which we can do what is necessary. The Government is committed to ensuring that we are healthy. Therefore, the Government has made arrangements to vaccinate Ghanaian citizens to prevent the disease so that we remain strong to work and help Ghana reach where we want it to be.

Even though we are in difficult times, you are united with us in carrying out our developmental projects, and we are making efforts to revive our economy for our progress. About 1.2 million students are receiving the right training in secondary schools for building their future. We addressed the issues of the collapsed banks and we paid the majority of those who saved money in these banks. In addition to this, we established an automobile assembly plant and the sale of vehicles manufactured in this assembly plant has begun, and some are already operating on our roads.

In fact, we encountered some challenges in executing our development projects. The government is working hard in various programs to eliminate these challenges so that we can find relief in our operations. For example, we supported small enterprises with an amount of 750 million Ghana cedis under the Coronavirus Alleviation Programme Business Support Scheme in May 2020, among all others.

Now, we are being asked to join hands with one another and work harder than we did before so that the nation will progress. We will do this by continuing with the important projects we are working on so that we will all see progress. We must work diligently in our activities for the development of the nation, because we have pledged from the bottom of our hearts to serve the nation of Ghana with all our strength.

May God bless us all.

Long live the nation of Ghana!

Ken Ofori-Atta, Minister of Finance

PREFACE

1. The 2021 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the activities carried out by the Government in the year 2020 and the revenue it expects to protect its plans and execute development projects in the year 2021. The theme of the 2021 Budget is "Consolidation, Completion, and Continuation." The reason for choosing this theme is that over the past years, plans were made to start new development projects in every new Budget. This resulted in existing projects being left abandoned. This practice puts pressure on our finances, and also causes us to make some unnecessary retrospective payments, while many contractors are not paid on time for work done. This leads to the cost of executing development projects becoming higher than necessary, because interest is paid on the amounts that were not paid on time.

2. In the year 2021, the Government planned to work through a new approach. The Government made this plan with the intention of eliminating the stalling of development projects so as to complete the development projects that had been started.

ECONOMIC PERFORMANCE IN THE YEAR 2020

Macroeconomics in Action

3. The breakdown of financial performance in the year 2020 is as follows:

• The country's core inflation excluding petroleum products in the first nine months of the year was 0.4, and it is expected to be 1.6 percent at the end of the year;

THE 2021 BUDGET AND FINANCIAL STATEMENT

Major Financial Goals in 2021

8. Government policies and fiscal operations (which are revenue mobilization and expenditure management) influence macroeconomic indicators over a medium term. The 2021 Budget was based on the following macroeconomic targets or parameters:

• The country's Gross Domestic Product will increase by 5.0%;

• The country's total goods excluding petroleum will increase by 6.7%;

• By the end of the year, inflation will drop to 8.0%;

• The amount of money that the Government will need in addition to the expected revenue to execute development projects will be 9.5% of the Gross Domestic Product;

• The amount of money the Government will require in addition to what it has in order to complete the development projects will be 1.3% of the gross domestic product; and

• The foreign exchange reserves of the country held by the Government shall not be less than the value of imports ordered by the Government over a period of four (4) months.

12. This section talks about how the COVID-19 pandemic damaged economic affairs around the world, especially in the nation of Ghana. It also talks about the plans the Government made regarding how to curb the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and reduce its damages.

COVID-19 Disease Complications

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that began spreading across the world around the end of 2019 caused severe damage to households, businesses, and the entire global economy. It caused many economies to plunge abruptly, many people lost their jobs, people's livelihoods were disrupted, and economic conditions worsened. In addition to this, the disease killed over 2.6 million people worldwide.

14. In the country of Ghana, up until the last week of July 2020, the spread of the disease increased from 11 people to 781 people per week before dropping to 104 people in the last week of December 2020. In the year 2021, another wave of the disease began to spread again and increased from 99 people in the first week of January to 880 people on the 5th day of February.

15. As part of efforts to stop the spread of the COVID-19 disease, the Government issued an order for Ghanaian citizens to stay at home. This directive affected many institutions that build the country's economy within the nation, especially the entertainment, agriculture, health, travel, commerce, construction, financial, and education sectors.

16. This brought suffering to transport workers, market women and other informal sector workers, and they do not benefit from any social protection. Although this directive was essential in curtailing the spread of the disease, it caused a total decline in the consumption of goods and the execution of many jobs.

17. Even though countries in West Africa have implemented these directives, many countries around the world have praised the Government of Ghana for the measures it has taken to stop the spread of the disease. This has further encouraged us to work even harder.

Definition of some special terms of the financial system

The One District One Factory Initiative: Establishing workplaces and factories will enable the nation to advance in manufacturing and the economy. The aim of this initiative is to bring many jobs into the country so that citizens can find work.

Accountability: It is the duty of the Government to account to the citizens for its activities, and the use of the nation's funds and resources under its control.

Resource and asset allocation: The process by which the Government distributes funds and assets to government agencies.

Mitigating problems: How to reduce a problem or suffering.

Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics: These subjects are important in education and curriculum development to promote science and technological literacy.

Budget: A budget is a plan made by the Government regarding where money will come from and how the money will be used. For example, a family can make a plan regarding the amount of money it has and how it will use it. (e.g. rent, electricity bill, water bill, and food) If the family needs more money, it must borrow money or reduce its spending.

Budget deficit: This happens when the Government spends more money than it receives or when the amount of money spent is more than the amount of money received.

The amount of money the Government uses in carrying out development projects: This is the amount of money the Government uses in carrying out major development projects such as constructing roads, building classrooms, building hospitals, constructing bridges, managing transport issues, providing clean drinking water, constructing paths, and others.

Compensation of Government Employees: Payment of salaries to government employees: This comprises the salaries of government employees and other monies paid by the Government to government employees, payments made by the Government to retired government employees (Social Security), gratuities/allowances, and others.

Development Partners: These are countries and organizations that support other countries through financial assistance to enable them to achieve their development goals. Partners can be an institution or a group of certain countries. For example, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank (AfDB), and others. A partner can also be just a single country. For example, Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States of America (USA), and others.

Prices of goods at the end of the year: How prices rise at the end of the year or in December (the twelfth month).

Overspending: This occurs if the money used by the government is greater than the budget.

ESLA Act: Energy Sector Levies Act

Evacuation: Procedures followed in evacuation operations in an area.

Free Senior High School: The Free Senior High School Policy implemented by the Government of Ghana under the leadership of President Nana Akufo-Addo in September 2017 provided an opportunity for junior high school students who passed their final examinations to attend Senior High School for free under the Free Senior High School Policy.

Financial Administration Law: This is the legislation that backs the collection and use of Government tax revenues.

Funds given to the Government: These are various supports that Development Partners provide to the Government for its development projects. Usually, the Government does not repay these funds.

Funds that the Government gives to government institutions: This is the amount of money that the Government puts into the District Assemblies’ Common Fund (DACF), the Road Fund, the Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), the Petroleum Fund, the National Health Fund, and others.

Gross Domestic Product / Total Output: This is the total of all goods produced and services provided in the country within a specific period of time.

Foreign Reserves of the Country: This is the country's capacity in terms of the foreign currencies it holds for purchasing goods and employing labor from foreign nations over a specific period. It is also the country's capacity regarding the strength of its currency, when compared with that of other foreign nations.

Value of goods: This is the value of goods and services produced within a specific period.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme: This programme is one of the NPP Government's flagship programmes aimed at eradicating poverty and creating equality between the have-nots and the haves, especially through infrastructure development in rural communities.

Payment of Government Debt: This is the debt paid to countries, banks, and financial institutions that lent money to the Government for its development projects. These lenders can be from within the country or from other countries.

Macroeconomic Indicators: These are measurements used to measure economic activities. For example, economic growth, prices of goods, changes that occur in employment matters, and trade with other countries. The success or failure of government economic policies and other measures taken by the Bank of Ghana affect the economy in the country.

Small Business Development Program: Small businesses are often enterprises that employ few people and operate with a small amount of capital.

Contingency Planning: It is the part of the work assigned by the government to an institution or an individual that deals with working on unforeseen events.

Mitigation Plan: This discusses existing measures to reduce the negative impacts of certain events.

Employment for the Unemployed: This is a program initiated by the Government of Ghana to provide employment for tertiary graduates to solve social problems in public and private institutions and to build their vocational skills to promote the economic growth of the country.

Small Scale Industries Board: This is a non-profit government agency. This department is under the supervision of the Ministry of Trade and Industry. The mission of this department is to promote the growth and development of small businesses.

Non-Oil Gross Domestic Product: All income earned by the country through other means. This is the total revenue the government generates from its economic activities, excluding revenue derived from oil; it also takes into account the value of goods.

Non-Tax Revenues: This is Government revenue that does not come from taxes. For example, license fees and fees paid for the use of public properties.

Government Arrears: This includes arrears or amounts that the Government pays to government institutions through the Ministry of Finance, as well as outstanding government debts in the Year 2016.

Budget surplus or deficit: This is the amount of money or debt that remains when the Government subtracts expenditure from its revenue (excluding debt repayment). Often, this amount is measured as a percentage of the country's gross domestic product.

The Public Financial Management Project: The objective of this project is to improve financial systems, fiscal discipline, and accountability in the operations of the Government of Ghana. This project will also ensure the efficient use of Government funds, allocate financial and material resources appropriately, and enhance service delivery through the strengthening of existing systems and protecting the relevant capacity that institutions should possess.
