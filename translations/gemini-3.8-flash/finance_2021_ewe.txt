MESSAGE FROM THE MINISTER FOR FINANCE

In December 2020, Ghanaians voted again for President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to continue the good work he was doing and others for another four years. The confidence that the citizens have in this Government is something we will not take for granted at all.

The 2021 Budget is a significant budget that spoke about how we will rebuild our economy to protect citizens, revive enterprises affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, and do our best to improve the nation of Ghana in the period following the COVID-19 pandemic. We built the Budget on our goal of bringing new transformations to the nation's development in order to be economically self-reliant.

Over most of the past four years, we have revitalized our economy through the implementation of many key initiatives: we promoted macroeconomic stability, began implementing tax enhancement programs, undertook various development projects, supported the private sector, and also promoted social welfare interventions. By the end of 2019, we had brought about significant transformation in our economy. This related to the macroeconomic targets we achieved and how the international community appreciated Ghana's progress during the times of the COVID-19 pandemic. This disease damaged economies across the world, and Ghana was not spared.

This COVID-19 pandemic has brought many challenges to our businesses and our daily lives. However, the government has recognized the impact the disease has had on us as a nation and has put in place various measures to revive businesses and restore normalcy. We have not lost all hope, because we have discovered many new ways to do what is necessary. The government has taken it upon itself to ensure that we are in good health. For this reason, the government has arranged for Ghanaian citizens to be vaccinated against the disease so that we can be strong enough to work toward building the Ghana we desire.

Even though we are in difficult times, you are united with us in carrying out our developmental projects, and we are making efforts so that our economy will rise again for our progress. About 1.2 million students are receiving the appropriate training in secondary schools to build their future. We have resolved the issues concerning the collapsed banks and we have paid the majority of the depositors of these banks. In addition to this, we have established an automobile assembly plant and they have started selling the vehicles produced in this plant, and some are operating on our roads.

In fact, we encountered some challenges in executing our development projects. The government is working hard through various programs to resolve these issues so that we may see relief in our operations. For instance, we supported small businesses with an amount of 750 million Ghana cedis under the Coronavirus Alleviation Programme Business Support Scheme in May 2020, among others.

Now, we are being asked to support each other and work harder than before so that the nation will move forward. We will do this by continuing the important projects we are working on so that we all achieve progress. We must work hard in our endeavors for the development of the nation, because we have pledged from the bottom of our hearts to use all our strength to serve the nation of Ghana.

God bless us all.

May the nation of Ghana live forever!

Ken Ofori-Atta Minister of Finance

PREFACE

1. The 2021 Citizens' Budget is a summary of the projects implemented by the Government in the year 2020 and the estimated revenue expected to protect its programs and carry out development projects in the year 2021. The theme of the 2021 Budget is “Consolidation, Completion and Continuation”. The reason for choosing this theme is that over the past several years, provisions were made for starting new development projects in every new Budget. This practice resulted in ongoing development projects being abandoned. This practice puts pressure on our finances, and also causes us to incur certain outstanding arrears while many contractors are not paid on time for the work done. This leads to the total cost incurred on executing development projects becoming higher than necessary, because interest is paid on the funds that were not paid on time.

2. In the year 2021, the Government made arrangements to work through a new approach. The Government made this plan with the intention of eliminating the backlog of development projects in order to complete the development projects that had been started.

12. This section talks about how the COVID-19 pandemic affected economic activities worldwide, especially in the nation of Ghana. It also talks about the plans the Government made regarding how to curb the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and reduce its negative impacts.

Impacts of the COVID-19 Disease

13. The COVID-19 pandemic that began spreading across the world around the end of 2019 caused extensive damage to households, businesses, and global economies as a whole. It suddenly destabilized many economies, led to widespread job losses, disrupted people's livelihoods, and worsened economic conditions. In addition to this, the disease killed more than 2.6 million people worldwide.

14. In Ghana, up until the last week of July 2020, the spread of the disease increased from 11 people to 781 people per week before decreasing to 104 people in the last week of December 2020. Explanation of some key terms of the financial system

One District One Factory Initiative: Establishing factories and production facilities will enable the country to advance in industrial manufacturing and economic development. The objective of this initiative is to attract numerous employment opportunities into the country so that citizens can find work.

Accountability: It is the duty of the Government to be accountable to the citizens regarding its activities, the use of public funds, and the resources under its control.

Allocation of funds and resources: The arrangement by which the Government allocates funds and resources to government agencies.

Reduction of problems: How to reduce a problem or suffering.

Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics: These subjects are important in education and curriculum development to promote science and technological innovation.

Budget: A budget is a plan that the Government makes regarding where it will get money from and how it will use the money. For example, a family can make a plan regarding the amount of money it has and how it will use it. (e.g. rent, electricity bill, water bill, and food) If the family needs more money, it must borrow money or reduce its spending.

Budget deficit: This happens when the Government spends more money than it receives, or when the amount of money spent is greater than the amount of money received.

The amount of money the government spends on development projects: This is the amount of money the government uses in carrying out major development projects such as road construction, building school buildings, building hospitals, building markets, managing transport affairs, providing good drinking water, expanding roads, and others.

Government Workers' Salaries: Payment of salaries to government workers: This includes the salaries of government workers and other monies that the Government pays to government workers, payments that the Government pays to retired government workers (Social Security), allowances, and others.

Development Partners: These are countries and organizations that provide support to other countries by providing them with funds so that they can achieve their development goals. Partners can be an institution or a group of certain countries. For example, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the World Bank, the African Development Bank (AfDB), and others. The partner can also be just a single country. For example, Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States of America (USA), and others.

Cost of Things at the End of the Year: How the cost of things increases at the end of the year or in December (the Twelfth Month).

Overspending: This occurs when the money spent by the Government is greater than the Budget.

ESLA Act: Energy Sector Levies Act

Evacuation: Procedures followed in evacuation operations from a place.

Free Senior High School Education: The Free Senior High School Policy implemented by the Government of Ghana under the leadership of President Nana Akufo-Addo in September 2017 provided the opportunity for basic school students who were successful in the final examination to attend secondary school for free under the Free Senior High School Policy.

Financial Law: These are laws that support the Government's revenue collection and its utilization.

Monies given to the Government: These are various assistances that development Partners give to the Government for its development work. Most often, the Government does not repay these monies.

Government transfers to government institutions: This is the amount of money the Government puts into the District Assemblies’ Common Fund (DACF), Road Fund, Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFUND), Petroleum Fund, and National Health Insurance Fund, among others.

Aggregate of Goods: This is the total of all goods produced and services performed in the country over a specific period.

The Country's Foreign Reserves: This is the country's ability in the foreign currency it holds to purchase goods and employ workers from foreign countries at a particular time. It is also the country's capacity regarding the strength of its currency when compared to that of other foreign countries.

Value of goods: This is the value of goods and services at a specific time.

Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme: This programme is one of the NPP Government's flagship programmes aimed at eradicating poverty and addressing inequalities, especially infrastructure development in rural communities.

Payment of Government debt: This is the debt paid to nations, banks, and financial institutions that lent money to the Government for its development projects. These lenders can be from within the country or from other countries.

Macroeconomic Indicators: These are measurements used to measure economic activities. For example, economic growth, prices of goods, changes in employment issues, and international trade. The government's economic success or failure and other policies implemented by the Bank of Ghana affect the country's economy.

Small Enterprise Promotion Scheme: Small enterprises are often businesses that employ a few people and operate with a limited amount of capital.

Contingency Planning: This is the part of the work assigned by the government to a department or an individual that deals with handling unplanned events.

Mitigation Plan: This discusses existing measures in place to reduce the impacts of certain events.

Employment for the Unemployed: This is a scheme introduced by the Government of Ghana to provide employment for graduates of tertiary institutions to address social problems in the public and private sectors, build their work skills, and advance the nation's economic development.

Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency: This is a non-profit government agency. This agency is under the supervision of the Ministry of Trade and Industry. The mission of this agency is to promote the growth and development of small enterprises.

Gross Domestic Product Without Oil: All the income the country generates through other means. This is all the money that the Government receives through its economic activities, which does not include the money it receives from oil; this also takes into account the value of goods.

Non-Tax Revenues: This is the money the Government receives that is not from taxes. For example, license fees and fees paid for the use of public properties.

Debts Owed by the Government: This includes debts or amounts paid by the Government to government institutions through the Ministry of Finance, as well as debts owed by the Government in the year 2016.

Surplus or deficit: This is the balance left when the government subtracts its expenditures from its revenue (debt repayments are excluded). Often, this amount is measured as a percentage of the Gross Domestic Product.

The Public Financial Management Programme: The objective of this programme is to improve financial systems, financial management, and reporting within the Government of Ghana. This programme will also ensure the effective use of Government funds, allocate financial and material resources efficiently, and enhance service delivery through the strengthening of existing systems and the protection of the necessary capacity of the institutions.
