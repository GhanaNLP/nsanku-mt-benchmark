14 Read 1 Chronicles 9:2 and the footnote. The “temple servants” were among the first people to return from Babylon to Jerusalem. What they did showed that they had great faith, because not even all the Jews who had gone to Babylon returned to Jerusalem. It may be that some of the Jews had become well-off in Babylon, so it was hard for them to leave behind the good life they had to come and help rebuild Jerusalem. In addition to that, when the Babylonians destroyed Jerusalem, Jehovah protected the descendants of the Gibeonites. Moreover, when the Jews returned from Babylon, Jehovah did not forget the Gibeonites; he allowed them to continue the work they were doing at the temple. Some of the Gibeonites even lived near the temple. (Ezra 2:70; Neh. 11:21) Because of the work they did at the temple, they paid no taxes at all.—Ezra 7:24.

I SOUGHT GOD IN MY CHILDHOOD

I was born and raised in Chicago, Illinois, in the U.S.A. My parents had four children, and I am the eldest. My parents moved from Japan to Chicago; their whole thought was that in America, life would be very good for them. They wanted their children to get a good education and succeed in the future.

My parents were devout Buddhists. In the house where my mother was raised, there was a Buddhist temple standing there. Her father and her two brothers were priests in Buddhism. So during my childhood, we did not take Buddhism lightly at all. Every day when I returned from school, the first thing I did was burn incense for the Buddha idol that stood in our house, and give it rice and water. Then when Sunday came, we would go to the Buddhist temple to bow down to an idol like the one standing in our house, but it was bigger than ours, and it was entirely covered with gold.

When I was about seven years old, I remember looking quietly at the idol in our house one day and asking myself, 'What will happen to me when I die?' Buddhism had taught me that when I died, I would go on to continue my life in the spirit realm. Right then it struck me that, alas, one day my life would come to an end. Perhaps I would be a spirit creature, but I would not be able to enjoy myself the way humans do anymore. I was deeply saddened! Then, all at once, I felt completely devastated.

My father told me: “If you dare drop out of the university, then you will not live in this house!” I was sad and confused. Be quick and settle matters with your brother!

Has a fellow Jehovah’s Witness done something to upset you, and has it now strained your relationship? If so, do not dwell on it at all; settle the matter with your brother so that peace may prevail.*

For example, if someone breaks a bone, they need to see a doctor to have it set so that gradually, the person's bone will heal. If the person just sits by and does not go to see a doctor, the pain will not stop, and the bone might never be able to heal.

Just as a person goes to see a doctor quickly to have a broken bone set, likewise, when we have a problem with a fellow Christian, we must settle it very quickly. If we do not do so, the pain we are experiencing will not go away, and we might harbor anger toward them, which could ultimately ruin our relationship with them completely. Jesus advised us that if we have a matter with someone, we should "settle it quickly with him." (Matt. 5:25) Therefore, if you have a problem with a brother, go to him and talk about it calmly with him. But this is not easy. Let us consider two reasons why we say so.

What Will Help Us Give Encouraging Comments

When we attend meetings, each of us has the opportunity to give comments to encourage our brothers and sisters. (Heb. 10:24, 25) What will help us to give comments that will encourage others?

• Pray to Jehovah. Ask him to help you give comments that will honor him and also encourage others.—Ps. 35:18.

• Make it your goal not to read the answer directly from the publication. When you answer in your own words, it will encourage others and show that you have faith in Jehovah.

• Keep your comments brief. When you attend meetings and give short, clear comments, it truly touches the hearts of others. Furthermore, it gives more people the opportunity to speak.—Prov. 10:19.

• Let your answers encourage others. When giving an answer, do not use it to advise or rebuke the brothers and sisters.

Even if you raise your hand and you are not called on, you can still encourage others. How can you do that? When someone is answering, listen attentively and commend them afterward.—Prov. 10:21.
